Group B · Home & auto services

The trades (HVAC) — your AC dies, you'll pay anything

Urgent, essential, and un-shoppable. The humble furnace-repair shop became a Wall Street asset.

Private equity discovered that boring home-services businesses — HVAC, plumbing, roofing, pest control — are goldmines: essential, recession-resistant, and urgent (your AC dies in a heatwave, you'll pay whatever they ask). So PE buys up local shops, bundles them into regional chains (keeping the names), standardizes prices upward, and pushes service plans and financing.

Why the trades

  • Urgency = no shopping around: nobody compares prices for a 2am emergency plumber.
  • A retiring generation: ~40% of trade-shop owners are 55+, many with no successor — so they sell to PE.
  • Post-acquisition, service calls typically rise 20–30%, with subscription maintenance plans and financing pushed on top.
~2019–2024
The HVAC roll-up wave, backed by Apollo, KKR, Blackstone and smaller funds — chasing a $100B+ fragmented market of 50,000+ independent shops.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Craftsmanship and trust erode as PE optimizes for speed and upsells — technicians pushed to sell premium replacements and warranties rather than just fix the problem, inviting overcharging and litigation risk.

The surprising part

The trades became a hot asset because urgent + essential = price-inelastic. Desperate customers don't comparison-shop, so the pricing power is enormous. Combine that with a wave of retiring tradespeople who have nobody to hand the keys to, and the furnace-repair guy got run through the same playbook as a fast-food franchise: buy, standardize, extract margin, roll up more.

In one sentence

The trades in a nutshell

PE figured out that a plumber's urgent, un-shoppable service is a goldmine when bundled into a priced-up regional chain — so the person who fixes your AC now answers to a fund.
