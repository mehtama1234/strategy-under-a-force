Group B · Home & auto services

Car washes — a gym membership for your car

A one-off $10 wash became a $25/month subscription — that profits most from the members who don't show up.

Car washes became a surprisingly hot roll-up around 2019–2023. Why? The express car wash converts customers to monthly unlimited memberships (~$20–35/month) — recurring, high-margin revenue with 60%+ margins. And most members wash far less than they think, so it's a cash machine, like a gym.

The subscription trick

60–70%
Margins on the membership model. Members typically wash 2–4×/month but expect to wash 10+ — so utilization is 20–40% of perceived value. Mister Car Wash (Apollo-backed) went public in 2021 at ~$1B.

PE bought up independent washes, converted them to memberships, and rolled them into chains — the real estate is a valuable second play (sale-leaseback).

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Unlike a gym, a car wash is truly optional — in a recession, members cancel first. And ~50% churn a year, so the model needs constant new sign-ups to replace them. If acquisition costs rise, margins compress fast.

The surprising part

PE turned a commodity service into a subscription that profits from members who rarely use it — the same "gym membership" arbitrage, but with tangible real estate and weekly customer contact. The washing is almost beside the point; the recurring monthly toll (and the land underneath) is the real, financeable asset.

In one sentence

Car washes in a nutshell

PE turned car washes into subscription toll booths, betting that $25/month memberships would outrun actual demand — a gym for your car, with better real estate.
