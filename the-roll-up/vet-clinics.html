Group A · Your health, rolled up

Vet clinics — your local vet may be owned by a candy company

You won't shop around when your dog is dying. That's exactly why the vet business got rolled up.

Your friendly neighborhood vet is increasingly a subsidiary of a giant. Mars Inc. — the candy company — is the biggest vet provider in America (Banfield, VCA, BluePearl); private equity bought up thousands more. The clinic keeps its old local name, so you don't notice — until the bill arrives.

The consolidation

~30–50%
Share of US general vet practices now corporate-owned (up from ~10% in 2013); over 75% of specialty/ER practices. Mars bought VCA for $9.1B (2017).

Pet-care costs are up ~60% since 2014 — far above inflation — with price jumps of 50–100% reported after acquisition. Senators (Warren, Blumenthal) opened an investigation into Mars in 2024.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The clinic's duty is to your pet; the owner's duty is to a fund seeking 20–30% returns. That structural conflict pushes procedure quotas, upsells, and prices on people who'd pay anything to save an animal.

The surprising part

The fragmented, cash-heavy, emotional vet industry is a perfect roll-up — because a desperate owner won't say no. And the pet-humanization boom (fewer kids, more "fur babies") supercharges it. The tell: it's not even a PE fad — a candy company is the largest owner, which means the consolidation is structural and here to stay. You only discover you've been rolled up when you see the invoice.

In one sentence

Vet clinics in a nutshell

Your local vet is increasingly a debt-financed arm of a giant that keeps the trusted name and raises prices — because your refusal to let your pet die makes you the perfect captive customer.
