Group D · The mechanism & the cost

The roll-up playbook — buy small cheap, bundle big, sell dear

The same six-step trick repeats in every industry. Most of the profit is financial, not operational.

Here's the mechanism under every page. Private equity finds a fragmented industry of small owner-operators (often aging owners ready to retire), buys a "platform" company, then bolts on dozens of small local ones — keeping the names — and turns the bundle into a big chain worth far more than the sum of its parts.

The six steps

  • 1. Find a fragmented market (vets, funerals, HVAC) with thousands of independents.
  • 2. Buy a platform; small operators sell cheap (~4–6× earnings, or 2–3.5× for trades).
  • 3. Roll up dozens more — a bundled chain trades at ~10–15× earnings (even 17–20× for HVAC).
  • 4. Load on debt (~3–5× earnings) to fund buyouts and pay investors early.
  • 5. Cut costs, standardize, raise prices on a now-captive base.
  • 6. Sell the chain in ~5 years for a profit.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the debt knife-edge

If growth stalls or customers flee when prices jump, the debt turns lethal fast — over-leveraged roll-ups (dental, funeral, healthcare chains) have collapsed into bankruptcy, taking jobs and services with them.

The surprising part

The core win isn't running the businesses better — it's "multiple arbitrage." A fragmented market is intrinsically worth less than a consolidated one, even with identical operations: just combining small firms (bought at 4–6×) into a big chain (worth 10–15×) creates paper value out of thin air. Add leverage, and PE can double or triple its money without improving anything. It's financial alchemy — and when the roll-up stalls, that same leverage compounds the collapse.

In one sentence

The roll-up playbook in a nutshell

PE bets that the financial alchemy of consolidation — multiple arbitrage plus leverage — will outrun the operational risk of a debt-loaded, price-dependent chain in a fragmented market.
