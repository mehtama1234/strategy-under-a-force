Group C · The quiet cash cows

Funeral homes — death is the perfect roll-up

Emotional, urgent, once-in-a-lifetime. Grieving families don't comparison-shop — and the industry knows it.

The funeral industry is quietly consolidated. Service Corporation International (SCI) — the giant — owns ~1,500+ funeral homes and cemeteries, mostly under local family names it keeps on purpose so you don't realize. It's a perfect roll-up: emotional, urgent, once-in-a-lifetime, where grieving families pay for "the best" without shopping around.

The premium

+47–72%
How much more a consolidator-owned home charges vs an independent for identical services. SCI bought Stewart Enterprises for $2.1B (2013); funeral homes now sell for 7–9× revenue (up from 3–5×).

With ~46% of funeral directors retiring within five years, a succession vacuum is feeding the consolidation.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Families don't realize the "local" home they trust is owned by a multi-billion-dollar corporation. Keeping the original name hides the ownership and exploits grief-driven price insensitivity.

The surprising part

The best part isn't even the funeral — it's the "pre-need" float. Families buy plans years in advance (lock in a ~$5,400 funeral at today's prices), so the company gets the money now but delivers the service years later — and invests the cash in between. For a chain with thousands of pre-need customers, that's a hidden finance business wrapped inside a funeral home, disguised as price protection. (It's the float trick again.)

In one sentence

Funeral homes in a nutshell

SCI owns ~1,500 funeral homes under local names nobody recognizes as corporate, charges 50–70% more, and collects billions upfront in pre-need plans — making death the perfect roll-up.
