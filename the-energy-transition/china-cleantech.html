Group D · The petrostates & geopolitics

China's clean-tech grip — it quietly built the factories for all of it

While the West debated climate policy, China built near-total control of the machines the transition runs on.

Here's the quiet geopolitical story of the whole transition. It was supposed to free the West from Middle-East oil. Instead it risks swapping that for a new dependence — on Chinese factories. While democracies argued about climate targets, China simply built the machines: the panels, the batteries, the EVs, and the refining behind them.

The grip, in numbers

~80–85%
Of the world's solar panels are made in China (2024).
~65–70%
Of global battery cells (CATL + BYD lead); China also refines ~85–90% of rare earths and ~60% of lithium, and makes ~60% of the world's EVs.

The West's late scramble

  • US Inflation Reduction Act (2022): $369B for clean energy with "made-here" rules — but domestic capacity is growing from a low base, slower than China grows.
  • EU tariffs (Oct 2024): up to ~38% extra on Chinese EVs, after a subsidy probe. The official word became "de-risking," not "decoupling."
  • China's leverage: in 2023–24 it restricted exports of gallium, germanium, and graphite — a quiet reminder of who holds the tap.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The supply chain is so concentrated that China could use it as leverage in a future crisis. A deliberate slowdown in batteries, solar, or rare earths would cripple the West's transition faster than the West could build substitutes. The asymmetry is the point: China has the choke, the West has only expensive, slow-to-build alternatives.

The surprising part

This isn't a resource monopoly like OPEC — China doesn't sit on the world's copper or lithium. It's a manufacturing monopoly, built with cost, scale, and decades of state industrial policy. And the most uncomfortable twist: the West handed it over. Rich countries outsourced the dirty, low-margin work of refining and battery-making to China back in the 1990s–2000s — then realized, too late, that the clean-energy future depended on exactly those factories. The dependence isn't an accident; we built it ourselves. This is the deep link to the US–China decoupling.

In one sentence

China's clean-tech grip in a nutshell

The transition swapped the West's dependence on Middle-East oil for dependence on Chinese factories — a shift from fuel to machines that China won quietly, with industrial policy, while everyone else debated climate policy.
