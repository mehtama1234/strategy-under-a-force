Group B · The disrupted incumbents

Gas stations — the money was never in the gas

A gas station is really a convenience store with pumps out front. Electric cars break the store, not just the pump.

Here's what most people get wrong: gas stations barely make money on gasoline. Fuel is a 1–2% margin loss-leader — it exists to pull you in. The real profit is the store inside: coffee, snacks, cigarettes, drinks, at 20–30% margins. Electric cars quietly attack that model from two sides.

The two-sided hit

Fuel sales fade

Slowly, over decades, gasoline volume shrinks as EVs grow (already ~14% of new US car sales in 2024).

The foot traffic breaks

About 80% of EV charging happens at home. And a charge takes 20–40 minutes, not 5 — so the "quick splash-and-dash into the store" habit disappears.

80% at home
Share of EV charging done at home overnight — for free, in the driveway. That's the customer who used to walk into the store twice a week, gone.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the foot-traffic collapse

The store was cross-subsidized by drivers popping in during quick fill-ups. Swap six quick visits a week for one 30-minute charge a month, and snack-and-drink revenue craters. The danger zone is roughly 2025–2035: fuel revenue halves, but store traffic doesn't come back, forcing thousands of small stations to close.

How some adapt

The survivors go big: Buc-ee's and Sheetz-style megastores with real food, seating, and destinations you drive to — places where a 20-minute charge is a feature, not a problem. But that costs $4–8 million a location, and about 80% of US stations are small franchises that can't afford it.

The surprising part

The transition doesn't kill "gas stations" so much as it kills the independent owner. Big oil brands are quietly turning their flagship sites into "energy hubs" with fast chargers and bigger stores. The 60% of forecourts run by small independents are the ones that vanish. And it's slower than it looks — even at 30% EVs, most miles are still gasoline; the pain is the awkward in-between decade, not a sudden death.

In one sentence

Gas stations in a nutshell

Because the profit was always in the store, not the fuel, electric cars hurt gas stations most by killing the quick-visit foot traffic — pushing the industry toward big corporate "energy hubs" while thousands of small corner stations quietly close.
