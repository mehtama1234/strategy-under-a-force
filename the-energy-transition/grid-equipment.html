Group A · The picks & shovels

Grid equipment — the boring transformer is the real bottleneck

Everyone watches solar panels and EVs. The thing actually holding up the grid is a giant metal box nobody thinks about.

A transformer is the unglamorous box that steps electricity up and down as it moves along the grid. For 30 years, utilities treated it as a cheap afterthought. Now there's a global shortage: wait times blew out from about a year to four years, prices jumped, and the sleepy companies that make this gear are sold out. It's the ultimate picks-and-shovels play — they sell to everyone, no matter which energy source wins.

Why it happened

  • Decades of underinvestment. The gear was treated as a commodity; nobody built extra capacity.
  • The workforce retired. Winding and testing a transformer is a craft skill; the average worker is ~55, and there's no pipeline. Retraining takes 2–3 years.
  • Demand shocked the system. Renewables and grid upgrades needed more — and then AI data centers arrived and blew past even that.
The order books tell the story

GE Vernova's electrification backlog blew past $40 billion, with orders up ~86% in a year. Siemens Energy, Hitachi Energy, Eaton, and Prysmian are in the same boat — booked out for years. You can't buy your way to the front of the line; there is no line left.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The odd danger here isn't a shortage — it's that lead times get so long that customers give up. If a utility can't get a transformer for four years, it may simply cancel the solar farm, the data center, or the transmission line. The bottleneck stops being a delay and starts killing projects outright.

The surprising part

The plot twist is who actually caused the crunch: not wind and solar, but AI data centers. Renewable projects come and go in waves, but a data center is a brand-new kind of customer — huge, steady, and permanent — that the industry had zero reason to prepare for. The most futuristic thing in the economy (AI) is bottlenecked by one of the least glamorous (a copper-and-steel box invented over a century ago).

Grid equipment in a nutshell

Decades of neglect left the grid's most boring part — the transformer — sold out for four years, turning sleepy electrical-gear makers into one of the transition's most valuable chokepoints.
