Group D · The petrostates & geopolitics

The Gulf petrostates — spending oil money to escape oil

Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Qatar are racing to build a post-oil economy before the world needs less oil.

These are whole countries whose economy is one product. They know the world will eventually need less of it. So they're in a race: convert oil money into a post-oil economy — sovereign wealth funds, tourism, sport, AI — before oil demand peaks. The bind: they fund that race by pumping oil harder, which only works if the price stays high.

The war chest

~$925B
Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF), the world's third-largest — buying stakes in AI, tourism, and sport (LIV Golf, football stars). Abu Dhabi's Mubadala (~$250B) and Qatar's fund (~$475B) do the same.

The bind

Spend big now

Vision 2030 and megaprojects like NEOM/"The Line" were pitched at ~$500B, and Saudi Arabia had spent ~$100B by 2024 — though timelines and budgets were quietly scaled back.

Needs high oil to pay

Saudi Arabia needs roughly $80–90 a barrel to balance its budget. In 2025–26 oil often traded below that — so the ambition is outrunning the income.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the clock vs. the budget

If oil falls below ~$60 a barrel and stays there, the budget breaks before the new economy is ready to fund itself. That forces cuts to the very megaprojects and investments meant to replace oil — a downward spiral that deflates the whole plan.

The surprising part

Their best weapon against the transition is the very thing the transition threatens: cheap energy. The Gulf states are now racing to host AI data centers, because their power costs a fraction of US rates. So they're using cheap, oil-era energy to build the digital economy that's meant to replace oil. If it works, they become "the Saudi Arabia of computing." If it fails, they've just spent their one advantage speeding toward their own obsolescence.

In one sentence

The Gulf petrostates in a nutshell

They're gambling peak-era oil revenue on building a post-oil economy of funds, tourism, and AI — a plan that works only if oil prices stay high long enough to pay for their own escape from oil.
