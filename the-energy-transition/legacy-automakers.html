Group B · The disrupted incumbents

Legacy automakers — a century of engine skill, suddenly worthless

Ford, GM, VW, Toyota are caught in a trap: too fast bleeds cash, too slow lets Tesla and BYD eat them.

The old carmakers are stuck in a genuine bind. Their gas trucks and SUVs still earn nearly all their profit, while their EV divisions lose billions. They have to spend enormous sums reinventing themselves for electric — but go too fast and they bleed money, go too slow and cheaper, better EV-native rivals corner them in a shrinking gas market.

The losses are real

$5.1B
Ford's EV division (Model e) loss in 2024, with similar losses guided for 2025 — even as EV sales dipped.
$65B+
Total losses and write-downs across the industry (2024–2026) as automakers scaled back and cancelled EV plans amid softer demand.

Losing the race on the numbers

  • BYD passed Tesla to become the world's biggest EV seller (~2.26M in 2025). Volkswagen and Volvo each hold only ~7–8% of the global battery-EV market; BYD holds ~22%.
  • Software is the new engine — and they're slow. Legacy makers take roughly twice as long as Tesla and BYD to build car software, because they stitch together supplier systems instead of building it whole.
  • The 2024–25 pullback. As EV demand growth slowed, GM, Ford, and others delayed targets and cut production — buying time, but also ceding ground.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

If they can't make their EV divisions profitable within 3–5 years, the gas-truck cash that funds the whole transition runs out before they reach cost parity — and they lose to scaled, integrated rivals like BYD. The clock is running, and it's not on their side.

The surprising part

Here's the cruel twist. The engine and gearbox — the disciplines these companies spent a century perfecting, worth tens of billions in accumulated know-how — are now nearly worthless. An electric car is mostly a battery, motors, and software; Toyota's legendary engine mastery doesn't transfer. They're competing for the future with skills they don't have, while exiting the business that made them who they are. Tesla and BYD, which never had to unlearn anything, just move faster.

In one sentence

Legacy automakers in a nutshell

The transition made a century of engine expertise worthless overnight and trapped the old carmakers between bleeding cash on EVs they must build and losing time if they don't — while EV-native rivals who never had to unlearn anything pull ahead.
