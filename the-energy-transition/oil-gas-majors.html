Group B · The disrupted incumbents

Oil & gas majors — record profits now, peak demand later

Their whole business is selling fuel over and over, forever. The transition threatens exactly that.

Big oil companies (ExxonMobil, Chevron, Shell, BP, TotalEnergies) live on recurring revenue — you buy their fuel again tomorrow, and the day after, forever. The transition puts a question mark over that with one idea: "peak oil demand," the point where the world stops needing more oil each year. But the response split in a surprising way.

The great split: green got punished

Europe tried to pivot

BP promised to cut oil and pile into renewables (2020). Its stock lagged. By 2025 it reversed — slashing green spending ~£5B/year, pushing oil-and-gas back up. Shell did a softer version of the same U-turn.

America doubled down

Exxon and Chevron mostly stayed with oil — and made record money. They then spent big to get more oil: Exxon bought Pioneer (~$60B), Chevron bought Hess (~$53B).

~$92B
Combined 2022 profit for Exxon and Chevron alone — the highest ever — as the Ukraine war pushed oil past $100 a barrel.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the sunk-cost trap

Oil projects take 15–20 years to pay back. If peak demand arrives before then, the billions poured into new wells today become stranded — assets with no buyer and no resale value. The faster the transition, the worse the trap. Forecasters violently disagree on timing: the IEA sees oil demand peaking around 2027; OPEC says it keeps rising past 2045.

The surprising part

The market flipped the moral of the story. Going green got you punished; staying oil got you rewarded — at least in the short run. BP's climate ambition tanked its share price; Exxon's oil focus and cash payouts made investors love it. By 2025 even the European majors had to walk their climate promises back or face shareholder revolts. Their real hedge became carbon capture — a way to keep selling oil while claiming to be low-carbon — rather than actually switching businesses.

In one sentence

Oil & gas majors in a nutshell

The transition threatens their sell-fuel-forever model with peak demand, but for now the market rewards pumping oil and punishes going green, so the majors are banking record profits while quietly betting the peak stays far away.
