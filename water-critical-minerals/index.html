Water & Critical Minerals

How the digital, electric, AI economy turned out to run on very physical, very limited stuff.

The 28th force. For a generation we treated raw materials as infinite and cheap and obsessed over software — but software runs on silicon, and silicon runs on water and rare minerals. Then three things landed at once: electrify everything, reshore the supply chains, and a warming climate drying the mines. Suddenly the constraint isn't money or code but water in the ground and metal in the rock. The one idea: the atoms, not the bits, became the limit — and whoever controls the refinery controls the chain.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did water and mineral scarcity make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? Making things was never the constraint; the water to process the ore, and the refinery that turns rock into battery-grade material, became the scarce, contested prize. The danger: the clean, electric future has a very physical, very dirty, very concentrated supply chain.
