# Water & Critical Minerals — strategy under a force

The 28th collection (Tier-2) — Family I (macro forces).

**The force:** the physical stuff modern life is made of — fresh water and a handful of critical minerals (lithium, copper, cobalt, nickel, rare earths, gallium) — is becoming scarce, concentrated in a few countries, and strategically contested. After decades of treating inputs as abundant and cheap, companies and countries are discovering that the digital, electric, AI-driven economy runs on very physical, very limited raw material. We ask: *what did water/mineral scarcity make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** the digital economy runs on very physical stuff — and the atoms, not the bits, are becoming the limit. Electrifying everything, reshoring factories, and a warming climate all landed at once, and suddenly the constraint isn't money or software but water in the ground and metal in the rock. Whoever controls the input — the aquifer, the mine, and above all the refinery that processes the ore — controls the industry built on top of it.
