Group E · The big picture

The pet economy

The pet became a child — and a $150-billion market grew around the idea.

Pull the thirteen pages together and one shift explains all of them: pets stopped being animals and became family members — for many owners, child substitutes. That reframing unleashed spending across food, medicine, insurance, retail, tech, and services. And underneath it sits a demographic engine: as people have fewer children, the parental money and love go to pets instead.

The one idea

The pet became a child. Be fair, though — it overstates the universal case. Humanization is most intense among younger, urban, and childless households; plenty of owners still see pets more functionally. But at the margin that sets prices, a vocal premium tier treats pets as children, and the whole market reorganized around them.

The whole force in numbers

~$150B
Annual US pet spending (2024) — rivaling what families spend on childcare, and climbing 7–10% a year.
1.6
US children per woman (2024–25), down from 2.1 — the demographic collapse that redirects parental spending to pets.
70% vs 40%
Households with pets versus with children under 18 — pets now outnumber kids in American homes.
2.5×
How much more Gen Z and Millennials spend per pet than Boomers — the humanized generation drives the growth.
+67%
Vet-cost rise since 2014, far above inflation — the source of both the healthcare boom and the affordability gap.
4.3%
US pet-insurance penetration — tiny vs 40% in Sweden, which is why investors see a long runway.

Winners vs losers

Winners
Premium and fresh food, pet pharma (Zoetis), insurers, specialist and corporate vet chains, Chewy, pet tech, breeders of designer dogs.
Losers
Mass-market kibble losing share, independent vets without specialist scale, budget pet stores, and shelters full of older and special-needs animals.
The biggest tension

Humanization created a guilt-driven, two-tier system. Owners feel they must spend $5,000–10,000+ a year or fail their "family member" — while lower-income owners face impossible choices, and 30–40% of clients skip treatments over cost. Wealthy outliers keep raising the baseline; everyone else lives with the guilt.

The surprising part

If this were really about the pet as family, rescue adoption would surge. Instead, designer breeds boom while shelters struggle. "Pet as child" often means "pet as my child, customized" — a puppy shaped to preference, the way a parent imagines shaping a kid. The humanization is real, but it's a humanization of ownership, not of universal animal welfare.

In one sentence

The pet economy is the residue of the demographic cliff — childless-by-choice and delaying adults pouring parental spending into animals — building a $150B market that splits into premium-and-guilty and affordable-but-guilty.

How this connects to the rest of the series

The pet economy sits between two siblings. Its deepest driver is the demographic cliff — fewer kids redirect parental spending. Its healthcare layer is a case of the roll-up — private equity and Mars consolidating local vets. And the "insure the pet, skip your own dental" instinct rhymes with the emotional, non-rational spending seen across Last Call and the wellness economy.
