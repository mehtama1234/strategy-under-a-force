Chewy & pet retail

A multi-billion business built by turning pet grief into a moat.

Chewy turned pet supplies into a subscription e-commerce giant — famous less for prices than for emotional loyalty. It sends handwritten cards and flowers when a pet dies, and paints portraits of customers' pets. Now it's using that trust to move into pet health: pharmacy, telehealth, insurance, and its own vet clinics.

The core bet

Chewy bet that owners treat pets as family worth premium, recurring care — and built a machine on auto-ship subscriptions plus genuine emotional loyalty. The next bet: that same trust lets it capture the vet visit too, moving from supplies to being your pet's pharmacy and doctor.

What the numbers say

$12.6B
Chewy revenue in fiscal 2025, up ~6%. A remarkable 83% of sales come from auto-ship subscriptions — recurring, sticky revenue.
21.3M
Active customers, each spending about $591 a year. The subscription model turns a bag of dog food into a relationship.
11M
Handwritten cards Chewy has sent — plus sympathy flowers when a pet passes. A founding practice, not a marketing stunt.
18 → 47
Owned vet clinics (18 by late 2025), expanding toward ~47 with the planned Modern Animal acquisition — moving Chewy into owning the vet experience itself.

Weaponized sympathy

Chewy's edge isn't convenience or price — it's emotion. When a pet dies, it sends flowers and a note, not an upsell. That gesture is so far from normal retail that it becomes unforgettable, earning billions in lifetime loyalty. Now it's spending that goodwill to become your pet's full healthcare provider.

What's newly valuable
Auto-ship recurring revenue, emotional loyalty, and a trusted brand to extend into pharmacy, telehealth, and clinics.
What's hard
Scaling a hand-written-card culture from warehouses to 47 clinics staffed by people, where turnover can erode the feeling.
The single biggest worry

Emotional loyalty is easy in a warehouse, hard in a clinic. Staff turnover could undermine the culture that built the brand — and regulators may push back on a retailer vertically integrating into vet care.

The surprising part

Chewy built a $12.6B business partly by weaponizing sympathy — flowers and handwritten notes at a pet's death, with no ask attached. It's the opposite of a sales tactic, which is exactly why it works. And now it's buying vet clinics to own the whole lifecycle, from puppy food to end-of-life care.

In one sentence

Chewy turned pet grief into a moat and recurring auto-ship into a habit — and is now buying vet clinics to own everything from the food bowl to the final visit.
