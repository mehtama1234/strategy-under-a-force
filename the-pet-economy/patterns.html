The patterns

Line up everyone the humanized pet touched, and one theme dominates: the pet became a child, and value flows to whoever premiumizes and medicalizes it.

Each page asks: what did pet humanization make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable for this company? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

Reframe an animal as a child, and an owner will spend like a parent — on premium food, advanced medicine, insurance, and services they'd never buy for "a pet." The emotional word "pet parent" is worth more than any product; it's the permission slip for the whole market. Underneath sits a demographic engine: fewer kids, more of the parental budget going to pets.
