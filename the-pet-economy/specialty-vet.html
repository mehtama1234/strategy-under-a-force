Group B · Pet healthcare

Specialty vet

MRIs, chemotherapy, and $10,000 bills — human medicine, for pets.

Pets now get human-grade advanced medicine: MRI and CT scans, chemotherapy and radiation for cancer, cardiology, orthopedic surgery, 24/7 ER hospitals, even blood banks. Owners will spend five figures to extend a pet's life. The line between "pet care" and "hospital care" has genuinely blurred.

The core bet

The bet is that owners who see a pet as family will pay for the same medicine a person would get. Build the specialist infrastructure — oncology, imaging, surgery, emergency — and a serious pet illness becomes a five-figure decision people are willing to make.

What the numbers say

$2,500–6,000
Cost of a dog MRI; CT scans run $1,200–3,500. Imaging that once seemed absurd for an animal is now routine at specialty hospitals.
$3,000–10,000
A full course of chemotherapy for a dog; radiation runs $4,500–10,000. Veterinary oncology is now a board-certified subspecialty with its own hospitals.
$269B
Global pet-healthcare market in 2025, up from ~$256B in 2024 — roughly 9% growth, driven by advanced care.
blood banks
24/7 specialty hospitals now run canine and feline blood banks — one Portland hospital performed ~500 transfusions in a year and shipped 200+ units nationally.

A parallel hospital system

A dog can now get an oncology consult, staged imaging, and a combined surgery-chemo-radiation plan — protocols lifted straight from human cancer medicine. Some owners finance $10,000–20,000+ treatments over months. "It's just a pet" has been replaced by "my pet deserves the same care my family would get."

What's newly valuable
Specialist hospitals, imaging, oncology, ER care, and the financing products that let owners say yes to a huge bill.
What's painful
Only wealthy or insured owners can afford it, leaving many facing an impossible choice.
The single biggest worry

Cost-driven euthanasia. Over 30% of owners avoid the vet to dodge bills, and even insured owners skip care over cost. Advanced medicine exists — but for a growing share of families it's out of reach, forcing a choice between death and debt.

The surprising part

A dog can now get the exact diagnostic workup and treatment a human would — oncology consults, staged scans, multi-part therapy. Veterinary oncology has board certifications, dedicated facilities, and protocols copied from human cancer care. The medicine caught up to the emotion; the affordability didn't.

In one sentence

The pet economy built a parallel hospital system where animals get human-grade medicine — turning a serious illness into a five-figure decision that not every family can make.
