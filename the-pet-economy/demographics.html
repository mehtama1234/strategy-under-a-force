Group D · The forces & the limits

Fewer kids

The deep driver: the money and love once meant for children, redirected to pets.

Why did pets become children? Because fewer people are having children. Birth rates have fallen to record lows, marriage comes later, and Millennials and Gen Z are pouring the money, time, and parental emotion once meant for kids into pets instead. This is the pet economy's direct link to the sibling demographic cliff.

The core bet

Falling birth rates freed up a huge pool of parental spending and feeling. That energy is flowing into pets, making them "starter families" or full substitutes for the children many are delaying or forgoing. The pet economy is, in large part, the residue of a demographic shift.

What the numbers say

1.6
US children per woman in 2024–25 — down from the 2.1 replacement rate and the lowest on record.
70% vs 40%
US households with pets (70%) versus households with children under 18 (40%) — a historic inversion. Pet homes now outnumber kid homes.
$6,103
What a Gen Z owner spends on pets a year, and $5,150 for Millennials — versus $2,454 for Boomers. A 2.5× generational jump in per-pet spending.
43.5%
One-year jump in Gen Z pet-owning households (to ~18.8M in 2024). "DINKWAD" — double income, no kids, with a dog — became a recognized lifestyle.

Symptom or cause?

It's tempting to say people are consciously choosing pets over kids. The honest version is subtler: housing, childcare, and health costs make both delayed parenthood and pet ownership look rational. The money didn't vanish — it migrated. Pets may be less a deliberate replacement than the affordable outlet for parental instinct in an expensive era.

What's newly valuable
Everything aimed at the childless-by-choice or delaying household — premium food, insurance, tech, experiences that echo raising a kid.
The caveat
Delayed marriage explains much of the fertility drop too — pets may be a symptom of economic anxiety, not its cause.
The single biggest worry

If pets are riding a demographic and affordability wave rather than driving one, the economic anxiety beneath both trends is the real story — and a shift in that anxiety (or in birth rates) could change the pet economy's trajectory in ways the industry can't control.

The surprising part

As birth rates hit record lows, pet spending by young people outpaced spending on children — and in many homes, pets now outnumber kids. Yet surveys show only mild evidence people are consciously choosing pets instead of children. The shift happened quietly, through affordability math, not ideology.

In one sentence

Younger generations are pouring the parental love and money they can't afford to spend on kids into pets — and the pet economy is growing faster than the child market ever did.
