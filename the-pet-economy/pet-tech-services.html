Group C · The pet lifestyle

Pet tech & services

Airbnb for dogs, GPS collars, pet cameras, and DNA tests.

Humanized pets get the full consumer-tech stack — pointed at animals. Dog-walking and sitting apps replaced the neighbor down the street; smart collars track a dog's location and health; cameras narrate what your pet is doing; DNA kits map its breed and disease risk. The same wearables, cameras, genomics, and gig-economy platforms humans use, now for pets.

The core bet

The bet is that pet owners will adopt the same digital services and gadgets they use for themselves — and pay recurring fees for them. Turn neighborhood favors (a dog-sitter, a walker) into app-based marketplaces, and add hardware and subscriptions on top.

What the numbers say

$2.3B
What Blackstone paid to acquire Rover (the dog-sitting marketplace) in 2024 — up nearly 2× from its 2021 public valuation in about two years. Peak institutional interest.
Rover / Wag
App-based marketplaces that turned dog-walking and boarding into a gig economy — "Airbnb for dogs" — taking a cut of every booking.
50,000+
Five-star reviews for Embark, the dog DNA test — a sign that mapping a pet's breed and health genetics went mainstream.
CES 2025
Satellite pet trackers, AI cameras that describe a pet's movements, and health-monitoring collars all launched — pet gadgets now debut alongside human electronics.

The full stack, pointed down

Pets now have DNA profiles, GPS trackers, AI cameras, health-monitoring wearables, portion-controlled auto-feeders, and telehealth vet consults. It's near feature-parity with human consumer tech. The shift is subtle but real: a pet is no longer just part of the household — it's an individual with its own data profile, health record, and service subscriptions.

What's newly valuable
Gig-economy marketplaces, recurring hardware subscriptions (trackers, cameras, feeders), DNA and telehealth — all extracting data and fees.
What's thin
Dog-walking and sitting are commoditized and margin-light; the unit economics are harder than the growth suggests.
The single biggest worry

The gig-service core is commoditized. Marketplaces like Rover and Wag rely on network effects, but sitters can defect to direct booking to skip the take rate — so profit depends on either higher fees (risking an exodus) or bolting on premium services.

The surprising part

A pet now has proprietary DNA testing, satellite GPS, an AI camera that narrates its behavior, a health-tracking collar, and a telehealth vet — the same categories as human wearables, genomics, and telehealth, just aimed at an animal. Pets went from home utilities to individual consumption units with their own data.

In one sentence

Pet tech replaced the neighborhood dog-walker with app marketplaces, wearables, cameras, and DNA tests — giving every pet its own data profile and stack of subscriptions.
