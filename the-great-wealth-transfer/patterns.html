The synthesis · what repeats across all 13

The patterns

Line up every corner of the handover — the heirs, the advisors, the insurers, the estate machinery — and the same handful of mechanics repeat, all flowing from one fact: the biggest pile of money in history is moving.

Each page asks: what did the great wealth transfer make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

The richest generation in history is dying, and ~$84–124 trillion is changing hands. The scarce, valuable thing isn't the money — it's the trusted relationship with whoever holds it, and increasingly with the heir who will inherit it, because whoever manages the money when it transfers keeps the fees for decades. So advice, estate planning, insurance, and even the family business became assets to consolidate — and the money mostly pools with big platforms, not the individual advisor or, really, most families.
