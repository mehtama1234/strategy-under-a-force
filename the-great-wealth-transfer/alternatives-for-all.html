Group C · The products & the plumbing

Alternatives for all

'Democratizing' private markets mostly democratizes the fees — and the illiquidity that can trap your cash.

Having saturated big institutions, the private-market giants — Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Ares — are chasing the wealth of individuals with semi-liquid funds, private-credit products, and interval funds aimed at the high-net-worth and mass-affluent investors inheriting money. The wealth transfer is the bullseye.

The core bet

The big private-market firms ran low on new institutional money, so they're opening private equity and private credit to individuals — chasing the trillions in the wealth transfer.

The illiquidity that protects the fund can trap you

These products look liquid — quarterly or monthly redemptions — but they can freeze exactly when you need the money, as BREIT's gates showed. The same lock-up that lets the fund hold long-term assets and claim higher returns transfers the risk to the individual, while the fund keeps earning fees either way.

The single biggest worry

The products are illiquid and can gate withdrawals, and many retail investors don't grasp that 'semi-liquid' means you might not get your cash back on demand. Institutions negotiate better terms; individuals pay the menu price.

The surprising part

The blunt read on 'democratizing' private markets is that it mainly democratizes the fees. Individuals get the 1–2% annual drag and the illiquidity risk; the manager gets a vast new pool of permanent-ish capital and the same rich fee stream public markets no longer tolerate.

In one sentence

The private-market giants are opening the vault to individuals not to share the returns but to capture the wealth transfer — charging fees, and passing on illiquidity, that public markets rejected.
