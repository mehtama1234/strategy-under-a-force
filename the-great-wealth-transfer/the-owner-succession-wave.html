Group A · The handover itself

The owner-succession wave

Millions of profitable businesses have owners retiring with no one to take over — the 'silver tsunami.'

A huge share of U.S. small and mid-sized businesses are owned by boomers who are retiring, often with no successor. This 'silver tsunami' of business sales is a major channel of the wealth transfer — the business is the asset that gets sold and passed on — and it feeds private-equity roll-ups, search funds, and entrepreneurship-through-acquisition (ETA).

The core bet

Millions of profitable small businesses have owners retiring with no heir to run them, so whoever can buy them cheaply and bundle them captures a generational transfer of Main Street.

What the numbers say

~50%
Share of U.S. small businesses owned by people 55+, with median owner age around 58.
~$30T
Business assets expected to change hands over the coming decade as boomers retire.
$500k–$2M
What a searcher raises to buy and run their first business — ETA is now a recognized career path.
~30%
Owners with no formal succession plan; many plan to sell to a stranger, not family.
2010+
When PE roll-ups accelerated in fragmented trades — HVAC, dental, accounting, pest control.

Boring businesses became hot assets

Plumbing, HVAC, tax accounting, landscaping, pest control — the unglamorous, sticky, profitable businesses became prized acquisition targets precisely because of who owns them (retiring boomers) and how loyal their customers are. Buying a retiring owner's company turned from a family handoff into a structured financial product.

The single biggest worry

Many of these businesses never actually sell, because they're too dependent on the owner — remove the founder and the value collapses, so the sale price is a fraction of what the owner hoped. A lot of Main Street value simply evaporates rather than transfers.

The surprising part

The surprise is how institutionalized the handoff became. What used to be 'sell it to my kid or close the doors' is now a recognized asset class with its own playbook — search funds, ETA programs, and PE aggregators all competing to catch the businesses before their owners retire.

In one sentence

Millions of aging owners with no heirs are selling profitable companies to PE buyers and searchers, transferring a generation of Main Street wealth — while many businesses that can't find a buyer just disappear.
