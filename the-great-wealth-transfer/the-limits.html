Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

The limits

Concentrated at the top, spent by 'die with zero,' or quietly eaten by the cost of care.

This is the skeptical piece. The rosy '$84T for everyone' story has big holes: the money is concentrated in the top ~10%, so most people inherit little; boomers may spend it down ('die with zero'); and long-term care and end-of-life healthcare can consume an estate before it ever transfers. The addressable transfer is far smaller than the headline, and it concentrates wealth rather than spreading it.

The core bet

The transfer is smaller and more concentrated than the headline suggests — much of the money is top-heavy, may be spent, or eaten by end-of-life costs before it ever moves.

Care costs are a silent wealth eraser

A boomer with a $1M nest egg can watch $600k+ vanish in nursing care before dying, leaving heirs with little — yet the headline still counts the full $1M as 'transfer wealth' until you look at who actually receives what. Long-term care quietly consumes the inheritance before anyone sees it.

The single biggest worry

For the wealth industry, the real market is a fraction of the headline. For society, the transfer concentrates wealth further — only the already-rich have estates big enough to survive care costs and reach the next generation intact.

The surprising part

The reframe that punctures the hype: this isn't a broad wealth spreading, it's a concentrated cleanup. The top 10% pass money to the next top 10%, most everyone else inherits medical bills, and the gap between them widens rather than closes.

In one sentence

The great wealth transfer is really a concentrated cleanup — the top 10% pass money to the next top 10%, most people inherit little, and end-of-life costs quietly eat the rest.
