Insurance & annuities

Your grandmother's annuity now quietly funds a private-equity loan book.

Life insurance (a tax-smart way to pass money on) and annuities (turning a nest egg into income) are core to the wealth transfer. The big shift: private-equity and alternative-asset managers bought insurers to get 'permanent capital' — the huge, long-term pool of policyholder money (the 'float') — and invest it in higher-yielding private credit.

The core bet

Retiring boomers need income and tax-smart ways to pass money on, so annuities and life insurance boom — and PE firms bought the insurers to control the huge, long-term pool of money and invest it their way.

Insurance as a permanent-capital machine

PE didn't win insurance through investing genius — it used retirees' need for guaranteed income as cheap, permanent funding, then lent that money out at higher rates through private credit. The annuity that promises grandma $30k a year is, under the hood, financing loans to companies and real estate.

The single biggest worry

PE-owned insurers loading up on riskier private credit worries regulators and rating agencies: if those loans sour in a downturn, the 'guaranteed' income that annuities are sold on could be threatened, and fire-sales could follow.

The surprising part

The quiet transformation is what insurance became. A product sold as the safest thing in finance turned into a way for private-equity firms to raise permanent, low-cost money and invest it aggressively — so the safety the customer buys and the risk the owner takes now sit uncomfortably close together.

In one sentence

Private equity bought the insurance industry to turn retiring Americans' paychecks and legacies into permanent capital for private-credit lending.
