The Great Wealth Transfer

The richest generation in history is dying — and whoever holds the money when it moves keeps the fees for decades.

The 41st force. Over roughly 2024–2045, an estimated $84 trillion — newer counts run to ~$124 trillion — passes from the Silent Generation and boomers to their heirs, spouses, and charities: the largest handover of money in history. It reshapes wealth management, financial advice, estate and tax planning, insurance, and it drives the aging-owner business-sale wave that feeds the roll-ups. The one idea: the biggest pile of money ever is moving, so everyone races to be holding the relationship when it does — because whoever manages the money as it transfers keeps the fees for a generation.

Seeded by the Inc. 5000 data "Financial Plumbing" is a top fast-growing theme — 677 companies, ~$44.4B represented — thick with wealth-tech, advice platforms, insurance, and money-movement firms, and "generational wealth transfer" recurs as a growth driver. This collection follows that money.
The one question, asked of everyone

What did the great wealth transfer make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? The trusted relationship — especially with the heir or widow who will inherit — became the scarce, valuable thing; picking investments became free, so tax and advice became the fee; and the aging owner's business became an asset to consolidate. The danger: the money is far more concentrated than the headline, ~70–80% of heirs fire the family advisor, and much of it is eaten by end-of-life costs before it ever moves.
