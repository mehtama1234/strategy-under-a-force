Group A · The handover itself

The $84 trillion handover

The richest generation in history is dying — and whoever holds the money when it moves keeps it.

The Silent Generation and boomers hold roughly half of all U.S. wealth, and as they age and die (about 2024–2045) an estimated $84 trillion — newer counts run to ~$124 trillion — moves to heirs, spouses, and charities. It's the biggest handover of money in history, and every financial firm is racing to hold the relationship when it happens, because whoever manages the money as it transfers keeps the fees for decades.

The core bet

The biggest pile of money in history is about to move, so everyone who touches it races to hold the relationship when it does — because whoever manages the money as it transfers keeps the fees for a generation.

The average inheritance is big; the median is tiny

Because so much sits at the very top, the numbers mislead: someone who inherits gets a large average ($300k–$500k+), but the median inheritance is around $50k — and most people get nothing at all. The trillion-dollar headline is really a story about what the richest families do with their money.

The single biggest worry

The 'everyone inherits' story is false — the money is locked in the top 1–10% and will concentrate further. And even among those clients, heirs often fire the parents' advisor within a couple of years, so no one holding the money today can assume they'll keep it.

The surprising part

The overlooked mechanic is that the transfer moves sideways before it moves down. The money first lands with widows — women, who live longer — often people the late husband's advisor barely knew, which is exactly why so much of it walks out the door when the transfer happens.

In one sentence

The richest Americans are about to pass down more money in twenty years than any generation in history, and whoever manages it when it changes hands keeps it.
