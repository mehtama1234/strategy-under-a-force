# The Great Wealth Transfer — strategy under a force

The 41st collection (Tier-4, seeded by the Inc 5000 analysis) — Family I (macro forces).

**Inc 5000 evidence:** "Financial Plumbing" is a top fast-growing theme — 677 companies, ~$44.4B — full of wealth-tech, advice platforms, insurance, and money-movement firms, and "generational wealth transfer" recurs as a growth driver.

**The force:** an estimated $80–100 trillion is passing from the Silent Generation and boomers to their heirs, spouses, and charities over roughly two decades — the largest handover of money in history. It reshapes wealth management, financial advice, estate and tax planning, life insurance and annuities, and it drives the aging-owner business-sale wave that feeds private-equity roll-ups. We ask: *what did the great wealth transfer make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** the richest generation in history is dying, and ~$84 trillion is changing hands — so everyone who touches that money is racing to be holding the relationship when it moves. The scarce, valuable thing is the trusted relationship with the person who has the money (and, increasingly, with the heir who will inherit it), because whoever manages the money when it transfers keeps the fees for decades. That turned financial advice, estate planning, and even the family business itself into assets worth consolidating — and set off a scramble for advisors, heirs, and aging business owners before the money moves.
