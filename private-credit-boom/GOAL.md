# The Private-Credit Boom — strategy under a force

The 29th collection (Tier-2) — Family I (macro forces / financial).

**The force:** since 2008, and explosively since 2020, lending has migrated out of banks and into private investment funds. "Private credit" (direct lending by Apollo, Ares, Blackstone, KKR, Blue Owl…) is now a ~$1.5–2 trillion market that lends straight to companies, bypassing banks and public bond markets — funded increasingly by insurance and pension money hunting for yield. It reshapes who controls credit, how companies borrow, and — crucially — where financial risk now hides. We ask: *what did the private-credit boom make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** the bank got disintermediated — lending moved into the shadows, and nobody's quite sure what it's worth. A whole parallel banking system grew up outside the regulated banks: funds that gather long-term money (annuities, pensions) and lend it directly to mid-sized companies and buyouts, keeping the fat spread. Borrowers love the speed and privacy; investors love the yield. But these loans rarely trade, so their value is a model's opinion, not a market price — which may make the system either genuinely safer (patient money, no bank runs) or just more opaque (risk hidden until it isn't). It has never been tested by a real downturn at this scale.
