The Private-Credit Boom

How lending quietly moved out of the banks and into a $2-trillion shadow nobody can quite price.

The 29th force. Since 2008, and explosively since 2020, lending migrated out of regulated banks and into private investment funds — Apollo, Ares, Blackstone, KKR, Blue Owl — that gather long-term money and lend it straight to companies, keeping the fat spread. It's now a ~$2 trillion market, fueled by insurance and pension money hunting yield, lending mostly to buyouts. The one idea: the bank got disintermediated — lending moved into the shadows, and nobody's quite sure what it's worth.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did the private-credit boom make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? Certainty and privacy became the scarce goods borrowers pay up for; permanent capital became the prize; and a daily market price became the thing that vanished. The danger: risk didn't disappear — it moved somewhere with less disclosure, valued by a model instead of a market, and never tested by a real downturn at this size.
