Subscription Fatigue

How everything became a monthly bill — until people stopped counting and started cancelling.

The 30th force. Turning a one-time sale into a monthly bill became the most prized trick in modern business — Wall Street pays a premium for predictable, recurring revenue, and a customer who pays by default. So software, streaming, razors, apps, and even car features all became subscriptions, until the average household was juggling a dozen quiet charges. Then came the wall. The one idea: everything became a monthly bill — until people stopped counting and started cancelling.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did the subscription boom — and its fatigue — make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? Predictable revenue became the prize; the subscriber who forgets to cancel became the quiet profit; and the honest, low-friction relationship became scarce. The danger: the model's value leans on inertia and friction — exactly what tired consumers and new regulators are now attacking.
