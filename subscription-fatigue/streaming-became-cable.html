Group B · The wall — the fatigue

Streaming became cable

We cut the cord to escape expensive bundles of channels we didn't watch — and rebuilt it, one app at a time.

People cut the cable cord to escape paying for a big bundle of channels they never watched. But to watch everything now, you need five or more separate streaming subscriptions that together cost as much as cable — with ads creeping back in. So the industry reinvented the very thing it disrupted, and is now literally re-bundling: Disney+/Hulu/Max packages, streaming baked into phone and internet plans. Full circle.

The core bet

Streaming promised individual choice — pick only what you want, ditch the bloat. But fragmentation across a dozen apps brought decision paralysis and spiraling cost, so the industry simply recreated the cable bundle, trapping viewers in the exact thing they fled.

What the numbers say

~$80–100/mo
the combined cost of five streaming services by 2025 — matching or beating the cable bill people cut.
2024
the Disney+/Hulu/Max bundle launched — the fastest-growing package in North America, a pivot straight back to bundling.
5–7
streaming services the average household now needs to see the major shows, up from 2–3 in 2018.
ads return
ad-supported tiers arrived at Netflix (2022), Disney+ (2022), and Max — the very thing cord-cutters escaped.
telecom bundles
Verizon, T-Mobile, and others now bundle Netflix/Max/Spotify into phone and broadband plans — the new aggregators.

The lesson learned, and ignored

The full-circle irony is that the industry saw it coming and bundled anyway. Wall Street rewarded churn-avoidance over pure growth, so platforms maximized revenue per subscriber through ads and bundles. Studios found it cheaper to accept bundled exclusivity than to fight for every viewer. Re-bundling wasn't consumer demand — it was a calculated return to the monopoly-like pricing power cable held for 30 years.

Who ends up ahead

The bundle owners and aggregators (Disney, telecoms) who recapture cable-style pricing power and lower churn.

Who's exposed

Viewers, who lost the à-la-carte choice that was the whole point — and small services shut out of the bundles.

The single biggest worry

Streaming has become what cable was: a near-necessity with inelastic demand. Because the catalog is split across owners, you must subscribe to five services to see the shows everyone's talking about — so the moment the industry realized it had a captive, re-bundling audience, margins recovered at the direct expense of the consumer choice that was the original promise.

The surprising part

The whole "pay only for what you watch" pitch is now hollow. To watch the hit on Max, the prestige show on Apple, and the comedy on Hulu, you need them all — so the value proposition that killed cable has been quietly reassembled into the same expensive bundle, ads and all.

In one sentence

Streaming killed the cable bundle to give people choice, then rebuilt it the moment viewers realized they needed seven subscriptions instead of one.
