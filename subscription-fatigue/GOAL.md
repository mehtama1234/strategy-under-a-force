# Subscription Fatigue — strategy under a force

The 30th collection (Tier-2, final Tier-2) — Family II (hidden-mechanism business models).

**The force:** the subscription / recurring-revenue model conquered almost everything — software (SaaS), entertainment (streaming), and then physical goods (razors, meal kits, subscription boxes), apps, even cars and features. Wall Street rewarded predictable recurring revenue above all else, so every company tried to turn a one-time sale into a monthly bill. Then it hit a wall: consumers are overwhelmed, forgetting what they pay for, cancelling, sharing passwords, downgrading to ad tiers. "Subscription fatigue" is the backlash, and the companies are fighting churn with ad tiers, password crackdowns, price hikes, and hard-to-cancel dark patterns — now drawing regulators (FTC "click to cancel"). We ask: *what did the subscription boom (and its fatigue) make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** everything became a monthly bill — until people stopped counting and started cancelling. Turning a sale into a subscription is the most valuable trick in modern business: predictable revenue Wall Street pays a premium for, and a customer who keeps paying by default. So everyone did it, and the average household now juggles a dozen quiet monthly charges. The fatigue is the reckoning — consumers wise up, churn rises, and the model's dark side shows (price creep, ad tiers, anti-sharing crackdowns, deliberately painful cancellation). Recurring revenue is still gold; but the scarce thing is no longer the subscriber — it's the subscriber who doesn't cancel.
