Group B · The wall — the fatigue

Price hikes & tiers

Grab everyone with cheap introductory pricing, then raise it again and again and quietly gut the cheaper tier.

Having grabbed subscribers with low introductory prices, streaming and other services now raise prices repeatedly and quietly degrade the cheaper tiers — dropping 4K, adding ads to the "standard" plan, cutting features. It's the classic playbook: get everyone hooked cheap, then extract. Netflix, Disney+, Max, YouTube TV, and Spotify all raised prices multiple times between 2022 and 2025.

The core bet

The land-grab was phase one; extraction is phase two. Once growth stalls and everyone's hooked, the services turn the price up and thin out what the cheaper plans include — betting that habit and switching-friction keep you paying more for the same or less.

Betting they already own you

The fear driving boardrooms isn't churn — it's the belief that they already own you. Once a service is part of your evening routine, they bet you'll grumble and pay the hike rather than lose access. The risk is that the playbook is now so visible that customers learn to bundle-hop and cancel on schedule, and a cheaper newcomer can peel off the price-sensitive crowd.

Who ends up ahead

Services with genuine habit-lock and must-have content that can raise prices without losing many subscribers.

Who's exposed

Price-sensitive subscribers squeezed by hikes and degraded tiers — and services that overplay their hand.

The single biggest worry

This is exactly the trap cable fell into: as prices rose and bundles tightened, cord-cutting accelerated. Streaming is now repeating it — hikes, ad insertion, and bundling all signal that growth has stalled and the easy money is gone, which teaches customers to churn strategically and invites a cheaper disruptor.

The surprising part

The counterintuitive part: the ad-supported tiers aren't really cheaper for the companies — they're more profitable, and they're priced to make ad-free feel expensive. Netflix and Max discovered that selling your attention to advertisers earns more than your subscription does, so the pricey ad-free tier exists mainly to push you toward the ads. The customer becomes the product, not just the payer.

In one sentence

Streaming traded growth for extraction — repeated price hikes and thinner tiers — betting habit beats the pain, even as the visible playbook teaches customers to churn on schedule.
