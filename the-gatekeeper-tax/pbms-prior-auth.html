Group B · Health gatekeepers

PBMs & prior auth

Invisible middlemen decide which medicines you can actually get.

Three companies you've probably never heard of — CVS Caremark, Express Scripts, OptumRx — sit between you, your doctor, your insurer, and the pharmacy, and decide which drugs are covered. They control ~80% of US prescriptions, extract rebates from drugmakers to set the "formulary," and make doctors beg permission ("prior authorization") before you can fill a script. And they own the insurer and the pharmacy too.

The core bet

The bet is to gatekeep the middle of the drug supply chain — the list of what's covered. Control the formulary and you control which drugs win, take rebates for placement, and use prior-auth to ration access. Because the same firms own the insurer and pharmacy, they gatekeep every side of the deal.

What the numbers say

~80%
US prescriptions flowing through the three biggest PBMs. No fourth competitor is close.
Feb 2024
The FTC opened investigations into all three — alleging prior-auth blocks needed care, and rebates push pricey drugs onto formularies over cheaper options.
~100M
Prescriptions delayed or rejected a year via prior authorization (rough estimate). 90%+ of doctors say it delays care; some hire staff just to fight denials.
own all sides
CVS owns Aetna + CVS Pharmacy; Cigna owns Express Scripts; UnitedHealth owns OptumRx + UnitedHealthcare. The insurer, the gatekeeper, and the pharmacy are one company.

The invisible decider

Your insurance card doesn't say "managed by a PBM." Your doctor doesn't say "your PBM denied this." Your pharmacy doesn't explain the denial. You think your insurer said no, or your doctor couldn't prescribe it — but a middleman you've never met, paid by the drugmaker, made the call, and also owns your insurer and pharmacy.

What's newly valuable
Control of the formulary, hidden rebate margins, and a vertically integrated grip on every side of the prescription.
What's harmed
Patients (delayed care, higher premiums) and cheaper generics blocked to protect rebate revenue.
The single biggest worry

PBMs can block your doctor's choice with opaque criteria, extract hidden rebates that raise your premiums, and face no real competition because they own the insurer and pharmacy too. If they deny a drug, you often have no alternative.

The surprising part

The most consequential gatekeeper in your medicine cabinet is one most people can't name. A hidden middleman — paid by the drugmaker, owning your insurer and pharmacy — quietly decides what you can take, and makes your doctor ask its permission first.

In one sentence

Three invisible middlemen gatekeep 80% of America's prescriptions and own the insurers and pharmacies on both sides of every deal.
