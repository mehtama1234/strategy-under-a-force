Group C · Credential gatekeepers

College accreditation

The club of existing colleges decides who gets federal student aid.

A college can't touch federal student aid — Pell grants, federal loans, the lifeblood — unless an accreditor approves it. And accreditors are largely run by existing colleges: a club of incumbents deciding who may enter. That blocks cheaper, newer competitors and helps keep the tuition-and-debt machine running. The gate that's supposed to guarantee quality mostly protects the schools that run it.

The core bet

The bet is that access to federal aid is the real prize, and the accreditor holds the key. Because loans and grants flow only to accredited schools, the permission slip is what lets incumbents keep prices — and student debt — climbing, safe from cheaper rivals they get to vote on.

What the numbers say

~$170B+
Federal student aid a year that flows only to accredited schools — the subsidy the gate controls. Total student debt is ~$1.7T.
peer review
Accreditor boards are staffed by people from existing institutions — so competitors get to vote on whether cheaper or online rivals are recognized.
process, not results
Accreditation mostly checks compliance (a library, a board, a mission statement), not graduation rates or earnings. A pricey school with poor outcomes keeps its status.
the triad
Accreditor + state + Dept of Ed co-regulate — in theory a check, in practice all three tend to protect the existing college system.

Quality gate or moat?

A cheap bootcamp with strong job placement can't access federal loans; a $50k/year school with weak outcomes can. Google, Amazon, and Coursera issue credentials without accreditation — but can't help students tap federal aid, so their reach is capped. The gate isn't about results; it's about membership.

The real value
A quality floor that helped shut down predatory for-profits (ITT, Corinthian) — burning it down entirely would risk flooding aid to bad actors.
The moat
Incumbents voting on competitors, propping up tuition and debt in the name of "quality."
The single biggest worry

The gate that's meant to guarantee quality is run by the colleges it regulates, so it mostly protects them from cheaper competition — and because federal loans flow only to the accredited, the permission slip is what lets tuition and debt keep climbing.

The surprising part

Accreditors measure process, not outcomes — whether a school has the trappings of a college, not whether its graduates get jobs. So the permission slip that's supposed to signal quality often just signals membership in a club that has every reason to keep the prices high.

In one sentence

Accreditation is run by the colleges it regulates, so it guards tuition and debt more than student outcomes — and federal aid flows only to those it lets in.
