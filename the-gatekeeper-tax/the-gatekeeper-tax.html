Group E · The big picture

The gatekeeper tax

You can't sell, practice, or trade without the incumbent's permission slip.

Pull the thirteen pages together and one shape repeats across finance, health, and credentials. A small set of gatekeepers who own neither supply nor demand control the permission to participate — and tax it. They're often paid by the party they judge, run by the incumbents they protect, and shielded by regulation. The toll ratchets up; the gate often fails; disruptors try to route around and sometimes become new gates.

The one idea

You can't sell, practice, or trade without the incumbent's permission slip. Be fair, though — "it's all a racket" overstates it. Many gates deliver real value: drug safety, financial trust, professional competence. The problem isn't that gates exist; it's capture, cost, and the near-total lack of accountability.

The whole force in numbers

~$130T
Debt the three credit raters sit astride — paid by the issuers they grade, and untouched by blowing 2008.
~51% / 4
Public-company audits done by just four firms — who also sell them consulting.
$13T
Passive money that must buy whatever a private S&P committee includes in the index.
~$170B
Federal student aid a year that flows only to schools an incumbent-run accreditor approves.
~22%
US jobs needing a license (up from ~5% in the 1950s) — ~$200B/yr in higher consumer prices, little safety gain.
~80% / 3
US prescriptions gatekept by three PBMs — who own the insurer and pharmacy too.

Winners vs losers

Winners
The gatekeepers (rent, no competition), the incumbents they protect (a higher wall), and insiders who know how to game the system.
Losers
Consumers (pay more, wait longer), new entrants and immigrants (locked out), and patients/students on the wrong side of the gate.
The biggest tension

Gates sometimes provide genuine value — safety, competence, market integrity. But the cost has ballooned (tuition, drug-approval time, licensing fees + prep) while accountability is near zero, and the regulators who should police the gatekeepers are often captured by them. Keep the gate or keep it cheap and honest — you rarely get both.

The surprising part

Gatekeepers keep becoming new gates. Visa and Mastercard started as payment routers and now tax merchants 2–3%. Credit bureaus were meant to prevent lending abuse and now enable it by selling data. Fintechs promised to disrupt ratings and mostly rebuilt them. The permission slip is one of the most durable business models there is.

In one sentence

Small groups that control permission — not production or demand — tax participation across finance, health, and credentials, often with the blessing of the regulators meant to check them.

How this connects to the rest of the series

This is the first of a new Family III: structural patterns. It's the cousin of the toll-booth economy — but where the toll-booth taxes the transaction, the gatekeeper taxes the permission to transact. Its index-committee arm feeds directly into passive investing; its capture mechanism echoes the financial and roll-up stories; and the whole idea sits one page over from who really captures the value in a mature market.
