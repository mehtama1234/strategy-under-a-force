# The Gatekeeper Tax — strategy under a force

The 21st "strategy under a force" collection — and the first of a new **Family III: structural patterns**.

**The force:** in industry after industry, one player owns neither the supply nor the demand — it owns the *permission slip*. The rule-maker, standard-setter, rater, or certifier charges a toll that no one can dodge, and the toll ratchets as the gatekeeper gets stronger. We ask: *what did the gatekeeper's permission make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** you can't sell, practice, or trade without the incumbent's permission slip. A credit-rating agency rates a bond it neither issues nor buys — but no bond sells without it. The FDA approves a drug it didn't invent. A licensing board decides who may cut hair or argue a case. An accreditor decides which colleges get federal aid. The gatekeeper sits *beside* the market, not in it, and taxes the right to participate. Value flows to whoever controls the permission; the risk is that the gate becomes a racket — captured, self-dealing, and unaccountable.

**How it differs from the toll-booth economy:** the toll-booth owns the transaction (a cut of each sale). The gatekeeper owns the *permission to transact at all* — it doesn't touch the goods, it grants or denies the right to exist in the market.

- **B — Health gatekeepers (permission to sell or practice):** FDA drug approval · medical licensing & the doctor-supply cap · PBMs & prior authorization
- **C — Credential gatekeepers (permission to be believed):** college accreditation · rankings & standardized tests · professional & occupational licensing
