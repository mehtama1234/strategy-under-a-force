The Gatekeeper Tax

How owning the permission — not the product — became the business.

The 21st force, and the first of a new family: structural patterns that shape many industries at once. In sector after sector, one player owns neither the supply nor the demand — it owns the permission slip. A rater, an auditor, a licensing board, an accreditor: they sit beside the market and tax the right to take part. The one idea: you can't sell, practice, or trade without the incumbent's permission slip.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did the gatekeeper's permission make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? Value flows to whoever controls the gate — often paid by the party they judge, run by the incumbents they protect, and shielded by regulation. How is this different from the toll-booth? The toll-booth takes a cut of the sale; the gatekeeper grants or denies the right to sell at all.
