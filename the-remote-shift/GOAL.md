# The Remote Shift — strategy under a force

The ninth "strategy under a force" collection (after AI, Ozempic, the US–China decoupling, the end of cheap money, the energy transition, the demographic cliff, the climate bill, and the attention economy).

**The force:** work stopped being a *place*. For a century, most white-collar work meant physically going to an office downtown, five days a week. Remote and hybrid work broke that assumption — and it turns out the load-bearing thing wasn't the office, it was the **commute**. An enormous economy was built around that daily journey. We ask of each company or sector: *what did the remote shift make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** for a century, an entire economy was quietly built around the daily commute — office towers, downtown lunch spots, transit, business travel, professional wardrobes, and the suburbs-as-dormitory model. Everyone assumed work = a place you go. When the commute stopped for millions of knowledge workers, everything anchored to it unravelled, and value moved from the **center** (the expensive downtown core) to the **edges** (the home, the suburbs, anywhere with wifi). The thing that got disrupted was never really the office — it was the journey to it. And the big caveat: only about a third of jobs can go remote, so this is largely a white-collar story, and flexibility became a new class divide between the "laptop class" and everyone who has to show up.
