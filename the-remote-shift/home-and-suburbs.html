Group A · The remote dividend

The home & suburbs — the donut effect

When the commute vanished, the home became office, gym, and school — and people wanted more room for it.

When the daily commute disappeared, the home stopped being just a place to sleep and became the office, gym, and school. People wanted more space — a spare room for a desk, a yard — and they were suddenly free to get it further out. Value flowed from the expensive city core to the cheaper ring around it.

The boom, and the "donut"

+15–25%
Price growth in suburbs and exurbs around major cities (2020–21), while dense downtown cores softened. Stanford's Nick Bloom and Arjun Ramani named this the "donut effect."
  • Home improvement boomed: Home Depot and Lowe's posted record DIY sales in 2020–21 as people invested in the homes they now lived and worked in.
  • The spare bedroom became a non-negotiable home office; demand for 3+ bedroom homes jumped.
  • By 2023–24, hybrid brought a partial rebound to city centers — but the downtown price premium never fully came back.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The flip toward space accelerated sprawl and car dependence. Critics worry it undid decades of investment in dense, walkable, transit-served neighborhoods — locking in longer drives and a bigger footprint per household, even if the commute is now optional rather than daily.

The surprising part

Remote work didn't kill the city — it flipped the city's value proposition. Expensive downtown real estate lost its moat, because the thing you were paying for (being close to your office) evaporated. The suburbs didn't boom because they were suburbs; they boomed because they were suddenly as connected as downtown. A nurse, a designer, and an accountant could all earn a good salary and live anywhere — so they chose space over status. The "death of the city" story missed it: the city didn't die, its center just lost its premium.

In one sentence

The home & suburbs in a nutshell

Remote work stripped the expensive city core of its commute-driven premium and rewarded cheaper, roomier suburbs — flipping the economics of geography, with the home becoming the new center of everything.
