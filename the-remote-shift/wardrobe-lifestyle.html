Group D · The wider ripples

Wardrobe & lifestyle — the suit died, and the commute budget came home

No office means no dress code — and the thousands of dollars a year the commute quietly cost got redirected.

No commute meant no dress code, no rushed morning, no parking meter. Formal workwear — the suit, the tie, the pressed shirt — became almost pointless overnight. Comfort won. And the surprisingly large budget that the commute quietly consumed got rerouted from the office to the home.

The collapse of the suit

Aug 2020
Tailored Brands (parent of Men's Wearhouse) filed for bankruptcy, months into lockdown, as formal-wear demand fell off a cliff. Business-wear sales dropped ~30–40% in 2020–22.
  • Athleisure boomed: Lululemon's stock roughly tripled from early 2020 to 2022 as the "work uniform" became leggings and hoodies.
  • "Business casual" became just casual — a 30-year dress norm evaporated in weeks and never fully came back.

The surprising part

The "commute tax" came home

Workers didn't just stop buying suits — they got their money back. Gas, parking, dry cleaning, coffee runs, work lunches, professional wardrobes: a hidden "commute tax" that could total $15,000–$20,000 a year for an office worker. When the commute vanished, that budget didn't disappear — it migrated to home improvements, fitness gear, streaming, and comfort clothing. Spending rotated from external performance (what the office sees) to internal lifestyle (what the home enables).

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Formal workwear didn't just dip — it may never recover. A generation of young workers never wore a suit to the office, and the cultural expectation reset downward. Tailors, menswear shops, and dry cleaners face structural decline, not a passing slump.

In one sentence

Wardrobe & lifestyle in a nutshell

Remote work killed the necktie and redirected the $15–20k-a-year "commute tax" from suits, gas, and dry cleaning into homes and comfort — shrinking a whole retail category and resetting what "work clothes" even means.
