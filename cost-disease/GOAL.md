# Cost Disease — strategy under a force

The 22nd collection — Family III (structural patterns).

**The force:** "Baumol's cost disease." Some sectors can't raise productivity the way factories can — a string quartet needs the same four players it did in 1820; a nurse can't safely see 4× the patients; a teacher can't teach 4× the class. But their workers' wages must still rise with the wider, productive economy — so the cost of those services climbs decade after decade, even though output stays flat. We ask: *what did cost disease make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** the sectors that can't get more efficient are the ones eating your paycheck. Manufacturing got radically cheaper; a TV costs a fraction of what it did. But health care, college, childcare, elder care, and housing keep getting more expensive — not because they're wasteful, but because they're labor-heavy and productivity-immune, and their wages track the rest of the economy. Value flows to whoever owns a productivity-immune service with captive demand (they can raise prices forever); the risk is that ordinary life — being cared for, educated, housed — drifts out of reach for ordinary people.
