Group D · The mechanism & the fight

Can AI break it?

Recorded music didn't kill the concert. Will AI be different?

The hopeful — and skeptical — page. Every prior technology promised to make human-touch services scalable and mostly failed to cut their core cost, because what people value is the human: the bedside nurse, the teacher who knows your kid, the live show. AI is the newest claimant. The honest bet: it may trim the admin and routine parts and ease labor shortages, but the caring-and-judgment core stays human — so AI likely softens cost disease rather than curing it.

The core bet

The realistic take: AI can cut the paperwork around care — medical scribing, billing, intake, first-draft tutoring — but the trust-and-judgment core (a nurse who knows the patient, a teacher who sees a kid struggle) stays human, and that's exactly where people concentrate their spending.

What history says

+150–300%
Real rise in concert-ticket prices (1950–2020). Recorded music didn't cheapen live shows — it made them rarer, pricier, and a luxury.
MOOCs
Online courses added no meaningful cost cut; enrollment shifted toward expensive flagship universities. The credential, not the lecture, was the product.
38%
Telemedicine adoption spiked during COVID — but health costs didn't fall; admin grew because both physical and virtual systems had to be run.
ATMs
Bank branches fell 35–40% but banking employment stayed flat — tellers became salespeople, because the real product was financial trust, not cash.

Tech compresses the commodity, prices up the bespoke

The pattern repeats: technology cheapens the routine part and raises the value of the human part. Recorded music made fans who'd pay more for a live show. Online courses made universities more selective. AI medical scribes (used by ~12% of practices) improved margins slightly — but physician wages and visit costs didn't fall. The human judgment is where the value, and the price, concentrate.

Where AI helps
Paperwork, scheduling, first drafts, and easing labor shortages — real relief at the edges.
Where it won't
The caring, trust, and judgment core that people pay for because a human does it.
The single biggest worry

The human-touch core is what people value and will pay for — they pay because it's done by a human. AI may make admin cheaper but push more cost into the human-judgment part, because that's where perceived value concentrates.

The surprising part

Past tech didn't just fail to cut costs — it often raised the quality bar and the floor at once. Telemedicine added a touchpoint rather than replacing the ER; online courses coexist with pricier, more selective colleges; recorded music created fans willing to pay more for live shows. In each case, tech compressed the commodity part and priced up the bespoke part.

In one sentence

Technology has never cured cost disease in human services because the human is what's being sold — it just makes the commodity parts cheaper while making the judgment parts dearer.
