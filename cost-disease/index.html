Cost Disease

Why the things you can't skip — care, learning, a roof — keep getting more expensive.

The 22nd force. Some jobs can't be sped up: a string quartet needs the same four players it did in 1820, a nurse can't safely see four times the patients, a teacher can't teach four times the class. But their wages must still rise with the wider, productive economy — so their cost climbs, decade after decade, even though the output stays the same. The one idea: the sectors that can't get more efficient are the ones eating your paycheck.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did cost disease make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? Manufacturing got radically cheaper; a TV costs a fraction of what it did. But health, college, childcare, elder care, and housing keep rising — not from waste, but because they're labor-heavy and productivity-immune, and their wages track everything else.
