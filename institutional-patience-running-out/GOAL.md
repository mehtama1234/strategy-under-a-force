# Institutional Patience Running Out — strategy under a force

The 34th collection (Tier-3) — Family I (macro forces).

**The force:** patient, long-horizon capital and institutions are vanishing. Everything is optimized for the short term — quarterly earnings, activist investors, private-equity flips, VC fund clocks, shrinking CEO tenures, algorithmic trading — so the things that only pay off over decades (basic research, infrastructure, biotech, forests, public companies, careful institutions) get starved of the patient money they need. We ask: *what did the draining of institutional patience make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** the money that could wait got rare — so anything that only pays off in twenty years struggles to get funded. For most of the 20th century, big institutions (corporate research labs, pension funds, public companies, universities) could think in decades. Now the clock runs fast: CEOs are judged quarterly, funds must return cash on a schedule, and traders hold stocks for seconds. Patience itself became the scarce resource. That starves basic science, long infrastructure, and slow-maturing industries — and hands an edge to the few who can still afford to wait (sovereign funds, family offices, foundations, a Buffett).

**Groups:**
- **A — Where patience died:** quarterly capitalism & short-termism (buybacks, guidance) · activist investors (forcing short-term moves) · the shrinking, aging public market (stay private / delist)
- **B — What's being starved:** the death of the corporate research lab (Bell Labs/PARC) · long-horizon infrastructure & energy (decades to build) · patient industries starved (biotech, deep tech, forestry/agriculture)
- **C — The impatient replacements:** private equity's short holds & flips · the VC fund clock (return cash in 10 years) · the acceleration of trading (algos, meme stocks, retail)
- **D — The mechanism & who keeps patience:** why capital got impatient (rates, liquidity, incentives, metrics) · who still plays long (sovereign wealth, family offices, foundations, Buffett) · can patience be rebuilt? (long-term exchanges, dual-class, evergreen funds)
