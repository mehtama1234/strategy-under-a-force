Group C · The impatient replacements

The acceleration of trading

The time we hold a stock collapsed from eight years to a few months — and for machines, to microseconds.

The time people hold a stock has collapsed: from about eight years in the 1950s-60s to under a year today, and high-frequency traders hold for microseconds. Algorithmic and high-frequency trading dominate volume; zero-commission apps, options, and meme-stock frenzies push even ordinary investors toward gambling-fast horizons; and the rise of same-day options is pure short-termism. The market became a machine that rewards speed, not patience.

The core bet

As holding periods collapsed from years to months to microseconds — and technology made trading nearly free — companies stopped being long-term bets and became short-term price targets, rewarding quick exits over building value.

A machine that rewards speed

When the market's tempo is microseconds, corporate management can't play the long game: a single earnings miss triggers a sell-off, so long-term R&D, employee retention, and supply-chain resilience get sacrificed to smooth the next quarter's EPS. The companies that invested heavily in the 1980s-90s could weather downturns and experiment; today's speed punishes "wasting" money on bets that pay off in five years.

Who ends up ahead

High-frequency traders, and the private owners (PE, pre-IPO) who capture value away from the frantic public market.

Who gets starved

Long-term corporate strategy — and public shareholders left holding the slow, mature, defensive businesses.

The single biggest worry

The best ideas increasingly skip the public market entirely, because founders and PE can extract value faster in private — leaving public shareholders with the slow, mature, defensive companies and the turnaround gambles. The market that was supposed to fund patient growth became one that punishes it.

The surprising part

The most ruthless short-termism isn't the Reddit day-traders — it's baked into passive investing. Index giants now control 20%+ of the S&P 500; they vote with the herd and rotate into whatever's trending, so the index becomes self-perpetuating momentum. The retail traders look chaotic, but they're rationally responding to a market where patient capital has been replaced by algorithm-driven flows — everyone is forced to move at the machine's tempo.

In one sentence

The market evolved from a mechanism that funded patient long-term growth into a machine that rewards speed and punishes patience — leaving only the fastest traders and the unlisted to capture real value creation.
