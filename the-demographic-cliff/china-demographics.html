Group D · The national cases

China — getting old before it got rich

The biggest and most consequential case: a giant aging fast, while still only middle-income.

China is the demographic cliff's biggest story. Its one-child policy plus breakneck development crashed its birth rate — and now its population is shrinking, its workforce is falling, and it's aging faster than almost anywhere. The famous worry is "getting old before getting rich": Japan and Korea got wealthy first; China is aging while still middle-income, so it may lack the money to support its old.

The turn already happened

peaked ~2021
China's population peaked around 1.41 billion in 2021–22 and now falls every year — the first sustained decline in 60 years. India overtook it as the world's most populous country in 2023.
  • Births collapsed to ~9 million in 2023, the fewest since 1949 — roughly half the level of a few years earlier.
  • The workforce peaked ~2015 and now shrinks 1–2% a year — a structural cliff, not a blip.
  • The 65+ group (~258 million and climbing) is one of the fastest-aging on Earth.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — old before rich

China's income per person (~$12,700) is roughly a third of what Japan and Korea had at the same age structure. The pension math breaks — from ~5 workers per retiree in 2010 toward ~2–3 by 2030 — and China can't easily tax-and-spend its way out at middle-income levels. It also resists immigration. So the shock lands on families, on women's labor, and on austerity.

The surprising part

China ended the one-child policy in 2015 and even started urging three children in 2021 — and births kept falling anyway. That proves the policy was never the real cause. The true drivers are the cost of housing, education, and childcare, plus women choosing careers and later marriage — none of which a government mandate can reverse. China's birth rate (~1.0) is now below Japan's. You can order people to be allowed more children; you can't order them to want them. This links to the decoupling: the cheap, young workforce that powered China's rise is exactly what's now disappearing.

In one sentence

China in a nutshell

China is aging as fast as Japan did but on a fraction of the wealth per person, its population and workforce already shrinking — and ending the one-child policy did nothing, because the real barriers to babies are cost and choice, not law.
