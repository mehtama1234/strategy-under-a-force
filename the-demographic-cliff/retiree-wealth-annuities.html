Group A · The silver economy

Retiree wealth — the biggest pile of money in history changes hands

The retiring generation holds most of the wealth. Two huge businesses grow out of that one fact.

The generation now retiring (the Baby Boomers, in the US) holds an enormous share of the wealth — more than half, despite being about a fifth of the population. As they age, that money does two things, and both create a scramble for whoever manages it.

Two shifts, one fact

1. The great handoff

Tens of trillions of dollars pass to heirs and spouses over the coming couple of decades — up for grabs by whoever manages it next.

2. Paycheck, not growth

Retirees no longer want "grow my money." They want "turn my savings into a monthly check that never runs out." That makes income products and annuities central.

tens of $ trillions
The "great wealth transfer" (estimates range widely, ~$40–80T) moving from the Boomers to their heirs through roughly the 2040s. Annuity sales have hit records (~$225B+/yr) as retirees buy guaranteed income.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the heirs walk

Wealth managers built their business on growth fees (a slice of assets that keep growing). Retirees need income, which earns lower fees. Worse, roughly 70% of clients' heirs fire the parents' advisor when the money passes down. So the race is to hold onto trillions as it changes hands to a younger, more skeptical, cheaper-platform generation.

The surprising part

The annuity — a product financial advisors spent decades telling people to avoid (high fees, opaque, often mis-sold) — has quietly become the safest play in a long retirement. When people live into their 90s, the scariest risk isn't a market crash; it's outliving your money. An income annuity is the one thing that guarantees a check until you die. So the product everyone warned you off at 40 becomes the sensible core of the plan at 70 — a complete reversal driven purely by people living longer.

In one sentence

Retiree wealth in a nutshell

An aging generation holding most of the wealth is handing over tens of trillions and shifting from growing money to guaranteeing income — reshaping the money-management business, and turning the once-scorned annuity into the safe play.
