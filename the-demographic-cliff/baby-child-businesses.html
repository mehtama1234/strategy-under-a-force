Group C · Built on the young crowd

Baby & child businesses — you can't market your way to more babies

Diapers, formula, toys, schools. Fewer births means a permanently shrinking customer base.

This is the clearest loser. Falling birth rates mean a permanently smaller pool of customers for everything built on children — diapers, formula, toys, maternity, schools. Unlike a recession, there's no bounce-back and no clever campaign that fixes it: fewer babies born today simply means fewer customers for the next 20 years.

The births are vanishing

2.1 → far below
The replacement rate is ~2.1 children per woman. The US sits ~1.6, South Korea ~0.7 (the world's lowest), and China's annual births roughly halved from ~2016 to the 2020s.

The vivid signal

Adult diapers passed baby diapers

In Japan, sales of adult diapers overtook baby diapers years ago — and the same diaper companies now grow by serving the old, not the young. It's the whole force in one product aisle.

  • Formula makers face shrinking home markets — China's infant-formula market contracted even as prices rose.
  • Colleges face an "enrollment cliff" from ~2025 on, as the smaller cohorts born after 2008 reach college age.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — capacity with no one to fill it

Toy factories, formula plants, and school buildings were built for a bigger child population. Now they sit half-used — and schools especially can't repurpose or relocate a building. It's dead weight that can't shrink gracefully.

The surprising part

The smartest pivot isn't just going abroad — it's turning away from children entirely. Baby-formula giants opened adult-nutrition lines; toy makers bought adult games and collectibles; and, most strikingly, schools in shrinking regions now recruit elderly adults into "lifelong learning" programs as a real revenue line. The businesses built on the young are quietly re-tooling to sell to the old — because that's where the people went.

In one sentence

Baby & child businesses in a nutshell

Fewer babies means a permanently shrinking market that no marketing can fix, so the businesses built on children are pivoting abroad — or, tellingly, pivoting to serve the old instead.
