Group A · The blueprint

Streaming ad tiers — they sold "no ads," then brought them back

Streaming spent a decade promising to save you from ads. Then it discovered ads pay more.

For years, the whole pitch of streaming was "escape cable TV — no ads." Then the industry did a complete U-turn and brought ads back. Netflix launched an ad tier (Nov 2022); Amazon put ads into Prime Video by default (2024); Disney+, Max, and Peacock all followed. Why? Because a viewer paying less plus watching ads is worth more than a viewer paying for ad-free.

The reversal

~59%
Share of new streaming sign-ups now choosing the ad-supported tier (2025) — ads became the majority, not the discount option. Netflix's ad tier alone passed ~90M users.
  • Connected-TV (CTV) advertising is the fastest-growing ad category (~$29B in 2024, heading toward ~$47B) — outpacing search and social.
  • Targeted TV ads are worth far more than old broadcast ads: they're aimed at your household using your data, not sprayed at everyone.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Delivering "relevant" ads means building a real-time behavioral profile of every viewer — what you watch, when, with whom. If viewers realize how much they're tracked (or privacy rules tighten), the trust and the model both wobble.

The surprising part

Streaming spent 2010–2022 selling "no ads" as the whole point — and positioning old TV as the enemy. Then it flipped so hard that it discovered a subscriber paying $6.99 + ads generates more total revenue than one paying $15.99 ad-free. The TV didn't disappear; it just became better-targeted — and, remarkably, viewers accepted the trade, choosing ad tiers the moment both were offered. The "cable" everyone fled came back wearing a streaming costume, and now it watches you.

In one sentence

Streaming ad tiers in a nutshell

Streaming sold a decade of "no ads," then discovered targeted ads on your viewing data are worth more than the subscription itself — turning every stream into an ad business that watches you back.
