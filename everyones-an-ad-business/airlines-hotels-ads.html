Group C · The surprising new ad sellers

Airlines & hotels — the seatback screen became ad inventory

They were already loyalty companies. Adding an ad business was a small step.

Travel companies realized they own two great ad assets: captive screens (the seatback, the in-room TV, the app) and rich loyalty data (they know exactly who you are and where you're going). So they built ad networks. United launched "Kinective Media" (2024); Marriott launched a "Media Network" (2022). Your flight and your hotel stay are now ad inventory.

The travelers-as-audience play

  • United Kinective Media (June 2024): 108M ID-verified MileagePlus profiles, ~100,000 seatback screens (~3.5 hours of captive attention per flight), plus the app. Early partners: Macy's, Chase, Norwegian Cruise Line.
  • Marriott Media Network (2022, with Yahoo): targets ~164M Bonvoy members across the app, in-room TVs, and email.
  • These "travel media networks" passed ~$2B in ad spend (2024), growing ~18% a year.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Surveillance dressed as "anonymized" targeting. The systems link seat assignments, cards, app behavior, and travel history to individual profiles — enabling re-identification and precise targeting with little passenger awareness, raising real privacy-law concerns.

The surprising part

The seatback screen went from entertainment to mandatory ad delivery — United now runs a 30-second forced ad before your show. And the "golden data" that powers the targeting (your age, home address, destination, how often you fly) comes not from cookies but from TSA identity verification and your booking — the airline already owned the identity graph and the screens. Because they were already becoming loyalty-and-points companies, becoming ad companies was the natural next step.

In one sentence

Airlines & hotels in a nutshell

Travel companies weaponized their captive screens and first-party loyalty data — turning the seatback screen and your MileagePlus profile into targeted ad inventory.
