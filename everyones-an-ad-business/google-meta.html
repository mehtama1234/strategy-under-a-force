Group A · The blueprint

Google & Meta — give the product away free, sell the audience

They proved the ultimate business model. Everyone else is now copying it.

Google and Meta wrote the playbook everyone else in this collection is following. They give away search, email, maps, and social free — because those free products exist to assemble a giant, data-rich audience, and the real business is selling access to that audience to advertisers. Ads are the highest-margin business in tech.

The template, in numbers

~80% ads
Share of revenue that is advertising — Meta ~99% ($114B of ~$115B), Google ~77% ($238B of ~$307B). Together they take over half of the ~$750B global digital ad market.
~90% margin
Once the audience and data are in place, an extra ad costs almost nothing — so incremental ad revenue is nearly pure profit.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — a rule can erase billions

Apple's 2021 privacy change cost Meta ~$10B in a year — proof that one company's setting can gut the data-harvesting that makes the model work. The US antitrust case (Nov 2024) over Google's control of the whole ad-tech stack, plus the EU's rules, threaten the very foundation: the ability to track and target at will.

The surprising part

The products you love — Gmail, Instagram, Maps, YouTube, WhatsApp — are not the business. They're the bait. Gmail isn't free out of generosity; every email tells Google about your interests and intent. Instagram is endless because that trains the ad-targeting model. The products are so polished precisely because they must be engaging enough to keep you generating the data that makes the ads work. You are the product being sold.

In one sentence

Google & Meta in a nutshell

Google and Meta proved the ultimate model — assemble a free, data-rich audience and sell access to it at ~90% margins — and now everyone from Amazon to your bank is copying it, turning the whole economy into one giant ad machine.
