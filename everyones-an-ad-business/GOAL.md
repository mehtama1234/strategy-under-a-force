# Everyone's an Ad Business — strategy under a force

The eleventh "strategy under a force" collection. Sibling to the attention economy (which was about MEDIA fighting for eyeballs) and the loyalty economy (first-party data). This one is the specific, non-obvious mechanism: **non-media companies bolting on an advertising business** because ads are near-pure profit.

**The force:** advertising is almost pure margin. If you already have an audience — shoppers, riders, viewers, a screen, or first-party data — selling an ad against it costs you almost nothing and drops straight to the bottom line. So every company that has an audience is turning into an ad business. Retail media (Amazon, Walmart) was the opening act; now it's Uber, your bank, your TV, your car, the gas pump, the airline seatback. We ask of each: *what did the "everything is an ad business" shift make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** once you have an audience, the ads are almost free money — so everyone became an ad seller. Advertising is the highest-margin business most companies will ever find: the audience is already assembled by the "real" product, so the ad is nearly pure profit. That flips the logic — the product you think you're buying (the ride, the groceries, the streaming show, the bank account, the TV) increasingly exists to **assemble the audience the ads are sold against.** Value flows to whoever owns an audience + first-party data; it drains from the open web, from publishers, and from the user's experience and privacy. The true product, more and more, is *you* — sold to advertisers, not just on social media, but everywhere.

**Groups:**

- **B — The store becomes a media network:** Walmart & Instacart & DoorDash · Uber & rides-as-ads · the physical screens (gas pumps, in-store, billboards)
