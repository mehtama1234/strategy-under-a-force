Group C · The surprising new ad sellers

Banks & fintech — your bank now sells ads on your spending

The institution with the most intimate data about you just became an ad network.

This is the most surprising new ad seller of all. Banks have the best data on earth — they know exactly what you buy, everywhere. In 2024, JPMorgan Chase launched Chase Media Solutions, an ad business that lets brands target Chase's ~80 million customers using their real purchase history. PayPal launched its own ad unit the same year. Your bank is now an ad network.

The new frontier

  • Chase Media Solutions (April 2024): reaches ~80M customers; brands (Mercedes, Walmart, DoorDash) pay only when a customer actually buys, triggered by transaction data. It drove ~$12B in customer spend in its first year.
  • PayPal Ads (2024): hired the executive who built Uber's $1B ad business, to monetize ~400M users.
  • These "financial media networks" are growing faster than any other ad category — heading toward ~$1.5B by 2026.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — trust

Surveys find ~87% of people are uncomfortable with banks selling their financial data for ads. Regulators watch "sensitive" data like income and spending closely. Your bank monetizing what you buy feels less like advertising and more like a breach of trust — and trust is the one thing a bank can't afford to lose.

The surprising part

The clever, unsettling twist: the bank doesn't sell your data — it stays in the middle. Chase shows you the ad; the advertiser never sees "this person buys coffee daily." That sounds privacy-friendly, but it arguably makes the betrayal worse: the bank knows you're a target because it saw you shop at a competitor, and it's quietly acting on your most private records — dressed up as a helpful cash-back offer.

In one sentence

Banks & fintech in a nutshell

Your bank now knows your exact spending everywhere and sells brands the ability to intercept you at the moment you're most likely to buy — building a new ad market on data that feels less like advertising and more like financial surveillance.
