Group D · The machinery & the backlash

Enshittification — the more it's an ad business, the worse it gets

The flip side: as every product bolts on ads, the product gets worse for the people using it.

Here's the cost of the whole shift. As every product turns into an ad business, the products get visibly worse for the people using them — and the surveillance needed to target the ads erodes privacy at scale. The writer Cory Doctorow named the pattern "enshittification" — and it was declared word of the year for 2023.

The pattern

The three-stage decay

Doctorow's cycle: a platform first is good to users to attract them, then abuses users to please advertisers, then extracts from everyone. Once you're locked in — by your friends, your payment history, your video library — switching is too costly, so the company can safely make the product worse.

  • Ad creep into things you pay for: Prime Video added ads (2024); Amazon search results are ~50% ads; paid apps still track you.
  • The surveillance cost: studies estimate the average person's data generates $200–500/year in ad-targeting value — and you see none of it and can't opt out.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The model rewards making the experience worse. Once network effects lock you in, a company can degrade service and tighten tracking because your switching cost is too high — and regulators move slower than product cycles.

The surprising part

Ads creeping into things you already pay for — your Prime membership, your Netflix subscription, your paid apps — is the tell that enshittification went mainstream. And the deepest twist: the people who feel most betrayed are the ones who pay AND get ads AND get tracked. They thought paying bought them out of being the product. That double-dip broke the unspoken deal — and it's sparking real defection (to ad-free tiers, open-source tools, privacy apps) more than any free-to-ads switch ever did.

In one sentence

Enshittification in a nutshell

As every product becomes an ad business, users increasingly pay money and get advertised to and get surveilled — which makes the products worse, not better, and that's now the backlash.
