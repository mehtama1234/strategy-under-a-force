Group A · The blueprint

Amazon retail media — the store is really an ad network

Amazon quietly built the world's third-biggest ad business — hiding inside the shop.

Amazon proved that a retailer's most valuable moment isn't the sale — it's the instant just before it, when you're searching and deciding. By selling brands the right to appear at that moment, using data on what you actually buy, Amazon built an ad business bigger than any traditional media company. This is the blueprint every retailer now copies.

The ad business inside the box

$14B → $56B
Amazon's ad revenue, 2019 to 2024 — now the #3 ad company on earth, behind only Google and Meta.
The margin flip

Amazon's retail operation runs at ~1–4.5% margin. Its ad business runs at ~40%. The result: ads are roughly a tenth of Amazon's revenue but contribute an estimated ~80% of its operating profit. Amazon now makes most of its money from ads, not boxes.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The ad business runs on Amazon's power over the merchants who sell on it and its control of shopping data — both regulatory flashpoints. The FTC and EU already view this leverage as anticompetitive; a forced separation of ads from the marketplace, or limits on data use, would wreck the margins.

The surprising part

Amazon accidentally proved something retail always suspected but could never measure: most retail profit comes not from selling goods, but from controlling shoppers' attention at the moment they decide to buy. The store operates near break-even; the ads make ~40%. That flipped the whole logic of retail — the shelves are now an ad network, and the selling of stuff is almost a loss-leader that assembles the shoppers.

In one sentence

Amazon retail media in a nutshell

Amazon built the world's third-biggest ad business by monetizing the moment of purchase intent — proving a store is really a physical ad network, and that the boxes are almost a loss-leader for the ads.
