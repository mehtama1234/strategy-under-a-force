Group B · The store becomes a media network

The retail-media gold rush — every store raced to copy Amazon

Once Amazon proved a store is an ad network, Walmart, Instacart, and every delivery app scrambled to build one.

After Amazon showed that shoppers are ad inventory, every retailer and delivery app raced to build its own ad network. The math was irresistible: retail and delivery run on razor-thin margins (5–15%), but advertising is 70–90% margin — and they already own the shoppers, the moment of purchase intent, and the first-party data.

The land grab

  • Walmart Connect: ~$3–4B a year and one of Walmart's fastest-growing, most profitable segments.
  • DoorDash, Target (Roundel), Kroger, Uber Eats: all launched ad networks (2019–2023), chasing the same CPG ad budgets.
  • The category hit ~$50–65B in the US (2024), growing 20–30% a year — the fastest-growing part of the entire ad industry.

The tell: Instacart's IPO

A grocery-delivery app that's really an ad company

Instacart's 2024 IPO filing gave away the secret: ads are ~30% of its revenue but more than half its gross profit — because ads carry 80%+ margins vs 30–40% for delivery. The delivery fee barely covers the cost of delivering. The real business is selling sponsored placement to brands. Delivery is the loss-leader; ads are the profit.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the vicious cycle

The ad network depends on shopper traffic and inventory. If e-commerce growth slows, the ad space dries up. And cramming in too many ads degrades the shopping experience — which erodes the very traffic that made the ads valuable in the first place.

The surprising part

Big consumer brands (P&G, Nestlé, Unilever) are quietly moving 20–30% of their ad budgets out of TV and into these retail networks — because an ad next to the "buy" button, aimed using real purchase data, converts far better than a TV spot. So the grocery aisle and the delivery app became more valuable as ad real estate than as places that sell food.

In one sentence

The retail-media gold rush in a nutshell

Every retailer and delivery app copied Amazon because first-party shopper data and purchase intent are worth far more than thin retail margins — turning stores and delivery apps into near-pure-margin ad networks.
