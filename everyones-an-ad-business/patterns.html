The patterns

Line up everyone the ad wave touched, and one theme dominates: ads are near-pure margin, so the product becomes the bait that assembles you — the real product — for sale to advertisers.

Each page asks one question: what did "everything is an ad business" make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable for this company? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions keep showing up. Click any name to jump to its page.

The one thing to take away

Advertising is the highest-margin business most companies will ever find, because the audience is already assembled by the "real" product. So any company with an audience, a screen, or your data turns into an ad seller — and the thing you think you're buying (the ride, the groceries, the TV, the bank account) increasingly exists to gather you, the true product, for sale to advertisers.

The recurring patterns

Pattern 01

Ads are near-pure margin, so the audience is the asset

Once you've built an audience, an extra ad costs almost nothing — ~90% margin at Google/Meta, ~40% at Amazon vs ~4% on retail. That gap is the whole force: nothing else a company sells comes close, so everyone chases it.

Pattern 02

The product is the bait; you are the product

The free search, the cheap TV, the ride, the streaming show — all exist to assemble and hold an audience whose attention and data get sold. The user isn't the customer; the advertiser is.

Google & Metacars & TVsthe whole force
Pattern 03

The high-margin ad business hides inside a thin-margin one

Look under the hood and the "real" business is the loss-leader. Amazon makes ~80% of its profit on ads, not boxes; Instacart earns more gross profit from ads than delivery; Uber's ads are pure margin versus thin rides.

AmazonInstacartUber
Pattern 04

Any surface with an audience becomes an ad network

Store, app, seatback, gas pump, elevator, TV, car dashboard, bank app — if people look at it or it holds their data, it gets turned into inventory. There's no category too unlikely.

physical screensairlines & hotelsbanks
Pattern 05

First-party data became the moat — that's why it all happened

When tracking cookies broke, the only durable targeting data was a company's own logged-in customers. So everyone with a direct relationship — retailer, bank, airline — raced to build a walled garden and sell ads inside it.

first-party databanksairlines & hotels
Pattern 06

No off switch — the offline world got screened and surveilled

The ads followed you off the internet. You can't refuel, ride an elevator, drive, or watch TV without meeting a targeted ad — powered by location and viewing surveillance you can't opt out of.

physical screenscars & TVsthe cost
Pattern 07

The middle skims, and the power concentrates

Up to half of every ad dollar disappears into ad-tech middlemen, and the "pure margin" only holds for giants who own supply and demand. So the wave enriches the walled gardens and centralizes power rather than spreading it.

the ad-tech taxwalled gardens
Pattern 08

The cost: the product gets worse

The more a thing becomes an ad business, the worse it gets for the user — "enshittification." Ads creep into what you already pay for, and you end up paying money and being the product and being surveilled.

enshittificationstreaming
