Group B · Membership & the club

Status & scarcity clubs

Some products create belonging by deliberately keeping people out — exclusion is the product.

Some products manufacture belonging by making sure you're not in: the Amex Centurion "black" card (invite-only), the Hermès Birkin (you must "earn" the right to buy by spending on other things first), luxury waitlists, invite-only apps (early Gmail, Clubhouse, Superhuman), and drop culture (Supreme, limited sneakers). Scarcity and exclusion aren't side effects — they are the product, the feeling of being one of the chosen few.

The core bet

Exclusion itself is what's being sold. Brands deliberately limit access — through price, invitation, lottery, or arbitrary gatekeeping — because what buyers pay for isn't the card or the bag, but proof they're rare enough to deserve it: "I am chosen."

What the numbers say

~$10k + $5k
Amex Centurion's annual fee plus initiation, invite-only since 1999 — the limit isn't spending, it's access.
$300k+ pre-spend
the cumulative spend on other Hermès goods buyers often make before being offered a Birkin; resale far exceeds retail.
10M → collapse
Clubhouse's downloads by March 2021 on invite-only hype, then a fast fade once exclusivity evaporated.
~500 units
a typical Supreme drop — releasing Thursdays only, reselling 5–20×; most units are speculation, not wear.
~10,000/yr
Ferrari's deliberate production cap — it could scale but won't, because scarcity is the status.

It sells the pain of not having it

The model doesn't make the thing more valuable — it makes not having it feel like failure. A fake Birkin gives 95% of the utility (a nice bag) and 0% of the signal. The value is pure belonging arbitrage: you're paying for the feeling of being inside a line most people are kept outside of.

Who ends up ahead

Brands that ration access — luxury houses, invite-only status products — extracting huge premiums on exclusion.

Who's exposed

Everyone kept out (by design); and the brand itself once the gate breaks and access normalizes.

The single biggest worry

The whole model collapses the instant access normalizes — and it's irreversible. Once Clubhouse invites were everywhere, it was just another app; once a drop is infinite, it's just a hoodie; once the Birkin waitlist clears, the magic is gone. Brands built on scarcity have no Plan B, because they can't pivot to "quality" without admitting exclusion was always the game.

The surprising part

Scarcity clubs thrive by making non-members feel like failures — you didn't get the invite, didn't earn the pre-spend, can't afford the drop. It's psychologically asymmetric: the excluded suffer far more than the included enjoy it. Clubhouse and Superhuman proved that when the exclusion is the only product, there's nothing left the moment the gate opens.

In one sentence

Manufactured scarcity sells the feeling of being chosen by making sure you're not — and when the exclusion mechanism breaks, there's nothing left to buy.
