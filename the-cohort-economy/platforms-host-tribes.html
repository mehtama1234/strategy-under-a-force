Group C · Community as the moat & engine

Platforms host tribes

As the open feed decays into noise, people retreat into subreddits, Discords, and group chats — and someone hosts them.

As the open social feed turns noisy and hostile, people retreat into smaller, curated spaces: subreddits, Discord servers, Facebook Groups, group chats — the so-called "cozy web." Reddit, Discord, and Meta don't create these communities; they host thousands of them and monetize the belonging. The value is being the home of other people's tribes.

The core bet

These platforms are landlords of tribalism. They bet that as public feeds become unbearable, people will pay and subscribe for managed neighborhoods — and the platforms extract value through data licensing, premium subscriptions, and ads inside closed communities.

Hosting tribes is lower-margin than the feed

Counterintuitively, hosting communities pays less than broadcasting to a feed. The audiences are smaller and fragmented, so ad loads and prices are lower — Reddit makes money licensing what people say and selling Nitro; Discord sells subscriptions, not ads. They survive because monetizing an open feed everyone hates no longer works, and because the cost of exit (your community's data and culture) is high.

Who ends up ahead

Platforms that become the home of other people's tribes — locking in communities and licensing their data.

Who's exposed

The platforms themselves if they over-monetize — the tenant tribes can pack up and leave for a clone.

The single biggest worry

The business breaks if the platform monetizes the tribes in ways that feel extractive to the hosts. Reddit's API revolt showed the fragility: communities rebelled and went dark. These are landlords renting to tenants who can leave — unlike Facebook, which owns both the land and the houses — so pushing rent (API fees, mandatory Nitro perks) too hard risks an exodus to Discord clones, Matrix, or self-hosted alternatives.

The surprising part

The surprising economics: being the host of belonging is a worse business than being the broadcast feed, yet it's where value is going, because the open-feed model is dying. The money comes not from eyeballs but from tribe-hosts paying for tools and from licensing the raw conversations locked inside closed spaces.

In one sentence

As open social collapses into noise, platform value shifts from reaching everyone to hosting anyone — and the money comes from tribe-hosts paying for tools and from licensing the data inside their closed rooms.
