# The Cohort Economy — strategy under a force

The 31st collection (Tier-3) — Family II (hidden-mechanism business models).

**The force:** identity, belonging, and community became the real product — and the real lock-in. More and more companies and creators sell membership in a tribe (a fandom, a subculture, a club, an identity) rather than just a product, because belonging is far stickier than any loyalty-points scheme. You cancel a rewards card; you don't cancel who you are. We ask: *what did the cohort economy make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** people don't churn out of an identity the way they churn out of a rewards program. The scarce thing modern business chases is no longer attention or even a subscription — it's belonging. A brand that becomes part of who you are (Harley, Apple, a fandom, a members' club) has a moat no discount can match, because leaving feels like betraying your tribe. The dark side: the same mechanics that build devoted community also build parasocial dependence, cult dynamics, and mobs that can turn on you.
