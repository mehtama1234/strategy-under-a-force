Group C · Community as the moat & engine

Crypto & DAO tribes

Crypto turned "join us and you'll get rich" into a contract — fusing belonging, identity, and a financial stake.

Crypto communities — Bitcoin maximalists, Ethereum, NFT projects like Bored Ape Yacht Club, meme coins, DAOs — fused identity, belonging, and a financial stake into one thing. Owning the token or NFT made you a member of a tribe that also had money riding on shared success, which supercharged the evangelism and the tribalism. It's community turned into co-ownership, with cult-like loyalty attached.

The core bet

Crypto weaponized token ownership: if you held the coin or NFT, you weren't a customer, you were a member with money riding on the outcome — which turned every holder into a forced marketer. Belonging and profit aligned, so evangelism became financial self-interest.

A community that has to be a cult

The dark structural truth: a crypto tribe where most of the value sits in early holders' wallets needs the price to rise to survive, so existing members have money riding on new members arriving. That breaks the social brake that lets normal communities exist at a stable size — you can't have a cozy neighborhood that only works if it keeps adding people. Crypto tribes don't just resemble pyramids; structurally many have to be.

Who ends up ahead

The genuinely useful infrastructure (Bitcoin's network, Ethereum's smart contracts) and the early holders who exited in time.

Who's exposed

Late arrivers left holding worthless tokens once the flow of new believers stopped — most NFT and meme-coin holders.

The single biggest worry

Because early holders profit only if new believers keep arriving, the community becomes a mandatory growth machine with cult recruitment mechanics baked into the economics. When the newcomers stop coming, the price — and the "belonging" that rode on it — collapses together, and there's often no product underneath.

The surprising part

The part that actually worked wasn't the tribe or the token — it was the problem-solving at the margins. The projects that survived (DeFi protocols with real fee revenue) were the ones where the token was almost an afterthought and the community was a side effect of useful code. The ~95% built only on tribe had nothing left once the pool of FOMO-able newcomers ran dry.

In one sentence

Crypto turned the oldest sales trick — "join us and get rich" — into a contract, making community a pyramid that collapses the moment new believers stop arriving.
