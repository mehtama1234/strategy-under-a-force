The patterns

Line up every corner of the cohort economy, and the same handful of mechanics repeat — all of them turning on one thing: identity is a switching cost nobody can undercut.

Each page asks: what did the cohort economy make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

The deepest moat in business is no longer a feature, a price, or an algorithm — it's belonging. When a brand becomes part of who someone is, leaving means abandoning their identity and their social world, so they don't. That gives free marketing, price insulation, and stickiness that outlasts the product. But the same force is a trap: the tribe owns you as much as you own it, dilutes the moment you scale it, and turns into a mob the instant it feels betrayed.

Pattern 01

Identity is a switching cost nobody can undercut

Points buy behavior and die to a better deal; belonging is made of who you are and the friends you'd lose, which no competitor can out-bid.

Pattern 02

The tribe markets and organizes for free

Superfans defeat bots, run campaigns, and stream in formation; ARMY built global strategies without being asked. The devotion that looks like spending is unpaid labor.

Pattern 03

Scale dilutes the very thing you're selling

Exclusivity and authenticity are strongest small; grow to satisfy investors and the aura evaporates (Soho House, co-opted subcultures, normalized invite-apps).

Pattern 04

The product can be worse — the friction is the moat

Harleys are worse bikes; Supreme sells basic tees; the $1,500 keyboard types no faster. The cost and inconvenience are what make the choice mean something.

Pattern 05

Exclusion sells the pain of being outside

Status clubs don't make the thing better — they make not having it feel like failure. Belonging arbitrage, psychologically asymmetric and irreversible once access opens.

Pattern 06

The parasocial bond is the engine

Fans feel a real relationship with a brand or creator who doesn't know them — which drives both the free evangelism and, at the dark end, arbitrage on loneliness.

Pattern 07

You become a hostage to your own base

Tie a business to an identity and you can't quietly pivot; the tightest bonds breed purity spirals, and one perceived betrayal flips the switching cost from infinity to zero.

Pattern 08

The moat is only community — so it's fragile at scale

Members are loyal to the tribe, not the company; toxicity, a scandal, or a botched pivot can reverse the flywheel faster than any rival could rebuild it.

The point of the series

This is the 31st collection, and the identity-deep cousin of the loyalty economy (belonging is the ultimate loyalty program — one you can't out-discount). It's what's left when the attention economy saturates (you stop renting eyeballs and start owning members), and the emotional flip-side of subscription fatigue (people cancel a bill, but not who they are).
