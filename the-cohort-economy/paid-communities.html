Group B · Membership & the club

Paid communities

Stop chasing ads and algorithms — get a few thousand true fans to pay you directly for belonging.

Creators and experts now sell direct access and belonging: Patreon (fans pay monthly to support a creator and join the inner circle), Substack (paid newsletters with community), Discord servers, Skool/Circle/Mighty Networks (paid communities and courses), Twitch subs. The pitch is Kevin Kelly's "1,000 true fans" — you don't need millions of followers, just a few thousand who pay you directly.

The core bet

Instead of depending on ad revenue and algorithmic reach, build a small, devoted audience willing to pay $5–50 a month for exclusive access and community. A thousand fans at $100 a year is a real income — with no platform or algorithm in between.

What the numbers say

The community is the moat, not the revenue

The membership fee often isn't the real money — it's permission to sell deeper. A creator with 2,000 members at $10/month ($240k/yr) frequently earns more from a $500 course sold to 10% of them, or a $5,000 annual cohort program sold to 5%, than from the memberships themselves. The paid community becomes the moat — proof of skin-in-the-game — not the revenue lever.

Who ends up ahead

Creators who already have an audience and own the direct relationship — free of any single platform's algorithm.

Who's exposed

Creators without an existing audience, who find the membership strategy stillborn — and any creator facing high churn.

The single biggest worry

The model only works if you already have an audience, usually built by giving away free work for 1–3 years first. And it swaps one dependency for another: paid communities see 3–5% monthly churn, so you're always recruiting, and the "no algorithm tax" is replaced by a membership tax and constant retention pressure.

The surprising part

The surprising inversion is who holds the leverage. Ad-and-algorithm platforms own your reach and can throttle it overnight; a paid community shifts the power to the creator, who owns the relationship. But the trade is retention dependence — you're no longer at the mercy of the feed, you're at the mercy of keeping a few thousand people feeling like they belong.

In one sentence

Paid memberships move leverage from platforms that own your reach to creators who own the relationship — but only if you already have an audience, and only by trading algorithm dependence for retention dependence.
