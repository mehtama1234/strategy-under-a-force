The Cohort Economy

How identity, belonging, and community became the deepest moat in business — and its most dangerous liability.

The 31st force. The scarce thing modern business chases is no longer attention or even a subscription — it's belonging. When a brand, creator, or product becomes part of who someone is — a Harley rider, a Swiftie, a CrossFitter, a Soho House member — they stop being a customer and become a member who won't churn on price and who markets to others for free. The one idea: people don't churn out of an identity the way they churn out of a rewards program.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did the cohort economy make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? Belonging became the un-copyable moat; the superfan and the vetted member became the most valuable customer; and an authentic, un-commercialized community became the scarce thing money can't easily buy. The danger: tie your business to an identity and the tribe owns you as much as you own it — and can turn into a mob overnight.
