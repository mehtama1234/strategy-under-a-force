Group C · The hard part

The hidden dependency

You reshored the last 5% — final assembly — while the deep 95% of the supply chain stayed Chinese.

Even reshored factories depend on Chinese inputs: raw materials, refined critical minerals, battery cells, components, and machinery. A US-assembled EV or solar panel still relies on Chinese-refined lithium, Chinese wafers, or Chinese cells. And "Mexican" or "Vietnamese" exports often contain Chinese content routed through to dodge tariffs. So reshoring often moves the final step home while the deep supply chain stays Chinese.

The core bet

Moving the visible final assembly home feels like resilience, but it's theater if the upstream — mining, refining, cells, wafers — stays routed through China. Reshoring the last step created the illusion of independence without the reality.

The deep chain stays Chinese

The supply chain has two tiers: a visible tier (final assembly, module mounting, welding) that got reshored, and a hidden tier (ore, refined metals, cells, wafers, power electronics) that stayed Chinese. Tariffs hit the final step but leave the upstream bottleneck in Chinese hands — so the West pays 30-58% more for the same fragile dependency, now buried and harder to see.

Who ends up ahead

China (which keeps upstream control and the margins), and firms that quietly kept the cheap Chinese inputs.

Who's exposed

Reshored plants that could go idle if China restricts a critical input — and taxpayers funding the illusion.

The single biggest worry

When — not if — China restricts rare-earth or lithium exports as leverage, reshored factories could go idle because they can't find non-Chinese inputs at any price. Tariffs moved the labor cost home but left the West holding supply-chain hostages: a US-assembled product made from Chinese-processed inputs that cost far more to source anywhere else.

The surprising part

Even aggressive tariffs haven't broken the dependency — they've just made it more expensive and more opaque. A US solar factory still sources ~97% of its wafers from China because a US wafer fab costs billions and takes five years. And Chinese firms are already relocating to Vietnam and Mexico to keep the exact same supply chain under the tariff wall. Reshoring the final step moved the problem sideways, not away.

In one sentence

Reshoring the last 5% of assembly while 95% of the chain — mining, refining, cells, wafers — stays controlled or routed through China created expensive resilience theater, not actual independence.
