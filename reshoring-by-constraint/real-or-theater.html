Group D · The mechanism & reality check

Real, or theater?

Construction spending tripled — but factory employment barely moved, and a lot of "reshoring" is re-labeling.

There's a big gap between announced factories and finished ones: press releases and groundbreakings pile up, but many projects are delayed, downsized, paused, or quietly cancelled. US manufacturing employment has barely moved even as construction spending soared, and some "reshoring" is really re-routing goods through Mexico or Vietnam. Yet some is genuinely happening — chips, defense. How much is real versus announcement theater?

The core bet

The honest verdict: the US is building factories and subsidizing production, but actual manufacturing jobs barely budge. Announcements pile up far faster than real output, and much of what counts as "reshoring" is logistics sleight-of-hand.

Employment barely moved

The bet rests on subsidies disguising an uncomfortable truth: US labor, land, and regulation make mass manufacturing uncompetitive without state support, and subsidies only work if permanent. Companies announce reshoring to lock in tax credits, then optimize for robots and lean staffing once the money is secure. A decade in, if employment hasn't moved despite $200B+ in investment, the bottleneck isn't geography — it's structural.

Who ends up ahead

Genuine capability areas (chips, defense) — plus the firms that captured subsidies and PR from the announcements.

Who's exposed

Communities promised jobs, and the credibility of "reshoring" once the announcements quietly scale down.

The single biggest worry

When incentives end or scale back, the political pressure to stay reshored evaporates — and much of the "return" is really re-routing goods through Mexico or Vietnam to hit trade thresholds, which delivers the label without the domestic jobs. The story is roughly one-third real capability, one-third subsidy theater, and one-third shell game.

The surprising part

The reshored factories cluster in swing and red states (Ohio, Arizona, Texas) where subsidies are highest and permitting fastest — not the rust-belt towns with idle infrastructure that needed them most. So reshoring advertises manufacturing's return and selects against the communities it could help most. And oddly, services (support centers, design hubs) reshored jobs faster than factories — the real "reshoring" is often talent-seeking, not cost arbitrage.

In one sentence

Reshoring is roughly one-third real capability (chips, defense), one-third subsidy theater (announcements that scale down quietly), and one-third shell game (goods re-routed through Mexico) — and none of it fixed why manufacturing left.
