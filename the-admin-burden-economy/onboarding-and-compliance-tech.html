Onboarding & compliance tech

Proving you're trustworthy became a bigger business than the thing being verified.

Beyond healthcare, a big slice of the admin-burden economy is onboarding and compliance: know-your-customer identity checks, background checks, tax and payroll onboarding, vendor verification, and security certifications like SOC-2. Startups and incumbents automate the paperwork of proving who you are and that you follow the rules — an endless, rule-driven need that only grows as regulation tightens.

The core bet

Every new customer, employee, or vendor triggers verification and compliance paperwork, so software that automates onboarding and proof-of-compliance sells into an endless, rule-driven need.

What the numbers say

$20B+
What U.S. banks spend a year on know-your-customer and anti-money-laundering compliance alone.
$8–10B
Size of the global identity-verification market (2023), growing ~15–20% a year.
$2.1B / $1.3B
Valuations of Vanta / Drata (SOC-2 compliance automation) — proving trust became a unicorn category.
$5k–$50k+
Annual cost of a SOC-2 audit — now table stakes to sell to big customers.
30–40%
Share of customers who abandon an application if identity verification takes too long.

You must buy compliance software just to sell

SOC-2 and KYC became prerequisites for market entry: startups now buy compliance software just to be allowed to sell to other companies. Proving trustworthiness turned into a mandatory purchase, and the software that certifies it can be worth more in venture money than much of what it verifies.

The single biggest worry

The whole business rides on regulation staying strict — deregulation or a shift in the rules could shrink it — and the tool meant to reduce friction can become friction itself, driving customers away if it's too slow.

The surprising part

The inversion is the surprise: the software that proves you're trustworthy (SOC-2, KYC, background checks) commands billion-dollar valuations, while the actual thing being verified is often a cost center. Proof of trust became a bigger, better business than the trust itself.

In one sentence

Compliance software sells because regulation and risk make onboarding and proof-of-trust mandatory, turning the paperwork of trustworthiness into a multibillion-dollar business riding on strict rules.
