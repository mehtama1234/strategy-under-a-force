Revenue-cycle management

Getting paid is so hard that hospitals outsource the whole money-chase — and one hack can freeze it all.

Revenue-cycle management (RCM) is the business of handling the entire money side of healthcare — registration, coding, claims submission, denial management, patient collections. U.S. billing is so tangled that hospitals and practices outsource it to specialized firms that take a cut of every dollar they collect. It's a large, consolidating, PE-heavy industry, and 'RCM consolidation' is a named Inc 5000 growth driver.

The core bet

Getting paid in U.S. healthcare is so hard that providers outsource the whole money-chasing process, creating a big recurring-revenue industry that takes a cut of every dollar collected.

What the numbers say

~$57B
Size of the U.S. RCM market in 2024, growing ~11%+ a year.
2–10%
The fee RCM firms typically take — a slice of everything they collect, so they scale with the complexity.
June 2024

$2.46B
What the February 2024 Change Healthcare ransomware attack cost UnitedHealth — it froze payments nationwide for weeks.
PE roll-up
Smaller practices' billing keeps getting bought into larger platforms, concentrating the plumbing.

A single outage can stop payments nationwide

The Change Healthcare hack in early 2024 made the fragility visible in real time: when one company controlled the payment pipes, hospitals couldn't access their own money for weeks. 'Getting paid' turned out to be not just a huge industry but a dangerously concentrated one.

The single biggest worry

Concentration is the danger — the 2024 Change Healthcare attack proved a single company's outage can freeze payments across the whole system — and AI claims-automation could erode the fat margins that make RCM so lucrative.

The surprising part

The scale is the surprise: the business of just handling billing and collections is worth tens of billions a year, bigger than many household-name industries. It exists only because being paid by insurers is such a nightmare — and it became a chokepoint that can halt the nation's health payments with one cyberattack.

In one sentence

Hospitals pay billions to firms that chase insurers and patients for money — a middleman business so profitable it became a PE roll-up and a chokepoint that one hack can freeze nationwide.
