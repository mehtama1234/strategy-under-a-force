Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

The limits

Can AI actually kill the paperwork — or will it just relocate the work and add an oversight tax?

This is the skeptical piece. Generative AI is the biggest threat and hope: it could genuinely shrink admin work — scribes, auto-coding, auto-appeals — which would gut the processing industries (BPO, RCM, coding jobs). But it might also just move the work into oversight, spawn AI-versus-AI arms races, and even raise total volume. Meanwhile reform (prior-auth rules, standard forms) could remove friction that firms monetize.

The core bet

The whole economy assumes the paperwork stays — but AI and reform could shrink it, threatening the very businesses built on processing it, or (more likely) just relocate the work.

The AI sellers are betting against their own customers

The firms selling 'AI for admin' are, in effect, betting against the industries that buy it: if the products truly work, they destroy the processing businesses they serve. The paradox is that the most bloated processes stay expensive precisely because they're profitable as-is — so the incentive to automate them away is weakest exactly where the burden is heaviest.

The single biggest worry

Either AI works and guts millions of admin jobs (mostly in lower-income countries), or it doesn't and just adds an expensive AI-oversight layer on top of the old bureaucracy — both outcomes are bad in different ways.

The surprising part

The twist that ties it together: killing the paperwork sounds like pure good news, but it would erase a multi-hundred-billion-dollar economy and millions of jobs. So the very industry most eager to sell automation is the one automation could destroy — and the friction it lives on is unusually well defended.

In one sentence

AI could kill the paperwork that employs millions and earns billions — but it's more likely to relocate the work and add an oversight tax instead.
