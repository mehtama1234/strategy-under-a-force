Group B · Who processes it

Business-process outsourcing

Entire economies were built on processing other countries' paperwork — and AI is coming for it.

A vast global industry — India, the Philippines, and others — runs the back-office paperwork of Western companies: claims processing, data entry, customer support, finance, HR administration. It's the labor-arbitrage engine of the admin-burden economy, built on shipping routine work to where wages are far lower. And it now faces an AI reckoning aimed straight at the repetitive tasks it was built on.

The core bet

The world's paperwork is so voluminous that companies ship it to lower-cost countries to process at scale, building a multi-hundred-billion-dollar labor-arbitrage industry.

The AI that clients bought may hollow out their back office

Generative AI directly automates the repetitive, rule-based tasks BPO exists to handle — form processing, data extraction, basic support. A $300B industry built on cheap labor for predictable paperwork now faces software that does that work for pennies per transaction with no wage bill, putting millions of jobs at risk.

The single biggest worry

AI threatens exactly the repetitive work the industry runs on, so the same cost-cutting technology Western clients hoped would trim their bills could hollow out the offshore workforce that did their admin.

The surprising part

The poignant part is what's being automated. India and the Philippines built middle classes and national policy on processing other countries' paperwork over three decades — treating it as a permanent export engine. Now the same AI clients bought to cut their own costs is being turned on the very industry that was those workers' path up.

In one sentence

The global back-office — a $300B labor-arbitrage machine that lifted millions in low-cost countries — is being automated away by the same firms that once outsourced to it.
