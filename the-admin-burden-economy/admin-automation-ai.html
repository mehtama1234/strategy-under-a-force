Group C · Who tames it

Admin-automation & AI

The most valuable AI in the hospital doesn't cure disease — it writes the doctor's notes.

On the other side of the burden are the software and AI companies promising to make it disappear: AI medical scribes, prior-auth automation, AI coding, and robotic process automation. Generative AI supercharged them in 2023–2025. They sell the dream of eliminating drudgery — but often just shift where the work happens, and profit from a complexity they don't truly kill.

The core bet

If the paperwork is the cost, sell the tool that eliminates it — AI scribes, automated coding, and prior-auth bots promise to remove the drudgery and capture a slice of the savings.

What the numbers say

Documentation time AI scribes save a doctor per shift — the clearest win so far.

Automation often relocates the work, not removes it

The doctor still reviews the AI's note; compliance still audits the automated prior-auth; finance still watches the coding bot for errors. Customers trade one drudgery (writing) for another (checking the machine), and the vendors profit from the complexity they claim to solve rather than from it truly disappearing.

The single biggest worry

Automation may just move the work — creating new review and oversight tasks — rather than remove it, so the paperwork migrates while the vendors still get paid.

The surprising part

The telling fact is where AI actually succeeded first: not diagnosing cancer, but writing the notes. A scribe task (audio to text, little judgment) proved easier and more valuable to automate than clinical reasoning — so the highest-value 'medical AI' so far is really administrative AI, capturing hundreds of millions by freeing physician time from paperwork.

In one sentence

Admin-automation vendors sell the dream of erasing paperwork but often just convert drudgery into oversight — pocketing the savings while a different burden remains.
