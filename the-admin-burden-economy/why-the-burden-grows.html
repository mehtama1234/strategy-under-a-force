Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

Why the burden grows

A ratchet that only tightens — because the friction is somebody's revenue.

This is the mechanism. Administrative burden grows through a self-reinforcing loop: complex multi-payer systems and layers of rules create friction; every new rule, fraud check, or verification adds paperwork; and — crucially — the friction is somebody's revenue, so the players who profit have no reason to simplify. Automation often adds an oversight layer rather than removing the work.

The core bet

The burden grows because complexity benefits the people who process it, so there's no one whose job is to make it smaller — it's a ratchet that only tightens.

Automation relocates the work, it doesn't shrink it

When hospitals automated eligibility checks, workers didn't disappear — they moved to 'eligibility-verification auditing.' When coding software arrived, coders shifted to 'exception handling and appeals.' Complexity is a choice nobody chose but everybody maintains: regulators avoid repealing old rules, vendors avoid obsoleting products, workers avoid losing their expertise.

The single biggest worry

The loop is locked in by incentives and politically sticky, because every layer of friction has a constituency — vendors, processors, compliance staff — that profits from keeping it thick and lobbies against removing it.

The surprising part

The counter-intuitive finding is that automation didn't shrink the burden — it moved it. Each tool that eliminated one task spawned a new one to supervise it, so total admin employment kept climbing even as the software got better. The paperwork is conserved, like energy, and merely changes form.

In one sentence

The administrative burden stays high not because the complexity is necessary, but because the people whose job is to manage it benefit from keeping it thick.
