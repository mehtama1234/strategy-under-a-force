Group A · The paperwork mountain

Billing, coding & credentialing

The invisible professions that translate a doctor's visit into money — and a doctor into a payable provider.

Every medical visit must be translated into standardized codes, billed to insurers, and every provider must be 'credentialed' — verified and enrolled with each insurer before they can bill at all. These are large, specialized professions and outsourced industries. Coding errors, credentialing delays, and billing complexity each create whole businesses that exist just to turn care into collected money.

The core bet

Turning a doctor's visit into a paid claim requires specialized coding, billing, and credentialing work, so a large industry exists just to translate care into money without losing it to errors and denials.

What the numbers say

~70,000
Diagnosis and procedure codes in ICD-10 (including W61.42, 'struck by a duck') — up from ~14,000 in ICD-9 (2015).
~220,000
U.S. medical billing-and-coding workers (median pay ~$42k–$50k); AAPC alone has 190,000+ members.
60–120+ days
How long it takes to credential and enroll a new doctor with a single insurer — before they can bill a cent.
$262B
Annual cost of denied claims (CAQH), ~40% of it from coding errors, prior-auth gaps, or eligibility mismatches.
~75%
Share of medical coding and billing now outsourced to third-party or offshore vendors.

You can't get paid until the paperwork says you can

A doctor can't collect a single dollar until credentialed with each payer — even after years at the same hospital, enrollment can take 120+ days. That delay is so universal that a whole sub-industry exists just to speed it up, charging hospitals a slice of revenue to compress timelines across the 50–100 payers they contract with.

The single biggest worry

AI auto-coding is improving faster than the workforce grows; if it reaches real-world reliability, a profession employing hundreds of thousands could shrink by half in a decade — offshore centers first.

The surprising part

The absurd detail that captures it: there are ~70,000 codes, including one for being struck by a duck, and a doctor who's worked somewhere for years still can't bill until a months-long enrollment clears. The professions nobody sees — coders, billers, credentialing specialists — are the machinery that turns care into cash.

In one sentence

The system needs hundreds of thousands of specialists and a quarter-trillion-dollar error budget just to translate visits into paid claims — and AI now threatens to automate the whole pipeline.
