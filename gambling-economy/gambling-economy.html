Group E · The big picture

The gambling economy

Every screen became a casino.

Pull the thirteen pages together and one shift explains all of them. Gambling escaped its walled garden — Vegas, the lottery, the back room — and migrated to every screen. Legal sports betting, online casinos, micro-bets, gamified trading, prediction markets, loot boxes, social casinos, and "skill game" loopholes made a bet as frictionless as opening an app. The smartphone made it constant; revenue-hungry states, leagues, and platforms all found a moat in the model.

The one idea

Every screen became a casino. Be fair, though — "it's all predatory" overstates it. Many people gamble responsibly for fun, and legalization did move volume out of illegal markets with zero protections and zero tax. The problem is scale, accessibility, engineered addiction, and the state's conflict of interest.

Winners vs losers

Winners
Operators (DraftKings/FanDuel), sports leagues, media, app stores, casinos, and states collecting the tax.
Losers
Problem gamblers and their families (debt, harm), state welfare/mental-health systems, and lower-income households.
The biggest tension

States and leagues got rich on legalization's promise to move gambling away from crime. But now those same governments depend on the tax revenue — a multibillion-dollar habit — so they'll never enforce addiction prevention hard enough, because losing that money means cutting schools or roads. They're the dealer defending the revenue stream while claiming to protect the customer.

The surprising part

The whale concentration is tighter than Vegas. In a physical casino, 20–30% of players fund most profit; in apps, a few percent of "high-value" users fund the whole thing. The system is engineered to maximize that small addicted fraction, not serve the casual player — because casual players are never profitable.

In one sentence

Gambling became ubiquitous, invisible, and endlessly renewable because states legalized the revenue model and tech made the friction free — and now nobody can afford to shut it down.

How this connects to the rest of the series

The gambling economy is the sharpest edge of a family of attention-and-money forces. Its engineered engagement is the attention economy weaponized for money; its whale model mirrors the loyalty economy's reliance on the heaviest users; and its blur of betting into "investing" is the flip side of everyone becoming a bank.
