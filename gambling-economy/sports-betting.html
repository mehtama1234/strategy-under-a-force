Group A · The legalization gold rush

Sports betting

One 2018 court ruling turned a fringe vice into a $148-billion one-tap market.

In 2018 the Supreme Court struck down the federal ban on sports betting and handed the choice to the states. Within six years, 38+ states legalized it, and Americans now bet nearly $150B a year — over 95% of it on a phone. DraftKings and FanDuel became a duopoly, and the very leagues and networks that once fought gambling now partner with it and take a cut.

The core bet

The bet: make betting as frictionless as opening an app, hook new users with "free bets," and let everyone with an audience — leagues, media, the state — take a slice. Legalization was sold as consumer protection; it also created a vast, always-on habit.

The leagues flipped

The speed was stunning. The NFL once banned betting ads and ESPN refused gambling content; now they eagerly partner with sportsbooks, sell official real-time data, and run ESPN Bet. One court ruling flipped the whole ecosystem from taboo to one tap away, faster than most app rollouts.

What's newly valuable
The betting app and its odds, official league data, and betting-adjacent media — everyone with an audience monetizes the bet.
What's harmed
Helpline calls nearly doubled (2020–23), and studies link access to more bankruptcies.
The single biggest worry

Studies tie betting access to rising personal bankruptcies, and helpline calls nearly doubled. That ~70% of profits comes from under 1% of users is a sign the industry profits most from the heaviest — and most harmed — bettors.

The surprising part

In under a decade, the same leagues and networks that fought gambling tooth-and-nail rushed to monetize it — one Supreme Court ruling turned sports betting from illegal almost everywhere into a $148B market controlled by two companies and endorsed by the leagues that once banned it.
