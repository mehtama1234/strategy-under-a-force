The synthesis · what repeats across all 13

The patterns

Line up everywhere betting now lives, and one theme dominates: the casino escaped its walls and moved into every screen.

Each page asks: what did the gambling economy make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

Once a bet became as frictionless as opening an app, gambling stopped being a place you go and became a layer on every screen — sports, stocks, games, elections. Value flows to whoever owns the betting screen and the odds; the harm concentrates in a small, engineered-for minority of heavy users, and the state took a cut and looked away.

The point of the series

This is the 24th collection. Its signature: "every screen became a casino." Its engineered engagement is the attention economy weaponized for money; its whale model mirrors the loyalty economy; and its blur of betting into "investing" is the flip side of everyone becoming a bank.
