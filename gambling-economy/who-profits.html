Group D · The forces & the harm

Who profits, who pays

Everyone with a hand on the screen takes a cut; the heaviest user pays the bill.

Follow the money. Everyone gets a slice of the betting screen: the operators (DraftKings, FanDuel), the sports leagues (data and sponsorship), the media (ESPN Bet, endless ads and podcasts), the app stores and payment rails, and the state (taxes). Meanwhile the losses fall disproportionately on lower-income and problem gamblers, with new research linking legalization to bankruptcy, worse finances, and even domestic-violence spikes.

The core bet

The bet is a closed loop: operators, leagues, media, app stores, and states all extract fees from the same betting screens, while the financial losses and harms concentrate in the households least able to afford them. It's regressive, and the harm is externalized.

A closed loop

The same media that report the scores sell the betting ads and take the sponsorship — profiting from both the game and the wager on the game. The state taxes it at 10–51%, so 41 states now lean on the revenue. And the losses cluster among lower-income households spending a dangerous share of income, so the whole thing works only if people lose more.

Who profits
Operators, leagues, media, app stores, and the state — every party with a hand on the screen.
Who pays
Lower-income and problem gamblers, and the health and welfare systems that absorb the fallout.
The single biggest worry

Lower-income households and problem gamblers fuel most of the profit while bearing most of the harm — bankruptcy, overdrafts, lost savings — and states grow dependent on a regressive tax that only works when people lose.

The surprising part

Legalization didn't just permit betting — it rewired the money flow so that upset losses now correlate with 10%+ spikes in intimate-partner violence in states with mobile betting. The concentration of losses in angry, impulsive bettors creates a measurable public-health externality that none of the parties collecting fees are required to pay for.

In one sentence

Legalized betting is a regressive tax on the households least able to lose — extracted by operators, leagues, media, and states, with the harms externalized onto the poor.
