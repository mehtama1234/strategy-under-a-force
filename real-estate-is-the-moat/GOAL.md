# Real Estate Is the Moat — strategy under a force

The nineteenth "strategy under a force" collection (scout-queue: real-estate-is-the-moat).

**The force:** for a surprising number of companies, the real business — the real moat, the real money — isn't what they appear to sell. It's the real estate underneath. Restaurants, retailers, casinos, and logistics firms are, on closer look, landlords and property plays wearing an operating-company costume. We ask: *what did "the land is the real asset" make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** the business is the front; the land is the asset. A company can look like it sells burgers, or storage, or search results, while the thing that actually compounds in value — and the thing that makes the money or backstops the debt — is the dirt and the buildings it owns. Value flows to whoever owns the ground everyone else has to stand on; the risk is that when you financialize the land (sale-leasebacks, REITs, cheap-money leverage), a rate shock can turn the moat into a millstone.
