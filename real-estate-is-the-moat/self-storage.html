Self-storage

Your clutter is their moat — and you rarely move it out.

Self-storage is one of the highest-margin, most recession-resistant real-estate plays there is. You own cheap, low-upkeep metal boxes, fill them with people's clutter, and quietly raise rents on tenants too committed to move their stuff. The stickiness comes from switching costs and pure inertia — once your junk is inside, you rarely take it out.

The core bet

The bet is that you're not really renting a shed — you're renting someone's inability to throw things away. Cheap to build, cheap to run, and customers who stay put even as the rent climbs 5–8% a year. That inertia is the moat.

What the numbers say

$47B
US self-storage market (2026), with 2.69B sq ft of space. Public Storage and Extra Space are the giant REITs at the top.
30–40%
Net operating margins on stabilized facilities after every cost — rivaling software, on a business that looks like a row of sheds.
~93%
Occupancy at stabilized REITs (late 2025). Tenants pay $50–300/month and mostly stay, even as rates rise on schedule.
48% → 31%
Mom-and-pop market share, 2014 to 2026, as REITs rolled them up — Public Storage's $10.5B buy of National Storage Affiliates (2026) is the latest. (See the roll-up.)

Renting inertia

Once a unit is full, moving out is a pain — so operators raise rents on existing tenants who won't bother to leave. Add ancillary revenue (locks, insurance, boxes, climate control) and a dumpy-looking business throws off cloud-software margins. You're monetizing the human reluctance to declutter.

What's newly valuable
Cheap buildings, sticky tenants, pricing power on existing renters, and a fragmented market ripe for roll-up.
What could bite
Oversupply in hot markets and softening move-in demand as the housing cycle turns.
The single biggest worry

Demand is softening — move-in rates fell ~11% year over year in late 2025 and occupancy growth stalled. A recession or housing crash could hollow out occupancy, exposing operators who leaned on the assumption that ~90% occupancy is permanent.

The surprising part

A metal shed full of other people's clutter earns margins that rival cloud software. The magic is that once a unit is full, the tenant almost never leaves — even as rent climbs every year. You're not selling a product; you're renting inertia, and inertia doesn't shop around.

In one sentence

Self-storage is the ultimate quiet moat: cheap sheds full of emotional baggage, tenants trapped by inertia, paying ever-higher rent because moving their stuff isn't worth it.
