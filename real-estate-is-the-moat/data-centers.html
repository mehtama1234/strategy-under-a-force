Group B · The new land grab

Data centers

The AI boom is, underneath, a land-and-power grab.

AI turned data centers into the hottest real estate of the 2020s. The scarce input often isn't the building or even the chips — it's power: land near cheap electricity and an available grid connection. Data-center REITs and the hyperscalers are racing for sites, and old industrial land near power is suddenly worth a fortune.

The core bet

The bet is that AI is becoming power-constrained, not chip-constrained. Whoever controls gigawatt-scale power on land they own holds a chokepoint as valuable as prime real estate was in past booms. The moat is the site plus the electrons.

What the numbers say

$260B → ~$680B
Hyperscaler capex (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta, Oracle) — ~$260B in 2025, with commitments toward ~$660–690B for 2026. Much of it is land, power, and buildings.
+300–500%
Appreciation of land near power substations in Virginia, Iowa, and Texas since 2023. Land is now 10–20% of project cost, up from 3–5%.
+40–60%
Rise in data-center REIT valuations (Equinix, Digital Realty) 2023–25 — they own the physical scarcity of land plus grid hookups.
65%
Share of delayed data-center projects that blamed power grid interconnection queues — not zoning or chips. Electrons are the bottleneck.

Electrons, not chips

A data center with budget chips and constant gigawatt power can beat a smaller center with the latest chips and shaky grid access. That flips the map: land near old coal and industrial sites — places nobody wanted — is now strategic, because it comes with power and transmission. In the Southwest, even water rights for cooling can be a deal-killer. This is the real-estate face of the energy transition.

What's newly valuable
Sites with power and grid access, brownfield industrial land near transmission, and the water rights to cool it all.
What's risky
Enormous capital sunk into buildings that could be stranded if AI demand or power economics shift.
The single biggest worry

If a handful of hyperscalers lock up the best power sites in the next few years, everyone else faces long waits or gets priced out — a winner-take-most grab. And if AI demand disappoints, some of the most capital-intensive real estate in history could sit underused.

The surprising part

The real barrier to AI isn't GPUs anymore — it's power. Land near a coal plant that nobody wanted is now as strategic as land in a tech hub. Cloud companies that began as pure software are now among the fastest land-grabbers in America, buying rural acreage faster than developers build office parks.

In one sentence

AI's hunger for power turned data-center land into the new oil derrick — whoever owns cheap, constant electricity on their own dirt holds the economy's next chokepoint.
