Real Estate Is the Moat

How, for a surprising number of companies, the land underneath is the real business.

The nineteenth force. Look closely at a lot of companies and the real moat isn't what they sell — it's the dirt they sit on. McDonald's is a landlord that serves burgers; casinos sold the Strip and rent it back; the AI boom is a land-and-power grab. Value flows to whoever owns the ground everyone else has to stand on. The one idea: the business is the front; the land is the asset.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did "the land is the real asset" make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? The durable moat is often the property, not the product — and the risk is that once you financialize the land (REITs, sale-leasebacks, cheap-money leverage), a rate shock can turn the moat into a millstone.
