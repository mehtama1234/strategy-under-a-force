Group A · The storefront & the rails

The marketing-app stack

A dozen apps just to run the store — a recurring tax on the act of selling.

Running a Shopify or DTC store means renting a stack of apps: email and SMS marketing (Klaviyo, Attentive), reviews, loyalty, subscriptions (Recharge), analytics, and dozens more — each a monthly fee or a cut. This 'app economy' is a reliable business layered on top of every merchant, charging whether or not the store makes a sale.

The core bet

Every online store needs a dozen tools to operate and market, so selling those apps to millions of merchants is a steady, recurring business — a tax on the act of running a store.

The 'owned' channel is the most profitable one

Email and SMS are the only channels a store truly owns — it keeps the customer list even if an app shuts down — and they deliver the highest return precisely because they bypass the ad duopoly and its rising prices. A TikTok campaign evaporates when you stop paying; an email list is an asset that stays.

The single biggest worry

The platform (Shopify) can build any of these features natively — email, reviews, loyalty — and make the app obsolete overnight, so every app-maker lives under the threat of being copied by the store it depends on.

The surprising part

The uncomfortable arithmetic is that the tools to run a store can cost more per month than the store makes. A merchant assembles a stack of a dozen paid apps to operate, and every one of them gets paid on schedule whether the store turns a profit or not — the recurring tax comes first, the merchant's margin comes last.

In one sentence

Running a store means renting a stack of marketing apps that can cost more per month than the store makes — and they all vanish if the platform changes its rules.
