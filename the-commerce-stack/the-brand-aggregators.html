Group C · The models on top

The brand aggregators

Raise billions, roll up hundreds of tiny Amazon sellers, flip the bundle — then watch it implode.

Around 2020–2021, 'aggregators' like Thrasio, Perch, and Berlin Brands raised billions to buy up small third-party Amazon and DTC brands and bundle them for scale. It was a hyped roll-up of e-commerce's long tail. Then rising rates, integration problems, and a cooling market broke the model — Thrasio filed for bankruptcy in February 2024.

The core bet

Buy hundreds of small Amazon brands cheaply, bundle them, cut costs and cross-optimize, and sell the bundle for a higher multiple — a roll-up of the long tail of e-commerce.

Buying brands was easy; running hundreds of them was not

The model assumed capital would stay cheap and that operating 200+ tiny brands was just arithmetic — buy them, install systems, harvest cash. But most were fragile, thin-margin operators that needed hands-on work, not spreadsheet optimization. When rates spiked and Amazon improved its own seller support, the desperate-seller supply and the easy-flip exit both dried up at once.

The single biggest worry

It rested on two false assumptions — cheap money and easy integration — and when both proved wrong, the debt used to buy fragile brands became the thing that sank the whole structure.

The surprising part

The blunt lesson is that it was financial engineering that mistook a spreadsheet for a business. The aggregators bought brands at 3–4x revenue expecting to sell at 6–8x, but that exit vanished the instant growth slowed and debt got expensive. They were betting on momentum, not a moat — and the reckoning came fast.

In one sentence

A $16B bet that you could roll up cheap Amazon sellers and flip the bundle collapsed when rates rose, e-commerce flatlined, and Amazon out-competed the aggregators.
