Group C · The models on top

Marketplaces vs owned

Amazon hands you customers but takes half; your own store keeps the margin but makes you buy every visitor.

Sellers face a fork: sell on a marketplace (Amazon, Walmart, Etsy) that brings traffic but takes a big cut and owns the customer, or run your own store (Shopify) where you own the customer but must buy your own traffic. Amazon's take on a third-party sale can top 50%, but its built-in demand is unmatched — so most brands end up on both.

The core bet

The trade-off is traffic versus ownership — a marketplace hands you customers but takes a big cut and the relationship; your own store keeps the relationship but makes you pay for every visitor.

What the numbers say

~50%+
Amazon's total take on a third-party sale: ~15% referral + FBA fees + ads to rank.
~60%
Share of Amazon units sold by third-party sellers, not Amazon itself.
~$140B
Amazon's seller-services revenue (fees + fulfillment) in 2024 — its fastest-growing unit.
~$50B+
Amazon's advertising business — sellers pay to be seen on a platform they already pay to list on.
85–90%
Share of high-volume sellers who run on both a marketplace and their own store to spread the risk.

Amazon makes more taxing sellers than selling its own goods

Amazon now earns more from charging its own third-party sellers (fees plus ads) than from selling products itself — pure margin, because it pushed the inventory risk onto the sellers. And the flip side bites too: 'owning your customer' on Shopify is a fantasy if you can't afford paid traffic, so many sellers go broke on their own store and crawl back to the marketplace that was handing them demand.

The single biggest worry

On a marketplace you never own the customer and the platform can undercut you with private label or change fees; on your own store you drown without paid traffic — neither path is safe, and both feed a toll-collector.

The surprising part

The sharp irony is that 'owning the customer' is worthless if you can't afford to reach them. A Shopify store that keeps 100% of a razor-thin margin still loses if each customer costs $0.80 in ads to find — so the freedom to own the relationship quietly becomes the obligation to rent the traffic.

In one sentence

Marketplaces are gravity wells that drain seller profit but hand you traffic; owned stores keep the margin but make you buy every customer.
