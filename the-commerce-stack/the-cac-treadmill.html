Group B · The two things that got scarce

The CAC treadmill

The real cost of a DTC brand isn't the product — it's the ad bill, and Apple broke it in 2021.

A direct-to-consumer brand's biggest cost isn't making the product — it's acquiring the customer, bought from Facebook and Google. As competition rose and Apple's iOS 14 privacy change broke ad targeting, customer-acquisition cost (CAC) soared and the DTC model cracked. The ad duopoly quietly sets the economics of the entire industry.

The core bet

In DTC the scarce, expensive thing is a new customer, and it's bought from Meta and Google — so the ad duopoly quietly sets the economics of the whole industry.

DTC was an arbitrage, not a business model

The DTC brands were, at bottom, arbitraging cheap and precise Facebook ads to reach the exact person who wanted their $95 glasses or $100 pillow. When Apple closed off the tracking data and ad prices doubled, the arbitrage evaporated — and the brands discovered they'd never solved the real problem of acquiring customers affordably at scale. They'd borrowed that solution from Facebook.

The single biggest worry

When the cost to acquire a customer exceeds what that customer will ever spend, the business breaks — no matter how good the product — and no amount of brand love fixes negative unit economics.

The surprising part

The startling part is how one company's privacy setting rewired a whole sector. A single Apple release turned a generation of 'disruptive' brands from profitable to underwater almost overnight, because their real engine was never the product — it was cheap, well-targeted ads they didn't control.

In one sentence

When Apple locked down iPhone tracking in 2021, it killed the cheap targeting that made DTC work, ad prices soared, and customer acquisition went from profitable to math-breaking.
