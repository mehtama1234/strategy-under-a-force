Group B · The two things that got scarce

Fulfillment & logistics

Customers expect Amazon-Prime speed — so speed became a feature brands must rent, not build.

Once a store makes a sale, it must store, pick, pack, and ship the product — and customers now expect Amazon-Prime-fast, free delivery. That spawned third-party logistics (3PL) firms and fulfillment networks (ShipBob, Amazon FBA). Logistics is the hard, physical, capital-heavy part of e-commerce, and it's nearly impossible for a small brand to match Amazon alone.

The core bet

Customers expect fast free shipping, so whoever can store and deliver a small brand's orders quickly — or match Amazon's speed — controls a scarce physical capability the brand can't build itself.

What the numbers say

1–2 days
The delivery bar Amazon Prime set (now often same-day) — table stakes across US e-commerce by 2019.
~$1.2–1.5T
Global size of the third-party logistics (3PL) market.
~$2.1B
What Shopify paid for Deliverr in 2022 to build fulfillment — before exiting and selling it to Flexport in 2023.
5–15%
Share of order value that shipping eats for most small retailers.
~$800B+
Annual global cost of returns — the hidden back-half of the logistics bill.

Even Shopify decided it couldn't win logistics

Logistics is brutal — low margins, high capital costs, and Amazon's unbeatable scale. Even Shopify, a $40B+ company, bet billions it could own fulfillment end-to-end, then concluded Amazon and Flexport had already won and folded. A small brand's choices are to hand Amazon 40–50% of order value, rent a 3PL, or try to build a warehouse — each of which squeezes the margin.

The single biggest worry

It's capital-heavy and low-margin, and matching Amazon's scale is nearly impossible — when even Shopify retreated from running it, the message to smaller players was clear.

The surprising part

The plain truth behind 'free shipping' is that it's never free — it's priced into the product or eaten by the brand as a loss leader. Speed stopped being something a brand builds and became a feature it rents from Amazon or a 3PL, which is exactly why the physical layer of e-commerce is such a reliable toll and such a hard business to win.

In one sentence

Customers expect Amazon-speed delivery, so any brand that can't ship fast must hand 40–50% of each order to someone who can — turning fulfillment into a tax on selling online.
