The Shopify economy

Arm millions of merchants against Amazon — and take a small slice of everything they sell.

Shopify lets anyone launch an online store in an afternoon and takes a cut of the merchant's activity — subscription, payments, apps, shipping. It positioned itself as 'arming the rebels' against Amazon, giving small brands the tools to sell direct. Like any good picks-and-shovels business, it profits from millions of merchants whether or not any single one wins.

The core bet

Sell the tools to millions of would-be store owners and take a slice of everything they sell — you win whether the individual store succeeds or not.

What the numbers say

~$290B
Shopify's gross merchandise volume in 2024 — roughly 10% of US e-commerce runs on it.
~5M+
Stores across 175+ countries built on Shopify.
~$8B
Shopify's 2024 revenue, up from ~$4.6B in 2021.
~70%+
Share of revenue from 'merchant solutions' (payments, shipping) rather than the store subscription (~20%).
$1 in $4
How much of the platform's volume runs through Shopify Payments — the real money-maker.

It earns far more from payments and apps than from the store

The store subscription is the smallest part of the business — Shopify makes roughly three times as much from processing payments and taking a share of apps. 'Arming the rebels' quietly built a distributed selling network that rivals legacy e-commerce in sheer volume, while Shopify itself owns no inventory and takes no inventory risk.

The single biggest worry

Its revenue is tethered to merchant health and payment volume, so a DTC downturn or rising ad costs hit it directly — and Amazon is building its own seller tools to pull the same merchants into its walled garden.

The surprising part

The neat inversion is that Shopify sells stores but makes its money on everything around the store. The $29 subscription is the hook; the payments, the apps, the shipping, and Shop Pay are the business. It got big not by charging rent for the storefront but by taking a toll on the commerce that flows through it.

In one sentence

Shopify arms millions of merchants against Amazon by taking a small slice of every sale, payment, and app — winning whether any single store survives.
