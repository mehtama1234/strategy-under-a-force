Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

The limits

The toll-collectors can win the battle and lose the war — they need merchants alive to pay tolls.

This is the skeptical piece. The commerce-stack economy has hard limits: for brands, customer-acquisition cost now often exceeds lifetime value; the chokepoint owners hold the pricing power; the tool stack can cost more than a store's profit; and new shocks loom — TikTok Shop dependence, an AI flood of look-alike stores, and tariff changes ending the cheap-import loophole that Temu and Shein exploit.

The core bet

The whole stack assumes brands can profitably acquire customers and pay all the tolls — but when the tolls exceed the margin, the brands can't survive, and the toll-collectors are left fighting over a shrinking base.

The arms dealers are hollowing out the base they need

A commerce system where the infrastructure captures 60–80% of the margin isn't stable — Shopify needs profitable merchants, Stripe needs volume, Meta needs customers with money to spend. If all the profit flows to the toll-collectors, there's nothing left for the brands that actually make things, and the toll-collectors slowly starve their own tax base.

The single biggest worry

The real danger isn't a rival to Shopify — it's that ultra-cheap direct-from-China imports and AI-generated stores attack the whole Western commerce stack at its root, removing the margin that pays for ads, fees, and brands in the first place.

The surprising part

The twist that ends the story is that the arms dealers can win every skirmish and still lose. They took the ad, payment, and logistics wars — but they built a system where the merchant, the one who generates the sale, keeps the least. Squeeze that layer to death, and the tolls have nothing left to tax.

In one sentence

Brands can't survive paying more to acquire a customer than that customer spends — and the toll-collectors can't survive if there are no brands left to tax.
