# The Commerce Stack — strategy under a force

The 44th collection (Tier-5, seeded by the Inc 5000 analysis) — Family II (hidden-mechanism business models).

**Inc 5000 evidence:** the commerce half of "Creator & Commerce" — the single biggest fast-growing theme (867 companies, ~$32.8B) — is packed with DTC brands, Shopify-ecosystem apps, agencies, payments, fulfillment, and marketing-tech firms.

**The force:** e-commerce exploded and starting an online store became nearly free, so a whole industry grew up selling the *infrastructure* of online selling — store software (Shopify), payments, fulfillment/3PL, ads and marketing (the customer-acquisition treadmill), reviews, email/SMS, and the brand aggregators who rolled up DTC brands (and then imploded). We ask: *what did the commerce stack make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** starting a store became free — so the money moved to the tools, the ads, and the shipping. Anyone can launch a brand in an afternoon, which means the store itself stopped being scarce; the scarce things became getting *seen* (customer acquisition, now taxed by the ad duopoly) and getting the product *to the door* (logistics, now judged against Amazon Prime). So the picks-and-shovels — Shopify, Stripe, the 3PLs, the marketing apps, Google and Meta — capture the reliable margin, while most direct-to-consumer brands get squeezed between rising ad costs and Amazon's gravity. The arms dealers win; most of the soldiers don't.

**Groups:**
- **A — The storefront & the rails:** the Shopify economy (arm the rebels) · payments & checkout (the toll on every sale) · the app-and-marketing stack (the tax on running a store)
- **B — The two things that got scarce:** the CAC treadmill (ad costs are the real cost of DTC; iOS 14 broke targeting) · fulfillment & logistics (3PL, last-mile, the Prime bar) · the trust layer (reviews and social proof as a service)
- **C — The models on top:** the DTC boom & bust · the brand aggregators (Thrasio and the roll-up that imploded) · marketplaces vs owned stores (Amazon's gravity)
- **D — The mechanism & tensions:** why selling online got commoditized · who profits (the arms dealers) · the limits (CAC > LTV; the duopoly and Amazon own the chokepoints)
- **E — The big picture:** the-commerce-stack (capstone) + patterns
