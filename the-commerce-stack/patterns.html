The patterns

Line up every corner of the commerce stack — the storefront, the payments, the ads, the shipping, the brands, the roll-ups — and the same handful of mechanics repeat, all flowing from one fact: starting a store became free.

Each page asks: what did the commerce stack make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

Starting an online store became nearly free, so the store stopped being the scarce thing. The money moved to the picks-and-shovels everyone still needs — the software (Shopify), the payments (Stripe, the card networks), the ads (Meta and Google), and the shipping (3PLs and Amazon). The scarce things became getting seen, getting shipped, and getting paid — and someone else owns all three. The arms dealers of e-commerce win; most of the brands get squeezed between rising ad costs and Amazon's gravity.

Pattern 05

The owned channel beats the rented one

Email and social proof you own out-earn paid ads, because they aren't taxed by the ad duopoly and don't vanish when you stop paying.

Pattern 07

Traffic vs ownership is a trap either way

Marketplaces take half but hand you demand; owned stores keep the margin but make you buy every visitor — both feed a toll-collector.
