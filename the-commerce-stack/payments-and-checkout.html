Group A · The storefront & the rails

Payments & checkout

A 2–3% toll on every online sale — tiny per order, enormous across all commerce.

Every online sale runs through a payment stack that takes ~2–3%: Stripe, Adyen, PayPal, with the card networks (Visa, Mastercard) underneath, plus checkout optimizers and buy-now-pay-later on top. These are the invisible tollbooths of e-commerce — you can't opt out, it happens in the background, and the fee scales automatically with every dollar of commerce.

The core bet

Take a small, unavoidable cut of every transaction — a tiny percentage, but on trillions of dollars it's enormous, and it grows automatically with commerce.

What the numbers say

~$1.4T
Stripe's total payment volume in 2024 — one of the largest money conduits on earth.
2.9% + 30¢
Standard card-processing fee, repeated millions of times a day.
~2%+
Interchange Visa/Mastercard take on nearly every card sale, underneath the processor.
~70%
Cart-abandonment rate — bad checkout costs merchants a fortune, so one-click flows lift conversion 10–25%.
~$25B
Annual buy-now-pay-later originations (Affirm, Klarna), adding another fee-and-credit layer.

The processor often earns more per sale than the merchant

On a $100 sale, the payment stack takes ~$3.20 — and many small merchants run on 10–15% net margins, so the processor can pocket more per order than the store does. A 2–3% toll on all of human commerce, with no inventory, no shipping, and no customer service, is one of the great businesses in the economy.

The single biggest worry

Regulators are capping interchange fees in some regions (the EU capped debit at 0.3%), which could erase billions if it spreads — and platforms like Shopify, Apple, and Amazon are building their own payment rails to keep the fee in-house.

The surprising part

The quiet math is what makes it remarkable: the payment layer adds no product and takes no risk, yet skims a fixed percentage of everything sold. As commerce grows, the toll grows with it automatically — which is why the payment companies are worth more than most of the merchants paying them.

In one sentence

Every online purchase quietly pays 2–3% to payment networks, making the tollbooth on commerce one of the most valuable and unavoidable businesses on earth.
