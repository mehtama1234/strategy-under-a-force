The synthesis · what repeats across all 13

The patterns

Line up every corner of subsidy harvesting — EVs, solar, carbon, factory deals, student aid, credit mills, brokers, lobbyists — and the same handful of mechanics repeat, all flowing from one fact: the government check is the business.

Each page asks: what did subsidy-harvesting make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

For a whole class of businesses, the product is the front and the subsidy is the real business. They're engineered to capture government checks, and a parallel economy of brokers, tax-equity banks, consultants, and lobbyists skims a cut at every step. The government becomes the real customer and the buyer is incidental — so the winner isn't whoever makes the best product, it's whoever is best at navigating the maze and closest to the pen when the rule is written. The catch: a business whose margin is the subsidy is one election from collapse.
