Tax-credit brokers

A $30-billion marketplace appeared in two years — purely because Congress made a subsidy sellable.

The 2022 Inflation Reduction Act made most clean-energy tax credits 'transferable' — a project developer can now sell its credits directly to any company for cash, instead of the complex tax-equity structure. That turned a deduction into a tradeable product, and a wave of brokers and platforms (Crux, Basis, Evergrow) appeared to match credit sellers with corporate buyers who want to cut their tax bill.

The core bet

The government made tax credits sellable like a commodity, so a marketplace and brokers appeared to match sellers (clean-energy projects) with buyers (companies wanting lower taxes) — taking a fee on the trade.

Companies now shop for tax credits like a commodity

Before transferability, a project had to pair with a tax-equity bank through complex partnership structures. After one line of legislation, C-corporations could buy tax credits like any product — creating an instant industry of brokers, platforms, and pricing. Most financial markets take years to build infrastructure; this one appeared almost overnight.

The single biggest worry

The whole market exists only because the IRA created the credits and made them sellable — a repeal or rollback (2025–26) threatens it, and buyers still bear recapture risk if a project is later disqualified.

The surprising part

The astonishing part is the speed: a $30B annual marketplace formed in roughly 24 months entirely because Congress made a government subsidy tradeable. It's a vivid demonstration that when you turn an incentive into a commodity, the harvesting industry assembles itself around it in months.

In one sentence

Congress turned clean-energy tax credits into a tradeable commodity in 2023, and within two years a $30B marketplace of brokers appeared to sell corporate buyers a discount on their tax bill.
