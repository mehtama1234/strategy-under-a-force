Group B · The subsidy-capture playbooks

The education subsidy

A for-profit college's real customer was the federal loan program — not the student.

For-profit colleges built businesses on federal student aid: they recruited students whose tuition was paid by government-backed loans, harvested the subsidy, and profited whether or not the student succeeded — often leaving graduates with debt and poor outcomes. The '90/10 rule,' gainful-employment fights, and big collapses (Corinthian, ITT Tech) define the story.

The core bet

The government guarantees student loans, so a school can enroll students, collect the federally-backed tuition, and profit whether or not the student succeeds — the subsidy is the customer.

Engineered to arbitrage federal lending, not to educate

Many of these schools were built from day one to convert government-backed loans into revenue: recruit students eligible for federal money, collect the check, deliver a diploma, and exit. Some had default rates above 40% yet kept growing, because the incentive to care about student outcomes simply wasn't there — the school got paid upfront regardless.

The single biggest worry

The model can profit from enrolling students who won't benefit, sticking taxpayers and borrowers with the loss — which is exactly what gainful-employment regulation tries to stop.

The surprising part

The uncomfortable clarity is who the customer really was: not the student, but the federal loan program. A for-profit college could be perfectly rational to enroll people who would fail and default, because the money arrived from the government the moment they signed — the student and the taxpayer ate the loss later.

In one sentence

For-profit colleges built a $30B+ business enrolling students whose tuition was paid by federal loans, keeping the cash whether or not they succeeded, and leaving taxpayers and borrowers the debt.
