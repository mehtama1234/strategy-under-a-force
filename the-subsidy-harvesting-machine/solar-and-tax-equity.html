Group A · Where the subsidy IS the business

Solar & tax equity

A solar farm's real customer is often a bank buying a tax credit — not anyone buying electricity.

Solar and wind projects run largely on the federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC) and Production Tax Credit (PTC) — but the developer who builds the project usually can't use the credit, so a bank invests in it mainly to claim the credit on its own taxes. That's 'tax equity,' and it means the subsidy is the financing. The 2022 IRA supercharged it.

The core bet

A solar project's economics run on the tax credit, but the developer can't use it, so banks invest to 'buy' the credit via tax equity — the subsidy is the funding source.

Wall Street 'clean-energy investors' barely care about electricity

A bank invests $100M in a solar farm for the ~$30M tax credit it gets to claim, then often exits. It's among America's largest 'clean-energy investors' — but the real return is the tax reduction, not the power sold. The farm's true customer was never the homeowner or factory; it was the bank's tax bill.

The single biggest worry

The projects depend on both the credits surviving and a small pool of tax-equity banks showing up — a policy change or credit repeal could strand developers who already borrowed against future credits.

The surprising part

The inversion is complete: a power plant became a tax-avoidance vehicle. The thing being financed isn't the electricity — it's the deduction, and the whole structure exists to move a government credit from a developer who can't use it to a bank that can.

In one sentence

The US funds solar and wind through tax credits that big banks buy to offset their taxes — making the subsidy the financing and turning power plants into tax-avoidance vehicles.
