Group A · Where the subsidy IS the business

Carbon credits & offsets

A hundred-billion-dollar market built on a commodity you can't see — and often can't verify.

Carbon markets turn policy into a product: compliance markets (the EU's ETS, California's cap-and-trade) where regulation forces buying allowances, and voluntary offset markets where companies buy credits to claim carbon-neutrality. A whole industry — project developers, registries, brokers — monetizes it. But the voluntary market has been rocked by evidence that many offsets are worthless.

The core bet

Regulation and corporate climate pledges created demand for a made-up commodity — a ton of avoided carbon — so whoever can generate and sell credits harvests a policy-created market.

The product is a promise about a counterfactual

A rainforest offset is a claim that a tree that might have been cut down was saved — but no satellite can prove what would have happened, so the commodity is essentially unverifiable. Companies paid billions for 'carbon neutrality' that often amounted to buying permission to keep polluting, backed by a scheme thousands of miles away that no one audits.

The single biggest worry

Many voluntary offsets don't represent real reductions — greenwashing that hollows out the market — while compliance markets depend on political will to keep the cap tight.

The surprising part

The uncanny part is that the market works exactly as designed — it monetizes climate pledges — while the product itself is often phantom. A $100B+ industry runs on a commodity nobody can touch and frequently can't verify, sold by a broker to a company that wanted the claim more than the reduction.

In one sentence

Carbon credits are a multi-billion-dollar bet that you can manufacture emissions reductions on paper and sell them to companies that don't want to cut their own pollution.
