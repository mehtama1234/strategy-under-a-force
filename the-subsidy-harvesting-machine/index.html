The Subsidy-Harvesting Machine

The visible product is the front — the real margin is the government check.

The 49th force. A whole class of businesses is designed to capture public incentives rather than to serve customers: EV and clean-energy credits, solar tax equity, carbon offsets, place-based deals (opportunity zones, film and factory tax breaks), for-profit-college federal aid, and R&D and pandemic-era credit "mills." And a supporting industry — tax-credit brokers, tax-equity banks, consultants, and lobbyists — exists just to harvest it. The one idea: for these businesses the product isn't the business, the subsidy is — so they design the product to capture the check, and a whole ecosystem skims a cut at every step between the taxpayer and the goal.

A fresh mechanism (Tier-7) Not from the Inc. 5000 taxonomy — surfaced by a Haiku discovery-scout pass for genuinely new hidden-mechanism business models. The lens: the government incentive, not the customer, as the real profit engine.
The one question, asked of everyone

What did subsidy-harvesting make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? The ability to navigate and monetize an incentive became the scarce, valuable skill — the broker, the tax-equity bank, the consultant, the lobbyist; the government quietly became the real customer; and a product that loses money on its own became profitable once the credit was added. The danger: subsidies are political, so a business whose margin is the subsidy is one election from collapse — as the 2025–26 IRA and EV-credit rollback showed.
