Group B · The subsidy-capture playbooks

Place-based incentives

States bid against each other to 'win' jobs that would mostly have been created anyway.

State and local governments hand out enormous incentives to attract factories, film productions, data centers, and headquarters — tax abatements, cash grants, subsidized land. Companies harvest these by playing places against each other: Amazon's HQ2 bidding war, film tax credits, chip and EV factory packages. Studies repeatedly find the subsidy per job exceeds the benefit.

The core bet

Governments compete to attract investment by handing out incentives, so a company can play states and cities against each other and collect subsidies that can rival or exceed the project's own economics.

The company extracts the deal by threatening to leave

Economic studies consistently find these deals are bad bets for taxpayers: the subsidy cost per job created often exceeds the job's wages or tax benefit by multiples. Companies extract them by threatening to go elsewhere — and much of the 'won' investment would have happened somewhere in the country regardless.

The single biggest worry

The deals repeatedly lose money for taxpayers — subsidy-per-job far above the benefit — and companies extract them by pitting jurisdictions against each other in a race nobody can win.

The surprising part

The hidden trick is that the competition is mostly zero-sum: states bid against each other to attract jobs that are only leaving one place because another place is paying for them. Film and data-center incentives especially trigger copycat credits with little evidence they create new activity — just redistribute where it lands.

In one sentence

States and cities spend tens of billions a year to bribe companies to build things that would mostly happen somewhere in the country anyway — and lose money on almost every deal.
