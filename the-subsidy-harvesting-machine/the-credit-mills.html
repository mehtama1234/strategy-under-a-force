The credit mills

The government had to freeze its own program because harvesters gamed it so aggressively.

A cottage industry of consultants exists to help companies claim tax credits — the R&D tax credit, and especially the pandemic-era Employee Retention Credit (ERC). Aggressive 'ERC mills' pushed businesses to claim credits they didn't qualify for, taking a cut; the IRS paused ERC processing in 2023 amid massive fraud. These firms harvest a fee from a government program.

The core bet

Whenever the government offers a credit, an industry appears to help businesses claim it — for a percentage cut — and some push claims well past what's legitimate.

What the numbers say

$230B+
ERC claims filed — far above the original ~$50B estimate as the program was gamed.
15–25%
Contingency fee 'ERC mills' charged on the credit — a $100k credit meant $15–25k to the advisor.
Sept 2023
When the IRS paused new ERC claims, citing fraud; 10–15% of claims were estimated improper.
20–30%
Fees the R&D tax-credit consulting industry charges on claimed credits (a model since 1981).
2024
When the IRS launched clawbacks and criminal referrals against ERC mills and promoters.

The mill keeps its fee even when you have to repay

The consultant profits whether or not the credit holds up: once the IRS audits and demands repayment, the business carries the tax bill, penalties, and interest — while the advisor walks away with the fee already collected. The harvester's incentive is to maximize claims, not to be right.

The single biggest worry

The mills profit from getting businesses to claim credits they may have to repay, leaving the business — and taxpayers — exposed to clawback, penalties, and years of IRS disputes.

The surprising part

The vivid case study is the ERC: an entire industry materialized overnight — law firms, payroll shops, and CPA firms rebranding as 'ERC specialists' — specifically to harvest one pandemic credit. It got gamed so hard that the government had to freeze its own relief program to stop the harvesters.

In one sentence

Tax-credit consulting is legitimate, but when the government offers big money fast, an industry springs up to push claims past the line — leaving businesses and taxpayers to clean it up.
