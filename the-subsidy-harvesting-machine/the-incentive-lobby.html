Group C · The plumbing

The incentive lobby

The company that collects the credit often helped write the rule that created it.

The deepest version of subsidy harvesting is helping design the subsidy itself: industries lobby to create and shape tax credits and incentive programs, then collect them. The IRA and CHIPS Act were shaped by intense industry lobbying; solar, EV, biofuel, and housing lobbies all fight to create and protect their credits. The returns on that lobbying are enormous.

The core bet

The highest-return move is to help write the incentive you'll collect — spend on lobbying to create or protect a subsidy worth far more than the lobbying cost.

What the numbers say

$400M+
Clean-energy, EV, battery, and chip lobbying spend around the IRA and CHIPS Act (2021–23).
$4–20
Return in policy benefits per $1 of lobbying, in various studies — among the highest-return corporate spend.
18 years
How long the ethanol/Renewable Fuel Standard lobby has protected biofuel mandates (~$5B/yr in support).
revolving door
Energy executives who draft the bill, then return to industry to claim the credits.
stock moves
How the EV-credit rules (which models qualify) moved carmakers' valuations by millions per tweak.

Capture makes a subsidy nearly impossible to remove

Once a subsidy is created by the industry that benefits from it, the same industry defends it as 'jobs' or 'clean energy' or 'national security,' even when the public cost far exceeds the public benefit. The credit gets captured — hard to shrink and harder to kill, because a well-funded constituency exists to protect it.

The single biggest worry

Subsidies get captured by the industries they benefit rather than serving the public interest, and become entrenched — defended by the very companies that harvest them.

The surprising part

The point isn't a side effect — it's the strategy. The IRA and CHIPS Act were drafted with direct input from executives whose companies then won billions in credits. Lobbying to design a subsidy is one of the highest-return investments a large company can make: you help write the rule, then you collect the check it prints.

In one sentence

Industries lobby to create the subsidies they then harvest, and because they wrote the rules, the subsidies are hard to remove even when they cost more than they deliver.
