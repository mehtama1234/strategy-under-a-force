Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

The limits

A business whose profit is a government check lives at the mercy of the next election and the next audit.

This is the skeptical piece. A business whose margin is the subsidy faces three big risks: political reversal (subsidies repealed with an election — the 2025–26 rollback of IRA clean-energy credits and the EV credit), malinvestment (capital chasing the subsidy not real demand, building things nobody needs), and fraud and clawback (aggressive harvesting invites freezes and repayment — the ERC moratorium). It's a business one signature from collapse.

The core bet

The subsidy that makes the business also makes it fragile — it can be repealed, it distorts investment, and it invites fraud and clawback.

The same program that built an industry can unmake it with one vote

A solar installer with 500 employees and three-year contracts can be insolvent in weeks if the credit is repealed. A business that spent its ERC cash — believing it legitimate — now faces a clawback letter for hundreds of thousands. The subsidy created the industry; politics can unmake it, because the real margin was never the product.

The single biggest worry

A business built on subsidies can collapse overnight when politics shifts, stranding investment and workers — and aggressive claims invite the clawbacks and audits that finish the job.

The surprising part

The stark truth is that a company can look healthy on paper because its revenue includes a government check — but that check is written by politicians, not by market demand. When the subsidy is repealed or the audit lands (both often after an election), the model vanishes, and the workers, equipment, and half-built projects are left stranded.

In one sentence

A business whose profit comes from a government check lives at the mercy of the next election and the next audit.
