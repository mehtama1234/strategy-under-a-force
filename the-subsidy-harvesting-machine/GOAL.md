# The Subsidy-Harvesting Machine — strategy under a force

The 49th collection (Tier-7, from the Haiku discovery-scout pass) — Family II (hidden-mechanism business models).

**The force / mechanism:** the visible product is the front; the real margin is the government check. A whole class of businesses is designed to capture public incentives — EV credits, clean-energy tax credits, carbon credits, place-based deals (opportunity zones, film and factory tax breaks), student-loan / Title IV money, R&D and ERC credits — and a supporting industry of brokers, tax-equity financiers, consultants, and lobbyists exists just to harvest them. We ask: *what did the subsidy-harvesting model make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** for a lot of companies, the product isn't the business — the subsidy is. When the government pays you to build EVs, install solar, hire in a "zone," shoot a film in a state, or enroll a student, the incentive can be worth more than the customer — so businesses design the product to capture the check, and a whole industry grows up to help. The scarce, valuable thing became the ability to navigate and monetize the incentive: the tax-credit broker, the tax-equity financier, the consultant who manufactures a claim, and the lobbyist who writes the rule. The catch is that subsidies are political — they can be switched off with an election (the 2025–26 IRA and EV rollback) — so a business whose margin is the subsidy is one signature from collapse.
