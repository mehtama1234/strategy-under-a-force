Group E · The big picture

The subsidy-harvesting machine

A parallel economy where government checks are the product — and it can collapse overnight.

Pull the whole collection together and one line runs through it. For a whole class of businesses the product is the front and the subsidy is the real business — engineered to capture government checks (EV and clean-energy credits, solar tax equity, carbon offsets, place-based deals, federal student aid, R&D and ERC credits), while intermediaries — brokers, banks, consultants, lobbyists — skim a cut at every step. The customer is the government check; the buyer is incidental.

The core bet

Winners and losers

The money sorts cleanly into who's positioned to capture the subsidy and who ultimately pays for it.

Who ends up ahead

Companies designed to harvest credits, the intermediaries (tax-equity banks, brokers, ERC and R&D mills), the lobbyists who draft the rules, and corporations buying credits to cut their taxes.

Who's exposed

Taxpayers funding the $1.8T pool, the intended beneficiaries (renters, EV buyers, students) who get a diluted share, and businesses left with no margin when a subsidy is switched off.

The single biggest worry

A business whose profit is the subsidy is one election from collapse — the 2025–26 rollbacks proved it instantly. Subsidies also distort investment (capital chases the credit, not demand) and invite fraud, and the company that collects the credit often helped write the rule.

The surprising part

The note to end on is the inversion at the core. The real customer is the government, not the person buying the product — a solar developer cares less whether the panels serve a home than whether a tax-equity bank will pay cash for the deduction; an ERC consultant doesn't audit your payroll, they manufacture a claim and sell it. The company that wins isn't the one with the best product; it's the one best at navigating the subsidy maze and closest to the pen when the rule is written. It's a parallel economy where the check is the product — and the whole machine can vanish the moment the politics change.

In one sentence

The US built a parallel economy where government checks are the product, intermediaries harvest a cut at every step, and the whole machine can collapse overnight when the politics change.
