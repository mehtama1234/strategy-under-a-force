Group E · The big picture

The recommerce spread

The object outlives the sale — so a middleman can skim a fee each time it changes hands.

Pull the whole collection together and one line runs through it. When a product is bought new, the maker gets paid once — but the object keeps living, getting resold, traded in, refurbished, and liquidated. A platform can skim a fee on every one of those later transactions without ever making anything. The supply is free (other people's stuff), so the business is the take-rate, and the scarce, valuable thing is trust — proving the used item is real and works.

The core bet

The same object gets resold repeatedly, and a platform can skim a fee each time it changes hands without ever making the thing — the supply is free and the moat is authentication.

Winners and losers

The engine sorts cleanly by how much the player touches the goods.

Who ends up ahead

Asset-light peer-to-peer platforms (Vinted, Poshmark, eBay), spread-plus-financing players (CarMax), off-price retailers (TJX), and brands that recapture their own resale — everyone who takes a cut without carrying the weight.

Who's exposed

Asset-heavy managed resellers (ThredUp, The RealReal) that lose money handling cheap goods, anyone hit by fakes, and the whole 'used is cheaper/greener' pitch when a new Shein shirt costs $5.

The single biggest worry

Handling used goods often loses money; the model rests on trust that fraud can break; and ultra-cheap new goods undercut the core premise. The economics only work when used is meaningfully cheaper, the item is high-ticket, or the trust is worth paying for.

The surprising part

The note to end on is that in recommerce, the profit runs away from the product. The most profitable firms do the least — Vinted warehouses nothing, eBay just takes a cut, CarMax makes more on the loan than the car. The real product isn't the sneaker, the jacket, or the phone; it's trust — the certified-real stamp, the grading standard, the warranty — and whoever can supply that cheaply, without touching the goods, collects a toll every time the same object is sold again. The maker got paid once; the middleman gets paid forever.

In one sentence

The same object can be sold again and again, and a middleman skims a fee each time — as long as it owns the trust, and the used goods stay cheaper or scarcer than ultra-cheap new ones.
