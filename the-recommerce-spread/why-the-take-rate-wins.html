Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

Why the take-rate wins

The supply is free, the fee repeats — and the product isn't the goods, it's trust.

This is the mechanism. Recommerce works because the supply is essentially free — it's other people's used stuff, so the platform has no manufacturing cost; the same object can be sold many times, and a middleman skims a fee on each; and the scarce, valuable thing is trust — proving a used item isn't fake or broken. That makes authentication the moat, and it makes the lightest models (never touching the item) the most profitable.

The core bet

In resale the inventory is free (other people's stuff), so the business is the fee on selling it again — and whoever can make strangers trust used goods owns the market.

What the numbers say

zero COGS
The supply costs nothing to make — the platform moves items that already exist.
0–50%
Take-rate range — Vinted near zero (buyer fees), GOAT/StockX ~9–15%, RealReal 20–50%.
many times
The same authenticated item can be resold repeatedly, each hop triggering a fee with no new cost.
hundreds of experts
The RealReal's authentication staff — the expensive moat that also drags its margins down.
profit vs loss
Vinted (never touches items) turned a profit; ThredUp and RealReal (handle everything) lose money.

The most profitable model does the least

Vinted reached profitability by letting sellers do all the work — photographing, shipping, handling disputes — and just taking a small fee. ThredUp and The RealReal, despite far higher take-rates, stay unprofitable because they touch the goods: grading, storing, and shipping cheap items eats the fee. The product was never the clothes; it's trust, and trust is cheap to provide when you don't have to handle anything.

The single biggest worry

If fakes slip through, the entire trust moat collapses and nobody pays a premium for 'used' — and when items are cheap, the cost of grading and handling exceeds the take-rate, so the platform loses on every transaction.

The surprising part

The paradox at the core is that adding value destroys the margin. The firm that guarantees authenticity and handles the logistics is doing the honest, hard work — and bleeding cash — while the firm that merely takes a cut and lets strangers transact directly is the one that gets rich. In resale, the moat is trust, but the money is in never touching the goods.

In one sentence

Recommerce profits by charging a fee on items that cost nothing to make — but only if it can prove they're real, which is cheap for peer-to-peer and expensive for the ones that handle everything.
