# The Recommerce Spread — strategy under a force

The 47th collection (Tier-6, from the Haiku discovery-scout pass) — Family II (hidden-mechanism business models).

**The force / mechanism:** the same object gets sold again and again, and a middleman takes a cut every time it changes hands. Resale and secondhand — sneakers (StockX, GOAT), fashion (ThredUp, Poshmark, Vinted, The RealReal), refurbished electronics (Back Market), used cars (Carvana, CarMax), trade-ins (Apple, Best Buy), and off-price/liquidation (TJX, Ross) — turned the used-goods take-rate into a durable business, riding sustainability and affordability tailwinds. We ask: *what did recommerce make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** in resale the supply is free — it's other people's stuff — so the business is the take-rate on selling it again. When a product is bought new, the maker gets paid once. But the object keeps living: it gets resold, traded in, refurbished, and liquidated, and a platform can skim a fee on every one of those later transactions without ever making anything. The scarce, valuable thing became trust — proving a $1,000 sneaker or a used car isn't fake or a lemon — so authentication and inspection became the moat. The catch: margins are thin, fraud is constant, and a flood of ultra-cheap new goods (Shein, Temu) can undercut the used market from below.
