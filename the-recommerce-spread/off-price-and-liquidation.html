Group B · Big-ticket recommerce

Off-price & liquidation

TJ Maxx became one of retail's best performers by selling other companies' mistakes.

A close cousin of resale is the off-price and liquidation business: TJX (TJ Maxx, Marshalls), Ross, Burlington, and liquidation marketplaces (B-Stock) that buy brands' overstock, canceled orders, and returned goods cheaply and resell them at a discount. The explosion of e-commerce returns created a giant secondary stream of usable goods that never appears on the original shelf.

The core bet

Brands' mistakes — overstock, cancellations, and returns — are cheap inventory, so buying it in bulk and reselling at a discount is a durable, counter-cyclical business.

What the numbers say

~$50–54B
TJX's revenue — one of retail's steadiest performers, built entirely on others' excess.
7–8%/yr
Off-price growth through the 2010s–20s while department stores shrank.
$700–900B
Annual US merchandise returns; a large share can't be resold as new and gets liquidated.
20–40%
E-commerce return rates (vs ~8–10% in stores) — the 'returns crisis' that floods the secondary market.
25–35%
TJX's gross margins — held even in downturns because it buys so cheaply.

One of retail's best risk-management tools — for everyone but the brand

A brand can tank when it overshoots inventory; TJ Maxx profits from exactly that. Your returned $80 shirt disappears into a liquidator, then reappears on an off-price rack next to the brand's own overstock. The model is a shock absorber for the whole industry's mistakes — just not for the company that made them.

The single biggest worry

Brands are learning to shrink their mistakes — tighter forecasts, their own outlet channels, faster resale of returns — and if they keep more of their own excess, the cheap supply that feeds off-price dries up.

The surprising part

The counter-cyclical twist is the surprise: off-price gets stronger when times are hard, because tight consumers hunt for deals and struggling brands dump more inventory. It's one of the few retail models that feeds on the industry's failures and a downturn at the same time.

In one sentence

Off-price turns brands' failures — overstock, cancellations, returns — into a $50B+ counter-cyclical business, because there will always be mistakes and a hidden supply chain to absorb them.
