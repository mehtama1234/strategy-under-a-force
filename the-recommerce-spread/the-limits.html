Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

The limits

Ultra-cheap new fast fashion and rampant fakes are breaking both of recommerce's legs at once.

This is the skeptical piece. Recommerce faces hard limits: thin margins (many managed resellers have never truly profited); fraud and authentication (the whole model rests on trust, which fakes threaten); undercut from below (Shein and Temu sell new so cheaply that 'used' isn't obviously cheaper or greener); and supply control (brands recapturing their own resale shrinks third-party supply).

The core bet

Recommerce assumes used goods are cheaper, greener, and trustworthy — but thin margins, fraud, and ultra-cheap new goods each undercut that.

The value-add loses money; the cheap alternative wins

The contradiction is both financial and ideological. The pitch is 'buy used, save money and the planet' — but a $5 new Shein shirt beats a $12 used one on price and perceived value, even if it's worse for the environment. Recommerce assumed shoppers cared most about sustainability; the market keeps voting for cost and novelty instead.

The single biggest worry

A model where the actual work — handling, authenticating, shipping used goods — loses money, while competitors win by selling new for less and one counterfeit scandal can evaporate trust overnight, is not obviously built to last.

The surprising part

The fragile joint is trust. The entire premium on 'used' rests on the belief that the platform's authentication is unshakeable — and it isn't. One wave of fake Hermès or Prada slipping through a resale site could vaporize customer confidence, taking the whole margin with it, because the product was trust all along.

In one sentence

Recommerce works only if used goods are both cheaper and trustworthy — but ultra-cheap fast fashion and rampant fakes are breaking both legs at once.
