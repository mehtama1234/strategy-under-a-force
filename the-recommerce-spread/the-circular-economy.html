Group C · Why it works now

The circular economy

Sustainability turned buying used from a poverty story into a virtue — a free marketing engine.

A cultural and regulatory shift toward sustainability — reduce, reuse, the 'circular economy' — removed the stigma from buying used and handed recommerce a values-based tailwind. Buying secondhand became a climate choice, even a status move, especially for Gen Z, and some regulation (EU right-to-repair, extended producer responsibility) pushes reuse.

The core bet

Sustainability made buying used aspirational instead of shameful, so recommerce gets a free marketing tailwind — the 'green' story sells the secondhand purchase.

Virtue signaling became a growth engine

When buying used stopped being embarrassing and started being admirable, resale platforms got the moral permission to go mass-market. The green story did the marketing for free — you feel good about the purchase, and the platform captures the fee. Sustainability didn't just help recommerce; it destigmatized it.

The single biggest worry

The sustainability story can be a facade — people buy one thrifted item while fast fashion booms, feeling virtuous about a single purchase while total consumption keeps rising — so the 'green' claim is often thin.

The surprising part

The revealing contradiction is that the same shopper thrifts and hauls $5 Shein in the same TikTok. Recommerce grew not because people bought less but because sustainability gave them a reason to buy differently — a virtue layer on top of consumption, not a brake on it.

In one sentence

A belief that buying used helps the planet turned recommerce from a poverty story into a virtue signal — and a growth lever for anyone who can claim the sustainability halo.
