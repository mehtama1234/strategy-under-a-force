Group B · Big-ticket recommerce

Used cars

The biggest recommerce category of all — and the real profit is the loan, not the car.

The used-car market is by far the largest recommerce category, worth close to $1 trillion in the US. CarMax pioneered no-haggle used-car retail; Carvana built an online version with a famous boom, near-collapse in 2022, and recovery. They buy, recondition, certify, and resell used cars — and finance them. It's recommerce at massive scale, where inspection and financing are the moat.

The core bet

Cars are resold many times over their life, so buying, certifying, and reselling used cars — plus financing them — is a huge recommerce business built on trust and the spread.

It's a spread-and-lending machine wearing a retail costume

Most people assume the profit is the ~$3,000 markup on the car, but financing is often the bigger prize: a $20,000 used car at 8–12% over six years throws off $3,000–4,000 in interest. A dealer who finances the sale can make as much from the loan as from the car — which is why CarMax and Carvana push their own financing so hard. The car is the hook; the loan is the money.

The single biggest worry

The business is capital-heavy (you buy thousands of cars upfront) and deeply cyclical — Carvana's stock fell ~99% in 2022 when it couldn't sell fast enough to cover its debt — and rate spikes crush both demand and inventory value at once.

The surprising part

The surprise is how much this looks like a bank. Behind the vending machines and no-haggle showrooms, the durable profit is a financial product — packaging auto loans, often to buyers who couldn't easily get credit elsewhere — with the used car as the collateral and the excuse.

In one sentence

The used-car business is really a spread-and-lending machine disguised as a store, where the profit is the financing at least as much as the car.
