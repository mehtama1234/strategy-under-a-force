The Recommerce Spread

The same thing gets sold again and again — and the middleman takes a cut each time.

The 47th force. When a product is bought new, the maker gets paid once — but the object keeps living. It gets resold, traded in, refurbished, and liquidated, and a platform can skim a fee on every one of those later transactions without ever making anything. Sneakers (StockX, GOAT), fashion (ThredUp, Poshmark, Vinted), refurbished phones (Back Market), used cars (CarMax, Carvana), trade-ins (Apple, GameStop), and off-price (TJX) all turned the used-goods take-rate into a business — riding sustainability and affordability. The one idea: in resale the supply is free — it's other people's stuff — so the business is the take-rate, and the scarce thing is trust.

A fresh mechanism (Tier-6) Not from the Inc. 5000 taxonomy — surfaced by a Haiku discovery-scout pass for genuinely new hidden-mechanism business models. The lens: the same object resold repeatedly, with a fee skimmed each time and authentication as the moat.
The one question, asked of everyone

What did recommerce make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? The supply became free (your closet, your drawer, your trade-in); the fee on reselling became the business; and trust — proving a $1,000 sneaker or a used car isn't fake or a lemon — became the scarce, valuable thing worth paying for. The danger: margins are thin, the whole model rests on trust that fraud can break, and a flood of ultra-cheap new goods (Shein, Temu) can undercut "used" from below.
