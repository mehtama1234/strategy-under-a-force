Group B · The ones AI is eating

Chegg — the clearest case of AI eating a business

A whole company built on selling homework answers, gutted by a free tool in under a year.

If you want to see, in one company, exactly what it looks like when AI destroys a business, look at Chegg. It sold students a cheap monthly subscription (~$15–20) for quick homework answers and step-by-step solutions. Then ChatGPT arrived and did the same thing — free, instant, unlimited.

The day it admitted the problem

May 2, 2023 — the confession

On an earnings call, Chegg's CEO said students had started using ChatGPT instead of paying, and it was hurting new signups. The stock fell about 48% in a single day. Chegg became the first public company to openly tell investors that an AI tool was wrecking its business — the textbook example of "AI is eating this."

Why it was so exposed

The one thing Chegg sold was access to answers. That was valuable only as long as answers were hard to get. ChatGPT made answers free and everywhere overnight — so the reason to pay simply vanished. A second, quieter blow came from Google's AI Overviews (answers shown right in search results), which cut off the free web traffic Chegg relied on to find new students in the first place.

The collapse, in numbers

~99%

5.1M → 3.2M

~45%

Everything it tried — and none of it worked

  • Built its own AI tutor ("CheggMate," using OpenAI's GPT-4). Students just used ChatGPT directly for free. It failed to hold subscribers.
  • Looked for a buyer or a way to go private. No deal — it stayed independent, then cut deeper.
  • Sued Google over AI Overviews stealing its traffic. A lawsuit, not a growth plan.
  • Pivoted to workplace-skills courses. Real, but tiny (~$70M) next to what it lost.

The lesson

The takeaway

A business can be erased almost overnight when a free, general tool does its core job better. Chegg saw AI coming and shipped an AI product within months — and it made no difference, because the problem wasn't its technology. AI destroyed the demand for the thing it sold. Being fast doesn't help when the very thing you charge for stops being scarce.

The bitter twist

Chegg partnered with OpenAI — the very company drowning it — to build its AI tutor, hoping to ride the wave. It couldn't, because its customers could just use ChatGPT for nothing. That's why Chegg ended up suing Google, not OpenAI: the deadlier wound was losing the free search traffic that fed it new customers.
