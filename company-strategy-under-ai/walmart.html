Walmart — the world's biggest store is now an ad-and-data company

It still sells groceries. But that's barely where the profit comes from anymore.

You picture Walmart as aisles and trucks. Increasingly, it's better understood as a technology and data company that happens to own a lot of stores — quietly copying the money machine Amazon built (ads, data, and AI-run logistics on top of retail).

The hidden profit engine

Selling groceries earns almost nothing per dollar. The real money is in what Walmart's shopper data unlocks — chiefly, selling ads to brands that want to reach its millions of customers.

Its advertising business in 2025 — up about 46% in a year. Add membership fees and marketplace charges, and…
The number that says it all

Walmart's finance chief said about one-third of its profit in a recent quarter came from advertising and membership fees — not from selling products. Those businesses have far better margins than groceries, so profit is now growing faster than sales.

The AI running underneath

  • "Sparky," an AI shopping assistant — tell it "I'm hosting a cookout" and it plans and fills the basket, instead of you searching.
  • It put Sparky inside ChatGPT and Google's assistant — but on Walmart's terms, so Walmart keeps the customer and the data (more on why that matters below).
  • AI supply chain — systems that predict weather and demand to move stock before shelves go empty; most stores now get freight through automated warehouses.
  • Even its software is AI-built — over 40% of new code is AI-generated or AI-assisted.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Walmart is challenging Amazon at Amazon's own game — and Amazon's ad business is still ten times bigger. Core retail margins stay thin, so the whole plan leans on ads and membership continuing to compound. The deeper danger: if shoppers start buying through AI assistants (ChatGPT, Gemini) instead of Walmart's own app, Walmart could lose the customer relationship and data that make its ad business valuable — which is exactly why it insisted on keeping checkout inside its own system.

The surprising part

The company most people picture as trucks and shelves increasingly earns its profit from advertising and data, and its ad business is growing roughly six times faster than its sales. The biggest store on Earth is turning into an ad-and-AI platform.

In one sentence

Walmart in a nutshell

It's using AI and shopper data to become a high-margin ad-and-tech company on top of its low-margin stores — chasing Amazon's playbook. The risk is doing it in Amazon's shadow, and losing the shopper to AI assistants.
