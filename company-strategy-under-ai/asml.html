Group A · The arms dealers

ASML — the quiet monopoly behind everything

Nvidia and TSMC get the headlines. Both of them depend on this one Dutch company.

To make an advanced chip, you have to "print" unimaginably tiny patterns onto silicon. The machine that does that printing at the cutting edge is called an EUV machine, and exactly one company on Earth makes it: ASML, in the Netherlands. No one else is close.

The core bet

ASML's bet is simple: every leap in AI needs smaller, denser transistors, and only its machines can print them. So as long as the world wants more powerful chips, the world has to come through ASML. It spent about 30 years and tens of billions perfecting a technology nobody else has managed to copy.

Why this is a real monopoly

The only shop in town

TSMC, Samsung, and Intel cannot buy this machine anywhere else. If you want to make the best chips, you buy from ASML or you don't compete. That's a rare kind of power — it can see demand years ahead and set its own prices. Its newest machines (called High-NA) cost roughly $350–400 million each, and it's the only maker of those too.

The main worry: governments decide who it can sell to

The single biggest risk

Because ASML's machines are the chokepoint for advanced chips, they became a weapon in the US–China tech fight. ASML is barred from selling its best machines to China — which was about a third of its sales and is being cut to roughly a fifth. That's a big hole in revenue created purely by politics, with no full replacement.

Its other weak spots come from being a monopoly with very few customers:

  • Almost all its money comes from just three customers: TSMC, Samsung, Intel. If any cuts back, ASML feels it hard.
  • Lumpy orders. Each machine costs hundreds of millions, so a few delayed orders can swing an entire quarter.
  • Customer troubles flow straight through — e.g. Intel's stumbles, or any AI-spending pullback.

The surprising part

The real king of the chain

Everyone crowns Nvidia and TSMC as the kings of AI hardware — but both sit downstream of ASML. TSMC can't make Nvidia's chips without ASML's machines, and there's nowhere else to get them. The quietest company in the whole chain is arguably the one with the most leverage: a single Dutch firm is the true chokepoint of the entire AI hardware economy. And its main threat isn't a competitor (it has none) — it's its customers' governments.

In one sentence

ASML in a nutshell

It's the only company that makes the machines that make the best chips, so the whole AI world runs through it — and its biggest danger is politics, not rivals.
