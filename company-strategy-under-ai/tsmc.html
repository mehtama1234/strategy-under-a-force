Group A · The arms dealers

TSMC — the one factory the world runs on

It doesn't design chips. It makes them — for almost everyone — and it's the only one who can make the best ones.

Most chip companies you've heard of — Nvidia, Apple, AMD — don't actually build their own chips. They design them and send the blueprints to TSMC in Taiwan, which does the fiendishly hard job of manufacturing them. For the most advanced chips, TSMC is essentially the only factory on Earth that can.

The core bet

TSMC bet that companies would rather buy chip-making than do it themselves. So it became the neutral factory that builds everyone's chips and competes with none of them — it sells no products of its own. A company can safely hand TSMC its most valuable design, because TSMC will never turn around and compete with it. The AI boom then made that factory the single most fought-over piece of hardware in the world.

How AI changed it

AI accelerators are huge, cutting-edge chips, and suddenly everyone wanted millions of them. That flipped TSMC's business:

~$122B
2025 sales, up about 36% in a year — driven by AI.
~58%
Share of its business that's now AI-type work — up from about 30% in 2020. Nvidia has passed Apple as its #1 customer.
90%+
Its share of making the most advanced AI chips. For the leading edge, there's basically no alternative at scale.

The twist: the bottleneck isn't the chip — it's the glue

The chokepoint inside the chokepoint

The hardest part right now isn't making a faster chip — it's packaging it: the step (called CoWoS) that stitches a chip to its stacks of memory. That packaging is sold out into 2026. TSMC is racing to multiply capacity and still can't keep up. An unglamorous gluing step became the thing holding back the world's AI.

The main worry: it's almost all on one island

The single biggest risk

Nearly all of TSMC's advanced production sits in Taiwan — one island that China claims. A blockade, a conflict, or even a big earthquake would choke off the world's AI and electronics supply overnight. There is no quick backup.

TSMC is spending to spread the risk — but it's slow and costly:

  • $165 billion to build factories in Arizona — billed as the largest foreign investment in US history.
  • Those overseas plants cost more to run and dent profits (a few percent) as they grow.
  • They won't help in a crisis for years — the cutting edge stays in Taiwan for a long time yet.

The surprising part

There's a theory that Taiwan is safe because the world needs its chips — a "silicon shield." But by building factories abroad to reduce its risk, TSMC is slowly weakening that very shield: the more the world can get chips elsewhere, the less it depends on Taiwan staying safe. And note the deeper layer — even TSMC depends on ASML for the machines that make these chips.

In one sentence

TSMC in a nutshell

It's the world's only factory for the best chips, so AI made it a gold mine that can't build fast enough. Its whole problem is that nearly all of it sits on one island next to China.
