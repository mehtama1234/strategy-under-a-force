# Microsoft AI-Strategy Research

Research folder analyzing Microsoft's strategy under AI: capex, the OpenAI relationship, custom silicon (Maia/Cobalt), Azure cloud economics, the Copilot/agent distribution engine, Microsoft's own models (MAI/Phi), and power.

Second instantiation of the reusable "company strategy under AI" template (see `GOAL.md` and the sibling `amazon-ai-strategy-research`). Microsoft is the **"own the demand" archetype** — the deliberate contrast to Amazon's "own the supply" — and a hybrid that exercises *both* the product-value-unit and the bottleneck-structure variants of the template.

## Core Question

Microsoft's edge is **demand**: frontier models via OpenAI plus the world's best enterprise distribution (Windows, M365, Teams, GitHub, Azure, Dynamics) to attach AI to hundreds of millions of paid seats. Its exposure is **supply and dependence**: it rents NVIDIA at scale (Maia silicon trails Trainium/TPU), and its frontier-model access runs through OpenAI — now a partner, investee, *and* competitor that is diversifying its compute away from Azure. The question is whether the demand advantage out-earns the supply-cost, dependence, and capex risks.

## Early Finding

Unlike Amazon (where AI changed the constraint structure but not the SKU), Microsoft's AI story touches the **product** directly: Copilot is a per-seat upsell on existing SaaS, and the emerging agent layer hints at consumption/outcome pricing. So Microsoft is a genuine hybrid — a product-transformation story riding on a bottleneck-structure story. The crux that has no analog in the Amazon case is the **OpenAI relationship**: simultaneously Microsoft's biggest AI asset, one of its biggest costs (equity-method loss share + compute funding), and a fast-rising competitor.

## Current Strategic Thesis

```text
Distribution-led monetization (Copilot/agents attached to a vast paid base + Azure AI)
  funded by heavy capex and NVIDIA rent,
  de-risked by building own models (MAI/Phi) and multi-sourcing (Anthropic, others),
  against a frontier-model dependence (OpenAI) that is asset, cost, and competitor at once.
```

The bet pays off if Copilot attach + Azure AI grow faster and at higher margin than the capex, OpenAI-loss share, and silicon cost required to serve them — and if Microsoft reduces single-partner model risk before that risk is tested.
