# Watchlist: What Proves the Thesis, What Breaks It, What To Track

Date baseline: 2026-08-05

The thesis (see `reports/strategy-thesis.md`): Microsoft monetizes AI through **distribution** — attaching Copilot/agents to a vast paid base and selling Azure AI — while de-risking its **OpenAI dependence** via own models (MAI/Phi) and multi-sourcing. It pays off if Copilot attach + Azure AI grow faster and at higher margin than the capex, NVIDIA rent, and OpenAI drag needed to serve them. This file is the falsification and tracking layer.

## 0. Scorecard as of Q4 FY26 (what has actually materialized)

```text
PROVE-signals that have fired (bull):
  [x] Copilot attach scaling         -> M365 Copilot 30M+ paid seats (fastest-ever add quarter), >90% Fortune 500
  [x] GitHub Copilot growing          -> 4.7M paid subs (+~75% YoY)
  [x] Azure demand booked ahead       -> Azure +40%, crossed $100B annual; commercial RPO $392B (+51%)
  [x] Capital self-funding            -> FCF still positive (~$67B FY26); depreciation tailwind (life 15->25yr)
  [x] Model de-risking real           -> OpenAI cliff removed (IP to 2032); MAI/Phi + Anthropic/Claude multi-source
  [~] Agent/consumption emerging       -> Copilot Credits, GitHub AI Credits, Agent 365 (~40M agents) - early $

BREAK-signals that have fired (bear):
  [x] AI margin pressure visible       -> Microsoft Cloud gross margin ~68%, DOWN YoY "on AI investment"
  [x] Heavy NVIDIA rent / weak silicon -> ~2/3 capex short-lived GPUs; Maia trails, inference-only, delayed
  [x] OpenAI drag on EPS               -> equity-method loss share (-$3.1B Q1 FY26); OpenAI now multi-clouds + competes
  [x] Power is the binding limit       -> Nadella "warm shells"; Hood "lost revenue" from capacity constraint
  [ ] Copilot attach stalls            -> NOT seen; attach accelerating
  [ ] Cloud share slips                -> mixed: Azure re-accelerating BUT Google Cloud growing faster

Net: demand PROVEN and balance sheet SOUND; the live risk is MARGIN quality (renting compute + OpenAI drag).
```

## 1. What Would PROVE the Thesis

1. **Copilot attach scales**: paid M365 Copilot seats becoming a large and rising share of the M365 base; GitHub Copilot subscriber growth; disclosed AI revenue run-rate growing fast.
2. **Azure AI margin holds**: Intelligent Cloud / Azure operating margin stable or up as the AI mix rises — evidence NVIDIA rent and the OpenAI burden are not eating the AI economics.
3. **Model dependence falls**: a rising share of Microsoft AI workloads served by MAI/Phi/Anthropic/open models rather than OpenAI — the dependence becoming a choice, not a necessity.
4. **Agent/consumption revenue emerges**: material revenue from agents priced by consumption/task, showing the value-unit shift is real and monetizable.
5. **Azure backlog/RPO keeps growing**: commercial bookings and "demand exceeds capacity" commentary confirming demand is booked ahead of capacity.
6. **OpenAI drag becomes tolerable/positive**: the equity-method loss share stabilizes or the stake value/commercial benefit clearly exceeds the cost.

## 2. What Would BREAK the Thesis

- **Copilot attach stalls**: enterprises pilot but don't broadly deploy $30/seat Copilot because ROI is unclear; attach plateaus at a small fraction of seats.
- **Azure AI margin compresses**: NVIDIA rent + OpenAI compute burden drag Intelligent Cloud margin down as AI scales — Microsoft paying full price for compute without a silicon offset.
- **OpenAI dependence bites**: OpenAI raises prices, diversifies compute away from Azure (Stargate/other clouds), competes directly (enterprise, agents, its own apps), or the relationship sours — and Microsoft hasn't de-risked in time.
- **OpenAI loss share balloons**: Microsoft's equity-method share of OpenAI's mounting losses becomes a large, growing drag on reported income.
- **Own models disappoint**: MAI/Phi fail to reach a quality bar that lets Microsoft actually reduce OpenAI reliance; multi-sourcing stays cosmetic.
- **Capex/FCF strain**: capex escalates (partly to serve OpenAI) while AI revenue lags, pressuring free cash flow and returns.
- **Cloud share slips**: Azure grows slower than Google Cloud/AWS AI, suggesting demand capture is weaker than the distribution thesis assumes.

## 3. Key Ratios To Track (from the primer scorecard)

```text
1. Copilot attach:      paid Copilot seats / M365 (and GitHub) installed base
2. AI attach economics: Copilot/agent revenue per seat vs cost to serve (compute + model)
3. Model de-risking:    share of AI workloads NOT dependent on OpenAI
4. Azure AI margin:     Intelligent Cloud / Azure margin as AI mix rises
5. OpenAI drag:         equity-method loss share + compute commitment vs value received
6. Capacity conversion: Azure RPO/backlog vs capex vs "demand exceeds capacity"
```

## 4. Track Each Quarter (Concrete)

- [ ] Total capex (purchased + finance leases) + trajectory language (Nadella/Hood); data centers online
- [ ] Azure revenue growth %, AI contribution (points of growth), Intelligent Cloud margin
- [ ] Microsoft Cloud revenue run-rate; commercial RPO/bookings
- [ ] Any disclosed Copilot / M365 Copilot / GitHub Copilot adoption, seats, or revenue
- [ ] Equity-method loss share from OpenAI hitting Microsoft's income (the "OpenAI drag" line)
- [ ] OpenAI relationship developments: Azure exclusivity/commitment, Stargate, restructuring follow-through, IP term
- [ ] MAI/Phi model releases and any statements on reducing OpenAI reliance; Anthropic/other models in Copilot
- [ ] Power deals (Constellation/Three Mile Island restart timeline, Helion) and capacity-constraint commentary
- [ ] Free cash flow and the capex/FCF relationship

## 5. Cross-Read Signals

- **OpenAI**: its revenue, losses, compute deals (Stargate/Oracle/other clouds), and any direct-competition moves — the single most important external variable for Microsoft.
- **NVIDIA**: data-center revenue/margin — the cost Microsoft is most exposed to (weak own-silicon offset).
- **Amazon / Google**: AWS and Google Cloud AI growth and margin — the demand-capture scoreboard Microsoft is measured against.
- **Anthropic / Google Gemini**: model competition that both threatens OpenAI (helping Microsoft multi-source) and competes with Microsoft's own models.
