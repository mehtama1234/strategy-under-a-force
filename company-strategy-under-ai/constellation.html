Group C · The power

Constellation — restarting nuclear plants for AI

A sleepy old utility became a growth story, because AI is starving for electricity.

Everyone talks about AI chips. Almost nobody talks about the thing that runs them: electricity — vast amounts of it, around the clock. Constellation owns America's biggest fleet of nuclear plants, which pump out steady, carbon-free power 24 hours a day — exactly what AI data centers desperately need.

How AI changed a boring business

For a decade, power companies were sleepy: US electricity demand barely grew. AI flipped that overnight. AI data centers draw enormous power nonstop, and they can't run on wind or solar alone (those aren't reliable 24/7). That made always-on, carbon-free power suddenly scarce and fought-over — and turned an old utility into a growth story.

~3×
Constellation's stock has more than tripled since ChatGPT launched in late 2022 — as tech giants raced to lock up power years in advance.

The headline deal: switching Three Mile Island back on

A first in US history

Constellation is restarting a shut-down reactor at Three Mile Island — the site famous for the 1979 accident — and selling essentially all its power to Microsoft under a ~20-year deal. Why it's remarkable: no one had ever restarted a fully closed US reactor before. That unit shut in 2019 because it couldn't make money. AI demand made it worth reviving. On the news, the stock jumped about 22% in a single day.

It didn't stop there: it signed a similar 20-year deal to keep an Illinois plant alive for Meta, and bought a giant gas-power company (Calpine, ~$22 billion) to become the largest power producer in the US. Tech buyers are paying premium prices for guaranteed clean power.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Constellation is now valued largely on a bet that AI's spending frenzy keeps going. If the build-out cools or over-builds, that premium story deflates. On top of that: the Three Mile Island restart is still years away (targeted 2027) and needs regulator approval — and a once-boring utility is now priced like a hot tech stock, so any disappointment hits hard.

The surprising part

The most notorious name in American nuclear history — Three Mile Island — is being switched back on for the first time ever, purely to feed one tech company's AI. Electricity, the most forgettable commodity there is, became so scarce and strategic that AI turned a 2019 write-off into a multi-billion-dollar prize.
