Apple — behind on AI, but it owns the phone

Does being late to AI matter, when 2.5 billion people already carry your device?

Here's the puzzle. Apple is the most valuable company on Earth, but its AI — the Siri assistant — is clearly weaker than ChatGPT or Google's Gemini. Apple's bet is that it doesn't have to win AI. It just has to put "good enough" AI onto the phone everyone already owns.

What AI put at risk

For 15 years, the way you reached everything on your phone was the home screen and the App Store — Apple's turf. But if people start just asking an assistant to do things, then whoever owns the best assistant owns the new front door. And Apple's assistant is the weak one. The risk: someone else's AI becomes the thing people talk to all day, and Apple is left selling the glass and metal it runs on.

The stumbles

Apple's AI rollout has gone badly:

  • Its AI features launched underwhelming — judged well behind ChatGPT.
  • The smart new Siri was shown off, even advertised — then pulled. Apple admitted it wasn't ready and pushed it to 2026 (and reportedly slipping further).
  • It's partnering instead of building — it bolted on ChatGPT, and reportedly agreed to pay Google about $1 billion a year to use Google's Gemini as the actual brain behind the new Siri.
  • Its AI leadership got shaken up — the long-time AI chief is retiring after the Siri stumbles.

Why it might be fine anyway

Distribution

~2.5 billion active devices. Apple can add AI as a feature the moment it's ready and reach billions overnight. It doesn't need to be first.

Privacy

It runs AI on its own chips, on the device — "the AI that doesn't spy on you." A genuine pitch only Apple can make credibly.

History backs the bet: Apple was late to music players, phones, tablets, and watches — and won each anyway. Owning the customer often beats being first.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Apple could become permanently dependent on rivals for the intelligence inside its own products. If Google and OpenAI supply Siri's actual brain, Apple owns the phone but rents the mind — and loses control of its most important surface. Add a second Google tangle: the roughly $20 billion a year Google pays Apple to be the default search engine (huge, pure profit) is under legal threat.

The surprising part

The company whose whole brand is "we make it ourselves" is reportedly paying its biggest rival, Google, ~$1 billion a year to supply the AI in its own phone — while collecting ~$20 billion a year from that same Google for search. Google pays Apple to own the search box; Apple pays Google to own Siri's brain. Each is the other's biggest customer and vendor at once.

In one sentence

Apple in a nutshell

It's betting that owning the phone beats being first at AI. It probably has time — but if the good AI keeps coming from Google and OpenAI, Apple ends up owning the shiny box while renting the brain inside it.
