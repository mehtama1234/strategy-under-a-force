OpenAI — the most important, and most precarious, company in AI

It built the thing that started all of this. It's also losing money at a scale few companies ever have.

OpenAI makes ChatGPT — the product that kicked off the whole AI boom. Its bet is to build the most capable AI and become the default everyone uses: both the app hundreds of millions open every day, and the engine that other companies' products quietly run on.

The scale is staggering

~900M
People use ChatGPT every week — up from ~250 million just over a year earlier. Almost no product in history has grown this fast.
~$20B+
Yearly revenue run-rate, up from a few billion a year before — from subscriptions, business seats, and letting other companies build on its AI.

But it loses money like almost nothing before it

The other side of the ledger

OpenAI lost roughly $9 billion in a single year — and it's planning to lose more before it (hopefully) turns the corner near the end of the decade. To stay ahead, it has to keep buying almost unimaginable amounts of computing power.

Spending like a nation-state

Here's what "the cost of staying ahead" actually means. OpenAI has lined up compute deals — with Oracle, Microsoft, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, and its "Stargate" data-center plan — reportedly totaling over a trillion dollars in commitments. No normal company signs deals like that. And there's a worry lurking in it: the chipmakers invest in OpenAI, which then buys their chips — a loop that can make the whole AI economy look bigger and healthier than it really is.

Product, or utility?

The deep question about OpenAI is what it is. Is it a product company (ChatGPT, the app you pay for)? Or a utility — the electricity of AI, that everyone plugs into and that fades into the background? It's trying to be both at once, while also untangling a messy history (it started as a nonprofit) and managing a partner, Microsoft, that is also becoming a rival.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Burning cash at epic scale with no proven path to profit. Rivals (Google's Gemini, Anthropic, and free/open models) are catching up and could turn AI into a cheap commodity, while OpenAI's compute bills stay enormous. The bull case: it becomes the default AI everyone pays for. The bear case: it's the center of an expensive bubble.

The surprising part

OpenAI may be the most important and the most financially precarious company in the world at the same time. It has the product everyone imitates and a nine-figure user base — yet it has to spend like a small country, and lose billions a year, just to hold its lead. Rarely have "clearly winning" and "could run out of money" sat so close together.

In one sentence

OpenAI in a nutshell

It started the AI era and leads it — but staying ahead costs more than almost any business has ever spent, and whether that turns into a durable company or a spectacular bubble is the biggest open question in tech.
