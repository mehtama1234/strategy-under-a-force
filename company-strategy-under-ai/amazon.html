Amazon — the "build it yourself" bet

Its worry isn't whether AI works. It's whether it can afford to build its way to the front.

The AI boom made four things suddenly scarce and expensive: AI chips, data centers, electricity, and cash. Amazon's answer to all four is the same — build and own it, don't rent it.

Why "build it yourself"

Almost everyone rents their AI chips from one company: Nvidia. Nvidia charges a huge markup, so every company that rents is paying that markup on top of its costs. Amazon's move is to skip the middleman:

  • Its own AI chips (called Trainium) instead of Nvidia's.
  • Its own buildings — Amazon already runs the biggest cloud, so it can build more.
  • Its own power deals, including nuclear, because AI data centers need enormous amounts of electricity.
  • A big anchor customer — Anthropic (the company behind the Claude AI) runs on Amazon's chips, which helps prove they work.

Is it working? On the business side, yes

+37%
Amazon's cloud business (AWS) is growing about 37% a year — its fastest in years — and it's very profitable.
$25B+
Its own-chip business is now running at over $25 billion a year. Other companies' AIs (including OpenAI's) are starting to use Amazon's chips too — a real sign they work.

The catch: it's burning cash

The real worry — money

Amazon plans to spend around $220 billion in one year building all this. That's so much that it now spends more cash than it takes in. The business looks like it's working, but the bill is enormous, and cash — not whether the technology works — is the thing to watch.

One more honesty note: a chunk of Amazon's reported "profit" lately is a paper gain on its Anthropic stake (the value on paper went up). That's not real cash in the bank, so its headline profit looks better than the day-to-day business really is.

The one-line summary

Amazon in a sentence

It's building its own chips, buildings, and power so it doesn't have to rent from Nvidia. The plan looks right and the cloud is booming — but it costs a fortune, and the question is whether Amazon can afford to finish building it.
