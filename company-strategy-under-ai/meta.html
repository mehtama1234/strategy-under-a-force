Group D · Giants playing a different game

Meta — why give your AI away free?

It spends billions building AI, then hands it out for nothing. That's not charity — it's a weapon.

Most companies guard their AI and charge for it. Meta (Facebook and Instagram) does the opposite: it builds a powerful AI model (called Llama) and gives it away free. That looks insane for something so expensive to make. It isn't.

Why give it away

Meta doesn't sell AI, so making it free costs it nothing — and gains a lot:

  • It undercuts rivals. If a free model is 90% as good, why pay OpenAI or Google? Free Llama makes it hard for them to charge.
  • It prevents a chokehold. Meta got squeezed by Apple and Google on mobile once. It refuses to let any one company own the AI everyone builds on.
  • It becomes the standard. Every developer who builds on Llama makes Meta's version the default — and improves it for free.

In short: giving it away is an attack on rivals' business, not generosity.

The real payoff: better ads

Where the money actually comes from

Meta's biggest AI win isn't a chatbot — it's better ad targeting and better feed/Reels recommendations, which is where almost all its money comes from. AI figures out which ad to show you and what to autoplay next, so people watch more and ads work better. That showed up plainly: ad revenue jumped 24% to about $58 billion in a single quarter, with both the number of ads and their price rising.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Enormous spending with no AI product that pays for itself yet. Meta is pouring well over $100 billion a year into AI with nothing that sells like ads do. And two cracks: its VR/glasses division still loses about $20 billion a year, and — tellingly — the "free and open" strategy is wavering: its latest big model got a mixed reception and reports say Meta is quietly shifting toward closed, secret models for its most powerful AI.

The surprising part

Two things flip the usual story. First, Meta's biggest AI win is the boring recommendation engine, not the flashy chatbot. Second, the "generous" free-AI giant may already be closing the door — keeping its most powerful models private after years of open-sourcing. The weapon might be getting put away.

In one sentence

Meta in a nutshell

It gives AI away to hurt rivals and quietly uses AI to supercharge the ads that actually pay the bills. The gamble is the staggering spend — and whether the "free and open" plan survives.
