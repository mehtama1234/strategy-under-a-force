# End-to-End Goal

Date baseline: 2026-08-05

## Objective

Build a source-backed research dossier on Alphabet/Google's strategy under AI, with enough technical and business detail to answer:

```text
Can Google convert its full-stack AI leadership (Gemini + TPU + Cloud + DeepMind)
into durable growth WITHOUT AI cannibalizing the Search advertising engine that funds everything -
and can it monetize AI enough (Cloud, Gemini, ads) to more than offset any Search erosion?
```

Google is the **"own both axes" archetype** and completes the hyperscaler set alongside the sibling `amazon-ai-strategy-research` (own the supply) and `microsoft-ai-strategy-research` (own the demand). Google is the only player that owns, at scale, *all* of:

- a frontier model it built itself (**Gemini**, via DeepMind),
- mature custom AI silicon with many generations (**TPU**),
- a hyperscale cloud to sell it through (**Google Cloud**),
- and the deepest AI research lab (**DeepMind**).

On the pure technology-and-supply axes, Google is arguably the *strongest-positioned* of the three. But it carries a risk the others do not: **AI threatens its core business.** Search advertising is the cash engine that funds Alphabet, and generative AI answer-engines are the most direct threat to the "ten blue links + ads" model in twenty years. So Google's strategic question is almost the inverse of its rivals':

```text
Amazon:     strong supply, must prove demand
Microsoft:  strong demand, must prove margin
Google:     strong on BOTH supply and demand, but must prove it can ride AI through its own core business
            without cannibalizing the Search ad engine faster than Cloud + Gemini can replace it
```

## The Core Tension

```text
Google has the best AI TECHNOLOGY position (own model + own silicon + own cloud + best research)
Google has the most to LOSE in its existing business (Search ads = the cash cow AI most directly disrupts)

The bet: use full-stack AI leadership to (a) defend Search by making it AI-native (AI Overviews, AI Mode),
(b) grow Google Cloud into a second engine, and (c) monetize Gemini - fast enough that the sum grows
even if classic Search economics erode.
```

## The Framework (Reusable Across Companies)

Same lens as the sibling dossiers:

```text
current business baseline
  -> AI exposure map (demand / cost / THREAT-to-core / new-infrastructure)
  -> bottleneck analysis (compute, chips, power, capital, models, distribution)
  -> strategic response (build / buy / partner / integrate / defend / price)
  -> evidence ledger
  -> investment implication (moat, margin, capex, pricing power, CANNIBALIZATION, new lines)
  -> watch items (what proves it, what breaks it, what to track next quarter)
```

For Google the analysis foregrounds the axis the others underweight: **cannibalization / defense of the core** (does AI erode Search economics?), alongside the full-stack supply advantage.

## Core Thesis To Test

```text
Google is using a uniquely complete AI stack - Gemini + TPU + Cloud + DeepMind - to defend Search by
making it AI-native, to scale Google Cloud (now profitable and TPU-differentiated) into a second growth
engine, and to distribute Gemini across a billion-user surface area. The bet pays off if AI-native Search
monetizes at roughly the old rate while Cloud + Gemini add enough new profit that Alphabet grows even as
classic Search is disrupted - and if TPU's cost advantage lets Cloud undercut NVIDIA-based rivals.
```

## Required Evidence Classes

### Tier 1: Primary Company Evidence
Alphabet 10-K / 10-Q, earnings releases + call transcripts, Google/DeepMind/Cloud official blogs. Calendar-year fiscal quarters.

### Tier 2: Technical / Product Evidence
TPU specs and external-customer deals (Ironwood/Trillium; the Anthropic-on-TPU deal), Gemini model releases and usage, AI Overviews/AI Mode reach and monetization, Cloud RPO/margin, power deals (Kairos, Commonwealth Fusion, Fervo), DeepMind research.

### Tier 3: Strategic / Market Evidence
Credible press and analyst estimates for capex, Cloud growth, Search-disruption dynamics, and the DOJ antitrust remedy — labeled reported/estimated.

## Deliverables

- `reports/strategy-thesis.md` — thesis: full-stack advantage vs Search-cannibalization risk; Cloud/Gemini as the offset; bull/bear.
- `reports/technical-primer.md` — first-principles map of Google's full-stack position (owns model + silicon + cloud + research) and why that is distinctive.
- `reports/search-disruption.md` — dedicated deep dive on the core-business risk: AI Overviews/AI Mode, monetization, Search revenue, DOJ remedy (the Google-specific crux).

- `sources/source-ledger.md` — sources with URL, use, evidence strength.

- `notes/research-notes.md` — working notes, open questions.

## Completion Criteria

Review-ready when it answers, with cited and strength-labeled evidence:

1. What are Google's capex plans, and how TPU/data-center-driven?
2. How mature and cost-advantaged is TPU, and is it winning EXTERNAL customers (Anthropic etc.)?
3. Is Gemini genuinely competitive at the frontier, and how widely is it distributed/used?
4. Is AI cannibalizing Search economics, or monetizing at ~the same rate? What did the DOJ remedy change?
5. How fast is Google Cloud growing, at what margin, and is TPU a real differentiator?
6. How is Google securing power (Kairos SMRs, fusion, geothermal)?
7. How does Google's full-stack position compare with Amazon and Microsoft?
8. Which claims are disclosed vs reported vs estimated vs speculative?
9. What would prove or break the "full-stack wins, core survives" thesis?

## Practical Framing

The central distinction to preserve:

```text
"Google has great AI":
  best models, best chips, best research - true, and widely acknowledged

"AI reshaped Google's strategic problem":
  the company with the best AI technology also has the most exposed cash cow;
  its real test is not building AI, but surviving AI's disruption of its own Search business
  while turning its full-stack lead into Cloud + Gemini profit
```

That distinction is the bridge from an "AI leadership" story to the real question: durable full-stack advantage vs core-business cannibalization.
