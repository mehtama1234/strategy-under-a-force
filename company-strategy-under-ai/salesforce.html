Group B · The ones AI is eating

Salesforce — do you still pay per employee?

The biggest unanswered question hanging over all business software.

For 25 years, Salesforce has made money on one simple rule: you pay for every employee who uses the software. More staff, more revenue. AI breaks that rule — and Salesforce is scrambling to get ahead of it.

The tension, in one picture

The old money-maker:

company has 100 staff→100 logins→Salesforce bills for 100

What AI does to it:

AI agents do the work→company needs 60 staff→Salesforce bills for 60
The problem in one line

If AI does the work, the software is doing more than ever — but the customer needs fewer people logged in, so Salesforce's bill shrinks. The value goes up while the seat count goes down.

Salesforce's answer: sell the AI workers themselves

Instead of only charging per human, Salesforce now sells "Agentforce" — AI agents that handle customer-service and sales work — and charges for how much work the AI does (per conversation or task). So if a customer swaps humans for agents, Salesforce keeps the money; it just moves from "per person" to "per task."

They're eating their own cooking

To prove the agents work, Salesforce cut about 4,000 of its own customer-support jobs (from ~9,000 to ~5,000), saying AI now handles roughly half of support cases. Its CEO put it bluntly: "I need less heads." It also froze hiring software engineers, citing big AI productivity gains.

The main worry — and the reality check

The "death of per-seat software"

If AI does the work, the pay-per-employee model that Salesforce — and Workday, ServiceNow, and others — all rely on gets undercut. Some customers have already cut their seat counts sharply after adding agents. That's the fear hanging over the whole software industry.

But there's a big "not so fast":

  • Is the AI even good enough? Agents that test at 80–90% accuracy reportedly drop to 50–60% in real use, and few customers have fully rolled them out.
  • It's still tiny. Agentforce is ~2% of sales; the company overall is growing a very ordinary ~10% a year — not AI-supercharged.
  • They keep changing the price. Salesforce has rewritten how it charges for agents three times in 18 months — a sign even the biggest believer can't yet figure out how to make agents pay without eating its own seats.

The surprising part

Salesforce is selling the disease and the cure at once: it tells customers "our AI lets you cut staff" while its whole business depends on those same customers paying for staff. It's trying to monetize its way out of that contradiction one price change at a time.

In one sentence

Salesforce in a nutshell

AI threatens the pay-per-employee model it's built on, so it's racing to sell AI workers by the task instead. The move is real and growing fast — but still tiny, unproven in daily use, and repriced three times. The big question is genuinely open.
