Group A · The arms dealers

Nvidia — the one selling the shovels

Everyone building AI has to buy from it. The real lock isn't the chips — it's the software.

In a gold rush, the surest way to make money is to sell the shovels. Nvidia makes the chips that AI runs on, and right now almost everyone building AI — every lab, every cloud — has to buy from it. That has made it one of the most valuable companies on Earth.

The core bet

Nvidia is betting that computing power is the new oil of the AI age, and that it can stay the one company selling the shovels no matter which AI company or cloud ends up winning. It doesn't need to pick the winner — it sells to all of them.

The clever part: the software lock, not the chip

Why nobody can leave

Nvidia's real advantage isn't that its chips are the fastest. It's a piece of software called CUDA that engineers have been building on for over 15 years. A whole generation learned AI on it. Switching to a rival's chip means rewriting tuned code and retraining your engineers — a cost measured in engineering-years, not dollars. So even a cheaper rival chip often loses, because the switch is what's expensive.

Nvidia also sells more than chips — it sells the networking that wires them together, whole server racks, and the software on top. So it captures a bigger slice of each AI build-out than a chip-only company would.

The main worry: its best customers want to replace it

The single biggest risk

A tiny number of giant buyers provide most of Nvidia's money — recently, its top four customers were about 61% of all sales (one of them alone was 22%). And those same giants — Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta — are all building their own chips to stop depending on Nvidia. If even one of them shifts its spending in-house, the hit is enormous.

Other worries:

  • Home-grown rivals. Google (TPU), Amazon (Trainium), Microsoft (Maia), Meta (MTIA), plus AMD — all aiming to do the same job for less.
  • A spending slowdown. The giants' AI budgets can't grow at today's pace forever. Some of the demand is even circular — Nvidia invests in AI firms that then buy Nvidia chips, which can make demand look bigger than it is.
  • China politics. Export rules are an on/off switch — a 2025 ban forced a multi-billion-dollar write-down. Nvidia later agreed to hand the US government 15% of its China chip sales just to be allowed to sell there.

What AI made scarce, and what it made risky

Newly valuable: gaming chips became the most fought-over industrial part on Earth. Scarce: Nvidia's own top chips — sold out roughly a year ahead. Risky: leaning on a handful of buyers who are also becoming rivals.

In one sentence

Nvidia in a nutshell

It sells the shovels everyone needs, and its software makes those shovels painful to put down — which is why it's so profitable. The danger: its handful of giant customers are quietly forging their own shovels.
