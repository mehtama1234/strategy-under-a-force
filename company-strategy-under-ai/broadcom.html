Group A · The arms dealers

Broadcom — the anti-Nvidia

It doesn't sell finished AI chips. It helps the giants build their own — so they can stop buying from Nvidia.

Everyone rents AI chips from Nvidia and pays a fat markup. Broadcom sells the escape hatch: it quietly helps the biggest tech companies design their own custom AI chips, and it makes the networking gear that wires thousands of those chips into one giant computer.

The core bet

Broadcom is the "build your own" option. If you're Google or Meta, spending tens of billions renting Nvidia chips, at some point it's cheaper to design your own — and Broadcom is the partner that helps you do it. It never sells a competing off-the-shelf chip; it profits from everyone's desire to escape the market leader.

How AI changed it

Broadcom was a steady, unglamorous chip supplier. AI turned its decades-old design skill into one of the world's most valuable companies almost overnight:

~2×
Its AI-chip sales are roughly doubling year over year (about $8–11 billion in a single quarter).
~$73B
Its pile of booked-but-not-yet-delivered AI orders — years of work already lined up.

Who it builds for

Its customers are exactly the giants with the deepest pockets and the strongest reasons to leave Nvidia: Google (the TPU chips), Meta, and now OpenAI (a deal for a massive amount of custom chips) and Anthropic. And there's a second, separate money stream: Broadcom owns the networking chips that connect AI clusters together — not a side act, a whole business of its own.

The quiet cushion

Nearly half of Broadcom's money isn't AI chips at all — it's software (VMware), a boring, steady, very-high-profit business that runs company data centers. It quietly pays the bills while the flashy AI-chip side grows in lumps.

The main worry: a handful of giant customers who are also partners

The single biggest risk

Most of Broadcom's AI money comes from just a few huge customers — who are also its partners. They could bring the design work fully in-house, switch to a rival, or simply slow their spending. Because the orders are giant and lumpy, one cancelled program dents the results badly. Markets also worry it keeps needing new named customers to keep the growth story going.

The surprising part

Broadcom "beats" Nvidia without ever selling a competing chip — it wins by helping Nvidia's own biggest customers stop needing Nvidia. And the tangle goes deep: the famous "$10 billion mystery customer" everyone assumed was OpenAI turned out to be Anthropic — buying Google's chips that Broadcom co-designs. Partners and rivals, all at once.

In one sentence

Broadcom in a nutshell

It sells the "build your own AI chip" kit to the giants trying to escape Nvidia, plus the wiring that connects it all — and a boring software business pays the bills. The risk: a few enormous customers who could always do it themselves.
