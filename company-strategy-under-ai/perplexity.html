Group G · The attackers

Perplexity — the startup attacking Google

A tiny company is landing real punches on the business everyone thought was untouchable.

Perplexity is an "answer engine." You ask a question and it gives you one direct answer, with its sources — instead of a page of ten links to click through. It's aimed straight at the heart of Google's business.

Why a startup can suddenly attack Google

The moat that AI dissolved

For 20 years, competing with Google meant building a copy of the entire web — a job worth tens of billions. AI changed the game. Perplexity doesn't build a giant index; it points a language model at the web and has it read and write the answer for you. Suddenly the fight is about the experience — one clean answer vs. a wall of links — and that's a fight a small company founded in 2022 can actually enter.

How far it's gotten

1B+
Questions answered per month — up several times over in a year.
~$20B
What investors value it at, with backing from Jeff Bezos and Nvidia. It also launched "Comet," an AI browser that can shop and book things for you.

The fight over how it gets its answers

Perplexity's answers come from other people's content, and the owners are furious. News publishers (including News Corp and the BBC) have sued or threatened it for using their work; Amazon sued it for having its browser push past Amazon's blocks to shop on a user's behalf. It's repeatedly accused of ignoring the "please don't scrape me" signals websites use. The legal cloud is real.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Google can just do the same thing — to billions of people, overnight. Perplexity handles about a billion questions a month; Google handles trillions, and it rushed out its own "AI answers" feature partly to blunt Perplexity. On top of that: producing AI answers is expensive (far more than serving links), it just dropped advertising and hasn't proven it can make real money, and the lawsuits could force it to pay for the content it feeds on.

The surprising part

Here's the cruel irony. The same AI that lets Perplexity attack Google is exactly what makes it vulnerable: the "answer engine" is not a secret Google can't copy — Google can build it instantly and ship it to everyone. So Perplexity's real danger isn't failing to beat Google. It's succeeding just enough to prove the idea works — and then watching Google do the same thing at planet scale. And the very speed that impresses people (bypassing Amazon's block in a day) is the behavior dragging it into court.

In one sentence

Perplexity in a nutshell

It proved AI can turn search into direct answers and rattled Google doing it — but it's tiny, loses money, fights publishers in court, and its whole idea is one Google can copy overnight.
