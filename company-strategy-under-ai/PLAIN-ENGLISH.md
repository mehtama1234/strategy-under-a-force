# Read This First — Plain English

Date: 2026-08-06

This explains all four company write-ups in simple words. The company folders have the full detail and the sources. This page is the short, plain version.

## What all of this is about

AI is changing how big tech companies make money. This project looks at four of them and asks one question about each:

**What did AI make risky, or newly valuable, for this company's business?**

Each company has a *different* AI problem. That's the whole point.

## The four companies, in one line each

- **Amazon** — building its own stuff so it doesn't have to rent from Nvidia. Risk: **money**.
- **Microsoft** — betting on ChatGPT's maker and selling AI to businesses. Risk: **profit and depending on a partner**.
- **Google** — has the best AI tech of all of them, but AI threatens its main money-maker. Risk: **AI eating its own business**.
- **Cadence** — sells software for designing chips; trying to sell "finished results" instead of just tools. Risk: **unproven that this actually changes how it makes money**.

## Amazon — the "build it yourself" bet

The AI boom made four things scarce and expensive: AI chips, data centers, electricity, and cash. Almost everyone rents their AI chips from one company (Nvidia), which is very expensive.

Amazon's answer: **build its own** — its own AI chips, its own buildings, its own power deals (even nuclear). If it owns all of it, it pays less over time.

Is it working? On the business side, yes — its cloud arm is growing fast and making good money. The catch: it's spending so much building all this that it's now spending **more cash than it takes in**. So the bet looks right, but it's expensive and cash is the worry.

## Microsoft — the "sell it to businesses" bet

Microsoft went the opposite way from Amazon. Instead of building its own chips, it **partnered** (with OpenAI, the ChatGPT company) and focused on **selling** AI features to businesses (its "Copilot" assistant).

Strength: it's great at selling to companies, and it has the most in-demand AI. Weakness: it mostly **rents** its chips from Nvidia (expensive), and it leans heavily on one partner (OpenAI). So its worries are keeping profits healthy and not being too dependent on that partner.

## Google — best tech, but AI threatens its own cash machine

Google is the only one that owns **everything**: its own AI model (Gemini), its own chips, its own cloud, and the best AI research lab. On pure technology, it's ahead.

But here's its unique trap: Google makes almost all its money from **search ads** — people search, click links, see ads. AI can answer your question directly, so you never click, and Google shows fewer ads. So the very AI Google is best at could **shrink its own main business**.

Google's plan: put AI *inside* Search so it keeps making money, and grow its cloud business to make up any difference. So far its ad money is still growing. But whether AI-answers make as much money as old-style search is the big open question — and it's a number Google will probably never fully show.

(Side note: while checking, I caught an error in the Amazon write-up that said Google's cloud grew 82%. The real number is around 30–34%. Fixed it.)

## Cadence — trying to sell results, not just tools

Cadence makes software that engineers use to design computer chips. Today it **rents out the software** (like a subscription).

The AI question: can Cadence shift to **selling finished results** — "we'll get your chip design to hit its targets" — and charge for the outcome instead of just tool access? Chip design is a good fit for this, because you can **actually test** whether the result is correct (a simulator checks it). You can only charge for a result you can prove.

Where it stands: this is still mostly a hope, not a fact. Today Cadence's AI is a **premium add-on** to the same old subscription — which is valuable, but not a real change in how it makes money yet. Watch for signs it starts charging by usage or by results.

(One caution baked into the write-up: don't credit Cadence with a "40x faster" claim floating around — that's a different, separate company called ChipStack.)

## The one idea tying them together

Same question, four different answers:

- Amazon: **can it afford** to build its way out?
- Microsoft: **can it keep profits up** while renting chips and leaning on a partner?
- Google: **will AI eat** its own ad business before its new businesses grow?
- Cadence: **can it sell outcomes** instead of tools?
