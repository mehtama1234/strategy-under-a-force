Group E · When AI replaces the workers

Klarna — it replaced its staff with AI, then quietly hired them back

The loudest "AI does the jobs" company became the clearest lesson in going too far.

Klarna (the "buy now, pay later" company) made the boldest bet on AI replacing workers — and then publicly reversed part of it. Its story is the honest one about what AI can and can't take over.

The bold bet

Klarna handed much of its customer service to an AI chatbot built with OpenAI, froze hiring, and let its staff shrink. Its CEO became one of the loudest voices claiming AI could do most jobs — he even presented earnings using an AI clone of himself.

700
The AI assistant was said to do the work of 700 full-time agents — handling 2.3 million chats a month, about two-thirds of all customer service.
5,500 → 3,400
Staff shrank by roughly 40% as AI took over and hiring froze. Great numbers to show investors before going public.

Then the walk-back

"We went too far"

In 2025, the CEO publicly admitted the cuts went too far — chasing cost, "what you end up having is lower quality." Customers hated the generic, empathy-free answers and the AI's failure on tricky or emotional cases. So Klarna started rehiring humans and promised customers could always reach a real person.

Important: this wasn't a retreat from AI. Klarna kept the AI for routine questions and re-added a human layer on top for the hard, emotional ones — a hybrid, not a reversal.

The lesson

What it actually teaches

Cutting people for AI can quietly erode quality and trust in ways that don't show up in the launch numbers. AI is great at routine volume, weak at judgment and empathy. Klarna's real mistake wasn't adding the AI — it was removing the humans. The fix was to use both.

The surprising part

Here's the twist: even as Klarna rehired people, its AI-efficiency numbers kept climbing (the bot's workload grew from "700 agents' worth" to over 850). So it's not that the AI failed — it's that a fully de-humanized service backfired. The loudest "AI replaced our staff" company ended up proving that the winning answer is machines plus people, not machines instead of people. (Klarna went public in late 2025 at about a $15 billion value.)

In one sentence

Klarna in a nutshell

It bet loudest that AI could replace staff, cut ~40% of its people — then admitted it overshot and hired humans back for the hard cases. The lesson for everyone: AI handles the easy work; people still matter for the rest.
