Group C · The landlords of AI

CoreWeave — the landlord that rents out AI computers

Amazon's build-it bet, but with borrowed money — dazzling if AI keeps booming, dangerous if it doesn't.

Not every company can afford to build its own AI computers. So they rent — and CoreWeave is one of the biggest landlords. It buys tens of thousands of Nvidia's coveted chips, packs them into data centers, and rents that computing power to AI companies by the hour.

The core bet (and a wild origin)

CoreWeave borrows enormous sums to buy chips, then rents them out for more than the loan costs. What makes it vivid: it started in 2017 as a crypto-mining company. When crypto crashed, the founders realized their pile of gaming chips was worth more renting to AI companies than mining coins. They pivoted — and the AI boom turned that leftover pile into gold.

How fast it grew

$1.9B → $5.1B
Sales in one year (2024 to 2025) — up about 170%. It went public in early 2025.
~$99B
Future revenue already booked under contract — bigger than many blue-chip companies. Its two giant customers: Microsoft and OpenAI.

The main worry: a mountain of debt

The single biggest risk

CoreWeave borrows against the chips themselves to buy more chips — its debt hit about $21.6 billion. And the chips lose value every year as newer ones arrive. So it's carrying huge loans backed by an asset that's aging fast. On top of that, one customer (Microsoft) was about two-thirds of its sales — lose them and the whole thing wobbles. And it still loses money (about $1.2 billion in 2025) even at $5 billion in sales.

The honest status

It's a real business with real, contracted revenue — but it's fundamentally a leveraged bet that AI demand keeps roaring. If it does, CoreWeave is brilliant. If demand cools, a big customer pulls back, or borrowing gets expensive, the debt could crush it. It works beautifully in a boom and is fragile in a bust.

The surprising part

Look at where Nvidia sits: it sells CoreWeave the chips, owns a stake in CoreWeave, and promises to buy CoreWeave's unused capacity. Supplier, investor, and backup customer all at once. Critics call this "circular" — Nvidia's money helps buy Nvidia's chips, which can make AI demand look bigger than it really is.

  • A failed crypto miner became one of the most important companies in AI.
  • In 2026 a CoreWeave loan (backed by chips) got a top credit rating — which quietly means pension and insurance funds now hold risk tied to chips that could be obsolete in a few years.

In one sentence

CoreWeave in a nutshell

It's a landlord for AI computing, built on borrowed money and a couple of huge customers. A brilliant boom-time business — and a risky one if the boom pauses.
