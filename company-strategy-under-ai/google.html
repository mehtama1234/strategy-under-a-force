Google — best tech, biggest trap

It has the strongest AI of the three. And the most to lose from it.

Google is the only one of these companies that owns everything it needs for AI: its own AI model (Gemini), its own chips, its own cloud, and the best AI research lab in the world. On raw technology, it's ahead. But it also has a trap the others don't.

Why its technology is the strongest

The others each rent or borrow a key piece. Google owns them all:

  • Its own AI model (Gemini) — it didn't have to partner with anyone.
  • Its own chips (called TPUs), which it's been building longer than anyone — so it doesn't depend on Nvidia.
  • Its own cloud to sell it all through.
  • The best research lab (DeepMind) — the one that won a Nobel Prize for AI work.

Owning both the AI model and the chips is a special advantage: Google can design them to fit each other perfectly, which nobody else can do as well. (Even Anthropic now rents a huge batch of Google's chips.)

The trap: AI threatens its own money machine

Here's the problem no rival has. Google makes almost all its money from search ads. The old way works like this:

you search→you see a page of links and ads→you click→Google gets paid

But AI can just answer your question directly. Then:

you ask→AI gives one answer→you don't click→fewer ads, less money
The trap in one line

The very AI that Google is best at could shrink its own main business. Amazon's store and Microsoft's apps get better with AI. Google's search ads could get eaten by AI. That's its unique risk.

Google's plan, and how it's going

Google's answer is to put AI inside Search itself (so the answer still shows up on a Google page with ads nearby), and to grow its cloud business to make up any difference. So far, its ad money is still growing. Two more things going its way: it won a big antitrust case (it won't be broken up), and unlike Amazon it's still bringing in more cash than it spends.

The big open question it can't fully answer: does an AI answer make as much money as the old links-and-ads page? Google says roughly yes. Nobody outside can check — it's a number Google will probably never fully show.

Small correction we caught: while checking, the Amazon write-up claimed Google's cloud grew 82%. The real figure is about 30–34%. Fixed.

The one-line summary

Google in a sentence

It has the best AI technology of the three — but it's also the only one whose main money-maker (search ads) is the thing AI most directly threatens. Best toolkit, hardest trap. The race is whether its new AI businesses grow faster than AI eats the old one.
