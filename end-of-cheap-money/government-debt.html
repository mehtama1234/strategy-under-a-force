Group D · The government & housing

The US interest bill — the biggest borrower was the government itself

Everyone talks about households and companies. The Treasury borrowed the most of all.

While everyone watched companies and homebuyers load up on cheap debt, the biggest borrower of the free-money era was the US government. Now that debt has to be refinanced at much higher rates — and the interest bill has exploded.

The bill

$344B → $1T+
Annual interest on the national debt, from 2020 to 2025–26 — it roughly tripled in six years.
The line that stops you

The government now spends more on interest than on its entire military. Interest is the third-biggest thing in the budget, behind only Social Security and Medicare — and about a third of the ~$38 trillion debt has to be refinanced within a year, at today's higher rates.

The main worry

A spiral: big deficits mean more borrowing; higher rates make each borrowed dollar cost more; so interest crowds out money for everything else — defense, healthcare, roads. "Higher for longer" makes it worse. If it keeps compounding, the path becomes hard to sustain.

The surprising part

The cheap-money era's single biggest beneficiary — and now biggest casualty — was the government itself. It borrowed trillions at near-zero rates; now, for the first time in modern history, it spends more just servicing that debt than it does on its whole military. The bill for a decade of free money is coming due at the national level.

In one sentence

The interest bill in a nutshell

The government borrowed most of all when money was free; now higher rates have tripled its interest bill past the entire defense budget — the largest, and least escapable, casualty of the end of cheap money.
