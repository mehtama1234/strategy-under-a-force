Group B · Built on free money

SPACs & crypto — the bubbles that popped when cash paid 5%

The two purest symbols of the free-money era — and its clearest casualties.

If free money had mascots, they were SPACs (blank-check shell companies that let startups skip the normal IPO) and crypto. Both soared on cheap money and endless optimism — and both cratered the moment rates rose and safe cash started paying.

The boom and bust

64% → ~0
SPACs were 64% of all IPOs at the 2021 peak, then issuance collapsed to almost nothing; dozens of SPAC-merged companies went bankrupt.
$69k → $16k
Bitcoin's crash from its late-2021 peak — a ~77% wipeout — with Terra/Luna vanishing and FTX collapsing into fraud (Nov 2022).

The main worry / the lesson

The lesson

Speculative bets that depend on cheap money and perpetual optimism get wiped out when money costs something and safe alternatives pay well. Cheap money doesn't make bad bets good — it just lets them hide until safe returns come back.

The surprising part

Here's the mechanism, in one line: when cash pays 5% risk-free, the appeal of a speculative money-losing bet collapses. SPACs and crypto didn't crash because they were suddenly discovered to be flawed — many people already knew. They crashed because the math changed. Once safe money paid 5%, "why bet on an unproven shell company or a token when I can earn a guaranteed 5%?" had only one answer. Rates quietly popped the bubble. (Crypto later partly recovered — but the free-money frenzy didn't.)
