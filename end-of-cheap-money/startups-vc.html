Group B · Built on free money

Startups & VC — the funding winter (except for AI)

In 2021, money was thrown at anything with a pulse. Then it vanished.

Startups and their venture-capital backers ran on a flood of cheap money chasing growth. When rates rose in 2022, that flood dried up almost overnight — the "funding winter."

The freeze

$681B → ~$220B
Venture funding fell roughly two-thirds from its 2021 peak. "Down rounds" (raising at a lower value than before) became normal.
−92%
The collapse in "exits" — startups couldn't go public (the IPO market froze), so VCs couldn't cash out. Thousands of startups simply shut down.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — stuck

A whole generation of startups was valued for a world of free money that no longer exists — and can't grow into those prices. Some once-hot companies marked at billions are now worth a fraction. VCs sit on paper gains they can't turn into cash, and "zombie" startups (30–40% of the venture-backed pool) limp along — unable to raise, sell, or die cleanly.

The surprising part

The winter was selective

Even as money fled almost every other kind of startup, it poured into AI — AI startups raised ~$110 billion in 2024 (up 62%) while overall startup funding fell. Nearly a third of all venture dollars went to anything with "AI" in the pitch. It wasn't a freeze so much as a flight to one theme. (Which is the whole subject of the AI collection.)

In one sentence

Startups & VC in a nutshell

Higher rates froze the flood of cheap money, crashing funding and exits and stranding a generation of over-valued startups — while AI quietly hoovered up whatever money was left.
