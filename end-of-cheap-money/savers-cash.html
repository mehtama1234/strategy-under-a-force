Group A · Cash is king again

Savers & cash — "cash is trash" became "cash pays 5%"

For the first time in ~15 years, doing nothing with your money paid you.

For a decade, holding cash was punishment — savings accounts paid basically nothing, so everyone was pushed into stocks. Higher rates changed that overnight: cash and safe short-term bonds started paying 4–5%. Suddenly, savers were winners.

The death of "there is no alternative"

Wall Street had a rule for the cheap-money years: "TINA — There Is No Alternative" to stocks, because nothing else paid. Higher rates killed it. Now there was an alternative — safe cash paying ~5% — and trillions rushed toward it.

~$7T+
Money parked in money-market funds — a record. Roughly a trillion dollars flowed in during a single year as savers chased the yield.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the window is closing

As the Fed cuts rates again, those juicy cash yields are fading (from ~5.5% toward ~4%), and money may rotate back into stocks. Two catches even now: cash barely beats inflation in real terms, and some brokers quietly skimmed the spread — paying customers far less than the market rate on idle cash (Schwab slashed its "sweep" yields), keeping the difference.

The surprising part

After a decade where "cash is trash" was gospel, cash quietly became one of the best low-risk deals around — and over two trillion dollars migrated out of stocks and into money funds. It shattered a multi-decade consensus that stocks were your only option. The most boring asset had its moment.

Savers & cash in a nutshell

Higher rates turned idle cash into a real 5% earner for the first time in 15 years, pulling trillions out of stocks — a window that's now narrowing as rates fall.
