Group C · The debt bombs

Commercial real estate — the slow-motion debt bomb

No dramatic crash. Just empty offices meeting expensive refinancing, quietly erasing fortunes.

Office towers were bought with cheap, short-term loans that have to be refinanced every few years. That was fine when rates were low and offices were full. Higher rates plus half-empty buildings turned it into a slow-motion crisis.

The double hit

Empty offices

Remote work pushed office vacancy toward 20%; values fell 39–50% (some buildings far more).

Expensive refinancing

Loans taken at ~3% must now roll over at ~7%+ — on buildings worth much less.

~$1.2T
The "refinancing wall" — commercial mortgages coming due, peaking around 2027. Office loan delinquencies hit record highs.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

A slow cascade as more loans mature and can't be refinanced profitably — owners walk away, lenders foreclose, and the losses land on banks (especially regional ones) heavily exposed to commercial real estate. It plays out over years, not a single bad day.

The surprising part

There was no "crash" — no building fell 70% in an afternoon. Instead a quiet two-step: first remote work drained the tenants, then higher rates made the debt unaffordable. The pain is mechanical — a maturing loan meets an emptier building at a much higher rate, and the math simply stops working. Some Chicago towers have sold at 90% discounts.

In one sentence

Commercial real estate in a nutshell

Empty offices plus loans that must refinance at double the rate are quietly wiping out building values — a slow-burning debt bomb whose losses roll toward the banks.
