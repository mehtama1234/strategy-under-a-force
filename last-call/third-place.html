Group D · The industry's response

The "third place"

The social role of the pub is moving to the run club and the sauna.

The pub was the classic "third place" — the spot between home and work where community happened, fueled by alcohol. As drinking fades, that role hasn't vanished; it's migrating. Run clubs, gyms, cold-plunge spas, coffee shops, and matcha bars are quietly inheriting the job the bar did for a century — with wellness and accountability, not a pint, as the glue.

The core bet

The insight is that people never mainly wanted the alcohol — they wanted the gathering. So the ritual is being rebuilt around anything but booze. The businesses that supply the new gathering spots capture the social spending, and the loyalty, that used to belong to bars.

What the numbers say

The inversion

Nightlife didn't just decline — it got rebranded as wellness. A run club does what a bar did (friendship, status, a weekly anchor); a sauna hosts guided sessions like a lounge; a matcha bar owns the daytime hangout. The social hunger was always there — the old bars just couldn't hold people once the drinking itself fell out of favor.

What's newly valuable
Gyms, run clubs, wellness clubs, coffee and matcha bars, and sober venues — the businesses supplying community without a bar tab.
What's draining
The wet-led pub and club, and the assumption that alcohol is what brings people together.
The single biggest worry

The new third places are often membership-based and expensive, which prices out lower-income and rural communities. As old free-ish gathering spots close and pricey wellness clubs replace them, the people who most need community can get locked out of it.

The surprising part

The ritual didn't break — it inverted. Instead of meeting at night to drink, people meet in daylight to sweat, plunge, or sip matcha, and get the same friendship and belonging out of it. A 2024 finding that most adults lack a meaningful group suggests the old bars were already failing to hold people; the demand just needed a vehicle that didn't wreck your next morning.

In one sentence

The pub's real product was community, not alcohol — and run clubs, saunas, and matcha bars are now selling the community without the drink.
