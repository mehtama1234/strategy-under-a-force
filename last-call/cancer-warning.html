Group C · Why it's happening

The cancer warning

Alcohol's image flipped from "heart-healthy" to "known carcinogen."

For thirty years, the story was that a glass of red wine was good for your heart. That story is gone. Health authorities have reversed course completely — there is "no safe level" of drinking, and even moderate use raises cancer risk. When the science that made alcohol feel responsible collapses, the whole "everything in moderation" defense goes with it.

The core bet

This is the information shock behind the decline. The old "moderate drinking is fine, maybe even healthy" consensus has been replaced by official warnings that alcohol causes cancer at any level. That reframing gives every wellness-minded person a hard, science-backed reason to cut back.

What the numbers say

"no safe level"
The World Health Organization's position, stated plainly in January 2023: when it comes to cancer, no amount of alcohol is safe. A blunt reversal of decades of "moderation" messaging.
Jan 2025
The US Surgeon General issued an advisory calling for a cancer warning label on alcohol, naming it a leading preventable cause of cancer alongside tobacco and obesity.
~100k / ~20k
Estimated US cancer cases and deaths per year linked to alcohol, per that advisory — numbers most drinkers had never heard attached to their habit.
2025
The American Heart Association said there's no proven link between alcohol and better heart health, and US Dietary Guidelines stripped out the old cardiovascular-benefit language.

Why it hits so hard

The "healthy glass of red" wasn't just wine marketing — it was the permission slip the entire industry leaned on. It let moderate drinkers feel virtuous. Remove it, and drinking goes from a mild indulgence you could rationalize to a habit you now know raises your cancer risk. For a health-focused generation, that's decisive.

What it destroys
The moderation defense, the wine-is-healthy halo, and the comfortable idea that a drink or two is harmless.
What it accelerates
Sober-curious behavior, warning-label momentum, and demand for the non-alcoholic and functional drinks in Group B.
The single biggest worry

Warning labels loom. If alcohol gets a cigarette-style cancer label, it changes the product at the point of sale forever — and once "alcohol causes cancer" is common knowledge, you can't un-know it.

The surprising part

The exact claim that lifted wine for a generation — "it's good for your heart" — is now the claim health bodies most want gone. One of the researchers behind the original heart-health findings was found guilty of scientific fraud. Alcohol's healthiest-seeming corner turned out to rest on some of its shakiest science.

In one sentence

When "a glass of red is good for you" became "no amount is safe," alcohol lost the one health story that made moderate drinking feel fine.
