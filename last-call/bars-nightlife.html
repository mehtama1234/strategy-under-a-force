Group A · Losing the drinkers

Bars & nightlife

The pub is being hollowed out by a generation that stays in.

For centuries the bar was the default place adults met, and nightlife ran on a simple assumption: each new wave of young drinkers would fill the room. That bet is breaking. Young people go out less, drink less, and spend less on a night out — so the venues built around alcohol are closing or barely holding on.

The core bet

Nightlife assumed the going-out habit renews itself every generation. Instead, the core customer is disappearing: young adults now socialize in person far less than any generation before them, and the room a bar was built to fill is emptier every year.

What the numbers say

−72%

−20% / −33%
Movie theaters and bowling alleys since 2001. The whole "go out at night" habit is fading — it's not just bars.

What's replacing it

The "third place" — a social spot that isn't home or work — isn't dying. It's being rebuilt around anything but alcohol: sober bars, coffee shops, run clubs, gyms, and daytime events. Young people didn't stop wanting to gather; they're gathering sober, in daylight, and often around fitness or caffeine instead of a pint. (That shift gets its own page: the "third place".)

What's draining
Late-night wet-led bars, nightclubs, and the whole ecosystem that hung off a big bar tab — cabs, late-night food, door staff.
What's rising
Daytime and sober venues, coffee-and-community spaces, and anywhere the draw is the people, not the drinks.
The single biggest worry

If the next generation never builds the going-out habit, the decline is structural, not a slump you wait out. You can't win back a customer who never showed up in the first place.

The surprising part

The bar isn't being beaten by a better bar. It's being quietly abandoned by people who would rather stay in, stay sober, or meet somewhere that never poured a drink. The social hunger is still there — the vehicle changed. (Honest caveat: US bar-closure data is patchy, and one industry tracker shows a short-term Gen Z uptick in 2023–2025, so the shift may be less total than the headlines suggest.)

In one sentence

The bar is losing not to competition but to abandonment — a generation that gathers sober, in daylight, somewhere without a tap.
