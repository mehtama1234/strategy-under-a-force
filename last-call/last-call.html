Group E · The big picture

Last call

The default drink stopped being alcohol — and four forces did it at once.

For a century, adult socializing defaulted to alcohol. "Let's grab a drink" meant booze, and an entire economy — beer giants, wine regions, bars, ad budgets — rested on the unquestioned assumption that adults drink. That default is ending. And the striking part is why: not one cause, but four independent forces hitting at the same moment.

The one idea

The default drink stopped being alcohol. Younger adults are choosing abstinence or moderation at rates well above their parents — and they're doing it because their peers, their bodies, the science, and the law are all, at once, pointing away from drinking.

The whole force in numbers

54%
Share of US adults who drink at all in 2025 (Gallup) — roughly a 90-year low. Per-person consumption is back near multi-decade lows.
12.6M > 10.5M
Daily cannabis users now outnumber daily drinkers — a reversal from a generation ago, when drinking led by a wide margin.
~$830B
Value shed by global drinks companies as the shift sank in; spirits giant Diageo fell roughly 30% in 2025.
+28–32%
Growth in non-alcoholic beer while regular beer fell — the clearest sign the money is flowing to whoever serves the sober drinker.

The four forces, together

Each of these would matter alone. Arriving at once, they make the decline feel structural rather than faddish:

  • Values (Gen Z & health). Not drinking became a normal wellness choice — no explanation needed.
  • Biochemistry (GLP-1 drugs). Ozempic and its cousins quietly mute the urge to drink as a side effect.
  • Information (the cancer warning). "A glass of red is healthy" became "no amount is safe."
  • Substitution (cannabis). Legal weed became the everyday relaxant alcohol used to be.

Winners vs losers

Winners
Non-alcoholic drinks, cannabis, functional beverages, RTD canned cocktails, and the new "third places" — coffee shops, gyms, run clubs, saunas.
Losers
Mass beer (Bud Light lost #1 to Modelo), wine (vines being ripped out), independent bars and clubs, and the whole nightlife ecosystem.
The biggest tension

The industry is trying to offset the decline with premium and non-alcoholic lines — Diageo bought a non-alcoholic spirit, brewers pour money into 0.0. But the math doesn't work: premium plus non-alcoholic growth (a few percent) can't offset a mass-beer and wine collapse. The industry can slow the decline. It can't reverse the reasons behind it.

The surprising part

This isn't one trend — it's a collision. Five independent systems (peers, the law, the body, the science, and generational replacement) are all pointing away from alcohol at the same time. That simultaneity is exactly why it feels inevitable: a young adult isn't saying no to a drink for one reason, but because everything around them quietly agrees.

In one sentence

The default adult drink shifted from alcohol to moderation because values, drugs, science, and cannabis all turned against drinking at once — a structural decline the industry can manage but not undo.

How this connects to the rest of the series

Last Call sits alongside its siblings. The GLP-1 thread is a direct branch of the Ozempic Effect — one drug reshaping many industries. The generational engine is the flip side of the demographic cliff: fewer, older, differently-behaving consumers. And the rise of wellness "third places" echoes how the remote shift rearranged where daily life happens.
