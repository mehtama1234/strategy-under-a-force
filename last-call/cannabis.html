Group B · The sober economy

Cannabis

More Americans now use weed daily than drink alcohol daily.

Alcohol's decline isn't only about people doing nothing instead — some of it is a straight substitution. Cannabis has gone mainstream and legal across much of the US, and for a growing number of people it's the way they relax at the end of the day. A quiet milestone passed around 2022: daily marijuana users overtook daily drinkers for the first time.

The core bet

The cannabis industry's bet is that weed is the new after-work drink — a legal, milder-feeling way to unwind that many people now prefer to alcohol. As legalization spreads and stigma fades, some of alcohol's most habitual customers simply switch.

What the numbers say

2022
The year daily/near-daily cannabis use surpassed daily drinking in the US — about 17.7M daily weed users versus fewer daily drinkers. A generation ago it was the reverse by a wide margin.
$1.1B
US sales of hemp-derived THC drinks in 2024 — cannabis beverages sold in ordinary stores, riding a loophole in the 2018 Farm Bill that made low-THC hemp products federally legal.
$4B
What beer giant Constellation sank into cannabis firm Canopy Growth starting 2017 — a bet the maker of Corona could hedge into weed. It was largely written down. Getting the substitution right is hard.

Why it matters for alcohol

The threat isn't just lost sales — it's the loss of the habit. If a 25-year-old's default "wind-down" is a gummy instead of a beer, alcohol never forms the lifelong routine it used to rely on. That's why beer and spirits giants keep circling cannabis: they'd rather own the replacement than be replaced by it.

What's newly valuable
Legal dispensaries, low-dose THC and CBD drinks you can buy at a normal store, and the "canna-curious" customer alcohol is losing.
What's risky
A patchwork of state and federal rules, banking that won't touch the industry, and hemp-drink potency that's largely unregulated.
The single biggest worry

The fastest-growing piece — hemp-derived THC drinks — exists in a legal gray zone. A single change to the Farm Bill could wipe out a $1B+ category overnight, and inconsistent potency is a public-health headache waiting for a crackdown.

The surprising part

You can now buy a mildly intoxicating THC seltzer next to the LaCroix at a regular grocery store in many states, thanks to a farm subsidy bill that was never meant to be about getting people high. The biggest new competitor to beer showed up through an agricultural loophole.

In one sentence

Weed didn't just get legal — it quietly became the everyday relaxant that alcohol used to be, and it's eating the habit at the root.
