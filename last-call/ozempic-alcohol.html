Group C · Why it's happening

Ozempic & alcohol

The weight-loss drug that quietly turns down the urge to drink.

There's a second, stranger force draining alcohol sales — and it came out of a diabetes and weight-loss drug. GLP-1 medicines like Ozempic and Wegovy dampen the brain's reward system broadly, and it turns out that cuts cravings for alcohol just as it cuts cravings for food. As tens of millions start these drugs, the industry faces a chemical headwind that has nothing to do with generational values.

The core bet

The drug wasn't designed to fight drinking — it does it as a side effect. GLP-1s act on the same reward circuits that drive all cravings, so a medicine sold for weight loss inadvertently turns down alcohol too. The more people take it, the smaller alcohol's customer base gets.

What the numbers say

41% vs 26%
Reduction in heavy-drinking days on semaglutide versus placebo in a 26-week trial (published in The Lancet). It also cut drinks per drinking day and alcohol cravings.
62%
Share of GLP-1 users who report drinking less often (Morgan Stanley survey, 2024–25). A University of Colorado study found roughly 50% cuts in drinks per drinking day.
24M
Americans projected to be on GLP-1 drugs by 2035 (Morgan Stanley) — a growing, real-world drag on spending for alcohol, snacks, and restaurants.
"the iceberg"
How Morgan Stanley described GLP-1s to the drinks industry: mild impact today, but potentially severe once adoption climbs and the drugs go generic and cheap.

Why it stacks on top of everything else

This force is independent of the Gen Z shift. Even the older, heavy drinkers the industry counts on to prop up volume can quietly fall away once they start a GLP-1 for their weight or blood sugar. It's a headwind that hits the exact customers least affected by the wellness trend.

Why it's scary for alcohol
The drug is cheap to make at scale and approaching generic availability. Once millions use it routinely for weight loss, not addiction, alcohol loses a huge base almost passively.
The genuine upside
These same drugs are among the most promising treatments ever found for alcohol-use disorder — a real public-health win, whatever it does to sales.
The single biggest worry

Nobody in the drinks business can market against a pill. If GLP-1s become a normal, generic medication taken by tens of millions, the erosion of alcohol demand happens as a quiet side effect — with no campaign that can answer it.

The surprising part

A blockbuster weight-loss drug is inadvertently solving part of alcohol addiction, because the same brain machinery drives hunger, drinking, even smoking. It's the rare case where a product built for one thing accidentally weaponizes itself against an entirely different industry's core revenue. This is the direct link to a sibling collection — the Ozempic Effect — where one drug reshapes a dozen industries at once.

In one sentence

GLP-1 drugs are set to shrink alcohol the same way they're shrinking snacks — by rewiring the brain's reward system that turns cravings on.
