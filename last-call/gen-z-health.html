Group C · Why it's happening

Gen Z & health

Not drinking became a normal, healthy choice — no explanation required.

The single biggest force pulling alcohol down isn't a product or a price — it's a generation. Gen Z simply drinks less than older cohorts did at the same age, and the reason they give is health and wellness. They treat cutting back the way they treat sleep, fitness, and mental health: as self-care, not deprivation.

The core bet

This is the deepest driver behind the whole collection. Gen Z reframed sobriety as a wellness choice — like going to the gym — rather than something weird or a sign of a problem. That quiet cultural permission does more damage to alcohol than any competing drink ever could.

What the numbers say

45%
Share of Gen Z adults (21+) who have never drunk alcohol (NielsenIQ, 2023) — versus 36% of Millennials. Far more of this generation simply never started.
50%
Share of young adults (18–34) who drink at all, July 2025 (Gallup) — down from 72% in 2001–03. Overall US adult drinking hit 54%, a roughly 90-year low.
53%
Share of Americans who now say 1–2 drinks a day is bad for you (Gallup, 2025) — the first time a majority believes this. Nearly two-thirds of young adults agree.
42%
US adults who call themselves "sober curious" (CivicScience, 2024) — up from 33% in 2022. The mindset is going mainstream fast.

Why this one is different

Earlier generations also drank less in their youth and then ramped up in their 30s. The worry for the industry is that a wellness-driven decline behaves differently: if you're not drinking because you think it's unhealthy, you don't suddenly start when you turn 35. The low baseline may stick for life.

Why it's so hard to fight
You can't out-market a values shift. When the reason for not drinking is "it's healthier," no ad campaign or new flavor changes the underlying belief.
Who it helps
Every sober-economy business in Group B — non-alcoholic drinks, cannabis, functional beverages — that gives this generation something to hold instead.
The single biggest worry

If the decline is generational and belief-driven, it doesn't reverse with age. The market damage is already showing — spirits giant Diageo fell roughly 30% in 2025, and global drinks companies shed a reported ~$830B in value.

The surprising part

For the first time it's socially normal to hold a soda at a party and not explain yourself. A generation reframed sobriety as a wellness choice, so the old pressure to drink to fit in largely evaporated. That cultural permission — not any single product — is the real engine. (Honest caveat: one 2026 global industry report claimed a Gen Z rebound and pinned the decline on Boomers instead. The US wellness story is well-supported; the "Gen Z is permanently sober" claim is genuinely contested.)

In one sentence

Gen Z isn't drinking less out of morality or money — they decided not-drinking is healthy and normal, and that quiet permission is doing more damage to alcohol than any rival product could.
