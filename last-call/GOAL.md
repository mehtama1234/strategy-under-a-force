# Last Call — strategy under a force

The sixteenth "strategy under a force" collection (scout-queue: the decline of alcohol / sober-curious).

**The force:** a generational, structural move away from drinking. Gen Z drinks far less than older generations; "sober curious," Dry January, and "damp" drinking are mainstream; new science (and a 2025 US Surgeon General warning) links even moderate drinking to cancer; and GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic) suppress alcohol cravings. For a century, adult socializing defaulted to alcohol — now that default is breaking. We ask: *what did the decline of drinking make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** the default drink stopped being alcohol. For generations, "let's grab a drink" meant booze, and an entire economy — beer giants, bars, wine regions, ad budgets — rested on the unquestioned assumption that adults drink. A younger, health-conscious generation is quietly opting out (helped along by weed and by GLP-1 drugs that mute the urge), and everything built on "everyone drinks" is scrambling. Value flows to whoever serves the sober or moderate drinker (non-alc, cannabis, functional drinks, new "third places"); it drains from the businesses that assumed the tap would never run dry.
