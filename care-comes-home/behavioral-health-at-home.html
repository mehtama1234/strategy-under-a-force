Mental health at home

Therapy moved to the phone and reached millions — then the race for scale cut corners on safety.

Mental health went virtual and mainstream: teletherapy and psychiatry platforms (Talkspace, BetterHelp, Cerebral, Headway, Lyra, Spring Health), employer mental-health benefits, and a flood of demand after COVID plus falling stigma. It's a huge growth area — but messy, with scandals over prescribing controlled substances and over selling users' data.

The core bet

Platforms bet they could combine lower cost with venture speed and scale — turning mental health into a consumer product sold directly to individuals, employers, and insurers. It democratized access, and it commoditized care.

Throughput over safety

The platforms optimized for speed and volume over safety: therapists rushed across many clients with no continuity, psychiatrists prescribing after a single video call, opaque AI matching. When Cerebral was scrutinized, it emerged that borderline-ADHD diagnoses drew stimulant scripts in one call; BetterHelp matched depressed users with underqualified "counselors." A business that stays solvent only by keeping clinician costs low turns therapy into a commodity, not a relationship.

Who ends up ahead

Employer and insurer platforms (Lyra, Spring Health) with aligned payers — and patients in therapy deserts.

Who's exposed

Patients exposed to rushed, unsafe, or data-leaking care, and the trust in the whole category.

The single biggest worry

The core liability is a business model that needs throughput, not outcomes, to stay solvent — so it inevitably cuts corners on clinical safety and consent. Prescribing scandals weren't rogue doctors; they were the system's incentives, and the reputational damage is structural.

The surprising part

The paradox is that teletherapy genuinely works and is more equitable than what it disrupted — trials show video CBT as effective as in-person, and therapy deserts suddenly have access at $60–150/week. Both truths hold at once: the same platforms that democratized access also enabled the fastest race to the bottom, because you can't build a 10× venture return on $60/week therapy without monetizing data or cutting corners somewhere.

In one sentence

Teletherapy made mental health affordable and available to millions — but revealed that venture-scale medicine defaults to optimizing throughput and data extraction over patient safety and privacy.
