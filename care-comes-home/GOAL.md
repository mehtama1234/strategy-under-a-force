# Care Comes Home — strategy under a force

The 37th collection (Tier-4, seeded by the Inc 5000 analysis) — Family I (macro forces).

**Inc 5000 evidence:** "Health & Longevity" is the BIGGEST fast-growing theme — 578 companies, ~$47.6B represented — with the single most repeated growth driver being "aging population → shift from institutional to home-based care," plus value-based care, home infusion, Medicare Advantage, behavioral health, and dental/longevity subthemes.

**The force:** the hospital is moving into the house. Care that used to happen in a hospital, nursing home, or clinic is shifting to the home and the phone — home health and senior care, hospital-at-home, home infusion, house-call primary care, teletherapy, remote monitoring — driven by an aging population, patient preference, and (above all) a payment flip: Medicare Advantage and value-based care now pay providers to keep people healthy and OUT of the expensive hospital. We ask: *what did care-comes-home make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** the money now rewards keeping people out of the hospital — so the hospital came to them. For a century, healthcare was built around the expensive building: you went to the hospital, and everyone got paid more the more you used it. Now the incentive flipped. Insurers (especially Medicare Advantage) pay a fixed amount per patient, so the provider profits by keeping you well and at home, where care is cheaper. That flip — plus a wave of aging boomers who'd rather age in place — turned the home into the new site of care, and spawned a boom in home-care agencies, monitoring tech, and value-based clinics. The catch: the aides who actually do the caring are among the lowest-paid workers in the economy, and "home" quietly shifts cost and burden onto families.
