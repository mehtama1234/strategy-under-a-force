Group B · The payment engine

The healthcare roll-up

Own the whole care chain — from the doctor to the home aide — to capture the Medicare Advantage dollar.

Private equity and giant insurers are buying up the fragmented home-care, home-health, and clinic industry and rolling it up. UnitedHealth/Optum bought the two biggest home-health firms; CVS bought Signify Health and Oak Street Health; PE consolidates local agencies, hospices, and physician groups. The strategy: own the whole care chain to control the patient and the Medicare Advantage dollar.

The core bet

Own the entire patient journey — diagnosis, primary care, home care — so you capture the whole Medicare Advantage dollar instead of paying other providers. Vertical integration (insurer + provider under one roof) is meant to squeeze out middlemen and lock in patient flow.

Rent extraction, not efficiency

Vertical integration hands insurers — who set premiums, deny claims, and pick which providers get paid — the power to steer patients to their own cheaper, in-house providers and pocket the difference. In-house agencies may cut corners (fewer nurse visits, rushed care) against a fixed capitation, and a two-tier system emerges: the well-off flee to independent doctors while MA patients get funneled into stripped-down clinics.

Who ends up ahead

The insurer/PE conglomerates (Optum, CVS) capturing margin across the whole chain.

Who's exposed

Patients steered to in-house care, independent providers squeezed out, and taxpayers if premiums rise anyway.

The single biggest worry

The concern is that consolidation becomes rent extraction rather than efficiency — the giant that owns both the insurance and the care has every reason to keep provider payments low and pocket the "savings" rather than reinvest them, while steering patients where it profits most.

The surprising part

The early evidence is unflattering: despite owning the two biggest home-health firms, UnitedHealth has raised insurance prices, not lowered them — keeping agency reimbursement low and transferring the "savings" to the insurance side, so patients saw no benefit. And CVS turned Signify into a data-driven cross-sell engine rather than a cost-cutter. The roll-up narrative (consolidation = efficiency) collided with reality (consolidation = rent extraction).

In one sentence

Insurers and PE are buying up home care, clinics, and physician groups to own the whole patient and control the Medicare Advantage dollar — but early evidence suggests it raises costs and lowers quality more than it delivers efficiency.
