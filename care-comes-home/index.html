Care Comes Home

The money now rewards keeping people out of the hospital — so the hospital came to them.

The 37th force. For a century, healthcare was built around the expensive building: you went to the hospital, and everyone got paid more the more you used it. Now the incentive flipped. Medicare Advantage and value-based care pay a fixed amount per patient, so the provider profits by keeping you well and at home — where care is cheaper. Add aging boomers who'd rather age in place and technology that makes it safe. The one idea: when the money rewards keeping people out of the hospital, the hospital came to them.

Seeded by the Inc. 5000 data "Health & Longevity" is the single biggest fast-growing theme on the list — 578 companies, ~$47.6B represented — and its most repeated growth driver is "aging population → institutional-to-home care." This collection follows that money.
The one question, asked of everyone

What did care-comes-home make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? The hospital bed became a last resort; prevention and home care became newly profitable; and the aide who does the caring stayed cheap and scarce. The danger: the shift rides on underpaid aides and unpaid families, and the same payment flip that rewards prevention also rewards denying care and gaming the risk scores.
