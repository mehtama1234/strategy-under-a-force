Group D · Attention eats everything

Politics & misinformation — outrage is what holds attention, so it wins

The feed doesn't take sides. It just rewards whatever keeps you watching — and that's conflict.

Engagement-optimizing feeds reward whatever holds attention — and what holds attention best is outrage, conflict, fear, and novelty. So the attention machine, without meaning to, amplifies division, extremes, and misinformation, because calm and nuance don't get clicks. That quietly reshaped politics.

The mechanics

6× faster
How much faster false news spread than true news on Twitter, per a much-cited 2018 MIT study — reaching far more people, because lies were more novel and more shareable.
  • "Flooding the zone": overwhelming the public with a firehose of claims so fact-checkers can't keep up. Provocation is free; correction is slow and costly.
  • Filter bubbles: engagement feeds show people less ideological variety, deepening division.
  • The new escalation: AI-generated content and deepfakes (2023–24 elections) make fakes nearly free to produce.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — a shared reality dissolves

When anyone can generate a convincing fake video in minutes for near-zero cost, and feeds reward engagement over truth, coordinated disinformation becomes the path of least resistance. The idea of a shared set of facts may not survive many more election cycles.

The surprising part

The algorithm isn't biased toward lies — it's neutral by design. But neutrality in an engagement economy is a bias toward outrage: a falsehood wrapped in anger travels farther than a boring truth, because anger holds attention. Nobody sat down and decided to amplify misinformation; it emerged from optimizing a single number (engagement) without weighing the effect on shared reality. Truth is slow, complex, and un-engaging — a structural disadvantage.

In one sentence

Politics & misinformation in a nutshell

The attention economy made outrage the currency of politics and lies cheaper than truth — not by anyone's design, but because a feed that optimizes for engagement structurally rewards whatever holds your eyes, and that's conflict.
