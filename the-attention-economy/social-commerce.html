Group D · Attention eats everything

Social commerce — the feed became the store, and the product finds you

You no longer go to a store or even search. Mid-scroll, the thing you didn't know you wanted appears — and checkout is one tap away.

Shopping merged with entertainment. Instead of going to a store or searching for a product, the product now finds you mid-scroll — an impulse buy triggered by a video in your feed ("TikTok made me buy it"). The gap between content and checkout collapsed to nearly nothing.

The feed is the store

  • TikTok Shop pushed hard into the US (2023+), with live-stream shopping and one-tap checkout inside the app.
  • China pioneered it: Douyin and Taobao live commerce is a much bigger market (tens of billions), with peak nights drawing 100M+ concurrent viewers.
  • Shein and Temu ride cheap algorithmic feeds and fast drops; Temu passed 150M+ monthly users, converting mindless scrolling into checkout at scale.
discovery > search
By ~2025, most purchases on these platforms started from the algorithmic feed, not a search bar. You weren't looking for anything — the feed decided.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Algorithmic impulse-buying at scale erodes agency — especially for young users. The "buy now" button sits a tap away from content engineered to trigger a dopamine hit, while returns are made deliberately hard (Shein, Temu) to protect margins on cheap, disposable stuff.

The surprising part

The whole logic of shopping inverted: discovery replaced search. You're not hunting for a product — the algorithm reads your latent taste and sells you a $15 item styled by an influencer, in the same breath as the entertainment. So retailers no longer compete mainly on findability or even quality; they compete on algorithmic virality and feed placement. A haul video sells more than any product page ever could — because the feed is the storefront and the influencer is the salesperson.

In one sentence

Social commerce in a nutshell

The attention economy collapsed the distance between entertainment and purchase — making discovery, not search, the sales engine, and turning the feed into a store where the product finds you mid-scroll.
