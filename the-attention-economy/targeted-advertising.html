Group A · The attention machine

Targeted advertising — your attention, auctioned in real time

Attention only becomes money once it's measured, targeted, and sold. That's what pays for the "free" internet.

Attention is worthless until someone turns it into money — and targeted advertising is how. A handful of companies (Google, Meta, and increasingly Amazon) turn your behavior into a product sold to advertisers in a real-time auction, millisecond by millisecond. The "free" internet is paid for exactly this way: you are the thing being sold.

The scale and the giants

~$600B+
Global digital ad market (2024), nearly triple 2010. Google, Meta, and Amazon capture most of it.

Two shocks that reshuffled it

  • Apple's privacy switch (2021): App Tracking Transparency let iPhone users block tracking. It cost Meta an estimated ~$10 billion in one year — proof that a single company can reprice the entire ad industry with one setting.
  • Retail media exploded: Amazon's ad business hit ~$40–50B a year, because knowing what you buy is the most valuable targeting data of all. The third-party cookie, the old tracking tool, is dying.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The whole system rests on mass surveillance and behavior-shaping at scale, concentrated in about three companies that own the ad networks, the audiences, and often the products too. If those few get the incentives wrong — or get hit by a shock like Apple's — the market that funds the free internet destabilizes.

The surprising part

The fastest-growing corner isn't the flashiest — it's retail media (Amazon, Walmart, Target selling ads). Not because the ads are more creative, but because they sit closest to the moment of purchase. Someone searching "running shoes" on Google might buy anywhere; someone browsing shoes inside the Amazon app is already in the store. That quietly made the retailer's ad network more valuable than the traditional media network — a reversal the industry didn't see coming so fast.

In one sentence

Targeted advertising in a nutshell

Attention becomes money only when it's measured, targeted, and auctioned in real time — and Apple proved one privacy switch can reprice billions, while Amazon proved that knowing what you buy beats knowing what you search.
