Group A · The attention machine

TikTok & short video — it replaced your friends with an algorithm

The winning product wasn't a network of people you know. It was a machine that reads what you like from how you watch.

For 15 years, social media meant a network of your friends (Facebook, Instagram). TikTok proved something else works better: an algorithm that figures out what you like purely from your behavior — what you watch, how long, what you rewatch — and serves an endless "For You" feed. No friends required. It won so decisively that everyone had to copy it.

The scale and the copycats

1B+ / ~60–90 min
Monthly users, and the time an average user spends per day — far above older social apps. Extreme engagement, built purely on the algorithm.
  • Instagram Reels (Aug 2020) and YouTube Shorts (2020–21) launched explicitly to copy TikTok.
  • Short vertical video (the 9:16 clip) became the default format of the whole internet after 2020.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

One company (China's ByteDance) controls the ranking algorithm for a billion-plus people's information diet, with no transparency into why anything is shown. The US treated this as a national-security threat and passed a 2024 law (the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act) forcing ByteDance to sell TikTok or face a ban — a saga still unresolved.

The surprising part

TikTok won by having fewer social features, not more. It threw out the "social graph" (your friends and followers) and replaced it with pure attention-reading. That inverted the power: a teenager with zero followers gets the same algorithmic shot as a star, because the feed cold-starts everyone from behavior alone. And the irony of the ban: the US only got alarmed once it realized the algorithm actually works — its power was the whole problem.

In one sentence

TikTok & short video in a nutshell

TikTok replaced the friend network with the attention machine — proving that an algorithm reading your behavior beats a network of people you know — and made short vertical video the default medium of the internet.
