Group B · The old media disrupted

Hollywood & the studios — they burned a profitable model to win an unprofitable war

Chasing Netflix, the big studios torched their own two most profitable businesses.

To chase Netflix, the big studios (Disney, Warner, Paramount) poured billions into their own streaming services to grab subscribers — and mostly lost money doing it. Worse, in the process they trained audiences to expect everything cheap and instant at home, which cannibalized their two actually profitable old businesses: theatrical box office and cable/licensing.

The self-inflicted damage

~$50B+
Estimated losses the traditional studios racked up chasing streaming (2019–2024). Disney+ alone lost ~$11B before pivoting toward profit.
  • Box office sits well below pre-2019 levels (US ~$7–8B vs ~$11B in 2019) and hasn't recovered.
  • The 2023 strikes: writers and actors struck for ~148 days — over streaming residuals (they earned far less than on cable) and AI protections.
  • "Peak TV" reversed: scripted originals peaked near ~500/year, then studios slashed spending as the money ran out.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

They've lost control of the audience's expectations. People now expect everything on-demand for ~$10–15 a month, and that's permanent. If streaming never becomes truly profitable at scale, the studios have destroyed their old profit model without building a working new one.

The surprising part

The wound was self-inflicted. Nobody forced the studios to chase Netflix — they chose to. Box office plus cable licensing would have stayed profitable in a streaming world; just smaller. Instead they bet everything on winning the subscriber war, burned billions, crushed their own theatrical windows, and taught audiences to expect cheap-at-home. Now they're cutting shows mid-season and laying off staff — while Netflix, which never owned a studio or a cable network, is the one streamer reliably making money.

In one sentence

Hollywood & the studios in a nutshell

Chasing Netflix, the studios burned their profitable box-office-and-licensing model to win a streaming war that doesn't make money — and there's no clear path back.
