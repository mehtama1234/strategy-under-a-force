# The Attention Economy — strategy under a force

The eighth "strategy under a force" collection (after AI, Ozempic, the US–China decoupling, the end of cheap money, the energy transition, the demographic cliff, and the climate bill).

**The force:** human attention is finite — 24 hours a day, a fixed number of eyeballs — while content and connection have become nearly free and infinite. So an ever-growing army of apps, feeds, and creators fights over the one thing that can't grow: your attention. Algorithmic short-form video ("TikTok-ification") became the dominant format. We ask of each company or sector: *what did the attention economy make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** for most of history the scarce things were physical — land, oil, labor, money. Now making content is nearly free and there's an infinite amount of it, so the one thing that stays fixed is **human attention**. The whole economy reorganizes around capturing it. Two consequences follow. First, because content is infinite, the bottleneck flips from *making* it to *surfacing* it — so **the algorithm (distribution) becomes more valuable than the content itself**. Second, attention is **zero-sum for time**: every minute on TikTok is a minute not on Netflix, the news, a book, or sleep — so the fight isn't for market share, it's for *minutes*, and everything competes with everything. Value flows to whoever captures and holds attention (platforms, algorithms, top creators); it drains from anyone who assumed a captive or paying audience (cable, newspapers, studios). And the dark catch: to win finite attention, everything gets optimized for *engagement* — which rewards outrage, addiction, and extremes, not truth or wellbeing.
