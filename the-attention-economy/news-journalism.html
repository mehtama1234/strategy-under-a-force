Group B · The old media disrupted

News & journalism — demand never fell, the ad money left

People still want the news. What vanished was the advertising that quietly paid for it.

Newspapers were never really funded by news — they were funded by ads (especially local classified ads) bundled with the news. The internet moved that ad money to Google and Facebook, and the news business collapsed even though people still wanted the news. The problem was never demand. It was the disappearing subsidy.

The collapse in numbers

~2,900+
US newspapers that have closed since 2005 — creating "news deserts," whole counties with no local paper at all.
  • Where the ads went: Google and Meta captured 60%+ of US digital ad spending. Classifieds moved to Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace, Zillow.
  • The survivors: a few national brands with direct reader relationships — The New York Times reached ~10 million digital subscribers. Local news, which couldn't build paywalls, was gutted.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — AI takes the last clicks

The last lifeline was search traffic. Now Google's AI answers and "zero-click" results give people the information without ever visiting the news site, and AI can summarize an article without linking to it. The one remaining trickle of traffic — and ad money — is being cut off.

The surprising part

People assume the news died because nobody wants news anymore. The opposite is true: readers still want court records, school-board decisions, local sports, investigations. What disappeared was the local car dealer's ad, the realtor's listing, the grocery flyer — the boring commercial subsidy that paid the reporters. When the internet made those ads global and free, the local newsroom couldn't survive, even with its audience still there and still reading.

In one sentence

News & journalism in a nutshell

The attention economy didn't kill demand for news — it redirected the advertising that funded it to Google and Facebook, gutting local journalism, and now AI answers threaten to take even the last clicks.
