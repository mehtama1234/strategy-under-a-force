Group C · The new attention economy

The streaming wars — they beat cable, then found the enemy was sleep

Netflix won the war against cable, then discovered the real competitor was every other minute of your life.

Netflix beat cable — and then discovered the enemy was never cable. The real fight isn't between streaming services; it's for your finite hours, against YouTube, TikTok, gaming, and sleep. A Netflix leader once said the quiet part out loud: "we compete with sleep." And having killed cable, streaming is now quietly re-becoming cable.

The turning point

Q1 2022
Netflix lost subscribers for the first time in a decade; its stock crashed ~70% over the year. It responded with a cheap ad-supported tier and a password-sharing crackdown.
  • Rivals (Disney+, Max, Peacock, Paramount+) lost billions chasing it; only Netflix reliably makes money.
  • The developed world is now saturated (US streaming penetration ~80%) — every new subscriber is stolen from a rival, not new growth.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — growth ran out

The model depended on new subscribers, and there are no more to get. The only way to grow now is to squeeze existing users — raise prices, add ads, crack down on sharing. Which is exactly the nickel-and-diming that made people flee cable in the first place.

The surprising part

Beating cable turned out to be a curse. By becoming the default, Netflix stopped being the insurgent and became the incumbent — and incumbents optimize instead of innovate. So streaming is now racing downmarket (cheap ad tiers) and upmarket (bundles, price hikes, lock-in) at once — re-creating the ads, bundles, and price creep of cable. Cable didn't lose because streaming was better; it lost because it was the only option and streaming looked revolutionary. Now streaming is the only option — so its flaws show.

In one sentence

The streaming wars in a nutshell

Streaming's real enemy was never cable but the infinite competition for your minutes — so, out of subscribers to win, the services are re-adopting cable's ugliest habits and slowly becoming the thing they replaced.
