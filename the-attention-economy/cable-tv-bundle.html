Group B · The old media disrupted

Cable TV & the bundle — the thing everyone hated was secretly brilliant

You paid for 200 channels to watch 10. It felt like a rip-off. It was actually a clever machine.

For decades, cable sold one giant bundle — hundreds of channels for a single monthly fee. Everyone complained. But it worked because of a hidden trick: everyone who wanted ESPN also had to pay for the dozens of channels they never watched, so a few must-have channels quietly funded all the rest. Streaming let people finally pick à la carte — and the whole machine collapsed.

The collapse

~105M → ~69M
US pay-TV households, from the ~2010 peak to 2025 — a loss of more than a third. Cable's household reach fell from ~88% to under 35%.

Rising sports-rights costs made the bill balloon (toward ~$125/month), forcing non-sports viewers to subsidize live games — and they left.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the death spiral repeats

Unbundling gave people choice but fragmented their viewing across many apps, and total spending often went up, not down. Now the industry is quietly re-bundling — the Disney Bundle, Netflix + Max deals, carrier packages — recreating the exact cross-subsidy cable ran, just with better marketing.

The surprising part

The bundle everyone loved to hate was economically brilliant — and it forced affordability. By cross-subsidizing hundreds of niche channels with sports, it kept the average bill manageable. Streaming promised cheaper à la carte, but juggling five or six subscriptions made most households pay more than one cable bill. And now the streamers are openly bundling again — quietly admitting the bundle was the right business model all along.

In one sentence

Cable TV & the bundle in a nutshell

The hated cable bundle was a clever cross-subsidy that kept TV affordable; streaming unbundled it, fragmented viewing, quietly made everything cost more — and the industry is now re-bundling the very thing it destroyed.
