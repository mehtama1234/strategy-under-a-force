Group C · The new attention economy

Music & streaming — the skip button reshaped the songs themselves

Streaming rescued the music industry's money — then quietly rewrote what a song sounds like.

Streaming (Spotify) rescued music revenue from the piracy collapse — but it pays artists fractions of a cent per stream, so the whole business tilted toward scale and virality. TikTok became the place hits are "broken." And here's the fascinating part: the format didn't just change how songs are found — it changed the songs themselves.

The economics flipped

~$0.003–0.004
What an artist earns per Spotify stream — roughly a million streams to make ~$3,000. Streaming is now ~84% of US recorded-music revenue, but paid per-stream, not per-album.

TikTok now breaks hits: a 15-second clip goes viral, and the song climbs the charts — inverting the old radio-to-charts pipeline (as with "Blinding Lights," "Levitating").

The surprising part

The format changed the art

Producers now optimize for a 15-second TikTok loop and the skip button before they think about a full song. So the average pop track got shorter (~3:00, down from ~3:30), intros got cut, and the hook moved to the very front — "the skip economy." In the album era, artists controlled the whole arc; now the distribution format dictates the composition itself.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the missing middle

Per-stream pay is too thin for emerging artists to live on, so only massive scale or sync licensing (TV/film/ads) pays the bills. The "middle class" of working musicians — the tier that once lived off album sales plus touring — got hollowed out by the math. Meanwhile old catalog songs dominate streams, and investors buy song catalogs (Dylan ~$300M, Springsteen ~$500M) as passive-income assets.

In one sentence

Music & streaming in a nutshell

Streaming saved music's revenue but pays artists in pennies and handed hit-making power to TikTok — and in response the songs themselves got shorter, hookier, and front-loaded, reshaped by the skip button and the 15-second clip.
