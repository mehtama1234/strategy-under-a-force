Group C · The new attention economy

Creators & influencers — being watched became a tradeable asset

When attention is the scarce resource, whoever reliably captures it becomes a media company of one.

Once attention became the scarce thing, the people who can reliably capture it — YouTubers, TikTokers, streamers — became genuinely valuable: a new kind of media company of one. Brands moved ad money from TV to them. But it's a brutal, winner-take-all world with no safety net.

The new industry

~$100B → ~$250B
Estimated size of the "creator economy," roughly 2022 to 2024, as brands shifted budgets from TV to influencers. MrBeast, the apex creator, earned ~$54M in 2023.

Platforms pay "creator funds" to stop talent defecting — but the payouts are deliberately tiny (TikTok's fund pays a few cents per 1,000 views). The fund is a leash, not a gift.

The brutal math

The single biggest risk — total platform dependence

Creators are companies of one with no employer, no benefits, no safety net — and total dependence on an algorithm they don't control. A demonetization wave, a shadowban, or a quiet ranking change can zero their income overnight, with no severance and no appeal.

top 1% ≈ 90%
Roughly the share of creator-economy money earned by the top sliver, while the vast majority earn less than minimum wage. A pure power law — and burnout is rampant.

The surprising part

A 20-something with a phone can now move more product than a multi-million-dollar TV ad — not because they're better marketers, but because audiences read them as real rather than polished, and that perceived authenticity is the most valuable real estate in attention. Yet the platforms keep paying creators pennies, precisely to keep the leverage and stop them leaving. The talent is essential; the pay is deliberately thin.

In one sentence

Creators & influencers in a nutshell

The attention economy turned being reliably watched into a valuable asset — but also created a precarious new labor class with no employer, no safety net, brutal inequality, and total dependence on an algorithm that can erase them overnight.
