Group D · The mechanism & the moves

Integrate or commoditize

When the profit is about to move to the next layer, you either capture that layer yourself — or you get left as the cheap middle.

If profit is migrating, the incumbent's move is to chase it: integrate backward into the scarce supplier (Apple building its own chips) or forward into the high-margin adjacent business (Amazon into cloud, ads, logistics) — before a competitor locks in that control point and leaves you stranded in the commoditized middle. It's the aggressive counter-move to value migration.

The core bet

Integration is the nuclear option against commoditization: expensive, slow, and risky, but the alternative is watching rivals seize the high-margin layers while you compete on commodity specs at 2% margins. The catch is that it only works if you already have the scale to pull it off.

It only works if you're already winning

The paradox: integration only pays if you already have pricing power or scale upstream — otherwise you've just moved the bottleneck and burned cash. Apple can demand good terms from TSMC because it moves 80 million units a year; a startup integrating into semiconductors just loses to rivals using ready-made parts. So the real race is to grab the control point before the layer commoditizes — once it has, integrating is too late.

Who ends up rich

Incumbents with existing scale who capture the next scarce layer early — Apple, Amazon, Tesla, Netflix.

Who gets thin margins

Firms that integrate too late, too small, or into the wrong technology — stranding capital while nimbler rivals iterate.

The single biggest worry

It's expensive and slow. A chip fab takes 3–5 years to reach volume, and betting on the wrong battery chemistry or chip design can strand billions. Competitors buying best-of-breed outsourced parts move faster and cheaper — so a mistimed integration can sink the very company trying to defend itself.

The surprising part

The threat of value migration forces you to integrate, but you can only do it profitably if you're already winning — which means the decisive move has to happen before the layer commoditizes. By the time the migration is obvious to everyone, the profitable window to capture the next layer has usually closed.

In one sentence

Vertical integration is the nuclear option against value migration — expensive and risky — but the alternative is watching competitors lock in the high-margin layers while you're left competing on commodity specs at 2% margins.
