Group C · The new bottlenecks

The AI stack

Every AI lab races to build the smartest model — and the profit keeps pooling in the one thing they all have to buy: the chips.

In the AI boom, the household names are the model-makers — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta. But the money is pooling somewhere else: with Nvidia, which sells the chips, and the cloud giants, which rent the compute. They capture most of AI's economic surplus because compute is the one non-substitutable bottleneck, and everyone upstream is fighting in a market that commoditizes by the month.

The core bet

The chip and the cloud account are the layers with real scarcity, so profit pools there. Model-makers operate upstream in a fast-commoditizing market where cheaper inference and open models compress margins toward zero — the same pattern as Wintel and Android, where the money settled in the irreplaceable layer.

The labs subsidize the chipmaker

Here's the twist: OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta are among Nvidia's largest customers — they effectively subsidize its growth by buying chips they can't yet profitably run. The "picks and shovels" sellers carry almost no product risk: demand is inelastic and arrives from every lab at once. Model-makers, by contrast, compete on cost and have no durable moat — a cheaper or open model can undercut them overnight.

Who ends up rich

The scarce layers — Nvidia's chips and the cloud giants' compute, where demand is inelastic and switching costs highest.

Who gets thin margins

The model-makers, burning billions to own a layer that commoditizes faster than they can monetize it.

The single biggest worry

If inference costs keep collapsing and models converge on similar benchmark performance, model-makers lose any moat and become bill-passers for cloud compute. Open models (Llama, Qwen, Mistral) already undercut proprietary ones — the drift toward free is structural, not a passing phase.

The surprising part

The companies everyone calls the AI leaders are, financially, Nvidia's customers — losing money buying chips to chase a commodity layer, while the chipmaker and the clouds collect the surplus. History rhymes: value pools where switching costs are highest, and right now that's the silicon and the server, not the model.

In one sentence

In the AI stack, profit pools in the only layers with real scarcity — chips and cloud compute — while model-makers burn billions trying to own a layer that commoditizes faster than they can charge for it.
