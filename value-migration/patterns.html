The synthesis · what repeats across all 13

The patterns

Line up thirteen industries where the profit left the maker, and the same handful of mechanics repeat every time.

Each page asks: what did value migration make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

Making the thing became cheap and interchangeable almost everywhere — so the profit drained off the maker and pooled at whatever layer stayed scarce: access, distribution, the standard, the tool, the loan, the customer relationship. Value creation and profit capture came apart. You can create enormous value and keep almost none of it, because profit rewards scarcity, not effort.

The recurring patterns

Pattern 01

Profit rewards scarcity, not effort

Two firms in the same chain work equally hard; one earns 60% margins and the other 8%, purely because one does something that can't be copied. The money is never in doing the thing better — it's in doing the thing nobody else can do yet.

conservation of profitssmiling curve
Pattern 02

The maker gets commoditized; the money moves one layer over

Generics, PC assembly, car manufacturing, factory work — the visible, capital-heavy job of making the product is exactly where the margin isn't.

pharmahardwarecars
Pattern 03

Cutting a middleman grows a bigger one

Disintermediation rarely ends with no middleman. Kill the label and the streaming platform takes over; the promised liberation installs a new, more powerful gatekeeper.

musicpublishingretail
Pattern 04

The scarce layer is often invisible

The most powerful firm in a chain is frequently one nobody's heard of — the machine-maker behind the chip-maker, the PBM behind the pharmacy, the design-software duopoly behind every chip.

chips & toolsPBMs
Pattern 05

Whoever owns the customer and the data wins

Control of the customer relationship, the transaction data, and discovery beats control of the product. The platform can then read the data and clone the maker.

marketplacesattention
Pattern 06

The non-swappable layer keeps the margin

Any two products with the same standard inside are interchangeable, so buyers pick the cheapest — and profit flees to the one part that can't be swapped: the OS, the chip, the instruction set.

Wintelthe AI stack
Pattern 07

Each cycle runs faster

Cloud commoditized infrastructure over five years; AI is commoditizing models in one or two. The runway between "scarce and profitable" and "cheap and worthless" keeps shrinking.

models deflatingspotting it
Pattern 08

The counter-move is to move first, and shrink what you defend

Integrate into the next scarce layer before it locks up — but only if you already have scale. Winners abandon the commoditizing layer instead of defending it.

The point of the series

This is the 27th collection, and a structural pattern like the toll-booth economy and locked-in. But where those describe one company's deliberate playbook, value migration is what happens to the whole market as competition plus time move the profit around. It's the deep current under the store is the brand (the retailer captures the maker's margin) and everyone's an ad business (attention becomes the scarce layer). Same method every time: pick a force, ask what it made scarce/cheap/risky/valuable, group by the answer, read the patterns.
