Publishing → platforms

The newspaper still pays the reporters and chases the story — and Google and Meta collect most of the ad money it attracts.

Publishers and newspapers used to own the scarce thing: printing presses and distribution. Now the scarce thing is attention, and the platforms own it. Google and Meta capture the ad revenue that news reporting attracts; Amazon owns the customer relationship for books; TikTok owns book discovery. The people who create the content became commodity suppliers to the platforms that distribute it.

The core bet

When distribution stopped being scarce and attention became scarce, the profit moved from the maker of content to the owner of the audience. The publisher still bears the expensive part — the reporting — while the platform collects the paycheck the reporting generates.

The work and the paycheck split apart

This is the exact reversal of the 1990s. Back then publishers owned the scarcity — presses and distribution — so they kept the money. Now platforms own the scarcity — audience attention — so they keep it. The newspaper still does the costly reporting; Google and Meta collect 60%+ of the ad revenue that reporting draws. BookTok sells books it was never paid to sell, keeping the one asset that matters: the engagement data.

Who ends up rich

Platforms that own distribution and attention — Amazon, Google, Meta, TikTok.

Who gets thin margins

Writers, publishers, and journalists — now commodity suppliers feeding the discovery machine.

The single biggest worry

Publishers have no counter-move. Readers follow algorithms, not mastheads, and platforms have zero incentive to share ad or data value. The producer of the expensive, verified content becomes an interchangeable input to a system that captures all the economics.

The surprising part

The newspaper pays the salaries, chases the leads, and fact-checks the story — and the platform that merely shows it next to an ad keeps most of the money. The value still flows from writer to reader, but a toll-taker sits in the middle who did none of the work.

In one sentence

Value migrated from the people who create content to the platforms that own distribution and monetize attention — leaving creators doing the expensive work for a shrinking share.
