Group D · The mechanism & the moves

Spotting the next migration

There's one diagnostic question that finds where the money is about to move: what just became cheap for everyone?

If profit always flees to the next scarce layer, the practical skill is seeing the shift early — recognizing when a layer flips from scarce-and-defensible to abundant-and-interchangeable, then spotting which bottleneck customers will pay for next. Winners move early; losers defend yesterday's margin until it's gone.

The core bet

The one diagnostic question is: what just became commodity-cheap for everyone? That's where the margin just fled from. Then ask who owns the customer lock-in and the hardest-to-copy logic in the layer above — because that's where the profit is about to pool.

What the numbers say

cloud → AI
once compute and storage got cheap, value moved up to the models and workflows that orchestrate them (2024+).
SaaS → outcome
as per-seat software matured and switching costs fell, margin moved to owning the workflow you can't exit.
EV → autonomy
batteries and motors are commoditizing across 50+ makers; the next margin is autonomy software, charging, and data.
bank → API
fintechs (Stripe, Square, Wise) commoditized payment rails and captured the merchant relationship directly.
publisher → first-party data
ad inventory got commoditized; margin pooled at the targeting layer and first-party data.

Winners shrink what they defend

The counterintuitive move: winners often narrow the scope they defend. Tesla shrank from "make the best car" to "own autonomy and energy." Stripe shrank from "become a bank" to "own the payment API." They abandon the commoditizing layer and pour capital into what's next — while losers keep trying to harvest margin from a layer ten rivals are already underpricing.

Who ends up rich

Firms that spot the cheapening early and reposition to the next lock-in layer above.

Who gets thin margins

Firms holding the old asset too long, that don't see the shift until 70% of the margin is already gone.

The single biggest worry

The trap is moving too early to the next margin while still clinging to the old asset. Companies that try to own both (print plus digital, network plus apps) burn cash on the transition and get outflanked by focused pure-plays. The real danger is not seeing the shift until most of your margin has already left.

The surprising part

The single most useful question isn't "what's growing?" — it's "what just got cheap and modular for everyone?" That's the layer the margin just abandoned. Follow it one step up to whoever owns the customer and the hardest-to-copy logic, and you've found where the profit is about to be.

In one sentence

Spot the next migration by finding the layer that just became cheap for everyone, then asking who owns the customer lock-in and hardest-to-copy logic one layer up — because that's where the profit is about to move.
