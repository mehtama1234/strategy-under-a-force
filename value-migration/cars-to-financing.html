Group A · The profit leaves the maker

Cars → financing & the dealer

Ford's lending arm often out-earns the entire business of building cars — the vehicle is close to a break-even hook.

Building a car earns a razor-thin margin — sometimes near zero. Lending the buyer the money to buy it, servicing it, and selling it software earns far more. So the modern carmaker is really a consumer-finance company with a factory attached: it sells the metal close to cost to hook you into the high-margin relationship that follows.

The core bet

Manufacturing a mid-market car is a low-single-digit-margin business; the captive finance arm earns 15–20% returns on its loan book, the dealer's service bay runs 40–50% margins, and software subscriptions are almost pure profit. The profit durably left the factory floor for the lending book and the service lane.

What the numbers say

~$2.7B
Ford Credit's net income in 2021 — about 40% of Ford's pre-tax profit, from a standalone lending operation.
$1,500–2,000
the dealer's finance-and-insurance profit per vehicle — often more than the maker's per-unit gross profit.
40–50% vs 15–25%
service-department margins vs new-vehicle-sale margins — the money is in the bay, not the showroom.
~$2.2B
Tesla's annualized software revenue (2023) — selling the car near cost, monetizing FSD and connectivity after.
~60%
share of US auto loans held by captive lenders — the maker is quietly a giant bank.

Wall Street prices the wrong company

In many years a Ford Credit or GM Financial out-earns the entire manufacturing division — yet the market still values the parent as a carmaker, not as a $150-billion-plus lender. Functionally these are finance companies with a production supply chain bolted on. Tesla's model — near-cost car, monetize software and charging — is now collapsing even that arbitrage from the other side.

Who ends up rich

Captive lenders, dealer finance-and-service departments, and software-subscription platforms.

Who gets thin margins

The manufacturing division itself — the visible, capital-heavy, people-heavy part that builds the actual car.

The single biggest worry

The whole profit stack bets that mechanical failure and financing friction outlast the customer. If EVs make cars far more reliable, warranty and service work — up to 40% of dealer gross profit — collapses, and captive lenders take losses if used-car values crater.

The surprising part

Open a Ford Credit or GM Financial earnings report and you'll often find the lending arm earning more than the company's entire car-building operation for the year. The automaker is, on the numbers, a consumer-finance company that happens to also manufacture the collateral.

In one sentence

Carmakers became financiers and subscription platforms because lending on and servicing a vehicle earns three-to-ten times more than the profit of building and selling it.
