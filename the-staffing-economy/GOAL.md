# The Staffing Economy — strategy under a force

The 36th collection (Tier-4, seeded by the Inc 5000 analysis) — Family I (macro forces).

**Inc 5000 evidence that seeded it:** "Staffing & Workforce" is a top theme — 351 fast-growing companies, ~$28B represented — plus the recurring growth-driver "talent shortage → flexible staffing models," and subpatterns for general staffing, healthcare staffing, security staffing, tech/IT staffing, PEO/HR outsourcing, recruiting, and BPO. Labor scarcity is measurably one of the biggest engines pulling money on the list.

**The force:** chronic labor shortages — an aging workforce, fewer young workers, skills gaps, and jobs people won't take — turned "getting the work done" into a huge business. When you can't hire, you rent the workers (staffing agencies, travel nurses), outsource the whole function (PEOs, business-process outsourcing), or automate the work no one will staff. We ask: *what did the labor shortage make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** when you can't hire the people, renting them — or the whole function — became the business. For decades labor was abundant and cheap, so companies barely thought about it. Now the worker is the bottleneck, and a fast-growing industry sits between the work and the people who do it: temp and travel-nurse agencies, PEOs that run small firms' payroll and HR, outsourcers, gig platforms, and automation that fills the shifts no one wants. The middleman between worker and work is where the money pools — and the worker often keeps the least of it.
