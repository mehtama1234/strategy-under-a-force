The Staffing Economy

When you can't hire the people, renting them — or the whole function — became the business.

The 36th force. For decades labor was abundant and cheap, so companies barely thought about it. Now the worker is the bottleneck, and a fast-growing industry sits between the work and the people who do it: temp and travel-nurse agencies, security guards, PEOs that run small firms' payroll, outsourcers, gig apps, and automation that fills the shifts no one wants. The one idea: when labor became the bottleneck, the middleman between worker and work became the growth business.

Seeded by the Inc. 5000 data "Staffing & Workforce" is a top theme on the fastest-growing-companies list — 351 companies, ~$28B represented — with the recurring growth driver "talent shortage → flexible staffing." This collection follows that money.
The one question, asked of everyone

What did the labor shortage make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? The worker became the scarce input; the matchmaker who connects worker to work became newly valuable; and cheap, abundant labor became a memory. The danger: the middleman keeps a durable cut while the worker often takes the haircut — and AI now threatens to replace the very work staffing was built on.
