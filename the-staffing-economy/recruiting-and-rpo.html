Group B · Outsourcing the function

Recruiting & search

Finding the right person is so hard that a whole industry charges 20–30% of a salary to do it for you.

Finding the right person is hard enough that a whole industry sells it: contingency recruiters (paid ~20–30% of first-year salary per hire), executive search firms (Korn Ferry, Heidrick & Struggles), RPO firms that run a company's entire hiring function, and LinkedIn as the dominant talent-data platform. In a tight market, matchmaking between employer and worker is a premium service.

The core bet

The bet is that finding good talent is expensive, risky, and knowledge-intensive, so employers will pay a fifth to a third of a hire's salary — or six figures for executives — rather than build the capability or bear the hiring risk themselves.

What the numbers say

20–30%
a contingency recruiter's fee (first-year salary); executive search runs 30–35% or $100k–400k+ flat.
~$2.7B
Korn Ferry's revenue — the largest pure-play executive-search firm.
~$15B+ · ~1B
LinkedIn's recruiting-tool revenue and its member base — the talent-data platform underneath it all.
~$8–10B
the RPO market, growing ~15–20% a year as enterprises outsource entire hiring functions.
$15–30k+
the true all-in cost per hire (time, false hires, turnover) versus $4–7k in direct costs.

Move up the value chain

Executive-search firms flipped from talent-placement into organizational-strategy consultants who happen to place people — culture audits, leadership assessment, succession planning, board advisory. They realized a single C-suite placement is lumpy, high-stakes revenue, so they command 2–3× the fee by selling the whole decision architecture around the hire, not just the introduction. They moved up-market by making the problem bigger.

Who ends up ahead

Executive-search and RPO firms that own the relationship and market data — and LinkedIn, taxing the whole field.

Who's exposed

Contingency recruiters selling "we have the network," as AI and in-house teams commoditize the search.

The single biggest worry

The industry is exposed to disintermediation: enterprises build internal recruiting teams, AI matches candidates automatically, and LinkedIn commoditizes the "proprietary network." A mid-market firm can now stand up a 5-person in-house team for less than a year of contingency fees — so the "we have the network and data" pitch is eroding fast.

The surprising part

The tell is that the survivors stopped selling placements. Korn Ferry and its peers reframed themselves as advisors on leadership and organization, where the fee is bigger and stickier — an admission that the pure matchmaking service is being automated and in-sourced away beneath them.

In one sentence

Recruiting made finding talent expensive and valuable, but AI, in-house teams, and LinkedIn are eroding that scarcity — so the survivors are moving from placement to strategy consulting.
