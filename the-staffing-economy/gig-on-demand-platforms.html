Group C · The automation escape

Gig & on-demand apps

Apps turned labor into an on-demand utility — infinitely fast, because infinitely disposable.

Apps turned labor into an on-demand utility: Uber and Lyft (driving), DoorDash and Instacart (delivery), and a wave of shift-work apps (Instawork, Wonolo, ShiftKey, Gigpro) that let warehouses, restaurants, and hospitals fill individual shifts from a phone. Workers are classified as independent contractors, not employees — cheaper and more flexible for the platform, but with no benefits or protections for the worker.

The core bet

By classifying workers as contractors, platforms avoid payroll tax, benefits, and labor law, and get unlimited, flexible supply at lower cost. The model works because supply (people needing cash) exceeds demand, so prices stay low and no one can bargain.

Speed requires desperation

Gig platforms succeeded not because they're cheaper for the customer — a $30 delivery is often subsidized by venture capital — but because they're cheaper for the platform, which externalizes cost and risk onto workers and uses algorithmic control to keep supply abundant and wages low. What's genuinely new is speed: a driver in 4 minutes, a shift filled in 2 hours. But that speed requires a pool of people with no better option.

Who ends up ahead

The platforms — which get flexible, cheap, abundant labor and keep a 20–30% take of every transaction.

Who's exposed

Gig workers — no minimum wage, no unemployment, no benefits, and income that collapses when demand drops.

The single biggest worry

The contractor model makes income catastrophically fragile: no minimum wage, no unemployment if demand falls, no healthcare if injured, and an algorithm that can deactivate an account overnight with no recourse. As workers flood each market, per-task pay falls, forcing more jobs for the same income — burnout and vehicle wear compound with no employer liability behind any of it.

The surprising part

The dirty secret is that the speed depends on desperation — only workers with no other option accept those wages, which is why platforms grew fastest where economic opportunity was thinnest. "Flexibility" sold as freedom is often experienced as helplessness, and the model made labor infinitely fast precisely by making it infinitely disposable.

In one sentence

Gig platforms made labor infinitely cheap and infinitely fast by making it infinitely disposable — externalizing all the cost and risk onto the worker.
