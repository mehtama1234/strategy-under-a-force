Group B · Outsourcing the function

Business-process outsourcing

Hand whole functions — support, back office, claims — to cheaper labor abroad. Then AI came for exactly that work.

Rather than hire staff, companies hand whole functions — customer support call centers, back-office processing, IT help desks, claims, moderation — to outsourcers, often offshore (India, the Philippines). It's a ~$300B+ global industry (Teleperformance, Concentrix, Genpact, TCS). Now AI threatens to automate the very call-center and back-office work BPO was built on, and the industry is scrambling.

The core bet

Companies moved support and back-office work to cheaper labor markets to cut costs 40–70% while keeping quality acceptable. The model worked because human labor was the bottleneck — talk to customers, type data, handle claims. AI can now do much of that for pennies, threatening the entire wage-arbitrage.

The arbitrage evaporates

The industry's whole foundation — that human labor is cheaper offshore than onshore — evaporates once AI handles 60–70% of inbound calls, simple claims, and first-line IT triage. A single trained AI agent can handle 500–1,000 interactions a day versus 50–100 for a human, at ~80% less than even Indian labor, with no training, turnover, or benefits. For vendors that's years of shrinking contracts; for 2M+ offshore workers it's direct displacement.

Who ends up ahead

Vendors that pivot to complex, regulated work — and, short-term, the AI providers eating the routine tier.

Who's exposed

Routine-work BPO vendors, and the millions of offshore call-center and back-office workers facing displacement.

The single biggest worry

AI eating the core call-center and back-office work is the existential threat: routine support, simple claims, and data entry — the high-volume bread-and-butter — are exactly what large language models do cheaply. The industry bifurcates, and the low-complexity half gets clobbered while retraining pipelines in India and the Philippines lag badly.

The surprising part

The survival play isn't "we'll become AI vendors" — capital there is brutal. It's that compliance, fraud, and regulated disputes resist full automation: a human must legally approve a denied insurance claim or take liability for a bank escalation. So vendors specializing in regulatory-heavy work may grow more valuable as AI screens the easy cases and hands humans the hard, high-value ones. The industry doesn't vanish — it splits.

In one sentence

BPO bet everything on geographic wage arbitrage — but AI just made geography irrelevant and labor optional, leaving only the complex, regulated, judgment-call work viable as human-outsourced.
