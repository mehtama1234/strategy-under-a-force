Group C · The automation escape

AI agents as labor

The newest twist: don't rent a worker — subscribe to an AI that does the job and never quits.

The newest twist on the staffing economy flips it on its head: startups selling AI "digital workers" that do back-office and support jobs outright — AI customer-support agents (Sierra, Decagon, Intercom Fin), AI sales/RevOps reps, AI coding and accounting assistants — priced per-task or per-seat and undercutting a human salary. The pitch: don't rent a worker, subscribe to an AI that never quits.

The core bet

AI agents do the work rather than assist it — replacing junior staff, not just helping them. The math is stark: cents per resolution or a few hundred dollars a month per "seat," versus $40–60k for an entry-level human doing the same high-volume work.

The junior rung vanishes quietly

The biggest disruption isn't customer support (where the brand suffers if AI fumbles) — it's junior back-office jobs disappearing silently: accounting reconciliation, invoice processing, junior underwriting, expense approvals. These are pure data-shuffling, automated so quietly that managers stop posting entry-level roles at all — draining the pipeline that once turned associates into senior experts.

Who ends up ahead

AI-agent vendors and the firms that replace headcount with software that scales and never churns.

Who's exposed

Entry-level workers and new grads — the classic back-office bootcamp rung is drying up faster than the rest.

The single biggest worry

The reliability floor is still shaky. AI excels at routine, high-volume work but fails on edge cases and judgment calls — and when it fails, the escalation lands on a thinner human team handling only the hardest problems with no ramp-up, a "failure spiral." Baked-in policy mistakes can also cascade across thousands of tickets before anyone notices.

The surprising part

The second-order cost is hidden: by automating the junior rung, firms lose the pipeline of new hires who'd become senior subject-matter experts in five years — so they end up overpaying for scarce mid-career hires later. The staffing economy's newest product doesn't just replace a worker; it quietly removes the bottom of the career ladder.

In one sentence

AI agents are making entry-level back-office labor obsolete almost overnight — hiding the cost in a collapsing career ladder and the growing complexity of managing failures humans barely see.
