Group A · Renting the workers

Guards & warehouses

Private security guards now outnumber police roughly two to one — and someone has to staff the warehouses too.

Two huge, unglamorous staffing markets. Physical security — private guards (Allied Universal, GardaWorld, Securitas) — now outnumbers police in many places, a ~$40–50B US industry growing on crime fear, retail theft, and "safety as a destination." And industrial/warehouse temp labor powers e-commerce fulfillment and reshored factories. Both are low-margin, high-volume, people-heavy businesses booming on labor scarcity.

The core bet

Staffing firms win by being the labor buffer — absorbing the hiring churn, training cost, and brutal turnover that security firms, warehouses, and factories can't absorb themselves. In a labor shortage, being nimble at hiring beats being cheap.

Automation didn't kill it

Both markets grew partly because automation failed to displace the human. A badge and a uniform deter crime by presence alone — no camera does that, which is why shrink rose despite ubiquitous CCTV. Warehouses could automate more, but human labor stays flexible (scale up for peak, down in the trough) and better at safety judgment. The staffing firm's margin survives on being nimbler at hiring than anyone can be in-house.

Who ends up ahead

Big security and industrial-staffing firms that master high-volume hiring churn at scale.

Who's exposed

The guards and warehouse temps (low pay, high turnover), and clients exposed if a bad hire breaks an SLA.

The single biggest worry

Turnover is the hidden cancer: replacing an entire guard force every five months means constant training cost, lost customer familiarity, and hiring friction. The firm survives only by hiring faster and cheaper than the client could — an edge that erodes as labor tightens, and one bad hire or a failed background check mid-contract can break the contract and tank confidence.

The surprising part

These are the least glamorous corners of the staffing economy and among the most durable, precisely because the work is physical, human-presence-dependent, and turnover-heavy — exactly the combination that keeps a nimble labor buffer in business. The margin isn't in being smart; it's in being fast at churn nobody else wants to manage.

In one sentence

Guarding and warehouse staffing grew because labor became the constraint, automation couldn't replace human presence, and staffing firms captured the margin by being nimbler at hiring churn than anyone in-house.
