Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

The worker's side

The shortage handed workers some leverage — but the middleman captured most of the gain, and precarity rose.

The honest counterweight. In a labor shortage workers gained some leverage — higher wages, the confidence to quit a bad job — but the staffing economy also has a dark side for them: temp and gig workers often get no benefits, no security, and less than the client pays for them; contractor misclassification strips protections; the middleman captures much of the wage gain; and "flexibility" can mean unpredictable income.

The core bet

The staffing economy promised flexibility and quick work, but often delivered precarity and wage capture. Workers briefly gained leverage to demand more, but the structural gains flowed mainly to platforms and agencies — leaving many with volatile income, no benefits, and legal ambiguity about whether they're even employees.

The haircut they don't see

Workers traded job security for speed. A traditional job means a known schedule and legal recourse; in the gig model an algorithm can cut you off overnight, lower your ratings for a dispute you didn't cause, or make your shifts vanish. You absorb all the volatility — income, scheduling, deactivation risk — while the platform keeps the pricing power and, in most states, you have no unemployment cushion.

Who ends up ahead

Workers with scarce, hard-to-automate skills who can genuinely command more — and organized labor where it revived.

Who's exposed

Temp, gig, and contract workers absorbing the volatility, the missing benefits, and the middleman's cut.

The single biggest worry

The flexibility sold as freedom is often experienced as helplessness: the worker bears the income swings, deactivation risk, and lack of protections, while the intermediary keeps the relationship, the data, and the pricing power. When income dries up, there's usually no unemployment insurance and no employer cushion behind them.

The surprising part

The wage gains of 2021–22 were partly an illusion — driven more by stimulus and benefits running out than by durable labor power. Once that faded, low-wage pay flatlined or fell (especially in gig work) while the middleman kept the pricing power. The truly scarce thing turned out to be not workers but predictable income — and only a traditional employer offers that.

In one sentence

Workers gained short-term wage leverage in the shortage but lost long-term security, benefits, income predictability, and legal protection to middlemen who captured most of the structural gains.
