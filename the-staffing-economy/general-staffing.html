Group A · Renting the workers

General staffing

Buy labor wholesale from workers, sell it retail to employers, and keep the spread — a ~$200B machine.

When employers can't hire fast enough, they rent workers by the day, week, or project through staffing agencies — Adecco, Randstad, ManpowerGroup, and thousands of smaller specialist firms. The agency finds, screens, and employs the worker, bills the client a markup, and pockets the difference. US staffing is a roughly $200B-a-year industry that booms when labor is tight.

The core bet

Staffing agencies buy labor wholesale (hiring and paying the worker, handling admin) and sell it retail to companies with urgent needs — a financial arbitrage of human availability and employer desperation. They make money on the spread between the pay rate and the bill rate.

What the numbers say

18–28%

~$80B+

<4%

It lives on chronic dysfunction

Agencies don't mainly profit from dramatic emergencies — they profit from chronic organizational dysfunction: companies that underfund their own recruiting, outsource hiring to dodge compliance risk, or use temps to test-drive workers without committing to salary or severance. The steadiest margin comes from predictable recurring demand (call centers, warehouses, seasonal retail), not one-off panics.

Who ends up ahead

Staffing agencies — capturing 15–30% of the wage as the price of outsourced recruitment, compliance, and churn.

Who's exposed

Employers who pay the markup, and the temp workers who take home less than the client is billed.

The single biggest worry

The business is radically cyclical: nearly all the profit comes from the tight-labor window, and revenue collapses the moment employers slow hiring. A recession or hiring freeze can wipe out 40–60% of temp placements almost overnight — companies cut temporary roles first — while the agency's fixed costs shrink slowly, destroying margin.

The surprising part

The model wins only when both sides stay fluid and anxious. If a company keeps a temp too long or converts them to a permanent hire, the agency loses the recurring placement fee — so the business quietly depends on labor, and loyalty, staying temporary. It monetizes the churn as much as the shortage.

In one sentence

Staffing agencies turn labor scarcity into a margin machine — making hiring faster for the employer while capturing 15–30% of the wage as the cost of outsourcing recruitment, compliance, and churn.
