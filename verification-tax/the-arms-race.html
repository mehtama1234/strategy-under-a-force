Group D · The mechanism & the paradox

The arms race

Every new shield forges a sharper sword — a race with no finish line.

Here's the engine under the whole collection. Every verification method invites a better forgery, which demands a better verification, endlessly — like spam versus spam-filters, or CAPTCHAs (which AI now solves, so they got harder for humans too). The same AI that fakes also detects, so both sides escalate. Costs and friction ratchet up for everyone, honest users included. There's rarely a final win — only a moving equilibrium.

The core bet

The structural truth: verification is a race with no finish line. Each shield spawns a new sword, so the "trust tax" only rises. And because the same AI powers both making and breaking trust, the tool vendors have no incentive to declare a winner — both sides are revenue.

What the numbers say

~95%
Share of email that is spam — after 40 years of filters. New detection just spawns new, polymorphic spam. The rate barely moves.
85%+
Accuracy of AI CAPTCHA-solvers by 2023–24 — so CAPTCHAs got harder for humans too, punishing real (and disabled) users.
~30%
Share of web traffic that is automated bots (Cloudflare, 2024) — forcing sites to add friction that lands on real people.
6–18 mo
Typical lag of deepfake detection behind evasion — a detector trained in 2023 is often obsolete by 2025.

Costs spread asymmetrically

The friction lands unequally. Honest users bear the tax — slower logins, harder CAPTCHAs, more checks — while attackers scale sideways with cheap solvers and APIs. Small platforms and individuals can't afford detection infrastructure, so they either eat the fraud or drop trust-dependent features entirely.

Who bears it
Honest users (friction), small operators (can't afford tools), and accessibility (harder security shuts out disabled users).
Who profits
The AI vendors — who sell both the sword and the shield, so the race is a revenue stream, not a problem to solve.
The single biggest worry

Costs spread asymmetrically: honest users pay the friction tax while attackers scale cheaply. Small platforms and individuals can't afford the infrastructure, so they accept fraud or abandon trust-dependent features.

The surprising part

The same machine learning that forges — deepfakes, spam, auto-solved CAPTCHAs — also detects them, and the AI providers have no motive to declare a clear winner because both detection and evasion are revenue. The quiet losers are accessibility: harder security makes the internet less navigable for disabled users, and nobody's optimizing that trade-off.

In one sentence

The verification tax is a structural escalator — each new shield forges a sharper sword, costs rise for everyone but the attackers, and the race never ends because the same AI powers both sides.
