Group A · Proving who you are

Identity verification

Prove you're you online — or an imperfect AI locks you out of your own account.

Proving "you are you" online became a multi-billion-dollar business — selfie-plus-ID checks, liveness tests, biometrics — used to open bank accounts, rent cars, verify age, and access government benefits. But AI deepfakes now threaten to beat these checks, forcing ever-fancier tests. And when the check fails on a real person, they can be locked out of their bank, job, or benefits with no human to appeal to.

The core bet

The bet is that identity, once a rare bit of friction (a passport, an in-person bank visit), is now an everyday gate — and automating that gate at scale is worth billions. The catch: the same AI that fakes IDs and selfies also powers the checks, so it's a self-escalating arms race.

What the numbers say

$8–12B
Global identity-verification market (2024–25), growing 15–20%/yr — Onfido, Jumio, ID.me, Persona and others.
2021–22
ID.me controversy — chosen to gate IRS and unemployment logins; backlash over mandatory face scans forced the IRS to back off, but it still gates many state benefits.
liveness vs deepfakes
AI can now inject fake selfies, so vendors add "liveness" tests (head turns, eye tracking) — an arms race where the faker and the checker use the same AI.
5–15%
Valid applications some systems wrongly reject — with racial disparity in face-match error, and often no human review or appeal.

The lock-out problem

A false negative — the system saying "you're not you" — can instantly bar you from your bank, your paycheck, or your benefits, with no human override. A credit-card fraud decline has a reversal path; an identity-check rejection often doesn't. And the standards for what counts as "proof" are proprietary and opaque.

What's newly valuable
Automated identity checks — a growing SaaS layer for banking, benefits, and age gating.
What it costs
Real people at the margins locked out, and biometric data handed over to private firms.
The single biggest worry

A false "you're not you" can instantly lock you out of your bank, job, or benefits with no human override — and the standards for "proof" are proprietary and unexplained.

The surprising part

The same foundation models and face encoders that create convincing deepfakes are repackaged to detect them — so identity verification is a self-escalating race, and the "tax" (time, biometric data, lock-out risk) falls hardest on people at the margin: unusual faces, poor lighting, older documents, prior fraud flags. Vendors accept wrongly rejecting real people as the cost of catching fakers.

In one sentence

Proving you're you online became a ~$10B-a-year checkpoint run by private firms whose imperfect AI can bar you from your own accounts with no recourse.
