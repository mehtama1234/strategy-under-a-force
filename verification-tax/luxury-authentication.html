Group B · Proving a thing is real

Luxury authentication

Resale marketplaces don't sell sneakers — they sell certainty.

The booming resale market — sneakers, handbags, watches — runs entirely on proving items are genuine, because counterfeits are everywhere. StockX verifies every sneaker before it ships; Entrupy authenticates bags with AI and microscope images; The RealReal built (and stumbled on) luxury authentication. Authentication isn't a side service — it's the product that makes resale trustworthy at all.

The core bet

The bet: a resale marketplace has no value without proof of authenticity, because buyers won't pay resale prices for possible fakes. So the real innovation isn't the exchange — it's the verification step in the middle. Solve trust first, and the marketplace becomes possible.

What the numbers say

$500B+
Estimated global counterfeit trade a year (some estimates far higher) — luxury goods among the most-faked, so proof-of-real is mandatory.
2016
StockX launched with "no fake sneakers" as the core promise — every item ships only after passing physical inspection. It grew to ~$1B+ on that trust.
Entrupy
Founded 2015, uses AI + macro-microscope imaging to authenticate handbags and watches — millions of items a year for resellers and platforms.
3–10%
Denial rates by category (StockX ~1–2% of sneakers; The RealReal higher) — fraud filtered out, but also marginal sellers.

The marketplace is a QC operation

StockX's genius wasn't the app or the auction — it was becoming a quality-control operation first, spending millions on staff, imaging, and facilities to inspect every sneaker. That's why it stayed sneaker-focused: authentication doesn't scale cheaply across all luxury. The RealReal tried to scale it, cut corners, and got sued (2021–23) before rebuilding stricter checks.

What's newly valuable
Credible authentication — the scarce thing that lets a whole resale economy exist.
What's hard
"Super-fakes" now need destructive testing to spot, and the cost structure doesn't scale across categories.
The single biggest worry

As counterfeits improve, authenticators are always a step behind — and a single high-profile miss (a fake sneaker shipping, a counterfeit bag "authenticated") destroys the platform's core trust claim and opens massive liability.

The surprising part

The resale giants aren't really retailers — they're authentication companies with a storefront attached. StockX spends millions physically inspecting shoes; the platforms that tried to scale verification across bags, watches, and jewelry all hit accuracy-vs-cost limits. You can't run a big resale marketplace without becoming a verification business first.

In one sentence

Resale platforms don't sell goods — they sell certainty, and authentication is the tax that makes that certainty credible.
