Group C · Proving content is real

Credential fraud

You can't trust a diploma, a résumé, or even a live video interview anymore.

Proving people are who and what they claim on paper is a big verification business. Résumés lie at scale, diplomas get forged, and now remote hiring plus AI make it worse — deepfake job interviews, and state-sponsored fake IT workers (a real 2024 FBI concern) infiltrating US firms. Employers increasingly can't trust a degree, a résumé, or a video call, so they pay a verification tax before onboarding anyone.

The core bet

The shift: remote hiring made credentials impossible to check in person, and AI made faking them easy. So companies must verify not just a claimed degree or title, but that the person they interviewed and hired is even a single, real human being.

What the numbers say

50–80%
Share of résumés containing inaccuracies (various HR studies) — fake degrees, wrong titles, inflated tenure the common lies.
2024
FBI/DOJ alerts on North Korean IT workers using fake identities and deepfake video interviews to get hired at US tech firms — active since ~2020, intensified 2023–24.
$400–500M
US diploma-mill market — thousands of fake degrees in circulation that many employers never catch.
$20B+
US background-check industry (HireRight, Checkr, Sterling, National Student Clearinghouse) — demand jumped with the remote-hiring boom.

Even the video call isn't proof

A decade ago, a diploma and a live video call were trusted. Now they're close to useless — the same tech that made hiring remote made it insecure. Attackers use AI to interview on a real candidate's behalf or to impersonate hiring managers, and verification firms now do multi-source checks (direct-to-university, transcript APIs, liveness) that add days and cost to every hire.

What's newly valuable
Background checks, credential verification, and liveness checks on candidates — a growing pre-hire tax.
What's harmed
Honest applicants slowed by the process, and firms exposed to IP theft by fake workers.
The single biggest worry

Employers can no longer assume a live video interview or a diploma proves who applied. Criminal and state-sponsored identity rings operate at scale, so companies must run third-party verification on every offer — adding days, cost, and risk.

The surprising part

It's not just scammers — the FBI warned that a foreign government (North Korea) runs coordinated hiring-fraud operations to place IT workers inside US firms, steal IP, and exfiltrate code. In verified 2023–24 cases, dozens of fake identities landed jobs at major companies, caught only when background checks flagged inconsistencies. A diploma and a video call went from trusted to nearly worthless in a decade.

In one sentence

Credential fraud jumped from rare fake diplomas to industrial identity rings and deepfake interviews — forcing employers to pay a costly verification tax before they onboard anyone.
