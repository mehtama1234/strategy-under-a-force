The Verification Tax

When faking got cheap, proving something is real became an industry — and a tax on everything.

The 23rd force. For most of history you could assume a face, a dollar, a handbag, a diploma, or a video was roughly what it claimed to be. That assumption is gone — AI can fake a voice, a factory can clone a bag, a mill can print a degree. So a whole "trust layer" grows on top of the economy just to prove reality. The one idea: when anything can be faked, proving it's real becomes the business.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did the verification tax make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? Value flows to whoever can credibly certify "this is real" — an identity check, an authentication, a provenance trail. The risk is an arms race with no finish line, where fakers and verifiers escalate forever and cheap things get medium-priced just to prove they're genuine.
