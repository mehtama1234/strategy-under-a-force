Group B · Proving a thing is real

Anti-counterfeiting

A unique, traceable serial number on every prescription drug — by law.

Counterfeit goods are a massive, sometimes deadly problem — fake medicines kill, fake parts fail. So governments and industries built a hidden infrastructure of "serialization": a unique code on every drug package, holograms, QR authentication, and brand-protection teams scanning marketplaces for fakes. It's a verification tax baked directly into products — an entire system whose only job is to prove a pill is what the box says.

The core bet

The bet: the only way to fight counterfeits at scale is to make every genuine item provable — a serialized, traceable identity per package, backed by law. That turns verification into a mandatory operational cost on every unit sold, and shifts liability onto the supply chain.

What the numbers say

Nov 2023
The US DSCSA fully required serialized track-and-trace on all prescription drugs — 14B+ packages a year now carry a unique, scannable serial number.
Jan 2019
The EU's Falsified Medicines Directive mandated anti-tamper seals + unique barcodes on every pack, verified before dispensing — costing the industry billions to build.
$200B+
Annual counterfeit-drug black market; the WHO links roughly 1 in 10 drugs in low-income markets to fakes, and ~1M deaths a year to substandard/counterfeit medicines.
~$6–8B
What Amazon says it spends a year fighting counterfeits across its platform — serialization, IP enforcement, brand partnerships.

The codes get cloned too

Serialization systems are themselves targets. Counterfeiters now clone serial numbers and fake holograms; a simple QR code can be duplicated in hours. The arms race escalates to blockchain and cryptographic signing — but cost and complexity mean many small producers still use cheap holograms that forge easily. Every new verification layer opens a new attack.

What's newly valuable
Serialization software, authentication servers, holograms/RFID, and brand-protection services — a whole compliance industry.
What's fragile
Cloned codes and forged marks; and most retail checks are passive, not true end-to-end verification.
The single biggest worry

The verification layer is a target: cloned serials and fake holograms defeat basic checks, and a foolproof, scalable system doesn't exist — every layer of verification opens a new vector for fraud.

The surprising part

Every prescription package in the US and EU now carries a unique, traceable serial number by law, and pharma spent billions building the tracking — yet pharmacists and consumers almost never verify end-to-end. Much of the billions spent is about supply-chain accountability and liability reduction as much as catching a specific fake.

In one sentence

Every verified product now carries a hidden serialization tax — a mandatory infrastructure whose only job is to prove a pill, package, or part is what it claims to be.
