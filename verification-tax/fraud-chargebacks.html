Group A · Proving who you are

Fraud & chargebacks

Every online checkout is silently scored — and the filter hurts twice.

Every time you buy online, the transaction is silently scored for fraud in milliseconds. A whole industry — Stripe Radar, Riskified, Signifyd, Forter — sells that scoring, some even guaranteeing chargebacks. Merchants pay a cut of every sale for the protection. The twist: blocking real customers by mistake ("false declines") costs merchants more than the fraud itself.

The core bet

The bet is that certainty at checkout is worth paying for — but certainty is expensive and incomplete. Merchants face a double tax: they pay for fraud detection, and they lose revenue when that detection wrongly blocks legitimate buyers. Both fraud and the cost of catching it rise together.

What the numbers say

~$41B
Annual US e-commerce fraud losses (2023), rising 10–15%/yr as AI-enabled scams grow.
$100B+
Value of legitimate sales wrongly blocked as fraud — false declines cost 2–3× more than actual fraud does.
0.15–0.5%
Added cost per transaction for third-party fraud tools (plus a revenue share on guarantee models) — a levy on every sale.
2.5–3%
Share of good transactions blocked as suspicious — and a rejected customer often doesn't retry; they go to a competitor.

The filter hurts twice

Fraud detection gets better at catching thieves and, at the same time, more aggressive at blocking legitimate shoppers — especially new customers, cross-border buyers, or anyone whose pattern looks unusual. Chargebacks get absorbed as fees; false declines quietly bleed the best customers to rivals. As AI-enabled fraud rises, vendors raise thresholds, which raises false declines.

What's newly valuable
Fraud-scoring SaaS with fat margins — and chargeback guarantees that sell peace of mind.
What it costs
Real customers wrongly rejected — a bigger loss than the fraud the filter catches.
The single biggest worry

Detection improves at catching thieves but also blocks more legitimate shoppers in high-growth, high-risk segments — a margin squeeze where merchants must accept more fraud or lose more good customers.

The surprising part

False declines cost merchants more than fraud itself — legit shoppers wrongly blocked, worth tens of billions in lost sales, versus roughly half that in actual fraud. Prevention gets outsourced to vendors who are rewarded for over-blocking, so the legitimate customer becomes the real cost of security.

In one sentence

Merchants pay a verification tax on every sale — in fraud-prevention fees and in customers the over-cautious filter wrongly rejects — turning legitimate shoppers into the true cost of security.
