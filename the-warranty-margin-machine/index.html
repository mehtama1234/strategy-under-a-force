The Warranty Margin Machine

The product is the loss-leader — the coverage is the profit, and it must fail just often enough.

The 50th force. Extended warranties, service plans, AppleCare, device protection, home warranties, and the car dealer's "finance & insurance" office all sell protection at enormous margins — often 50–70%+ — because the price is set on the gap between what you pay and how rarely you actually claim. A store can make more profit on the warranty for a TV than on the TV itself. The visible product is the phone, the sofa, or the car; the hidden engine is the protection plan sold at the register and quietly backed by an underwriter you've never heard of. The one idea: the retailer makes more on the coverage than on the thing it protects — and quietly needs the product to fail just often enough, but not too much.

A fresh mechanism (Tier-7) Not from the Inc. 5000 taxonomy — surfaced by a Haiku discovery-scout pass for genuinely new hidden-mechanism business models. The lens: the low-margin product as bait for the high-margin protection plan.
The one question, asked of everyone

What did the warranty machine make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? The product got cheap and commoditized (thin margins), while the peace-of-mind plan on top became the scarce, high-margin profit — priced where you can't comparison-shop; and the buyer's forgetfulness (and the difficulty of claiming) only helped. The danger: the whole thing rests on customers misjudging the odds, so the incentives to deny, delay, and design friction run against them — which is exactly what invites regulators and erodes trust.
