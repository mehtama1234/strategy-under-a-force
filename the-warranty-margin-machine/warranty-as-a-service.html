Group C · The machinery

Warranty-as-a-service

The register upsell became an API — a startup's pitch is "we'll add pure profit to your checkout."

A new generation of companies (Extend, Cover Genius, Mulberry) offers 'warranty-as-a-service': a plug-in that lets any online retailer add a protection-plan upsell at checkout, with the tech company and an underwriter handling the rest and splitting the margin. It industrialized the warranty upsell and spread it across e-commerce — turning a specialty product into a checkbox on nearly every purchase.

The core bet

Make it trivial for any online store to add a high-margin protection-plan upsell at checkout, take a cut of the new revenue stream, and spread the warranty machine to everything sold online.

The pitch changed from 'protect your stuff' to 'free money'

By turning the warranty into an invisible, frictionless API bolt-on, these firms rewired how merchants think about margins. A retailer no longer asks 'do I want to offer warranties?' — they ask 'why wouldn't I add a $15 upsell to every $500 checkout?' The technology made the product feel inevitable.

The single biggest worry

It's still the same low-payout product, now everywhere — and the 2022–24 funding pullback hit several of these startups as retail pressure mounted.

The surprising part

The tell is who the customer of these startups is: not the shopper, but the store. Their entire pitch is 'we'll add pure profit to your checkout' — the warranty upsell, once a skill of a trained Best Buy clerk, became a line of code any merchant can switch on in an afternoon.

In one sentence

Tech companies turned the high-margin warranty upsell into an API any store can switch on, splitting the profit with insurers and spreading it to nearly every online purchase.
