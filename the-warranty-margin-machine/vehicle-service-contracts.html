Group B · The big-ticket version

Vehicle service contracts

"Your car's warranty is about to expire" — those robocalls exist because the product is so profitable.

Vehicle service contracts (marketed as 'extended car warranties') and GAP insurance are a big, high-margin business — sold by dealers, direct marketers (the infamous robocalls), and administrators like Endurance and CarShield. Many carry heavy markups and low payout ratios, and the robocall side is a consumer-protection sore point. The product passes through several middlemen, each taking a cut.

The core bet

Sell drivers protection against expensive car repairs at a price far above expected claims, layered with markups for dealers, marketers, and administrators — the margin is the gap.

What the numbers say

$30–40B
The US extended-car-warranty market (~$150–200B globally).
$500M–1B
What CarShield and Endurance each spend a year on ads and robocalls.
2B+ / $11M
Auto-warranty robocalls Americans got (2015–22); one major operation was fined ~$11M in 2022.
30–40%
Loss ratio on these contracts — markups of 150–300% before the driver sees the price.
$60M
GAP-insurance refunds the FTC ordered dealers to pay for overpriced coverage (2023).

The robocalls aren't a bug — they're the business model

A $2,000 contract that pays out ~$400–800 in claims leaves $1,200–1,400 for sales and admin — so blasting billions of robocalls that cost pennies each, landing 0.5–2% of listeners, is still profitable. By the time a contract reaches your car it's been marked up 4–5 times, each middleman convinced they're 'adding value.'

The single biggest worry

Heavy regulation and reputational damage — robocalls, denied claims, class actions — threaten the cheap customer acquisition the margins depend on, and many contracts pay out little.

The surprising part

The thing to see is why the calls never stop: the product is so profitable that even a wildly inefficient sales channel pays off. The robocall isn't a nuisance attached to a normal business — it's a rational feature of one where the gap between price and claims is wide enough to fund an army of dialers.

In one sentence

The extended-car-warranty business charges $2,000 for ~$400 of expected claims, and every middleman takes a cut — which is exactly why the robocalls won't stop until regulators block the channel.
