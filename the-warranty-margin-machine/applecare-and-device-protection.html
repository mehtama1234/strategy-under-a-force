Group A · The register upsell

AppleCare & device protection

When you claim, you still pay a deductible — so the insurer wins whether the phone breaks or not.

AppleCare and carrier phone insurance (backed by Asurion) are extremely profitable. Device protection is a multi-billion-dollar business: customers pay monthly fees that far exceed the cost of the occasional repair, and even when they claim, a deductible applies on top. AppleCare is a big, high-margin line inside Apple's Services business.

The core bet

Phones are expensive and people fear breaking them, so protection plans sold monthly generate recurring, high-margin revenue that dwarfs the actual repair costs.

The two-layer payout means the insurer wins both ways

A customer who pays $150 a year and never claims hands the insurer $150 of pure profit; one who pays $150 and claims still owes a $29–149 deductible, so the insurer keeps most of the premium anyway. The premium-plus-deductible structure means the math is tilted the seller's way from day one.

The single biggest worry

Consumer groups and state regulators say phone insurance is often poor value compared with self-insuring, and denied or slow claims carry reputational risk — especially in states with strict insurance oversight.

The surprising part

The clever design is the deductible. Most 'insurance' pays you when the bad thing happens; device protection charges you again at the moment of the claim, so the seller collects on the many who never break their phone and on the few who do. It's one of the most profitable 'insurance' products in consumer tech.

In one sentence

Phone and device insurance is a monthly cash machine — fees far above repair costs, plus a deductible when you claim — making it one of the highest-margin products in tech.
