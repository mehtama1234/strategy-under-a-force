Group B · The big-ticket version

Protection on everything

You can now buy a warranty on a $30 item — often duplicating coverage you already have for free.

Protection plans have spread far beyond electronics and cars: furniture stores sell fabric and stain protection, jewelers sell warranties, mattress and appliance sellers, eyewear, sporting goods — and now almost any online purchase comes with an 'add protection' box (via Allstate, Assurant, or Extend). It has become the default upsell on nearly everything.

The core bet

Attach a high-margin protection plan to almost any product, and the retailer earns extra profit on a purchase they've already made — protection became a default upsell everywhere.

Much of it duplicates coverage you already own

Many plans overlap with the free manufacturer warranty, your credit card's purchase protection, or your homeowner/renter insurance — so people often pay to protect something already covered. And the fine print is dense: jewelry plans deny for 'lotions and perfume,' mattress plans for 'normal wear,' furniture plans for 'general soiling.'

The single biggest worry

Fine-print denials are rampant, and many plans duplicate coverage buyers already have — which is exactly what fuels complaints and regulatory attention.

The surprising part

The absurd frontier is the $30 warranty. Protection turned from a big-ticket safeguard into a reflexive checkout box on almost anything, because it's near-pure profit for the retailer — no inventory, no shipping, low claims — even when the plan quietly duplicates coverage the buyer already has.

In one sentence

Retailers turned nearly every purchase into a two-product sale by bolting on a high-margin protection plan with low claim rates and fine print that often duplicates coverage you already own.
