Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

Why coverage out-earns the product

The product margin is thin enough to attract you; the warranty margin is fat enough to fund the store.

This is the mechanism. The product itself sells at thin margins — competition drives the price to near cost — but the protection plan sells at fat margins (50–70%+) because there's no comparison shopping, the buyer overestimates failure risk, and claims are low. So retailers use the product as a loss-leader to sell the plan, and the incentive quietly flips: the seller benefits when the product does not break.

The core bet

The product is a competitive, low-margin commodity, but the warranty on it is a high-margin add-on with no price comparison — so the real profit is the plan, and the product is bait.

What the numbers say

5–15% vs 50–70%
Product margin (electronics; cars 1–3%) versus warranty margin — the whole reason the plan is the prize.
no comparison
You can't shop warranties the way you shop TVs — they're sold at the register, bundled, in the moment.
60–80%
Share of buyers who overestimate how often things break and how much repairs cost.
flipped incentive
The seller's profit rises when the product doesn't fail — lower claims, higher margin.
commission
Staff are often paid more to sell the plan than the product itself.

The retailer needs the product reliable AND you afraid it isn't

Here's the strange double bind: a product that actually fails a lot triggers recalls and refunds, so the seller wants it reliable — but a product you fear might fail sells the warranty, so the seller wants you anxious. The product's quiet durability is the plan's best salesman, and the real business isn't fixing broken things but collecting for breakdowns that won't happen.

The single biggest worry

The model relies on information asymmetry and consumer misjudgment — overestimating failure, loss aversion, buying with your guard down — which invites regulation and erodes trust as buyers get informed.

The surprising part

The elegant, uncomfortable logic is that the cheap product exists to sell the expensive promise. Competition strips the margin out of the TV so the store can lure you in; the warranty, sold without any way to compare it, puts the margin back. The thing on the shelf is a coupon for the thing that actually makes money.

In one sentence

The product margin is thin enough to attract buyers; the warranty margin is fat enough to fund the whole store.
