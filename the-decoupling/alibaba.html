Group C · The China side

Alibaba & Chinese tech — cut off, and forced to build its own

The West walled China off from the best AI chips. It backfired in a surprising way.

The sharpest edge of the decoupling isn't tariffs — it's chips. The US barred China from buying Nvidia's best AI chips and threatened to kick Chinese companies off US stock markets. For giants like Alibaba, that meant being walled off from Western money and the hardware everyone builds AI on.

The squeeze

  • No top chips. US export controls cut China off from Nvidia's most powerful AI chips.
  • No easy access to Western money. Delisting threats hang over hundreds of Chinese firms on US exchanges.
  • A splitting world. Two separate tech ecosystems — a US-led one and a Chinese one — are forming, with their own chips and software.

The backfire

Cutting them off made them scrappy

Here's the twist. Locked out of the best chips, Chinese firms got creative. DeepSeek — a Chinese AI lab — trained its models frugally on weaker hardware and now charges roughly a third of what US models cost. Alibaba poured about $52 billion into its own AI and cloud, and China's chip self-sufficiency jumped fast. The controls meant to slow China down partly spurred its self-reliance. (This ties straight to the AI collection.)

The main worry

The single biggest risk — a split world

China is still locked out of the very best chips, and the world is hardening into two separate tech stacks — duplicated, incompatible, and more expensive for everyone. China's home-grown path is real but slower and costlier — and the divide raises the risk of a tech cold war.

The surprising part

The controls were meant to preserve US dominance. Instead they helped produce a cheaper Chinese AI (DeepSeek) and accelerated China's push to make its own everything. Blocking the front door pushed China to build a whole new house — a riskier, more divided outcome than a single shared market.

Alibaba & Chinese tech in a nutshell

Walled off from the best chips and Western capital, they were forced into self-reliance — which hurts, but also produced cheap home-grown AI and a splitting, two-stack tech world.
