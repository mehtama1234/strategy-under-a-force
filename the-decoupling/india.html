Group A · Reshoring winners

India — the "China+1" winner the US then tariffed

A quarter of the world's iPhones now come from India. Then the same US that pushed firms there taxed it.

When companies decided they couldn't depend on China alone, they went looking for a "plus one" — another big place to make things. India put its hand up, and the clearest prize was Apple: a huge and growing share of iPhones is now assembled in India.

The wins

~25%
Of the world's iPhones are now made in India — up from almost nothing three years ago (about 55 million units in 2025).
2 → 300+
Phone-making plants in India (up from 2 in 2014). Smartphones are now India's single biggest export, ahead of oil and jewellery.

The government helped, paying cash bonuses (called "PLI") to companies that build and export from India. Apple's suppliers Foxconn and Tata poured in billions.

The main worry: the US tariffed India too

The rug-pull

The same US tariffs that pushed companies out of China and toward India then hit India with a 50% tariff (2025) — the highest on any major trading partner. That's a problem: the whole reason to move to India was to dodge tariffs, and now Indian goods face steep ones too. It undercuts the cost advantage that drove the shift.

India also still isn't China: ports and roads are slower, red tape is thick, and its supply chains aren't as deep — so the question is whether India can truly scale up, or stays a niche for phones and simpler goods.

The surprising part

It's a policy tripping over itself. Apple invested in India because of US tariffs on China — then, months later, US tariffs on India made that same investment marginal. The goal (bring supply chains to friendlier countries) collided with the tool (tariff everyone, including the friendly country you wanted things moved to).

In one sentence

India in a nutshell

It's winning the race to be the next factory floor — a quarter of iPhones already — but the US then tariffed it too, and its infrastructure still trails China, so the boom's staying power is the open question.
