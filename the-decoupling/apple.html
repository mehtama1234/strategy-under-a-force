Group B · The exposed

Apple — its perfect China machine became its biggest liability

The company most praised for its supply chain is now the most exposed to the split.

For 20 years, Apple built the most efficient supply chain in history by making almost everything in China — a tight cluster of 150+ suppliers that could turn out iPhones at incredible speed and scale. Decoupling and tariffs punish exactly that.

Doubly exposed

Apple is caught two ways at once:

  • It makes there. Around 86% of iPhones are still assembled in China — so US tariffs land directly on its costs.
  • It sells there. China is worth roughly 15–17% of Apple's revenue — so China could retaliate against Apple and boost home-grown Huawei.
~$900M
Tariff cost Apple disclosed for a single quarter. That comes straight out of profit.

The escape is slow

Apple is shifting iPhone production to India (aiming for about half by 2027) and some assembly to Vietnam. But the China supply chain is deep — a decade-long project to replicate elsewhere — and making things in India costs 5–8% more. You can't move it overnight.

The main worry

Caught between two superpowers

Tariffs and politics squeeze it in the US; slowing sales and rising Huawei squeeze it in China. Every day, Apple is a little less efficient than it was designed to be — paying billions to unwind the exact machine that made it a $3-trillion company.

The surprising part

Tim Cook's legendary supply-chain mastery — the thing that made Apple a margin machine — is now its biggest vulnerability. That tight, China-centric web is precisely what decoupling attacks. The strategy that built Apple is the strategy decoupling punishes, and there's nowhere to fully hide.

In one sentence

Apple in a nutshell

The world's best China supply chain turned into its biggest liability — taxed making things there, threatened selling there, and slowly, expensively trying to move without breaking the machine.
