Rare earths — China's chokehold on the materials everything needs

A few grey rocks turned out to be a bigger weapon than any tariff.

Inside every electric car, wind turbine, phone, fighter jet, and missile are tiny magnets made from rare-earth minerals. And China doesn't just mine most of them — it does almost all the tricky, dirty work of processing them into usable form. That's a chokepoint.

China turned it into a weapon

The squeeze

In 2025, in retaliation for US tariffs and chip controls, China restricted exports of rare earths and magnets. Western industry and militaries panicked — because they suddenly realized they had no backup. No tariff war, no shots fired — just an export control on a dozen elements, and the whole West scrambled.

~90%
China's share of rare-earth processing — and ~99% of the heaviest, most critical types. It's a near-total chokehold.

The scramble

The West is racing to build its own supply — and it's so urgent the Pentagon became the biggest shareholder in America's main rare-earth company (MP Materials), guaranteeing prices and buyers. Allies (Australia, Canada) are ramping up too. But it's slow: building processing from scratch takes years, costs billions, and is chemically nasty.

The main worry

The chokehold can't be broken quickly. Even with government money, Western supply won't rival China's for years — likely into the 2030s. China's decades of state investment and cheap, dirty processing can't be matched fast. Until then, Beijing can throttle the West's EVs, electronics, and weapons at will.

The surprising part

Everyone watches chips and tariffs — but obscure minerals became the bigger weapon. China revealed that in a high-tech world, the quiet physical chokepoint (processing a few grey rocks) can matter more than the headline fight — and it's harder to break than anyone wanted to admit.

In one sentence

Rare earths in a nutshell

China controls ~90% of processing the magnets everything needs, weaponized it in 2025, and the West — even with the Pentagon writing checks — can't build an alternative for years.
