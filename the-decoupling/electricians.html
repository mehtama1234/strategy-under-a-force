Group A · Reshoring winners

The electricians of reshoring

Nobody's excited about transformers and switchgear. Except investors, suddenly.

Every factory brought back home, every new power line, every AI data center needs the same unglamorous stuff: transformers, switchgear, wiring, and control systems. The companies that make it — Eaton, GE Vernova, Vertiv, Rockwell — became some of the market's hottest stocks.

Why demand exploded

Three things hit at once: the reshoring boom (building factories in the US), big government programs (chips, clean energy), and the AI data-center frenzy — all of which need enormous amounts of electrical hardware. Supply couldn't keep up.

The surprising part

The boring bottleneck

The makers of transformers and switchgear — about as dull as industry gets — became hot stocks because two giant trends need them at once: bringing factories home and building AI. There simply isn't enough gear. Reportedly, half of planned US data centers face delays — not for lack of money, but for lack of electrical equipment.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

It's a cyclical boom. Reshoring and AI spending could cool, and then the orders dry up. There's also a shortage of skilled workers (electricians, technicians) to actually build all this — now cited as a bigger worry than tariffs themselves.

In one sentence

The electricians in a nutshell

Bringing factories home and building AI both run on the same electrical gear, and there isn't enough — so the dullest companies in the market are riding a huge boom, as long as it lasts.
