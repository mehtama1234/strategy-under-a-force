Group B · The exposed

Big-box retail — squeezed from every side

Walmart and Target sell cheap imported stuff at thin margins. Tariffs put them in a vise.

Walmart, Target, Home Depot, Best Buy — their whole model is moving enormous volumes of cheap imported goods, mostly from China, at razor-thin profit. Tariffs make those goods pricier, and there's no comfortable way out.

The impossible choice

When tariffs raised the cost of Chinese goods by 30% or more, retailers had two bad options:

Eat the cost

Absorb it and watch already-thin profits vanish.

Raise prices

Pass it on and watch price-sensitive shoppers buy less — and anger politicians.

Most did both — quietly absorbing much of the cost for months while nudging some prices up.

The vise from both sides

The single biggest risk — no good move

Costs push up from above, price-sensitive shoppers push back from below, and — remarkably — politics pushed from the side. When Walmart warned it would have to raise some prices, it got publicly attacked for it. The one lever these stores had to survive tariffs — raising prices — had its permission slip torn up. A structural lose-lose.

What they're doing

Scrambling to buy from anywhere but China (Best Buy and Target cut their China sourcing sharply), leaning on suppliers to absorb costs, and using their sheer scale to negotiate. The big players can grind through it; smaller retailers got wrecked.

The surprising part

The most powerful price-setter in America — Walmart — was shamed for warning that prices would rise. Normally a giant retailer simply passes costs along. Here, the political pressure not to raise prices collided head-on with tariffs raising costs, leaving the stores squeezed in a way that scale alone couldn't fix.

In one sentence

Big-box retail in a nutshell

Tariffs raised their costs while politics forbade raising prices and shoppers resisted them too — a three-way squeeze on a business that runs on thin margins and cheap imports.
