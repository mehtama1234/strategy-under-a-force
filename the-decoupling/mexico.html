Group A · Reshoring winners

Mexico — next door, and now America's #1 supplier

Factories fled China and landed right on the US border. Then the same tariffs came knocking.

If you're a US company that wants to leave China, the obvious move is to make things close by — same time zone, a short truck ride, and inside a tariff-free trade deal (USMCA). That's Mexico, and it became the big "nearshoring" winner.

The wins

#1
In 2023, Mexico overtook China as America's top source of imports for the first time in 20+ years — and is now the US's #1 trading partner.
~$41B
Record foreign investment poured in (2025). Industrial parks are sprouting on the border with essentially zero empty space left.

The surprising twist: China came too

The "backdoor"

Here's the irony. Chinese companies themselves built factories in Mexico — investing billions — to get around US tariffs: bring parts into Mexico, assemble them into "Mexican" goods, and ship them north duty-free under the trade deal. The very split that hurt China helped it sneak in through the back door.

The main worry: the tariffs followed

Whiplash

Then the US hit Mexico with tariffs too (2025) and began cracking down on the Chinese "backdoor" — and signaled it may not renew the trade deal that made the whole boom possible. Mexico even slapped its own tariffs on Chinese imports to close the loophole. On top of the politics: Mexico's power grid and water are running short, and cartel violence adds risk. The winner's crown is wobbly.

The surprising part

Mexico's boom was built partly by the very China it was supposed to replace — Chinese firms using it as a launchpad into the US. That success invited a backlash from both Washington and Mexico's own government, who are now trying to shut the door the boom came through.

In one sentence

Mexico in a nutshell

Being next door and tariff-free made it America's new #1 supplier — but tariffs, a China backdoor crackdown, and shortages of power and water make the win fragile.
