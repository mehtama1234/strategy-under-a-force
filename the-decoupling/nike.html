Group B · The exposed

Nike — tariffs on an industry that can't come home

The whole point of tariffs is to bring factories back. Sneakers literally can't.

Nike and the clothing brands built their profits on cheap Asian factories — Nike makes about half its shoes in Vietnam, plus China and Indonesia. Tariffs tax exactly that. And here's the trap: unlike a chip plant, a sneaker factory can't move to America.

The hit

~$1.5B
A year in extra tariff costs for Nike alone — money that comes out of profit, because it can't easily raise prices on cautious shoppers.
$3B → $6B
The whole footwear industry's yearly tariff bill roughly doubled in a year.

Why there's nowhere to run

The single biggest risk

Moving production just goes from one tariffed Asian country to another — leave Vietnam (46% tariff) for Cambodia (49%) and you've saved almost nothing. And you can't come to the US: America makes about 1.2 billion pairs of shoes a year but buys 2.6 billion — there simply isn't a home industry to move to. Tariffs are taxing goods that can't be made at home.

The surprising part

It's the core paradox of the whole reshoring push. Tariffs are meant to "bring manufacturing home" — but the US apparel and footwear industry can't come home, because there is no home. There's no factory base to make billions of sneakers here. So the industry is trapped abroad by the very policy meant to bring it back — paying the tax with nowhere to go.

In one sentence

Nike in a nutshell

Tariffs cost it over a billion a year with no escape — it can't reshore (no US shoe factories) and can't dodge to a cheaper country (they're all tariffed too), so the cost just eats into profit.
