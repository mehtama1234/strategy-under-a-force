Group A · The store brands that won

Costco Kirkland

A store brand people trust more than the famous names.

Kirkland Signature is the gold standard of store brands — so trusted that people buy Costco memberships partly to get it. By revenue it's bigger than many famous national brands. It's often made in the same factories as the name brands, sometimes by the brand owners themselves — just cheaper. It flips the old hierarchy: here, the store's name beats the manufacturer's.

The core bet

Costco bet that shoppers would trust the store's quality guarantee over a manufacturer's advertising. So Kirkland isn't a discount afterthought — it's the draw, a reason to pay the membership fee and shop Costco first. The store's reputation became the brand.

What the numbers say

$50–60B
Estimated Kirkland revenue a year (2024) — roughly 25–30% of Costco's sales. That makes one store brand bigger, by revenue, than Nike or Coca-Cola.
1983
When Kirkland launched — as a cost play. It grew into a trusted brand with a quality guarantee that members now seek out on purpose.
20–40%
Typical savings versus the national brand, often from the same factory making the same product with the same specs.
10–11%
The margin Costco deliberately caps Kirkland at (vs 20%+ on national brands) — using the low price as membership glue, not a profit engine.

Trust moved to the store

Surveys show a majority of Costco members actively prefer Kirkland on key items — batteries, vitamins, coffee, chocolate. The trust that used to attach to a hundred-year-old brand now attaches to the retailer. Shoppers don't switch brands; they switch to trusting the store.

What's newly valuable
The store's quality guarantee, membership stickiness, and a "brand" Costco fully controls — price, supplier, and margin.
The fragility
Kirkland has no brand equity of its own; all of it borrows from Costco's reputation.
The single biggest worry

If the quality promise ever breaks — a contamination, a recall, a downgraded factory — Kirkland loses its moat instantly. Members have no loyalty to "Kirkland," only to Costco. The store is the insurance policy.

The surprising part

Kirkland's annual revenue exceeds Nike or Coca-Cola on their own — yet almost nobody knows "Kirkland" as a brand outside Costco. The real surprise is that shoppers trust an anonymous store label more than famous century-old brands. The retailer's reputation became more powerful than the factory's name.

In one sentence

Kirkland proves the store brand can become the primary brand when the retailer's quality guarantee is worth more than the manufacturer's advertising.
