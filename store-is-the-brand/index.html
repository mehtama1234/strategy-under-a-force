The Store Is the Brand

How private label is quietly eating the national brands it used to just carry.

The eighteenth force. For a century, brands like Coca-Cola and Kraft were king and retailers were just shelves. Now the retailer uses two things it controls — the shelf and the shopper data — to launch its own copy of every top seller at 20–40% less, and quietly takes the sale. Inflation supercharged it; a generation raised on reviews and "dupes" feels no loyalty to the name. The one idea: the store became the brand.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did private label make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? The middleman became the manufacturer's rival. Value flows to whoever owns the shelf and the data; it drains from national brands whose main asset was a name people used to trust.
