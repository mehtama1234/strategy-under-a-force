Group D · The forces & the fight

Inflation trade-down

People switched to save money — and never switched back.

The 2021–2024 inflation surge is the accelerant behind everything in this collection. As grocery prices jumped, shoppers "traded down" from national brands to cheaper store brands — and many never traded back. The reason is simple: once you try the store version and find it fine, the brand habit breaks. Inflation didn't just dent the brands temporarily; it permanently converted millions of shoppers.

The core bet

The mechanism is "try and stay." Inflation forced the trial; the discovery that the store brand was "good enough" at 30–40% less made the switch permanent. And national brands' aggressive price hikes widened the gap, turning a recession tactic into a lasting shift.

What the numbers say

~24% → 28–32%
Private label's share of US grocery, pre-pandemic (2019) to 2024 — an all-time high driven by the inflation trade-down.
12–15%
Yearly price hikes national brands pushed (2021–23) while store brands rose only 6–8% — nearly doubling the gap shoppers saw at checkout.
71%
Shoppers who tried a store brand during inflation (2022–23) — most who switched cited "no quality difference" as the reason they stayed.
40–50%
Share of inflation-driven switchers who did not return to national brands in 2024, even as prices eased — the switch stuck.

A permanent discovery, not a blip

Once someone found a store brand fine at 30–40% cheaper, the psychological cost of switching back rose — not out of love for the store, but because the old price gap suddenly looked irrational. That's a permanent preference anchored to a new price expectation. Younger and middle-income households locked in hardest.

Why brands are stuck
Cut prices to win switchers back and crater margins; hold prices and keep losing shelf and loyalty. The middle path fragments their own lineup.
Who benefits
Every store brand in this collection — handed millions of new, sticky customers by the inflation shock.
The single biggest worry

For national brands, there's no clean exit. Winning switchers back means gutting margins; not trying means watching store brands keep expanding into their shelf space and their loyalty.

The surprising part

Inflation didn't create temporary switchers — it created a permanent discovery moment. Once a shopper saw the store brand was "good enough," the price gap felt irrational in hindsight, so going back felt like overpaying. National brands' price hikes — often above what costs required — were the accelerant that turned a downturn tactic into a structural shift.

In one sentence

Inflation forced the trial; a wide price gap and "good enough" quality made the switch permanent — the store became the brand when national brands chose margins over volume.
