Group D · The forces & the fight

Death of brand loyalty

Reviews and store reputation replaced the brand's 100-year-old promise.

Underneath all of it is a cultural shift. For a century, a brand name was a promise you paid extra for — a shortcut to trust. Younger shoppers don't need that shortcut. They grew up comparing prices and reading reviews, they trust peer ratings and creators over ads, and they feel no shame in store brands. When trust becomes free, the brand-name premium loses its reason to exist.

The core bet

The deep insight: a brand name was 20th-century trust infrastructure — a way to know a product was decent without checking. Now reviews, ratings, and the store's own reputation provide that trust for free, in seconds. The markup you paid for the name no longer buys anything.

What the numbers say

81%
Gen Z and Millennials who switched brands in the past year (2024). Only ~36% of Gen Z feel loyal to any brand, versus ~64% of Gen X.
$330B
Private-label sales in 2025, growing ~4× faster than national brands (4.4% vs 1.1%). Gen Z chose private label ~76% of the time in 2026, up from 52% in 2020.
74%
Trust in influencer recommendations (2026) — and 41% of shoppers buy based on a creator's promotion. The endorsement replaced the brand.
can't-name
Shoppers increasingly can't name the brand they bought — only the platform or creator that showed it to them. Algorithmic discovery replaced brand recall.

Where the trust went

The replacement for brand trust isn't better advertising — it's transparency, reviews from strangers, and creator endorsements. A younger shopper will compare three near-identical products in thirty seconds and pick the cheapest one with a 4.8-star rating. The trust that used to live in the brand name moved to the information around the product, and that information is free.

What's collapsing
The brand-name premium, expensive brand marketing, and the "pay more for a trusted name" deal of the last century.
What replaced it
Reviews, ratings, creators, and the store's own reputation — trust that costs the shopper nothing to verify.
The single biggest worry

Once a brand can't command a premium, its whole model — expensive marketing, premium manufacturing, distribution margins — stops adding up. National brands built on 20th-century trust are losing pricing power to faceless store labels and 4.8-star strangers.

The surprising part

The generation most loyal to private label is the one that grew up without brand loyalty as a concept — so they feel no "loss." And trust didn't disappear; it consolidated. Costco and Walmart are now more trusted than individual brands in many categories. The customer doesn't switch brands anymore — they switch stores.

In one sentence

Gen Z killed the brand-name premium by replacing a 100-year-old promise with reviews, creators, and store reputations that cost nothing to check.
