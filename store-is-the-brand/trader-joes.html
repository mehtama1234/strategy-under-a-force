Group A · The store brands that won

Trader Joe's

A cult national chain built by removing national brands almost entirely.

Trader Joe's is almost entirely private label — roughly 80% of what it sells is its own brand. That lets it control quality, price, and margin, and it creates cult loyalty around exclusive products you literally can't buy anywhere else. Small curated stores, exclusive goods, and the highest sales per square foot in US grocery.

The core bet

Trader Joe's bet the opposite of every big grocer: instead of stocking more brands, it removed nearly all of them. By making itself the only place to buy its products, it turned scarcity and curation into the draw — the store itself is the brand.

What the numbers say

~80%
Share of inventory under a Trader Joe's own label (about 3,200 of ~4,000 items). Almost no national brands on the shelf.
$1,750
Sales per square foot — more than double Whole Foods and among the highest in US grocery. Small stores, huge throughput.
~$21B
Estimated annual revenue (2025), from ~650 US stores in tight 10,000–15,000 sq ft formats. Owned by Germany's Aldi Nord since 1979.
exclusive
The peppermint bark, the ginger shots, the seasonal items — you can only get them here. Forced uniqueness is what pulls people in.

Scarcity as the product

Most retailers grew by carrying everything. Trader Joe's built a multi-billion-dollar cult by carrying almost nothing you can find elsewhere. The curation — a tight, rotating, own-brand assortment — is the offer. You come for products that exist nowhere else.

What's newly valuable
Exclusivity, curation, control over the full margin, and a loyal following no national brand can buy its way into.
The dependency
The whole promise rides on consistent quality from its suppliers — the store's name is on every failure.
The single biggest worry

Private label works only if manufacturing stays reliable. One supplier failure or contamination that damages the Trader Joe's name could crater loyalty fast — because customers chose the store because it was the store.

The surprising part

Everyone else built scale by adding brands. Trader Joe's did the reverse — it stripped national brands out and built a $21B business by making scarcity and curation the product. You can't get its cult items anywhere else, and that forced uniqueness is exactly the engine.

In one sentence

Trader Joe's proved you can build a cult retail brand by becoming the only place to buy your own stuff — 80% private label, tiny stores, double the sales per square foot, and loyalty money can't buy.
