Walmart & Kroger

Store brands are no longer just cheap — they're going premium too.

The biggest US grocers push their own brands hard. Walmart's Great Value is quietly one of the largest food "brands" in America. And the strategy has shifted: it's not just about undercutting anymore. Walmart launched a premium private label, "bettergoods," to trade shoppers up within its own house. Store brands now own the whole price ladder.

The core bet

The bet is that owning your own brands — cheap and premium — lets you capture the full margin and own the customer relationship end to end. Reduce dependence on national brands entirely by offering a house label at every price point.

What the numbers say

Top 10
Great Value (launched ~1993) is among the best-selling food brands in the US — an estimated $15–20B+ a year, ~20–25% of Walmart's grocery sales.
2024
Walmart launched "bettergoods," a premium private label priced above Great Value but below national brands — a play to trade shoppers up inside its own portfolio.
$15–18B
Kroger's Our Brands (Private Selection, Simple Truth) — grown from ~17% to ~25% of Kroger's sales in a decade, spanning value, organic, and premium tiers.
+10–15 pts
Higher margin on private label vs national brands. In grocery, where net margins are 1–2%, that gap is close to existential.

Owning the whole ladder

The old model was "store brand = the budget option." Now the store brand runs from cheap (Value) through organic (Simple Truth) to premium (bettergoods, Private Selection). Shoppers increasingly don't distinguish "a Kroger brand" from "a brand at Kroger" — so the retailer owns every rung, from budget to indulgence.

What's newly valuable
A full-ladder house brand, higher margins, and direct loyalty that skips the national-brand middleman entirely.
The risk
Too many private tiers can blur the message and confuse shoppers about what each brand stands for.
The single biggest worry

For national brands it's a slow squeeze: once Walmart or Kroger convinces shoppers a premium store label is "good enough" for less, category share erodes — and with grocers earning 1–2% net margins, private-label growth is existential for everyone.

The surprising part

Great Value is already one of America's largest food brands by volume — not a scrappy challenger but a giant quietly outselling beloved names. And launching premium private label reveals the real insight: the store itself is the brand now, so retailers can own the entire price ladder, budget to premium, under one roof.

In one sentence

Walmart and Kroger stopped fighting national brands and started replacing them — becoming multi-tier house brands that own the relationship from cheap to premium.
