Group B · Platforms clone their sellers

The data weapon

The retailer knows more about the sale than the brand does.

This is the deep mechanism behind the whole collection. Retailers see every sale — exactly which products sell, at what price, to whom, and where. They use that data to decide which national brands to clone, how to price the copy, and where to place it on the shelf or page. The middleman knows more than the manufacturer whose product it's copying.

The core bet

Whoever owns the shelf and the transaction data can reverse-engineer any winner into a cheaper own-brand version and place it right next to the original. The brand can never match that information advantage. Shelf plus data is close to unbeatable.

What the numbers say

25–30%
Private label's share of US grocery sales (2024), led by Walmart, Kroger, and Amazon — up 2–3 points a year since 2010.
$50B+
Walmart's private-label revenue (Great Value and others) — over 20% of its total. Kroger's own brands add $30B+. Store brands are the margin engine.
zip-code
Amazon and Walmart see every click and checkout — what sells together, at what price, in which neighborhood. Brands get aggregate panel data months late.
$50B+
Retail-media revenue (Amazon Ads, Walmart Connect, Kroger) in 2024 — the same shopper data, used as a second weapon to bury rivals and promote own-brand.

The insider advantage

A private-label detergent gets placed directly across from Tide, priced 30–40% lower, informed by exact sales-velocity data the retailer alone holds. The brand did the R&D and built the trust; the retailer, watching from the register, harvests both. It's the same shopper data that powers the sibling retail-media boom — one dataset, two weapons.

What's newly valuable
Transaction-level data, shelf and search control, and the ability to clone the exact winners before the brand can react.
What's lost
The brand's only edge — knowing its own market — because the retailer now knows it better.
The single biggest worry

National brands have no way to compete on information parity. They see aggregated reports months late; the retailer knows what's working before the brand does, and can build a cheaper copy while the brand is still planning next quarter's launch.

The surprising part

The retailer doesn't just win on price — it wins on data. Amazon or Walmart knows the exact profile of who buys a given product in a given zip code. When they launch their own version, they're not guessing — they're betting on information the brand's own team can't see. The middleman became the insider.

In one sentence

Retailers with full shopper data can clone winners faster and cheaper than the brands making them — because the shelf plus the data is almost unbeatable.
