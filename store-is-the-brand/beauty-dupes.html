Beauty & dupes

For Gen Z, buying the cheaper copy isn't shameful — it's the flex.

In beauty, younger shoppers actively hunt for "dupes" — cheaper products that duplicate a prestige item — and brands and stores lean in proudly. e.l.f. Cosmetics built an empire on affordable dupes of luxury makeup; TikTok drives "dupe hauls" with millions of views. The old status logic flipped: finding the dupe is a point of pride, not embarrassment.

The core bet

The bet is that "affordable alternative" is now the selling point itself. Instead of hiding that a product copies a prestige item, brands advertise it. For a value-conscious generation, the dupe is smart shopping — and that reframing is a direct threat to the whole prestige-premium model.

What the numbers say

$2B+
e.l.f. Cosmetics (founded 2004, IPO 2023) built a multi-billion-dollar business almost entirely on affordable dupes of prestige makeup.
viral
TikTok "dupe hauls" became a category around 2021–23 — creators openly compare a $3 dupe to a $60 luxury version, racking up millions of views.
no shame
Walmart, Amazon, and drugstores now stock dupes prominently and label them as such — same shelf visibility as the prestige brands they copy.
acquire
Prestige players are responding by launching affordable sub-brands or buying the disruptors, rather than trying to crush them.

The status inversion

The dupe always existed — what changed is that admitting you use one is now the cooler move. Posting a dupe haul signals you're clever and value-savvy; paying full price for prestige signals you fell for the marketing. Gen Z didn't just find cheaper products — it flipped the shame vector. Luxury beauty now has a status problem, not the dupes.

What's newly valuable
The "smart shopper" identity, honest affordable brands, and the ability to advertise a dupe as a feature.
What's draining
The prestige premium — and the assumption that young shoppers will "graduate" to luxury as they earn more.
The single biggest worry

Prestige margins collapse if Gen Z never graduates to luxury. The old pipeline — affordable now, prestige later as a status symbol — is breaking. If dupes stay the default into adulthood, $60 makeup becomes a niche, not the mainstream.

The surprising part

The inversion isn't that dupes exist — it's that using one is now the flex. A dupe haul says you're clever; buying prestige full-price says you got played. Gen Z flipped the status logic entirely, and the luxury brands are the ones left with the image problem.

In one sentence

Gen Z turned the dupe into a status symbol by making the prestige brand the embarrassing choice — collapsing the margin logic that built modern beauty.
