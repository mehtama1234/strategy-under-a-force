Strategy Under a Force

Twenty-four forces reshaping how companies really make money — each in plain, everyday language.

Pick one big force. Ask every company and sector the same question — what did it make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable? Group the answers, and the repeating patterns fall out. Twenty-four collections, each a small self-contained site: a grouped hub, ~13 plain-English write-ups, and a "patterns" synthesis.

The method, once

Every collection follows the same recipe: one force → what it made scarce/cheap/risky/valuable → group by the answer → research → read the patterns. The research was run on a fast, cheap model and the facts kept separate from the hype. Start anywhere — each stands alone.

The pattern behind the patterns

Several of Family II are three views of one machine: your data is at once a loyalty hook, an ad target, and loan collateral — and, in #20, a product in its own right. Others are about chokepoints: the toll-booth taxes what crosses before the sale; lock-in taxes the refill after it; real estate owns the ground it all stands on. Read together, they describe how modern companies quietly turned everyday relationships into recurring, financialized revenue.
