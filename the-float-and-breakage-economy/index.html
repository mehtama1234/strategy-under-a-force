The Float & Breakage Economy

They hold your money before you spend it — and profit most when you never do.

The 46th force. A huge, quiet business runs on money companies hold before you spend it — and money you never spend at all. A Starbucks balance, an airline mile, a security deposit, an insurance premium, an annual subscription: in every case you hand over cash now for something later, and the company gets to use, or keep, the gap. Two hidden profits fall out. Float is a free, interest-free loan from customers — worth a fortune when interest rates are high. Breakage is the slice you forget, let expire, or never claim, and it drops almost entirely to profit. The visible product is coffee, a flight, or coverage; the hidden engine is the billions sitting in the middle.

A fresh mechanism (Tier-6) Not from the Inc. 5000 taxonomy — surfaced by a Haiku discovery-scout pass for genuinely new hidden-mechanism business models. The lens: money held before it's spent, and money never spent, as the real profit engine.
The one question, asked of everyone

What did the float-and-breakage model make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? Your prepaid cash became a free loan the company earns on; the money you forget to claim became near-pure profit; and a dollar you prepay became worth more to the seller than a dollar you spend right away. The danger: the whole model leans on high interest rates, loose rules, and forgetful customers — and rate cuts, escheat and cash-sweep laws, and one-tap digital reminders are quietly eroding all three.
