The patterns

Line up every corner of the float-and-breakage economy — the gift cards, the miles, the deposits, the insurance float, the cash sweep — and the same handful of mechanics repeat, all flowing from one fact: they hold your money first.

Each page asks: what did the float-and-breakage model make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

A quiet profit engine runs on money companies hold before you spend it (float) and money you never spend at all (breakage). It's doubly profitable — the cash earns returns while it's in their hands, and a chunk never leaves at near-100% margin. A dollar you prepay is worth more to the company than a dollar you spend now, because they get to use it, keep part of it, and pay nothing if you never come back. The visible product — coffee, a flight, coverage — is the costume; holding your money is the body.
