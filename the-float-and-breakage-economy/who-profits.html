Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

Who profits

The same hidden engine powers a coffee chain, an airline, an insurer, and a crypto token.

This is the follow-the-money piece. Float and breakage quietly enrich a wide field: retailers (gift cards), airlines and hotels (points), Starbucks (stored value), insurers (premium float), brokerages and payment apps (cash sweep, balances), stablecoin issuers, landlords (deposits), and gyms and subscription firms (prepay plus non-use). It's a hidden profit layer across the whole economy — worth far more when interest rates are high.

The core bet

The money flows to whoever gets to hold your cash before spending it or keep what you never claim — a hidden profit layer under many ordinary businesses.

Many 'product' companies are secretly money-holding businesses

Look under the hood and the pattern is everywhere: an airline doesn't really make money flying you, it makes money selling miles to banks; Starbucks makes money holding your card balance; Buffett makes money investing premiums before claims land; Tether makes money on the dollars parked in its token. The visible product is the costume; holding your cash — and keeping what you forget — is the body.

The single biggest worry

As rates fall the windfall evaporates, and regulators are tightening on every front — escheat laws, cash-sweep suits, stablecoin rules — while digital wallets and instant payments shrink the time money sits idle and the odds you forget it.

The surprising part

The unifying surprise is how unrelated these businesses look and how identical they are underneath. A coffee chain, a trillion-dollar insurer, an airline, a brokerage, and a crypto token are close cousins — each profits from money you give them that sits, earns, or vanishes. Once you see the engine, you see it everywhere.

In one sentence

Hundreds of billions flow yearly to companies that get to invest money you gave them before you spend it — or forget you gave it at all.
