# The Float & Breakage Economy — strategy under a force

The 46th collection (Tier-6, from the Haiku discovery-scout pass) — Family II (hidden-mechanism business models).

**The force / mechanism:** a huge, quiet business runs on money companies hold before you spend it — and money you never spend at all. Gift cards, prepaid balances, deposits, loyalty points, annual subscriptions paid upfront, and insurance premiums all put your cash in someone else's hands first. Two hidden profits fall out: the FLOAT (they hold your money interest-free, or invest it, before they owe you anything) and BREAKAGE (the balances, points, and rebates you never redeem, which become pure profit). We ask: *what did the float-and-breakage model make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** they hold your money before you spend it — and profit most when you never do. A Starbucks gift-card balance, an airline mile, a security deposit, an insurance premium: in every case you hand over cash now for something later, and the company gets to use (or keep) the gap. Float is a free, interest-free loan from customers; breakage is the slice you forget, let expire, or never bother to claim, and it drops almost entirely to the bottom line. The visible product is coffee, or a flight, or coverage; the hidden engine is the billions sitting in the middle.
