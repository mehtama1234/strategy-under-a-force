Group C · Float at scale

The payment float

Tether makes ~$6 billion a year with a tiny team — purely from the dollars parked in its token.

Payment companies hold money in transit and idle balances: PayPal and Venmo balances, prepaid debit cards, remittance holds, merchant settlement timing, and now stablecoin issuers (Tether, Circle) that earn interest on the dollars backing their tokens. The float on money moving through the system is a huge, quiet profit source — supercharged when interest rates are high.

The core bet

Money sitting in the pipes — in-transit funds, idle balances, and reserves backing tokens — can be invested by whoever holds it, so payment companies profit from the timing gap and the balances.

What the numbers say

~$6B
Tether's annual profit (2024), mostly from interest on ~$100B+ of Treasuries backing its USDT token.
~$20B
Customer balances held across PayPal and Venmo — interest income the company keeps.
$400–500M
Circle's (USDC) annual earnings on its reserves — the same reserve-yield model.
2022–24
When the Fed's rate hikes roughly doubled or tripled payment-float income across the industry.

Stablecoin legislation shaping who keeps the yield on the reserves backing tokens.

A payments app is quietly a money-market fund

Tether is a multi-billion-dollar profit machine with fewer than 200 people — it offers no product beyond holding the dollars people parked in its token and earning interest on Treasuries. PayPal and Venmo run a money-market fund on top of a payments app, keeping 100% of the yield and calling it 'platform revenue.' The float is the business; the app is the wrapper.

The single biggest worry

Regulators are asking whether the interest on customer balances and token reserves should go to the customer, and rate cuts in 2024–25 are shrinking the windfall — a business built on holding other people's money is exposed on both fronts.

The surprising part

The purest example in the whole collection is a stablecoin: a company takes your dollar, gives you a token, invests the dollar in Treasuries, and keeps the interest. No lending, no product, no risk to speak of when rates are high — just the yield on money that isn't theirs, at a scale of tens of billions.

In one sentence

Payment and stablecoin firms profit by investing the money customers hand them while it moves or sits — and when rates are high, that profit is enormous.
