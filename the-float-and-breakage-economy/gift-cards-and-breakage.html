Group A · The prepaid float

Gift cards & breakage

A product secretly designed so that a slice of buyers never use it — and that slice is pure profit.

The gift-card industry is enormous, and a meaningful share is never redeemed — 'breakage' that becomes near-pure profit. Retailers get cash upfront (float) and keep the unspent balances (breakage). It's one of the purest float-and-breakage businesses in the economy: a promise sold for money today that a chunk of buyers never collect.

The core bet

Selling a gift card is selling a promise for cash today, and a chunk of those promises are never redeemed — so the retailer gets an interest-free loan plus free money from the cards nobody uses.

What the numbers say

$200B+
Annual U.S. gift-card sales — cash collected upfront across the retail economy.
~3–10%
Share of gift-card value never redeemed (higher for some retailers) — the breakage rate.
~$3B / ~$21B
Unredeemed U.S. gift cards per year, and the estimated lifetime pile of unused balances.
ASC 606
The accounting rule that lets retailers book expected breakage as revenue before it's even abandoned.
~100%
Margin on a never-redeemed card — no cost of goods, no labor, no shipping.

The best outcome is a card that's never used

The economics are unlike almost any other product: a $50 card that's redeemed earns the normal retail margin, but a $50 card that's never redeemed becomes $50 of pure profit. Companies model exactly how many cards will be forgotten and bake that into the plan — it's one of the few products a business wins by the customer not showing up.

The single biggest worry

Consumer-protection and unclaimed-property laws limit expiration and can claw back breakage, and digital cards with app reminders make redemption easier — so the forgetting the model depends on is shrinking.

The surprising part

The quiet absurdity is that billions of dollars of gift cards sit forgotten in drawers and phone apps every year and simply become free profit. A gift card is, in effect, a product designed with the hope that a predictable slice of buyers won't use it — and for decades enough people obliged.

In one sentence

Retailers sell gift cards for cash today, keep the ones you never redeem, and count on enough people forgetting to book billions in free profit a year.
