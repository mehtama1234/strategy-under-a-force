The rebate maze

The awkwardness is engineered — the form is designed to exhaust you into giving up.

Mail-in rebates, cash-back offers, and reward redemptions are often built with friction — forms, deadlines, mailing requirements — so many people never complete them. The 'slippage,' the unredeemed rebates, is the point: the advertised discount that most buyers don't actually get. It's breakage engineered on purpose.

The core bet

Advertise a discount that requires effort to claim, and profit from the large share of customers who never complete the steps — the rebate is a marketing tool that mostly doesn't pay out.

What the numbers say

Historical share of mail-in rebates never redeemed — in some categories, over 60%.
processing firms

Friction was the whole business model

The forms demand the original receipt (already thrown away), the UPC from the box (already recycled), and a postmark by a tight deadline. The goal was never to verify legitimacy — it was to wear you down until you gave up. When digital redemption made claiming a one-tap affair, slippage collapsed and the rebate stopped being profitable, which is exactly why the mail-in version died.

The single biggest worry

Regulators and class-action lawyers now scrutinize deliberately difficult rebates, and digital, transparent redemption drops slippage toward zero — turning the rebate from hidden profit into a real cost.

The surprising part

The tell is what happened when it got easy. A rebate you can claim with one click pays out to almost everyone, so it stopped making money — proof that the annoyance wasn't a flaw in the old system, it was the system. The maze was the product.

In one sentence

Companies advertised discounts they banked on you never claiming — the redemption maze was the profit, and digital transparency is killing it.
