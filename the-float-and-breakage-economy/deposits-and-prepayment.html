Group A · The prepaid float

Deposits & prepayment

Your security deposit is really an interest-free loan you made to a company.

Many businesses collect money before delivering: security deposits, utility and phone deposits, prepaid plans, layaway, and memberships paid upfront. The company holds — and sometimes invests — that cash, and keeps whatever isn't reclaimed. It's float and breakage hiding in the most ordinary corners of commerce.

The core bet

Collect money before you owe the service, hold it (and earn on it), and keep whatever the customer forgets to reclaim — a float-and-breakage engine hidden in ordinary deposits.

What the numbers say

~$45B+
Estimated U.S. renters' security deposits held by landlords — largely interest-free.
$5–10B
Customer deposits held across U.S. utilities and telecoms, often paying customers little or nothing.
~$15B
U.S. prepaid cell-phone market — cash collected upfront before service is used.
~$58B
Money sitting in U.S. state unclaimed-property accounts — much of it forgotten deposits and refunds.
Rhino · Jetty
Deposit-alternative startups that arose to free up renters' cash — shrinking the float pool.

The tenant lends money to the landlord

Collectively, renters have made a tens-of-billions-dollar interest-free loan to landlords, and for years many states let the landlord keep any interest earned on it. 'Your deposit' isn't your money sitting safe — it's cash you handed over that someone else gets to hold and use until you remember to ask for it back.

The single biggest worry

Regulation is tightening — more states now require returning deposits promptly and paying interest — and deposit-alternative products are shrinking the pool, so the easy float is getting harder to keep.

The surprising part

The reframe is simple but rarely noticed: a deposit is a loan, and you're the lender who charges nothing. Today's tenant is quietly financing tomorrow's landlord; the utility customer is financing the utility. It's a wealth transfer hidden inside the fine print of everyday contracts.

In one sentence

Businesses turn ordinary customers into unwitting bankers by collecting deposits upfront and keeping whatever you forget to reclaim.
