Escrow & cash sweep

A brokerage can make more from your idle cash than from trading — which is how 'free' trading got paid for.

When a broker holds your uninvested cash, a bank holds escrow, or a title company holds earnest money, they earn the interest on it — often paying you almost nothing. This 'cash sweep' spread became hugely lucrative when interest rates rose in 2022–24, drawing lawsuits and regulatory scrutiny. It's the same float engine, hidden inside brokerages, payment apps, and escrow.

The core bet

Hold customers' idle cash and earn the interest yourself, paying them little — a spread business hidden inside brokerages, payment apps, and escrow.

'Free' trading was funded by your idle cash

Zero-commission trading looked like generosity, but a major brokerage can make more from parking your uninvested billions than from any trade. The float became the real business, hidden inside a 'free' product — you got no-fee trades, and the firm quietly kept the interest on the cash you left sitting.

The single biggest worry

Lawsuits and regulators are targeting brokerages for steering cash into low-yield accounts they own and paying customers too little — and customers are wising up, moving idle cash into higher-yield options ('cash sorting') that drains the captive float.

The surprising part

The reveal is that the fee you don't pay is paid another way. When trading went free, the money didn't disappear — it moved to the spread on your sleeping cash, a charge you never see on a statement because it's simply interest you didn't earn.

In one sentence

When you leave cash idle in a brokerage, escrow, or payment app, the company earns the interest — often ~5% while paying you nothing — a spread so fat it can beat trading itself.
