Group B · Points as a currency

The annual prepay

Costco makes almost all its profit from the upfront membership — the groceries are the moat, not the money.

Companies push customers to pay for a year upfront — annual software plans, Amazon Prime, Costco memberships, gym dues, annual passes. That hands the company all the cash on day one (float and deferred revenue), locks in the customer, and profits most from those who prepay and then barely use it. The unused gym membership is the model in one image.

The core bet

Get the whole year's payment upfront — you hold the cash, lock in the customer, and profit most from the ones who pay and then don't use it.

What the numbers say

~2 months free
The typical annual-plan discount — the price of grabbing your cash a year early.
$15–40B
Deferred revenue big SaaS firms (Salesforce, Microsoft, Adobe) each hold — prepaid cash working on the balance sheet.
~$4.8B
Costco's annual membership-fee revenue — roughly half or more of its operating profit.
$139/yr
Amazon Prime's upfront fee — billions collected before a single package ships.
~50%
Share of gym members who pay and rarely show up — 'breakage' via non-attendance.

Priced assuming most people won't use it

Gyms and warehouse clubs are priced on the assumption that many members won't use what they paid for. A gym sets its dues knowing that of 100 who join in January, half will stop coming by March but stay charged; Costco charges upfront, sells groceries near cost, and pockets the fee. The product is the moat; the prepay is the profit.

The single biggest worry

Annual prepay raises cancellation and refund friction — exactly what regulators are targeting with 'click to cancel' and auto-renewal rules — and it can mask churn, hiding customers who've quietly stopped using the service until renewal.

The surprising part

The counter-intuitive fact is that the best customer is often the one who barely comes. Costco's real business isn't groceries and a gym's isn't fitness — it's the upfront fee from people who join with good intentions. Prepay converts hope and forgetfulness directly into cash on day one.

In one sentence

Companies discount annual plans to grab your cash on day one — and profit most from the people who pay and then stop using it.
