Group A · The rewards flywheel

Starbucks — a bank that happens to sell coffee

Load $25 onto the app and Starbucks gets an interest-free loan. Do that a billion times over.

Starbucks Rewards is the clearest example of the loyalty flywheel — and it has a twist nobody else does. When you load money onto the Starbucks app or a gift card, you hand the company cash before you've bought anything. Multiply that across millions of customers and Starbucks is sitting on a pile of prepaid money that behaves exactly like bank deposits.

The float

~$1.5B
Customer money sitting in Starbucks app and gift-card balances — more prepaid "deposits" than many small banks hold. It's an interest-free loan from customers, and some of it is never redeemed.
~40%
Share of US company-operated sales driven by Rewards members — the loyal core that comes back again and again.

Why it matters

Three things at once

The app gives Starbucks (1) frequency — members visit more; (2) data — it knows your order and can personalize offers; and (3) float — that ~$1.5B of prepaid money it holds and invests before you spend it. On the balance sheet, this stored value is booked as a liability, like a deposit — which is why people only half-joke that Starbucks is "a bank that sells coffee."

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The float only works while customers keep topping up faster than they spend. If loyalty cools, or people stop pre-loading the app, the free money flows back out — and heavy discounting to keep members engaged can eat the very margins loyalty was meant to protect.

The surprising part

The coffee is almost beside the point. What Starbucks really built is a payment-and-deposits business wrapped around a café — customers pre-pay, forget, and lend it money for free, while the app quietly logs every order for targeting. Fifteen years ago, the idea that a coffee chain's most bank-like asset would be its gift-card balance would have sounded absurd.

In one sentence

Starbucks in a nutshell

Starbucks turned its app into a loyalty-and-payments machine that holds ~$1.5B of customers' prepaid cash interest-free — making it, in effect, a small bank that happens to sell coffee.
