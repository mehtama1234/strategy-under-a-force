Group B · Pay to belong

Amazon Prime — shipping is the bait; ads are the business

You think you're paying for fast delivery. You're really being locked into a data-and-ad machine.

Prime looks like a deal on shipping. It's really a loyalty flywheel and a lock-in. The ~$139/year fee is almost beside the point — the goal is to make you spend far more, shop nowhere else, and generate the data that powers Amazon's real profit engine: advertising.

The lock-in

~220M / 2.5–3.5×
Prime members worldwide (2024), who spend roughly 2.5–3.5× more per year than non-members. Renewal rates top 90% in mature markets.

Amazon bundles video, music, groceries, photo storage, and deals into Prime — each one raising the cost of ever leaving.

The real profit engine

Where the money actually is

Fast shipping is a loss leader — the $139 barely covers it. The profit machine is Amazon's advertising business (~$50B+ a year, up from ~$14B in 2019): ads shown to you mid-search and on product pages, sold against the shopping data of 200M+ loyal customers. In 2024 Amazon even started putting ads inside Prime Video. Amazon's retail-media ads generate several times more profit per member than shipping does.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the flywheel can reverse

If ads get too intrusive (especially on Prime Video) and Prime stops feeling "premium," churn could spike. And the flywheel runs backward: lower retention → fewer members → fewer ad impressions → less advertiser money per member.

The surprising part

Everyone thinks Prime is about cheap, fast shipping. Amazon's own math says shipping is the bait and ads are the trap. The membership fee exists mostly to change your behavior — to make you default to Amazon for everything — so it can watch what you buy and sell that attention to brands. The delivery van is the marketing cost; the ad slot is the product.

In one sentence

Amazon Prime in a nutshell

Prime locks in ~220M customers with a shipping promise, then monetizes them through data and a ~$50B ad business — turning a break-even logistics service into Amazon's fastest-growing, highest-margin engine.
