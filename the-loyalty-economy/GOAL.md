# The Loyalty Economy — strategy under a force

The tenth "strategy under a force" collection (after AI, Ozempic, the US–China decoupling, the end of cheap money, the energy transition, the demographic cliff, the climate bill, the attention economy, and the remote shift). This one grew out of a McDonald's annual-report thread — the "old-school company that's really a data/tech company now" archetype.

**The force:** companies stopped selling a *product* and started owning a *relationship*. For a century, the sale was the end of the story — you bought the burger, the coffee, the flight, and left. Now the sale is just the doorway. The real goal is to enroll you — an app, points, a membership, a stored balance — and turn a one-off transaction into a recurring, data-rich relationship. We ask of each company or sector: *what did the loyalty economy make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** the product is the **bait**; the relationship is the **business**. Once a company enrolls you, it monetizes that relationship many ways at once — more frequent visits, personalized upsell, **first-party data** (which got priceless the moment tracking cookies died), **retail-media ads** sold against your own shopping, interest-free **"float"** on prepaid balances, and — most extreme — **selling your loyalty points in bulk to a credit-card bank**. Again and again, the loyalty program, the membership, the data, and the float turn out to be worth **more than the product itself** — sometimes worth more than the entire company. Value flows to whoever owns the customer, the app, and the data; it drains from whoever's stuck selling a commodity through someone else's channel.

**Groups:**
- **A — The rewards flywheel (points → frequency → data):** McDonald's · Starbucks · Domino's
- **B — Pay to belong (membership):** Costco · Amazon Prime · Nike
- **C — Loyalty worth more than the business:** airline miles · hotel loyalty · casinos
- **D — Monetizing the relationship:** grocery & retail media · credit-card points · subscriptions everywhere
- **E — The big picture:** the-loyalty-economy (capstone) + patterns

**Method / constraints:**
- Research on Haiku, done directly by each agent (NO sub-agents), facts separated from hype.
- Plain, everyday language. No jargon, no clichés. Short sentences, real numbers with dates, concrete examples.
- Self-contained dark-theme HTML pages sharing styles.css, grouped hub + patterns synthesis, cross-linked to the nine sibling collections (esp. the attention economy — first-party data / retail media).
