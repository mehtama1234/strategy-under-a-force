Group C · Loyalty worth more than the business

Hotel loyalty — the points IOU now rivals the rooms

Marriott and Hilton discovered the points-and-card business can out-earn the actual hotels.

Hotels run the same play as airlines. Marriott Bonvoy and Hilton Honors don't just reward stays — they sell points in bulk to credit-card banks (co-brand cards), turning loyalty into a high-margin financial business. And it's grown so big it now rivals the money the hotels make from actual rooms.

The scale

295M+ members
Marriott Bonvoy enrollment (2026), driving 80%+ of the company's revenue. Its co-branded credit-card revenue is climbing fast — approaching the scale of the fees it earns from the hotels themselves.

The unredeemed points are a giant liability on the books — one estimate put Marriott's points "IOU" at ~$11.6 billion — money owed to members in future free nights.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the owners revolt

Here's the tension: Marriott and Hilton mostly don't own their hotels — franchisees and owners do. As the card-and-points business balloons past the room business, those owners are demanding a cut (as flared up in 2026), arguing the parent company is profiting from a loyalty machine built on their buildings.

The surprising part

The loyalty program quietly became a separate, richer business bolted onto the hotel chain — one that earns steady, high-margin card fees while the messy, capital-heavy work of running hotels is done by someone else. Just like airlines, the hotel giant is drifting toward being a brand-and-points licensing company that collects fees, with the rooms as the raw material that generates the points.

In one sentence

Hotel loyalty in a nutshell

Marriott and Hilton turned their loyalty programs into billion-dollar points-and-card businesses that now rival the room business itself — so much so that the hotel owners are demanding their share.
