Group D · The lock itself

The repair monopoly — control the aftermarket, own the customer

The deepest lock-in: control the parts and service so tightly that customers must pay you for the product's whole life.

The deepest form of lock-in is controlling the aftermarket — parts, service, repair — so tightly that customers must pay whatever you ask for the rest of a product's life. And the law has recognized this as a real form of monopoly power for over 30 years.

The legal backbone

Kodak, 1992
A landmark US Supreme Court case established that a company can have monopoly power in the aftermarket for its own products' parts and service — even if it competes in the main market — because once you've bought the machine, you're locked to its parts.
~$1.6T
The global aftermarket parts & service market — often 2–3× more profitable than the original product sales over a product's lifetime.

The FTC's "Nixing the Fix" report (2021) documented how makers restrict repair; John Deere, Apple, and others faced rising scrutiny.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

As products become software-defined, the lock shifts from physical parts to code — makers can flip a switch to disable third-party batteries or tools without replacing anything, and no court has fully solved how antitrust law applies to code-based monopolies.

The surprising part

The law already knew this 30+ years ago. Kodak (1992) said explicitly that aftermarket monopoly power is legally separate from main-market competition. Yet makers kept doubling down — iPhone parts-pairing, tractor firmware, printer chips — because enforcement was sparse. The surprise isn't the rule; it's how long regulators took to act on it. The whole "locked in" economy rests on this one idea: control what happens after the sale.

In one sentence

The repair monopoly in a nutshell

The repair monopoly is the deepest lock-in because the law says you can monopolize your own product's parts and service after the sale — and modern software makes that monopoly both invisible and nearly unbreakable.
