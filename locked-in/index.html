Locked In

How the sale became the bait — and the refill, the service, and the repair became the business.

The fifteenth force — from the discovery scout's queue (merging razor-and-blades with the aftermarket/service model, because they're one mechanism). Sell the device cheap, then make the real money on the thing you can't do without afterward — and make sure you can't buy it anywhere else. The one idea: give away the razor, sell the blades forever.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did lock-in make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money for this company? The one-time sale is the bait; the recurring, captive refill, service, or spare part is the profit — and the whole game is making sure (with proprietary parts, chips, firmware, and "parts pairing") that you can't get it from anyone else.
