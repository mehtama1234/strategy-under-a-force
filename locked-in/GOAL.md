# Locked In — strategy under a force

The fifteenth "strategy under a force" collection (merges the scout's "razor-and-blades" and "aftermarket / power-by-the-hour" — they're one mechanism).

**The force:** the sale is just the beginning. Sell the device cheap (or even at a loss), then make the real money on the thing you can't do without afterward — the refill, the service contract, the spare part — and lock the customer so they can't buy it anywhere else. We ask: *what did the lock-in economy make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** give away the razor to sell the blades forever. Printers are cheap; the ink costs more than champagne. A jet engine is sold near cost; the airline pays "power by the hour" for decades of service. A tractor is yours, but only the dealer can fix it. The one-time sale is the bait; the recurring, captive follow-on — the consumable, the service, the part — is the profit, and the whole game is making sure you *can't* get it from anyone else. Value flows to whoever controls the refill and the repair; it drains from the customer, who owns the device but not the freedom to use or fix it cheaply. The battleground now is **right-to-repair** — the fight over who's allowed to service the thing you bought.
