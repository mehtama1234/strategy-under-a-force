Group B · The machine, then the meter

Tractors & equipment — own the tractor, but you can't fix it

A farmer can own a $500,000 tractor, have the part and the skill — and still not be allowed to repair it.

Farm and construction equipment makers, especially John Deere, make big money on parts and service — and increasingly lock farmers out of repairing their own machines. Modern tractors are full of software; many repairs require a Deere-authorized dealer with proprietary diagnostic software. The farmer can physically fix the part, but the tractor won't run until a dealer "authorizes" it.

The lock

$2B+
Deere's annual parts & service business — often bigger than new-equipment sales. Dealer service calls run $300–$1,000+, using Deere's proprietary "Service Advisor" software.
  • 2021–22: farmers bought hacked Ukrainian firmware to bypass Deere's locks — risking farming illegally rather than abandon a broken machine.
  • 2023: Deere signed a deal with the Farm Bureau promising more repair access — but kept emission-control settings dealer-locked.
  • 2026: an FTC settlement forced Deere to open its repair software for the next 10 years.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

A farmer can own the machine, have the skill and the parts, and still lose a harvest because the tractor won't start until a dealer 500 miles away authorizes the repair — a single point of failure with no legal workaround.

The surprising part

The tractor is fully owned by the farmer but not controlled by them. The hacked-firmware episode revealed the truth: the locks were never about safety or security — they were purely about protecting the repair revenue. Farmers were willing to break the law to fix their own machines rather than pay Deere's toll, which is why it became a national political fight and the seed of the whole right-to-repair movement.

In one sentence

Tractors & equipment in a nutshell

John Deere sells farmers a $500,000 machine but withholds the software permission to fix it — turning repair into a permanent, high-margin lock and sparking a national right-to-repair war.
