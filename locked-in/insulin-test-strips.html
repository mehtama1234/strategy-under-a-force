Group C · Health & tech lock-in

Insulin & test strips — the blade is survival

Razor-and-blades at its cruelest: the refill is proprietary, non-negotiable, and you die without it.

Diabetes care is full of lock-in where the "refill" is a matter of life and death. Blood-glucose meters are cheap or free, but the test strips are proprietary (only that brand fits that meter) and cost a fortune. Insulin pens use proprietary cartridges. And insulin itself became infamous for price gouging — because the customer can't switch, and can't skip.

The cruelest lock

~1000%
Rise in insulin's list price over ~two decades (from ~$25–35 to $150–300+/month) — for a century-old drug — via patent "evergreening" and proprietary pens. Three firms control ~90% of the market.
  • Test strips ($1–3 each) are brand-locked and quantity-capped, so patients ration testing.
  • Deaths: Alec Smith (28, uninsured) rationed insulin and died in 2018 — outrage led to a $35/month cap (Medicare, 2023; then some manufacturers/states).

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The $35 cap plugs only the Medicare leak. The largest group — working-age uninsured diabetics — still face $150–300/month, and device lock-in blocks any escape to cheaper alternatives. Ration or die.

The surprising part

This isn't price-gouging on a luxury — it's price-gouging on survival, made possible by proprietary devices. Makers bet that diabetics have zero price sensitivity (you can't choose to skip insulin), and they were right — until social media made a 28-year-old's death an unavoidable political fact. The economics were always this cruel; policy just didn't catch the lock-in until there was a body count.

In one sentence

Insulin & test strips in a nutshell

Diabetes care is healthcare's cruelest razor-and-blades — meters are loss leaders, strips and pens are proprietary consumables you need to live, and prices rose ~10× with no technical change until a death made it politically toxic.
