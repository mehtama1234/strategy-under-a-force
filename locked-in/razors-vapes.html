Group A · The classic razor-and-blades

Razors & vapes — a 120-year-old trick, now with addiction

Gillette invented the model in 1903. Juul cloned it a century later — and added a second, chemical lock.

This is where the phrase comes from. King Gillette (1903) sold the razor handle cheap and made the money on the disposable blades, at high margin and locked to the handle. Juul cloned it 112 years later: cheap device, expensive proprietary nicotine pods — and this time the refill itself is chemically addictive.

Old and new

  • Gillette (1903+): the razor sold cheap; blades kept adding blades (2→3→5) and climbing from ~$1 to $4–5 each — until Dollar Shave Club (2012, sold to Unilever for $1B in 2016) undercut it with cheap subscription blades.
  • Juul (2015): cheap device, proprietary high-nicotine pods (only Juul pods fit); ~70%+ of the US vape market by 2018.
  • The youth crisis: Juul flooded schools; 39 states sued; it paid a $438.5M settlement (2022).

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Proprietary refills trap you in a high-margin moat that blocks cheaper rivals and lets prices rise without innovation. With nicotine, that trap becomes chemical — you can't switch even if you want to.

The surprising part

The razor-and-blades model is 120+ years old and still works — but Juul weaponized it. Gillette's lock was mechanical (blades fit only our handle); Juul added a second lock that's biological. The device is the bait, the proprietary pod is the profit, and the nicotine itself is the ultimate lock-in. The backlash didn't kill the model — it just proved that when the refill is addictive, regulators and juries notice.

In one sentence

Razors & vapes in a nutshell

Cheap device, expensive proprietary refill is a century-old trick that Juul perfected by making the refill addictive — and then lost by doing it too visibly.
