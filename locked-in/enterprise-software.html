Group C · Health & tech lock-in

Enterprise software — re-buy the software every five years

Oracle and SAP make the real money on "maintenance" — and use audits as a weapon to keep you paying.

Big enterprise software vendors (Oracle, SAP, IBM) make enormous, high-margin recurring money on "maintenance and support" — typically ~20–22% of the license price per year, so you effectively re-buy the software every ~5 years. Once your company runs its core operations on their system, switching is so risky and costly that you're captive, and the fees only rise.

The lock

~20–22%/yr
Annual maintenance fee on the license price. A $1M license costs ~$200k/year in support — so after five years you've re-paid for software that hasn't materially changed.
  • Oracle's audits: it audits hundreds of companies a year, finds them "out of compliance," and hits them with $5M–100M+ bills — a lever to force upgrades or cloud migration.
  • Switching cost: ripping out SAP/Oracle costs $50M–500M and 2–4 years — so nobody does it.
  • The cloud version: "data gravity" — cheap to put data in, ~$0.09/GB to move it out (AWS egress fees).

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Once you're running payroll and finance on Oracle/SAP, the vendor knows switching is a multi-year, billion-dollar gamble — so it can raise fees with near-impunity and use audits as a threat to extract more.

The surprising part

"Maintenance" was sold as bundled support and patches, but it became pure margin — charged annually on software that hasn't meaningfully changed in years, with zero leverage to negotiate because leaving is unthinkable. The cloud is the same trap in modern dress: nearly free to enter, ruinously expensive to leave. Data gravity is just the digital version of "we own the refill."

In one sentence

Enterprise software in a nutshell

Enterprise software is a lock-in machine where ~20%/yr "maintenance" means you re-buy it every five years — and because switching is too costly, vendors wield audits and cloud egress fees to keep you captive.
