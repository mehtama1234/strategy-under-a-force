The Compute Build-Out

AI made raw computing power the scarce thing — so the software revolution now bottlenecks on chips, power, and concrete.

The 40th force. For fifty years computing got cheaper and more abundant every year, so software could scale almost for free. AI broke that. Training and running models needs staggering amounts of specialized chips, giant buildings to hold them, and electricity and cooling on a scale the grid was never built for. Suddenly the bottleneck to the software revolution is physical — silicon, copper, megawatts, water — and the money pools with whoever owns those scarce inputs while the model-makers spend fortunes betting the economics eventually close. The one idea: when compute became scarce, the profit moved to whoever owns the chip, the power, and the building.

Seeded by the Inc. 5000 data "AI Infrastructure & Tooling" is a top fast-growing theme — 575 companies, ~$31.5B represented — and the GPU shortage and data-center power crunch run through the whole list. This collection follows that money.
The one question, asked of everyone

What did the compute build-out make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? The GPU, the gigawatt, and the data-center building became the scarce things; the picks-and-shovels suppliers (memory, cooling, power) became quiet giants; and cheap, abundant computing became a memory. The danger: the spend is hundreds of billions ahead of AI revenue, the financing is increasingly circular, and the chips everyone's borrowing against lose a third of their value every year.
