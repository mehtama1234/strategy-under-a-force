Group B · The silicon & the toll

The neoclouds

Buy a mountain of GPUs on borrowed money, rent them by the hour — and pray the chips don't decay first.

A new category of 'GPU cloud' companies — CoreWeave, Lambda, Crusoe, Nebius — buys billions of dollars of Nvidia chips and rents them by the hour, often financed by debt secured against the GPUs themselves. They grew explosively, but they carry heavy leverage and dangerous customer concentration, and the collateral loses value fast.

The core bet

If GPUs are scarce, buy a huge pile of them on borrowed money and rent them out — become the AWS of AI compute before the giants lock the supply up.

Borrowing against assets that expire

The fragile core: a company borrows billions to buy H100s at ~$40k each, but in two years they're ~$15k chips and the debt is still due. Success depends not just on rental demand but on obsolescence staying slow enough to repay the loan before the chip is worthless — a razor-thin bet. And Nvidia sometimes invests in these buyers of its own chips, a circular loop.

The single biggest worry

The debt is secured against fast-depreciating GPUs, so even a small drop in rental prices or demand can break the math. Heavy reliance on one or two giant customers means a single defection can be fatal.

The surprising part

The unsettling part is what the loans are really backed by: chips that may be near-obsolete before they're paid off. It's vendor-financed in places too — Nvidia funding the customers who buy its GPUs — so the same enthusiasm props up both demand and the financing, which is exactly what makes it fragile.

In one sentence

Neoclouds bet they can borrow billions against chips to undercut in-house GPUs — but debt secured on fast-decaying hardware breaks if demand or prices dip even slightly.
