Group A · The physical build-out

Cooling & the plant

The faster the chips run, the more the game is won by whoever moves water and electrons through the racks.

Packing thousands of hot GPUs into a building creates a massive heat-and-power problem, and solving it became a fast-growing, high-margin business: liquid cooling, power distribution, backup generators, water systems. It's the unglamorous 'picks and shovels' layer of the build-out — chipmakers get the headlines, but the companies that move heat and electrons through the racks capture outsized value.

The core bet

AI chips run so hot and hungry that the plumbing around them — cooling, power delivery, backup — became a scarce, high-margin business. The boring layer turned into one of the best returns.

The hardest problem is getting the heat out of the building

A campus with 100,000 GPUs throws off as much waste heat as a power plant, and moving it needs chillers, pipe networks, and water treatment that take 18+ months to build. That's why sleepy electrical and cooling firms — Vertiv, ABB, chiller makers — have often been more capital-constrained and higher-return than the chip and cloud companies themselves.

The single biggest worry

The whole market exists only while the build-out accelerates — a slowdown in GPU orders collapses cooling demand overnight. Water scarcity and community backlash in dry regions are also starting to gum up permitting and force redesigns.

The surprising part

The counter-intuitive lesson: some of the best AI returns came from companies with no AI at all — the ones that just pump water and balance electrical loads. AI's real physical constraint in 2025 wasn't chips or code; it was thermodynamics, and the firms that mastered the heat quietly rode the boom.

In one sentence

The faster AI chips run, the more the winners are the unglamorous companies that pump water and electrons through the racks, not the chipmakers themselves.
