Group A · The physical build-out

The data-center boom

The software revolution flipped: the constraint is no longer code, it's concrete, cooling, and megawatts.

Training and running AI needs enormous buildings packed with GPUs. Hyperscalers — Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta — plus specialists and giant projects like the OpenAI/Oracle 'Stargate' are spending hundreds of billions to build them. The scarce inputs are land, power, and the buildings themselves, and for the first time the building has become a strategic weapon.

The core bet

AI made compute scarce, so whoever can build data centers fast — with power and cooling already locked in — captures the boom. The building itself became a strategic asset, not just plumbing.

A data center is a power plant with computers attached

For decades software 'ate the world' because it scaled almost for free — add a million users, add a bit of server cost. AI reversed it. Now the limiting factor isn't the code, it's the kilowatt, and every breakthrough slams into the same ceiling: can you get the power and pour the concrete in time?

The single biggest worry

If AI demand disappoints — training gains plateau or inference gets cheap faster than expected — the industry faces a massive overbuild. More immediately, power and land permitting in the hottest markets (Virginia, Texas) can stop projects cold before a single rack is installed.

The surprising part

The reversal is the whole story: the most abstract, weightless industry on earth is now gated by the most physical things imaginable — poured concrete, water, and megawatts. A data center looks like a tech asset but behaves like heavy industry, and the winners are the ones who secured the power first.

In one sentence

The race to build data centers faster than power grids can feed them is now the bottleneck deciding which AI company wins.
