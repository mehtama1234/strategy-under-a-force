Group B · The silicon & the toll

Picks & shovels

Behind Nvidia sits another scarce layer — the memory, packaging, and networking every chip depends on.

Behind Nvidia sits a deeper set of bottlenecks: high-bandwidth memory (HBM) from SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron; advanced packaging from TSMC (CoWoS); and networking — switches and interconnects — from the likes of Broadcom and Arista. These scarce suppliers gate the whole build-out and quietly capture enormous value, often with zero public attention.

The core bet

Every AI chip needs scarce memory, packaging, and networking. The companies that own those bottlenecks tax the boom just like Nvidia does — only quieter.

The sleepy memory business found pricing power

Memory was long a brutal, commodity, boom-bust business where price wars ate profits. AI flipped it: HBM demand outran supply, and SK Hynix and Samsung suddenly charge 40–60% premiums because customers have no alternative. Broadcom, meanwhile, quietly became an AI giant by selling the networking plumbing nobody notices.

The single biggest worry

Memory and packaging are historically cyclical — a demand slowdown can flip today's severe shortage into a painful glut by 2027, collapsing prices and profits overnight. The pricing power is real but rented from the cycle.

The surprising part

The overlooked truth is that the AI boom doesn't just enrich Nvidia — it taxes the entire supply chain through the scarce, expensive glue that holds systems together. The most cyclical, unloved corners of the chip world (memory, packaging) suddenly hold the leverage, at least until the cycle turns.

In one sentence

The AI boom taxes the whole supply chain through the memory, packaging, and networking suppliers who control the scarce glue between chips.
