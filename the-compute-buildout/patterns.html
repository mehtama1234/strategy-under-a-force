The patterns

Line up every corner of the AI build-out — data centers, power, chips, memory, neoclouds, the capex loop — and the same handful of mechanics repeat, all flowing from one fact: AI made compute the scarce thing.

Each page asks: what did the compute build-out make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

For fifty years computing got cheaper every year, so software scaled almost for free. AI reversed it: models need so much compute that chips, power, and concrete became the bottleneck to the software revolution. So the profit moved to whoever owns the scarce physical inputs — the chip, the gigawatt, the building — while the model-makers spend fortunes betting the economics eventually close. The whole thing is a race between falling cost-per-token and rising spend, and nobody yet knows which wins.
