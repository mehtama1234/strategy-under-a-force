Group C · The money machine

The capex super-cycle

$700 billion a year — and increasingly, the giants lend each other the money to keep buying.

The biggest tech companies are spending unprecedented amounts on AI infrastructure, and the money increasingly moves in circles: Nvidia invests in OpenAI, which commits to buy compute from Oracle and CoreWeave, which buy Nvidia chips. Critics warn this 'circular financing' echoes the fiber-optic overbuild of the dot-com bust. A handful of firms' spending is now the engine of the whole stock market.

The core bet

Whoever spends the most on compute wins the AI race, so the giants pour hundreds of billions into infrastructure — and increasingly finance each other to keep the flywheel spinning.

What the numbers say

The same dollar can count as revenue several times

As money loops — Microsoft funds OpenAI, which buys from CoreWeave, which buys Nvidia, which invests back in OpenAI — each hop can show up as 'revenue' somewhere, inflating the appearance of demand. And a dozen executives' capex decisions now move the entire market: strip out AI and the index's two-year gain shrinks from 142% to 16%.

The single biggest worry

If AI revenue doesn't catch up to the spend, the circular deals unwind. Hyperscalers borrowed heavily just to keep spending; if creditors pull back, the build-out stops and you're left with dark data centers and depreciating chips — the uncomfortable echo of 1999–2001.

The surprising part

The vertigo-inducing part is the circularity: the boom increasingly funds itself, with the same firms appearing as investor, customer, and supplier to one another. That can look like roaring demand right up until the loop needs real, outside revenue to close — and that's the test nobody has passed yet.

In one sentence

Tech giants are spending ~$700B a year on AI by lending each other the money and buying each other's services, betting real revenue appears before the bill comes due.
