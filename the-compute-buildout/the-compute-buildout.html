The compute build-out

The most abstract industry on earth turned out to be gated by the most physical things — atoms strike back.

Pull the whole collection together and one line runs through it. The AI boom turned raw computing power from abundant to scarce, and triggered the largest infrastructure build-out in tech history — data centers, GPUs, power, cooling, memory, neoclouds, and a capex super-cycle. Because compute became the scarce thing, the money pools with whoever owns the physical inputs — chips, power, buildings, memory — while the model-makers spend fortunes and bet the economics eventually close.

The core bet

AI made compute the scarce thing, the software revolution now bottlenecks on chips, power, and concrete, and profit pools with whoever owns those inputs — not necessarily whoever writes the code.

Winners and losers

The build-out sorts cleanly into who owns the scarce physical thing and who's betting the demand shows up.

Who ends up ahead

Owners of scarce inputs — Nvidia, TSMC, the memory and networking suppliers, the power and cooling vendors, and the data-center landlords — plus whoever locks up gigawatts of electricity.

Who's exposed

Money-losing AI apps that can't cover their compute bill, over-levered neoclouds holding depreciating chips, and late-cycle overbuilders left with dark capacity when demand or efficiency shifts.

The single biggest worry

The spend is hundreds of billions ahead of AI revenue, the financing is increasingly circular, and a demand or efficiency shock could strand the hardware. The boom funds itself for now; the unanswered question is whether real, outside revenue arrives to close the loop.

The surprising part

The note to end on is the great reversal. For two decades tech was the most weightless thing on earth — pure software, scaling for free. AI made the whole industry depend on the most concrete things imaginable: rare chips, power grids, cooling water, poured concrete. The companies that win now own foundries, power, and real estate — the opposite of what made the 1990s boom work. Atoms turned out to be scarcer than code.

In one sentence

AI made compute the world's most valuable scarce resource, so trillions now flow to whoever owns chips, power, and concrete instead of whoever writes the best software.
