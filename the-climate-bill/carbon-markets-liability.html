Group D · The big shifts

Carbon markets & liability — the fight over who pays the bill

If a hotter planet is a bill, this is the messy, contested fight over who actually pays it.

Climate change made the atmosphere scarce — turning pollution from a free waste dump into something that ought to be priced. Several mechanisms try to fold the cost of emissions back onto whoever caused them. All of them are patchy, gamed, and politically contested, because the fossil-fuel economy still has the power to weaken them.

The tools, and their cracks

  • Carbon pricing: the EU's Emissions Trading System held around €80–90/tonne (2024) — real pressure — and its new carbon border tax (CBAM) charges imports for their carbon (phasing in through 2026). But it covers only part of emissions and exempts big sectors.
  • Voluntary offsets: boomed to ~$2B/year, then collapsed in 2023 when investigations found ~70% of the biggest verifier's rainforest credits were essentially worthless. Trust — and prices — cratered.
  • Climate lawsuits: a Dutch court ordered Shell to cut emissions 45% (2021) — the high-water mark — but an appeals court overturned it in 2024.
  • ESG investing: peaked around $35T (2021), then hit a political backlash (2022–24) that revealed how thin the consensus was.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — expensive theater

If carbon prices stay weak and lawsuits keep losing on appeal, emissions won't actually fall — the mechanisms become costly theater that hits consumers and small emitters while big fossil-fuel firms pass the cost through and lobby for exemptions.

The surprising part

The offset collapse exposed that "the market will fix it" was never real — the verifiers earned money on volume, so they had every reason to inflate credits, and nobody could cheaply audit a rainforest. The same auditing problem haunts any scheme built on self-reporting. And there's an uncomfortable twist: carbon pricing is regressive — it works precisely because it's brutal, taxing ordinary people's energy and heating bills, while oil executives are largely untouched. The tool that's meant to make polluters pay ends up landing hardest on the people with the least.

In one sentence

Carbon markets & liability in a nutshell

These are the attempts to make polluters pay the climate bill — carbon prices, offsets, lawsuits — but all are weak or gamed enough that the fossil-fuel economy is outrunning them while appearing to cooperate.
