Group C · The adaptation winners

Air conditioning — from luxury to survival, and the trap inside it

As deadly heat spreads, cooling stops being a comfort and becomes a matter of life and death.

As the planet heats and deadly heatwaves spread, air conditioning shifts from a luxury to a necessity for survival — a rare, defensive growth market where demand is driven not by preference but by rising death tolls. The big makers (Carrier, Daikin, Trane, Johnson Controls) win. But cooling carries a trap inside it.

The boom

2B → 5.5B
Air-conditioning units worldwide, today to ~2050 (IEA) — roughly tripling, driven by hot, fast-growing countries like India, across Southeast Asia and Africa.

Heatwave deaths have climbed sharply; 2023 was the hottest year on record; India saw hundreds of official heat deaths in a single early-summer stretch (the true toll far higher). For much of the world, AC is becoming the difference between a livable home and a dangerous one.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the vicious cycle

Cooling runs on electricity, still largely fossil-fueled. So every bit of new AC demand burns more fuel → more warming → more demand for AC. On top of that, the refrigerant gases inside these units are themselves potent greenhouse gases (hundreds to thousands of times stronger than CO₂). Cooling ourselves is quietly heating the planet.

The equity gap

Cooling protects those who can afford it. Heat kills the poor and the elderly who can't — and demand is highest in exactly the hot, poor countries with the weakest power grids and the least ability to pay. The people who did least to cause the warming bear the most of its heat, and often can't afford the very thing that would keep them safe.

The surprising part

The winners here aren't slick climate-tech startups — they're century-old industrial giants (Carrier from 1902, Daikin from 1924, Trane from 1913). What matters isn't clever new tech but boring strengths: supply chains, installers, and service networks. And a quiet defeat for the greener option: "passive cooling" (shade, ventilation, smart building design) works, but it's slow and needs planning and capital upfront — so poor, hot countries will simply buy an AC unit instead of redesigning their buildings.

In one sentence

Air conditioning in a nutshell

A hotter planet turns cooling from luxury into survival — a clear growth market for old industrial giants — but it's also a carbon trap, where cooling burns fuel that warms the world and the poorest pay the heat's price without being able to afford the cure.
