Group E · The big picture

The insurance retreat

The backstop is walking away — and uninsurable becomes unmortgageable becomes unsellable.

Pull the thirteen pages together and one shift explains them all. Insurance is the invisible backstop that lets modern life work — mortgages, business, driving, building. Now insurers are pulling back across the board: climate-exposed homes, cyber, liability, auto, long-term care — because risk became too correlated, too trending, or too uncertain to pool. The gap fills with underfunded state pools and bare self-insurance, and the harm cascades onto individuals and taxpayers.

The one idea

The backstop is walking away. Be fair, though — it's arithmetic, not just greed. Pooling genuinely breaks when a single tail event wipes out years of premiums. And what was invisible is now glaring: cheap insurance in dangerous places was a hidden subsidy that encouraged people to live there.

Winners vs losers

Winners
Reinsurers and cat-bond investors who can price the risk, wealthy self-insurers with captives, and risk-data firms.
Losers
Lower-middle-class homeowners in fire/flood zones, small businesses (liability, cyber), LTC-needing families, lenders, and taxpayers.
The biggest tension

Regulators want to force affordability (rate caps, mandates), which kills the carrier; carriers want to price risk (high premiums, exits), which orphans populations and crashes values. Neither solves the core problem: the risk is real and rising. Cheap insurance in a dangerous place was never honest — it was cross-subsidized — so when the cross-subsidy collapses, someone has to eat the true cost.

The surprising part

The thing nobody talks about: mortgages require insurance. No insurance → no mortgage → no sale, even for a cash buyer whose appraisal assumes coverage exists. So when insurers exit a neighborhood, the property market doesn't just soften — the ability to transact vanishes, and homeowners get trapped. It's not just an affordability crisis; it's a liquidity crisis.

In one sentence

Insurance is retreating because risk stopped being independent; pooling is breaking; and the backstop is walking away, leaving individuals and states holding a bill the market always knew was real.

How this connects to the rest of the series

The insurance retreat is the financial transmission belt of several forces. It's the pricing arm of the physical climate bill and the tripwire under real estate (uninsurable → unmortgageable). Its cost spiral rhymes with cost disease (elder/long-term care), its telematics pricing borrows from surveillance/data, and its liability leg runs on litigation finance.
