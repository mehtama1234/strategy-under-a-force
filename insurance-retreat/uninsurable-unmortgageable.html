Group A · The climate exit

Uninsurable → unmortgageable

An insurer's spreadsheet, not a storm, is what strands a neighborhood's wealth.

Here's the cascade. Lenders require insurance to issue a mortgage. If a home can't be insured — or the insurance is unaffordable — it can't be mortgaged. If buyers can't get a mortgage, the home can't sell except for cash at a discount. So property values in high-risk areas can crash. Insurance is the canary: it prices climate and risk before the market does, and links physical risk to the whole financial system.

The core bet

The mechanism: insurance is the upstream cause, not a downstream symptom. The moment an underwriter's model says "no," a home becomes unmortgageable within days — and then unsellable. Values follow the insurer's decision, not the next storm.

Insurance leads, values follow

We assume storms crash home values. In fact, an insurer's decision to stop writing a block of policies reaches the mortgage market — "uninsurable" means loans get denied — before the next storm even forms. No mortgage means no buyer means fire-sale prices for cash-only deals. The tripwire is the spreadsheet, not the weather.

The cascade
Uninsurable → unmortgageable → unsellable → stranded wealth, tax-base erosion, and outmigration.
The signal
Insurance prices risk earlier and more honestly than markets — a warning worth heeding.
The single biggest worry

A self-reinforcing collapse: as insurance withdraws and values drop, the tax base shrinks, cutting funding right as risk intensifies — accelerating outmigration and abandonment, while lenders tighten and cut off even cash buyers.

The surprising part

Insurance isn't downstream of home values — it's upstream. An insurer's decision to non-renew reaches the mortgage market in days, and values follow. That inverts the usual story: we think storms crash prices; actually, insurers' risk models crash them before the next storm even forms, because no mortgage means no buyer.

In one sentence

Insurance is the tripwire that turns a physical risk into a financial one — the moment a place becomes uninsurable it becomes unmortgageable and then unsellable.
