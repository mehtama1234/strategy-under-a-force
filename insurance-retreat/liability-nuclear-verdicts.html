Group B · Beyond climate

Liability & nuclear verdicts

Juries are handing out ever-bigger awards — and pricing whole sectors out of coverage.

Liability insurance — for businesses, trucking, doctors, product makers — is getting scarcer and pricier because of "nuclear verdicts": jury awards over $10M, increasingly over $100M, rising fast. Outside investors bankroll the lawsuits, anti-corporate sentiment runs high, and mega-verdicts push premiums up until some coverage (trucking, certain products, abuse liability) is hard to get at any price. A legal-system force is reshaping who can even get insured.

The core bet

The bet: verdicts over $10M are spiking — fueled by litigation funders bankrolling lawsuits (see the sibling litigation-as-asset-class idea) and juror anger at corporations — making liability insurance so expensive or unavailable that whole sectors (trucking, product, abuse-related) get priced out or go bare.

A second economy on the courtroom

Litigation funders openly bet on specific lawsuits and take a cut of the verdict — like venture capitalists backing startups. That flips the incentive: instead of settling fast, funders want verdicts as huge and lengthy as possible. Juries, often skeptical of corporations and facing well-funded trial teams, award sums that track anger more than damages — inflating the system faster than insurers can price it.

Who's priced out
Truckers, doctors, small businesses, and product makers who can't afford — or can't find — liability cover.
Who benefits
Litigation funders and plaintiffs' firms turning lawsuits into an asset class.
The single biggest worry

Liability insurance is becoming a luxury for wealthy companies and unavailable for small ones — so the liability system itself breaks down when ordinary businesses can't insure against being sued, leaving victims uncompensated and businesses shuttered.

The surprising part

Outside investors now finance lawsuits and receive a cut of the verdict, layering a second economy on top of the civil-justice system. Their incentive is bigger, longer trials — and juries, skeptical of corporations and facing funded trial teams with unlimited budgets, award sums that don't track damages but track anger, faster than insurers can keep up.

In one sentence

Investor-funded "nuclear verdicts" are becoming the norm, pricing liability insurance out of reach for trucking, small business, and healthcare — a courtroom force reshaping who can get covered at all.
