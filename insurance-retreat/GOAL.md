# The Insurance Retreat — strategy under a force

The 26th collection (Tier-2). Signature: "the backstop is walking away."

**The force:** insurance is the quiet backstop that lets modern life function — you can't get a mortgage, run a business, drive, or build without it. Now insurers are pulling back: refusing to cover whole regions and risk classes (climate-exposed homes, cyber, liability, some auto and health), raising prices, or adding exclusions. When cover disappears, assets become uninsurable → unmortgageable → unsellable, and the risk lands on owners and governments. We ask: *what did the insurance retreat make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** the backstop is walking away. For decades, insurance made risk invisible — a stable, affordable promise that if the worst happened, you'd be covered. That promise is being withdrawn where risk got too big, too correlated, or too uncertain to pool: wildfire and flood zones, ransomware, "nuclear" liability verdicts, EV repair costs, long-term care. Value flows to whoever can price, transfer, or avoid the new risk (reinsurers, parametric insurers, self-insurers, risk-data firms); the danger is a cascade — uninsurable becomes unmortgageable becomes unsellable — that quietly strands assets and dumps the risk on the public.

**How it differs from the climate-bill collection:** climate-bill touched insurance as one symptom of physical climate change. This collection is the broader, cross-sector retreat — cyber, liability, auto, and health, not just weather — and the mechanism of un-poolable risk itself.
