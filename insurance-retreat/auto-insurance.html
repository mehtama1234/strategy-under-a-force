Group B · Beyond climate

Auto insurance

A modern car is a computer on wheels — so a fender-bender can total it.

Car insurance premiums jumped sharply — US auto insurance rose ~20%+ in 2023–24, a real driver of inflation — because cars got far more expensive to repair. Modern vehicles are packed with sensors, cameras, and screens, so a minor bump can total the car; EVs are especially costly because battery damage can write off the vehicle. Some insurers hesitate to cover EVs, and lean harder on telematics to price you.

The core bet

The squeeze: cars are engineered with crumple zones and sensor density that make totals inevitable on impacts older cars shrugged off. Battery and sensor costs push loss ratios past sustainable levels — so insurers raise premiums, retreat from unprofitable segments, and track how you drive to price the risk.

What the numbers say

Told to buy the car they won't insure

The paradox: regulators and makers push the very high-tech vehicles — autonomous-ready sensors, EV batteries — that insurers now cover reluctantly. A modern SUV or EV, praised as the future of driving, is the nightmare for underwriting: one airbag deployment or battery inspection can total it and wipe out a year of premium. Buyers are told to go electric, while the market quietly prices EVs into the luxury tier.

What's newly valuable
Telematics data, repair networks, and insurers who can price tech-heavy vehicles.
What's harmed
Affordability for young and low-income drivers — and privacy, as pricing ties to tracking.
The single biggest worry

Modern cars are built with sensor density and batteries that make totals inevitable on minor impacts, locking insurers into a choice between unsustainable loss ratios and pricing ordinary drivers — especially the buyers of affordable EVs — out of coverage.

The surprising part

A small crash that once cost $500 can now total the car because the bumper is full of sensors — and that, plus EV battery costs and lawsuit inflation, sent premiums soaring and made insurers wary of the very high-tech cars we're told to buy. You're told to go electric for the planet; the insurance market is quietly pricing that choice into the luxury segment.

In one sentence

Soaring repair costs from sensor-packed, battery-dependent cars forced insurers to raise premiums and retreat from unprofitable segments — while tracking drivers ever more closely to manage the risk.
