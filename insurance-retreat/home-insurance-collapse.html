Group A · The climate exit

Home insurance collapse

In the richest country on earth, whole regions now struggle to buy cover at any price.

Major insurers are pulling out of disaster-prone states. State Farm and Allstate stopped writing new home policies in California; many insurers fled Florida; premiums soared. Some homeowners can't get coverage at any price. Wildfire, hurricane, and flood losses made these markets unprofitable — so insurers simply walked away, and without insurance you can't get a mortgage.

The core bet

When catastrophe exposure outran premiums, insurers made the cold call to leave rather than lose money. The result: in California and Florida — the two richest states in the wealthiest nation — homeowners can be unable to buy insurance no matter what they'll pay. The free market produced no private solution.

Not expensive — impossible

The turning point isn't that insurance got pricey; it's that in some places it became impossible to buy. And because mortgages require insurance, when private insurers flee and state backup plans max out, homeowners can't borrow and houses can't change hands. The market freezes.

What's newly valuable
Anyone who can still price and carry the risk — or the state pools now absorbing it.
What's stranded
Homeowners in fire and hurricane zones, and the housing market that depends on cover.
The single biggest worry

Insurance becoming structurally unavailable — not just expensive — in whole regions threatens the housing market itself: mortgages require insurance, so when private carriers flee and state plans max out, homes can't be borrowed against or sold.

The surprising part

This isn't a poor country with weak infrastructure — it's California and Florida, the richest states in the wealthiest nation. People live in desirable places (beaches, hills) precisely because they're sought-after, yet are now locked out of insurance. The math simply broke: insurers would lose money at any price, so they quit — leaving the free market with no private answer.

In one sentence

Insurers walked away from California wildfire and Florida hurricane risk, freezing the housing market for millions — because you can't get a mortgage without insurance you can't buy.
