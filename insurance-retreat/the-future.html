The future

The fixes all point one uncomfortable way: cheap cover in dangerous places was a hidden subsidy.

How the industry and society are responding. Parametric insurance pays out automatically when a trigger is hit (wind over X, quake over Y) instead of assessing damage. Mitigation discounts reward fire-hardening, flood-proofing, and cyber controls. Risk-based pricing and managed retreat face the hard truth that some places may need to be rebuilt or left — and that cheap insurance there was a hidden subsidy. Catastrophe bonds move risk to capital markets.

The core bet

The bet — and the reckoning: the industry is admitting cheap coverage in catastrophe zones was never sustainable. It masked real risk instead of pricing it. Honest insurance now means either hardening your property or relocating — parametric payouts and mitigation incentives just make the true stakes visible and fast.

The hidden subsidy, revealed

For decades, the federal flood program and state pools kept premiums artificially low in dangerous places — which encouraged building there by hiding the true cost. Once risk-based pricing corrects that, an uncomfortable truth appears: many people were living where they couldn't afford to if insurance had been honest. Parametric cover and mitigation rewards accelerate the reckoning by making risk transparent.

What's promising
Fast parametric payouts, mitigation incentives that lower real risk, and capital-market risk transfer.
The hard part
Honest pricing prices out the poor before they can afford to harden or move.
The single biggest worry

If risk-based repricing happens too fast or unevenly, poor and middle-income households in vulnerable regions get priced out of insurance entirely before they can afford mitigation or relocation — and the social cost of managed retreat falls hardest on those least able to absorb it.

The surprising part

For decades cheap insurance in dangerous places was a quiet subsidy that encouraged people to live there. Honest risk-based pricing — plus parametric payouts and mitigation rewards — means either hardening where you are or leaving, because the era of cheap coverage that hid the real risk is ending. The fixes all point the same uncomfortable way.

In one sentence

The industry is abandoning the quiet subsidy of cheap cover in dangerous places — replacing it with parametric payouts, mitigation incentives, and honest pricing that forces either expensive hardening or migration.
