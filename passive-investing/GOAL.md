# Passive Investing / Common Ownership — strategy under a force

The 25th collection (the last of the Tier-1 scout picks).

**The force:** the shift from active stock-picking to passive index funds moved trillions of dollars into a handful of giant asset managers — BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street — who now hold big stakes in nearly every large public company at once. That "common ownership" and concentration quietly reshapes competition, corporate governance, and who really controls the market. We ask: *what did the rise of passive investing make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** three firms quietly own everyone's competitors. Index funds are sold as cheap, simple, and "automatic" — buy the whole market, pay almost nothing. But because everyone bought the same funds, three managers ended up owning ~20%+ of the S&P 500 and sizeable stakes in every airline, bank, and carmaker simultaneously. That makes them the biggest shareholder of firms that are supposed to compete — softening rivalry, concentrating voting power, and creating a strange "ownerless" corporation whose owners are indifferent index funds. Value flowed to the low-fee giants and to ordinary savers; the risk is concentration, weakened competition, hidden fragility, and capital quietly fleeing public markets.
