Passive Investing

How a cheap, boring idea handed three firms ownership of nearly everything.

The 25th force. Index funds are sold as cheap, simple, and "automatic" — buy the whole market, pay almost nothing. But because everyone bought the same funds, three managers — BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street — ended up holding big stakes in every airline, bank, and carmaker at once. That "common ownership" quietly reshapes competition and control. The one idea: three firms quietly own everyone's competitors.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did the rise of passive investing make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? It saved ordinary savers a fortune in fees — genuinely great. But it also concentrated ownership, softened rivalry, handed three firms enormous voting power, and created a strange "ownerless" corporation whose owners are indifferent index funds that can never sell.
