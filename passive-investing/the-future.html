Group D · Forces & limits

The future

The fix for "three firms hold the votes" may be handing them back to millions of people.

Where it's heading. "Direct indexing" (owning the individual stocks of an index yourself, for tax control and custom tweaks) is spreading as fees fall to zero. "Voting choice" lets index-fund holders direct their own proxy votes, defusing the concentration-of-power worry. Active ETFs offer a middle ground. And policymakers float limits on common ownership. The surprise: the fix may be unbundling the very index fund that created the concentration.

The core bet

The emerging answer isn't mainly regulation — it's technology that disaggregates the index. Direct indexing returns to owning individual stocks; pass-through voting returns the vote to millions of holders. The bet is that zero-commission tech makes fragmentation cheaper than the concentration it replaces.

Unbundling the bundle

The remedy for "three firms control all the votes" is oddly retro: give people the tools to own the index stock-by-stock (direct indexing, a return to pre-1970s stock ownership) and to vote their own shares (pass-through voting). What looks like a radical fix for institutional power is really just unbundling the index fund — betting that modern tech makes the old, fragmented model cheap again.

What's promising
Direct indexing, active ETFs, and pass-through voting genuinely hand control back to individuals.
The behavioral catch
Most people will still ignore their vote and auto-harvest taxes — apathy, not concentration, was the root cause.
The single biggest worry

If direct indexing and pass-through voting take off, most retail investors will still ignore voting — collapsing back to the same apathy that created the concentration. The structure looks better, but inertia may just shift the power, not dissolve it.

The surprising part

The solution to "three firms control the votes" isn't regulation — it's giving people the tools to disaggregate their own ownership. Direct indexing literally returns to 1970s-era stock-picking, and pass-through voting hands the ballot back to millions. A radical-looking fix for institutional power is really just unbundling the bundle — though most people will likely still click "vote with management."

In one sentence

Direct indexing and pass-through voting are rebuilding 1970s stock ownership with 2020s tech — betting the fix for Big Three power is making fragmentation cheaper than concentration, if only people bother to use it.
