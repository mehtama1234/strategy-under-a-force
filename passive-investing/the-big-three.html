Group A · The index revolution

The Big Three

Three firms became the biggest owner of nearly every major US company — by accident.

Because everyone bought index funds, three firms ended up managing a staggering share of the market. BlackRock, Vanguard, and State Street together manage well over $20 trillion and are the single largest shareholder in most large US companies. They didn't buy this power to control anything — it accumulated passively as savers poured in. It's an unprecedented concentration of ownership.

The core bet

The Big Three didn't set out to own corporate America — they simply ran the cheapest index funds, and tens of millions of people bought them. The result: three managers hold the largest voting bloc in nearly every big company, an ownership structure that never existed before in this form.

What the numbers say

~$23T
Combined assets under management (BlackRock ~$10–11T, Vanguard ~$8–9T, State Street ~$4T) — much of it index funds that must simply hold the market.
~20%+
Share of the S&P 500 the Big Three hold collectively — and they're often the largest single shareholder of each company.
~90%
Share of S&P 500 firms where the Big Three are, together, the biggest owner (per the 2017 "Hidden Power of the Big Three" study).
~5% → ~50%
Human individuals fell from most US stock ownership decades ago to a small slice today; institutions — led by index funds — hold the rest.

Power without intent

This is the strange part: the concentration happened by accident. No one plotted to become the biggest owner of every airline and bank. It's the mechanical result of a great deal for savers (cheap index funds) scaling to tens of trillions. The power came free with the market share, whether the managers wanted it or not.

What's newly valuable
Scale itself — the low-fee giants that captured the passive flood and the influence that came with it.
What's concerning
Three firms as the largest owner of thousands of "competing" companies at once.
The single biggest worry

Three companies became the biggest shareholder of almost every major firm — an unprecedented concentration of ownership and voting power that no one designed, and that's hard to unwind without breaking the retirement savings it rests on.

The surprising part

The largest owners of corporate America gained that role without trying to take over anything — simply because tens of millions of people bought the same cheap index funds. The most powerful ownership bloc in the economy is an emergent side effect of a product designed to be boring and passive.

In one sentence

Three firms became the biggest owner of almost every major US company — not by seeking control, but because everyone bought the same cheap index funds.
