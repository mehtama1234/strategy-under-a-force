Group C · The re-localization

Alt-protein & the lab

The 'future of food' crashed hard while the old system just kept working.

One response to a fragile, land-and-animal-dependent food system is to make protein differently: plant-based meat, cultivated (lab-grown) meat, and precision fermentation (dairy proteins from microbes). It was framed as more resilient, sustainable, and local. But plant-based hit a wall, cultivated meat is nowhere near cost-competitive, and the money — and the hype — pulled back hard.

The core bet

Making protein in factories instead of on farms could sidestep land, animals, and fragile supply chains — a bet on resilient, local, controllable food.

The old system proved cheaper and more resilient than expected

The 'future of food' narrative crashed not because the tech failed but because conventional agriculture is remarkably cheap and resilient. Startups burned billions on R&D while farms and slaughterhouses kept humming with unbeatable economies of scale — and the mass consumer demand needed to justify the factory build-out never showed up.

The single biggest worry

The economics don't work yet — cultivated meat is far too expensive, consumer demand for plant-based stalled, and political backlash (state bans) is rising to protect ranchers.

The surprising part

The twist is political: some US states banned lab-grown meat outright to protect cattle ranchers, turning the food supply into a political prize. The hyped disruption stumbled while the incumbent quietly won on price — a reminder that in food, cheap and proven usually beats novel and expensive.

In one sentence

Lab-made protein was meant to make food more resilient and local, but it proved too expensive, demand didn't follow the hype, and the old system just kept working.
