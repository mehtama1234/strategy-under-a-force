Group B · The shocks fragmenting it

Biosecurity & disease

Your eggs got expensive because a virus forced the killing of millions of birds — and shut the border overnight.

Animal and plant diseases — avian influenza, African swine fever, foot-and-mouth — force mass culls and instant trade bans, fragmenting the food system. Bird flu has devastated poultry and eggs and jumped to dairy cattle in 2024; African swine fever wiped out a huge share of China's pigs in 2018–19. A single positive test can close a country's entire meat export in hours.

The core bet

A single disease outbreak can force the culling of millions of animals and an instant export ban, so biosecurity and disease-resistant, localized supply become newly valuable.

Industrial density spreads disease — and it can jump species

Packing thousands of animals shoulder-to-shoulder in industrial farms spreads pathogens fast and wide. And the systemic danger is a jump: bird flu moving from poultry to cattle to — potentially — humans could hit agriculture and public health at once, which is exactly the kind of shock that shuts borders and fragments trade.

The single biggest worry

Concentrated industrial animal agriculture is a disease amplifier, and a pathogen jumping species (bird flu to cattle, or people) is a systemic risk that can slam the whole system at once.

The surprising part

The everyday shock is at the breakfast table: eggs doubled in price not because of inflation but because a virus forced workers to kill ten million birds in 72 hours, a cost that flows straight to the store. And a country's entire meat-export business can stop dead on one blood test — turning months of production into waste in a day.

In one sentence

Diseases that destroy millions of animals in weeks and shut borders overnight reward whoever can source food locally and rebuild supply fast.
