The synthesis · what repeats across all 13

The patterns

Line up every corner of the food system — the fertilizer, the seeds, the traders, the export bans, the droughts, the disease — and the same handful of mechanics repeat, all flowing from one fact: cheap global food was borrowed against conditions that are ending.

Each page asks: what did the deglobalization of food make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

Cheap global food for seventy years rested on three bets — cheap energy, a stable climate, and open trade — and all three are breaking. So the food system is fragmenting into regional blocs, and power is pooling at the choke points: the fertilizer, the seeds, the silos, and the land. We optimized the whole thing for efficiency and, without noticing, deleted its resilience — so a war, a drought, or a virus far away now moves your grocery bill, and the scarce, valuable things are the inputs nobody can substitute.
