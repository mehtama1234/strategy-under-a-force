Group C · The re-localization

Food security as policy

China holds half the world's stored grain; rich desert nations secure food by owning other countries' farmland.

As food deglobalizes, governments treat it like oil: building strategic grain reserves, subsidizing domestic production, restricting food-land sales to foreigners, and — for import-dependent rich nations — buying farmland abroad. China holds enormous reserves; Gulf states buy African and Asian farmland; countries push self-sufficiency. Food security became statecraft.

The core bet

Food became a strategic asset like oil, so governments stockpile it, subsidize growing it at home, and secure supply abroad — food security is now statecraft.

The rich buy the land; the poor pray the ships arrive

When wealthy nations lease the best farmland in poorer countries and ship the harvest home, local farmers can lose access to soil their families worked for generations. And self-sufficiency drives are often wasteful — paying farmers to grow crops in places where importing would cost far less — so 'food security' can quietly mean cost and inequity.

The single biggest worry

'Land grabs' abroad can exploit poorer nations by extracting their best soil, and expensive self-sufficiency schemes can be more wasteful than the trade they replace.

The surprising part

The double irony is striking: China holds more than half the world's stored grain — much of which will never be eaten, it just sits as insurance — while the richest desert nations, unable to feed themselves, chose to buy pieces of Africa and Asia outright, betting that owning the land beats trusting the market.

In one sentence

Governments now treat food like oil — storing it, growing it expensively at home, and buying farmland abroad to guarantee supply whatever happens to trade.
