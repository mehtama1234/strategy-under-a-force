Group B · The shocks fragmenting it

Climate & crop failure

Your chocolate got expensive because of a heatwave in Ghana you never heard about.

Climate change is hammering food production: droughts, heat, floods, and shifting rainfall cut harvests and drive up prices. Cocoa, olive oil, coffee, and orange juice all spiked on climate-driven crop failures in 2023–24. And as multiple breadbaskets risk failing in the same year, the world's thin grain buffer looks alarmingly fragile.

The core bet

A stable climate was a hidden input to cheap food, and as it destabilizes, harvests get less reliable — so resilient supply, water, and adapted crops become newly valuable.

What the numbers say

~3–4x
How far cocoa prices rose from mid-2023 to 2024 after drought and heat in West Africa (~70% of world cocoa).
~2x
Olive-oil prices after Spain's driest year in over a millennium (2022) — retail from ~$15 to ~$30+/liter.
~50% / ~35%
Coffee and orange-juice futures spikes on Brazilian drought (2024).
~11 weeks
Global grain reserves as weeks of consumption — down from ~17 weeks in 2000, a thinner buffer.
40%
Share of global agriculture under water stress; irrigation already uses ~70% of freshwater.

The world runs on a handful of breadbaskets with thin reserves

If a heatwave hits the US Corn Belt (~40% of world corn) the same year the Indian monsoon fails and Australian wheat floods, the global grain buffer vanishes in months and prices can jump 20–50% overnight. Poor importers can't buy their way out, and starvation and political chaos can follow. We're resilient until we're not — often one bad year away from a cascade.

The single biggest worry

Several major growing regions failing in the same year could trigger a global food crisis with no buffer to absorb it — a 'multiple breadbasket failure' the thin reserve system isn't built to survive.

The surprising part

The surprise is how much of recent food inflation is pure physics, not money. Most people assume prices move on wars or currency, but a large share of the 2024 spike came from drought and heat cutting harvests — your chocolate got dear because of weather in Ghana. And the whole system leans on just five breadbaskets, with stockpiles too small to plug a big gap.

In one sentence

A stable climate was a hidden subsidy to cheap food; as it destabilizes, harvests get less reliable, prices spike, and the buffer to feed 8 billion thins.
