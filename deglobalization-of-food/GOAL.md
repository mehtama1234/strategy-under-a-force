# Deglobalization of Food — strategy under a force

The 48th collection (Tier-7, from the Haiku discovery-scout pass) — Family I (macro forces).

**The force:** for 70 years the food system globalized — cheap grain, global supply chains, just-in-time inputs, and a handful of trading houses moving calories around the planet. Now it's fragmenting. War (Ukraine grain and fertilizer), export bans (India rice, Indonesia palm oil), fertilizer and energy shocks, tariffs, climate-driven crop failures, and animal-disease biosecurity are breaking the food system into regional and "friendly" blocs. We ask: *what did the deglobalization of food make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** cheap global food was a hidden subsidy, and the bill is coming due. For decades the world assumed it could always buy grain, fertilizer, and inputs from wherever they were cheapest — so food got cheap and supply chains got thin. Now that assumption is breaking: countries hoard their harvests, fertilizer chokes on Russian potash and natural gas, and a bird-flu outbreak can shut a border overnight. So the scarce, valuable things became the inputs nobody can substitute — fertilizer, seeds, and the ground itself — and the middlemen who move calories (the grain traders) and control the inputs (the seed and chemical giants) hold the leverage, while nations scramble for food security.

- **D — The mechanism & tensions:** why cheap global food was a hidden subsidy · who profits · the limits (you can't grow everything everywhere; cost; alt-protein hype)
