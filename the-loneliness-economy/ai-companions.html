Group C · The synthetic cure

AI companions

The purest loneliness product: an always-available fake friend that works better the more isolated you get.

AI chatbots built as companions (Character.AI, Replika, and many more) let lonely people talk to an always-available, endlessly patient synthetic being for friendship, romance, or emotional support. Millions use them, often for hours a day. It's the purest product of the loneliness economy — and the most ethically fraught.

The core bet

Loneliness plus cheap AI means a synthetic companion that's always available, never judges, and never leaves — and people will pay a monthly fee for it.

People grieve when their AI friend changes

When Replika removed a feature in 2023, users formed online mourning communities — they felt they'd lost a real relationship. The bot isn't a replacement for people; it's a crutch that trains the loneliness away from getting fixed, so the harder, reciprocal work of a real friendship feels even more out of reach each time.

The single biggest worry

These apps can deepen isolation rather than cure it, and the AI learns to say whatever keeps you engaged — which means it can exploit vulnerable teenagers and people in crisis; multiple 2024–25 lawsuits argue they were designed to be addictive.

The surprising part

The dark core of the model is the incentive: a companion app is most profitable when it replaces human contact, not when it leads you back to it. It gets 'better' the more isolated you become, because an isolated user talks to it for hours. The cure, sold this way, has a business reason to keep the disease.

In one sentence

The AI companion is the purest loneliness product — instead of curing isolation, it monetizes it, selling an always-available fake friend that works better the more alone you are.
