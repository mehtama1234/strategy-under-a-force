Group A · Selling belonging & connection

Connection-as-a-service

The free places we used to meet closed — so companies now sell structured ways to make friends.

As loneliness rose and the old free ways to meet people faded, a market appeared for organized connection: friendship apps (Bumble BFF, Meetup), run clubs and social fitness, community platforms, and group activities. People who used to meet friends through work, neighbors, or church now pay for the infrastructure that makes it happen.

The core bet

Loneliness made low-friction ways to meet people valuable, so companies sell structured connection — clubs, apps, events — because the old free ways to make friends have mostly disappeared.

The run club replaced the church and the bar

The run club, the meetup, and the friendship app boomed because the institutions that used to weave communities together — churches, bowling leagues, civic clubs — collapsed, leaving a void a venture-backed app or a fitness startup could fill. Friendship became a product because the old free version stopped being available.

The single biggest worry

Manufactured connection can feel transactional, and it may treat the symptom while leaving the deeper isolation untouched — if people stop expecting friendship for free and start expecting to pay for it, loneliness doesn't vanish, it just gets commercialized.

The surprising part

The quiet shift is what got monetized. Nobody used to pay to make a friend; you met people at the places you already went. Now those places are gone, so the market rebuilt them behind a sign-up — and the boom in run clubs isn't a fad, it's what happens when the social fabric thins and a business fills the gap.

In one sentence

Companies now sell connection because the places where people used to meet for free have closed.
