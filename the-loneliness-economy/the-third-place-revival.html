Group A · Selling belonging & connection

The third-place revival

The gym, the café, and the club are really selling belonging — the product is just the ticket in.

A 'third place' — not home, not work — is where community used to happen. As bars, churches, and civic clubs declined, businesses rebuilt them as paid experiences: boutique fitness (SoulCycle, CrossFit, Equinox), coffee shops, social clubs (Soho House), pickleball. The monthly fee is really the price of admission to a community that wouldn't exist otherwise.

The core bet

With free community spaces gone, businesses sell the 'third place' — the gym, café, or club is really selling belonging, and the product is the ticket in.

What the numbers say

$150–300/mo
What boutique-fitness gyms (CrossFit, SoulCycle) charge — priced for community and identity, not just the workout.
145,000+ / $500M+
Soho House members and revenue — a business built almost entirely on belonging and access.
2.8M → 8.9M
Pickleball players, 2021 → 2024 — the fastest-growing U.S. sport, powered by the social-club model.
25–50%
How far club, church, and civic-league membership fell since the 1960s ('Bowling Alone').
$200+/mo
Equinox's membership — sold as a lifestyle and social club, not a gym.

People pay for the community, not the workout

Surveys of premium-gym and social-club members put community and friendship above any fitness or product benefit as the reason they pay. Many SoulCycle riders never check their heart rate — they come for the music, the instructor, and the people beside them. The space is almost secondary; the fee buys access to a curated group that won't form unless someone charges to run it.

The single biggest worry

Paid belonging excludes those who can't afford it — a $200-a-month gym or a Soho House membership is a luxury — and the 'community' can be shallow: people show up for the brand or the status, and when the membership lapses, so does the friendship.

The surprising part

The revealing part is the inversion of what's being sold. The coffee is the excuse; the class is the wrapper. What people are actually buying is a reliable room full of familiar faces — the thing a neighborhood used to provide for free — now packaged, curated, and priced as a subscription.

In one sentence

The third place didn't vanish; it moved behind a paywall, and businesses learned people will pay more for belonging than for the service itself.
