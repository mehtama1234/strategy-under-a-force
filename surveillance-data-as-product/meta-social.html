Group B · You are the product

Meta & social

Profiled in detail — even if you never made an account.

Facebook and Instagram are free because your attention and behavior are the product. Meta builds detailed profiles from what you post, like, and click — and tracks you across much of the rest of the web through the Meta Pixel. It even builds "shadow profiles" of people who never signed up. You can be profiled without ever having an account.

The core bet

Meta's business rests on surveillance. You don't pay for the apps — your behavior patterns and social graph are the real products, sold as precision ad targeting. Meta knows what you like, who you know, what you buy, and how long you linger. The profile is so detailed it can predict and nudge your choices.

What the numbers say

$5B
The FTC fine (2019) over privacy failures — the largest such settlement at the time. Ad revenue is ~98% of Meta's business.
~87M
Facebook profiles harvested in the Cambridge Analytica scandal (revealed 2018) via a personality quiz, then weaponized for political targeting.
~2M sites
Websites embedding the Meta Pixel — a tracker that follows you across the web and feeds your behavior back to Meta even when you're off its apps.
shadow profiles
Meta builds files on people who never joined, using contact lists and details uploaded by others. You can be profiled with no account.

Tracking beyond the app

The "like" buttons and invisible pixel code on retail sites, news outlets, and shops feed ad profiles even for people who've never visited Facebook. Meta's reach isn't the app you closed — it's a tracking layer spread across much of the web, quietly assembling a picture of you from everywhere else you go.

What's risky for you
Psychological targeting at scale, cross-web tracking, and a profile built even without your consent or knowledge.
What's valuable for them
The most precise ad-targeting asset in history — attention plus a full behavioral and social map.
The single biggest worry

Meta's profiles are detailed enough to enable psychological micro-targeting — campaigns and even authoritarian actors can segment people by personality and susceptibility. The infrastructure that made Cambridge Analytica possible still exists.

The surprising part

Shadow profiles mean Meta may know about you even if you've never signed up. If a friend uploads their contacts, emails you, or tags a photo, Meta can build a file complete enough to target you — and you have almost no way to see it. Opting out of the account doesn't opt you out of the profiling.

In one sentence

Meta's real customers are advertisers, and the product is you — profiled in such detail, even without an account, that the system can predict and shape your choices before you make them.
