Group A · The original data merchants

Data brokers

An enormous, invisible industry holding a dossier on nearly everyone.

Data brokers are the shadow industry almost no one has heard of. They buy, combine, and resell profiles on nearly every American — thousands of data points each: age, income, purchases, health interests, life events, even inferred behaviors. They sell to advertisers, insurers, and political campaigns. The whole thing works precisely because you don't know the files exist.

The core bet

The bet is that quietly assembling a detailed dossier on everyone, then renting it out, is a great business — as long as it stays invisible. No storefront, no consent, no easy way to see or delete your file. Just profiles, sold on repeat.

What the numbers say

~5,000
Attributes per person that the largest broker, Acxiom, has reportedly held — with profiles on billions of people worldwide.
~$100–150B
Rough size of the broader data industry. Brokers like LiveRamp, Oracle Data Cloud, and CoreLogic combine and resell profiles across it.
2023
California's Delete Act — the first real attempt to let people delete themselves from brokers. The FTC called deletion rights "a sea change."
2024
An FTC report called data brokers a "persistent surveillance machine" operating with "minimal transparency and negligible oversight."

Inferred, not just collected

Brokers don't only record what you do — they infer what they can't see. From purchases and web activity they guess health conditions (diabetes, mental health, reproductive choices), income, and vulnerabilities, then sell those inferences to insurers and campaigns. You can't audit a guess, and you can't correct it.

What's risky for you
Inferred health and financial profiles you can't see, used to price or target you — and rebuilt within weeks even if you delete.
What's valuable for them
A comprehensive, resellable dossier on essentially everyone, refreshed constantly and lightly regulated.
The single biggest worry

Insurers use inferred health profiles to price or deny coverage on data no one can audit; campaigns micro-target personal anxieties. And there's no national right to be forgotten — even after you delete, new purchases rebuild the file.

The surprising part

An industry almost no one can name holds detailed dossiers — thousands of attributes each — on nearly every person, and sells them daily. Until very recently there was no way even to see your file, let alone delete it. It's the most consequential business most people have never heard of.

In one sentence

An invisible, trillion-data-point shadow economy sells detailed dossiers on everyone to whoever pays — and until recently you couldn't even look at your own.
