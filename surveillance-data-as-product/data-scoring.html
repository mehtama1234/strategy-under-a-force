Group D · The scoring machine & the fight

Data-as-scoring

A web of secret scores quietly prices you.

The data isn't only sold for ads — it's used to score and price you. Insurers set premiums on driving and inferred health; lenders use alternative data; retailers and airlines test "personalized" pricing that charges different people different amounts. Beyond your credit score sits a whole web of hidden scores — tenant risk, return-fraud risk, lifetime value — deciding your price with no transparency.

The core bet

The bet is that your data exhaust can predict how risky or profitable you are — and that prediction is worth acting on. Companies build secret scores from data you never knowingly shared, then use them to set your rate, your terms, or whether they'll serve you at all.

What the numbers say

100+
Parallel scores beyond the familiar credit score — chargeback risk, tenant reliability, "attrition risk," return-fraud propensity — most of which consumers don't know exist.
30–50%
Premium swings in usage-based insurance (Progressive Snapshot, Allstate Drivewise), based on tracked driving and location.
2024
The FTC opened a probe into "surveillance pricing" — different prices for different people based on browsing history and inferred traits.
health proxies
Insurers infer health from prescription fills and web activity to adjust quotes — legal under carve-outs, invisible to you.

A shadow credit system

It's not one score — it's a spiderweb of unrelated ones. Your tenant score can sink you with a landlord while a payment processor's fraud score decides if a merchant will take your card. None talk to each other, none are uniformly regulated, and some are built on data you thought was anonymous — a retailer buying your zip, age, and browsing to infer income and set your price without ever knowing your name.

What's risky for you
Being priced, denied, or downgraded by opaque algorithms trained on data you never consented to — with near-zero appeal.
What's valuable for them
Precision pricing and risk-sorting that squeezes more margin from every customer.
The single biggest worry

You're scored and priced by invisible systems trained on data you never shared — income, health proxies, default likelihood — with almost no transparency or appeal beyond "contact customer service."

The surprising part

The scoring isn't one monolithic number — it's a hidden mesh of dozens. Your tenant score can lock you out of housing while your chargeback score blocks you as a merchant, and neither is regulated the same way. Worst of all, some scores are inferred from data you assumed was anonymous — the price you see may already reflect a guess about who you are.

In one sentence

A shadow scoring system now prices and rates you across insurance, lending, retail, and housing using hidden, opaque scores built from your data — with little transparency and less recourse.
