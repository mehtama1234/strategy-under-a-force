The patterns

Line up everyone who turned your data into a product, and one theme dominates: you are not the customer, you are the product.

Each page asks: what did "your data is the product" make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable for this company? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

The thing you use — the search box, the app, the car, the TV — is the bait. The record of you using it is the business. Value flows to whoever captures and fuses the most data about you into a saleable asset: an ad target, a score, a face in a database. And the whole edifice rests on permission structures a platform or a law can revoke.

The point of the series

This is the twentieth "strategy under a force" collection. Its signature: "you are not the customer, you are the product." It's the collection engine beneath the ad economy and the loyalty economy (same data, sold three ways), underlies the attention economy, and powers the shopper-data weapon in private label. Same method every time: pick a force, ask what it made scarce/cheap/risky/valuable, group by the answer, read the patterns.
