Group A · The original data merchants

Location data

A warrant loophole built on a permission you tapped years ago.

Apps quietly harvest your phone's precise location and sell the trail through brokers. Advertisers and hedge funds buy it — and, more troublingly, so do law enforcement and immigration agencies, using it to sidestep warrants. You tapped "allow location" on a weather app once, and that became a way for the government to buy your movements instead of asking a judge.

The core bet

The bet is that "allow location" is worth more than the app itself. Harvest precise GPS trails, sell them to brokers, and let advertisers, funds, and agencies buy the movements of millions — turning a routine app permission into a saleable surveillance feed.

What the numbers say

~3,000
Apps that have quietly collected and sold location data. Brokers like SafeGraph, Venntel, and X-Mode/Outlogic aggregated and resold it (2019–2023).
2021
A Catholic newsletter outed a priest by buying his Grindr location history from a broker — the starkest proof of how personal this data is.
2024
The FTC banned Outlogic (X-Mode's parent) from selling sensitive location data, after finding agencies had bought movement trails to track people.
no law
No federal law bans the sale itself — only the FTC's "unfair practices" authority and patchy state laws reach brokers, and rarely the government buyers.

The warrant workaround

The Fourth Amendment normally requires a warrant to track someone's movements. Buying the same data from a broker skips that entirely — brokers even marketed it to police as a "legal workaround." Agencies including immigration enforcement and the military were among the biggest buyers before the 2024 crackdown.

What's risky for you
Your precise movements — to a clinic, a protest, a place of worship — sold and searchable, with no warrant and little recourse.
What's valuable for buyers
Hedge funds predict earnings from foot traffic; agencies track people; advertisers geo-target — all off one app permission.
The single biggest worry

If police can buy your movements from a broker, the warrant requirement weakens into a permission you tapped years ago on a weather app. Constitutional protection quietly becomes optional.

The surprising part

Most of these apps never needed precise location for their actual feature — weather works off your IP, games don't need GPS. The permission was added purely to resell. The app's real business was selling you, not serving you, and the buyer of last resort turned out to be the government itself.

In one sentence

The location-data trade is a warrant loophole built on forgotten app permissions: you allow GPS once, the app sells it, and the state buys your trail instead of asking a judge.
