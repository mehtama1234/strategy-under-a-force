# Surveillance / Data as Product — strategy under a force

The twentieth "strategy under a force" collection (scout-queue: surveillance / data-as-product).

**The force:** many companies that look like they sell something else — search, social, a credit card, a car, a TV — are really in the business of collecting data about you and selling it, scoring you with it, or using it to predict and shape what you do. We ask: *what did "your data is the product" make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** you are not the customer — you are the product. Your attention, your location, your purchases, your face and voice are collected, packaged, and sold or scored. Value flows to whoever can capture the most data about you and turn it into a saleable asset (an ad target, a credit score, a risk model, a surveillance feed). The risk is a backlash — privacy laws, platform changes, and public revulsion — because the whole model runs on watching people who mostly never agreed to be watched.

**How this differs from the ad/loyalty siblings:** those are about spending YOUR data to sell ads or hooks. This one is about the DATA ITSELF as the commodity — brokered, scored, and surveilled — the credit bureaus, data brokers, location traders, biometric and government-surveillance markets. Cross-linked, not duplicated.
