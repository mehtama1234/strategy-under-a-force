Group B · You are the product

Google & search

The most useful free tools ever built — because you're the product.

Google gives away search, maps, email, Android, and Chrome — and makes hundreds of billions a year selling ads targeted with the data those free tools collect. It's the archetype of "surveillance capitalism": the free product is the bait, and the detailed behavioral profile it builds is the real business. This overlaps the ad economy, but here the point is the profiling itself.

The core bet

Google's insight: if you collect what people search, where they go, what they watch, and who they know, you can predict what they want better than they can — and sell that prediction. The searches and locations you hand over are refined into the most detailed profile ever assembled, and that profile, not the search box, is the product.

What the numbers say

~$350B
Alphabet's 2024 revenue, ~80%+ from advertising — roughly $280B from selling access to user profiles, not from selling search results.
1.5T+
Searches a year — each one a signal about intent, identity, and location, feeding the profile.
Gmail·Maps·Android·YouTube
Each free product is a different angle on the same person — messages, places, phone behavior, videos — fused into one profile.
2019
Shoshana Zuboff named it "surveillance capitalism" — Google doesn't just react to your searches, it predicts and sells your future behavior ("behavioral surplus").

Free by necessity

The shock isn't that Google is free — it's that it has to be. A paid Google where you buy searches would never generate the data. The model requires you to be so comfortable handing over intent, location, and identity that you forget you're the product. The ad network is the tail; the profile is the dog.

Genuinely useful
Search, Maps, and Gmail are excellent and would cost money without the ad subsidy — the utility is real.
The cost
An intimate behavioral map — what you search alone, where you go, what you watch at 2 a.m. — that can be sold, subpoenaed, or geofenced.
The single biggest worry

Google knows where you are when you think no one's watching — and that map is reachable by advertisers, law enforcement, and campaigns. A single "geofence warrant" can reveal everyone who was near a protest, clinic, or place of worship at a given time.

The surprising part

The most useful free service ever built had to be free to extract the data. If you paid for search, you'd guard what you typed; because it's free, you pour your whole intent into it. The business isn't answering your question — it's the record of you asking it.

In one sentence

Google gives you the world's most useful tool for free so it can build and sell the world's most detailed portrait of you.
