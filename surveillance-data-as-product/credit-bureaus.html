Group A · The original data merchants

Credit bureaus

You never signed up, can't leave, and aren't the customer.

Credit bureaus are the original "you are the product" business — and they predate the internet. Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion keep detailed financial dossiers on nearly every adult and sell reports and scores to lenders, landlords, employers, and insurers. You never became their customer; you're the thing being profiled and sold. And you can't opt out.

The core bet

The model is pure data extraction and resale: collect financial data on everyone without asking, package it into reports and scores, and sell it to whoever decides your loan, apartment, or job. When something goes wrong — a breach — you bear the risk while the bureau pays a fine and carries on.

What the numbers say

147M
People exposed in the 2017 Equifax breach — names, Social Security numbers, birth dates, addresses. The 2019 settlement (~$575M) was roughly a tenth of one year's revenue.
~220M
Americans profiled by the three bureaus — no permission needed. Banks report your payments automatically; public records get scraped in.
$40–50B
Rough size of the US credit-reporting and consumer-data industry — a business built entirely on data about people who aren't the customers.
300–850
The opaque score range. The same underlying data yields different scores sold to different buyers, by formulas you can't see.

The same firm, many masks

Bureaus also run marketing-data and identity-verification arms that resell your profile to advertisers and other brokers under different brand names. So the company selling your credit history to a lender may also be selling your shopping behavior to a marketer — you just don't realize it's the same entity.

What's risky for you
You can't leave, can't easily audit the file, and eat the fraud risk when they're breached.
What's valuable for them
An involuntary, comprehensive dataset on every adult — the ultimate captive product.
The single biggest worry

Breaches keep happening, and each time millions are exposed — but the bureau pays a fine and the model survives intact. You carry the identity-theft risk; they treat the penalty as a cost of doing business.

The surprising part

This surveillance business is over a century old — Equifax traces back to the 1890s. Long before "big tech," three private companies had already built involuntary dossiers on nearly everyone and made them the product. The internet didn't invent data-as-product; it just scaled a model the credit bureaus proved a hundred years ago.

In one sentence

Credit bureaus are the original surveillance machine: they own your financial dossier, sell it constantly, and when they're hacked you pay the price while they pay pocket change.
