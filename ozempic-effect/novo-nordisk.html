Group A · The makers

Novo Nordisk — invented Ozempic, then lost the race

A quiet Danish insulin maker became Europe's most valuable company — and gave the crown back in 18 months.

Novo Nordisk is where the whole story starts. It made a diabetes drug called semaglutide — then discovered it caused dramatic weight loss. That became Ozempic (for diabetes) and Wegovy (for weight loss), and for about two years, Novo owned the biggest new market in medicine.

The rocket

~$26B
A year in sales from its two semaglutide drugs — about 70% of the whole company. A sleepy insulin maker became a phenomenon.
~$636B
Its peak value (mid-2024) — briefly making it worth more than any other company in Europe, and more than the entire Danish stock market.

Then it lost the race

Two things went wrong at once. Novo couldn't make enough — Wegovy was in shortage, frustrating patients. And a rival showed up with a better drug: Eli Lilly's tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) produced more weight loss. Doctors and patients switched.

57% vs ~38%
By 2025, Eli Lilly had taken the majority of the US market; Novo was shrinking.
The blow that confirmed it

In early 2026, Novo's big next-generation drug (CagriSema) was tested head-to-head against Lilly's — and lost (20.2% weight loss vs 23.6%). The stock dropped sharply. Its CEO, who had led the whole obesity push, was replaced.

−75%
The stock fell about 75% from its peak — roughly $440 billion of value gone — and Novo now faces its first sales decline in modern history.

The main worry

All the threats point the same way: Lilly is ahead and pulling further. Its drug works better, it has a convenient pill coming, and cheap copies flooded the market during the shortage — pushing prices down. Novo went from "can't make enough" to "fighting to keep customers" in under two years.

The surprising part

Novo's rise and fall is a lesson that being first isn't the same as being best. It discovered the biggest drug category in a generation almost by accident — an old diabetes molecule that happened to melt away weight — and briefly became more valuable than its entire home country's stock market. Then it lost the lead not to a cleverer idea, but to a rival with better chemistry and more factories.

In one sentence

Novo Nordisk in a nutshell

It invented the weight-loss-drug era and briefly ruled it — then couldn't make enough, got out-performed by Eli Lilly, and watched most of its value evaporate. First, but not best.
