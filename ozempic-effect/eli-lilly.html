Group A · The makers

Eli Lilly — the winner of the weight-loss race

A 148-year-old drugmaker became the first $1-trillion healthcare company — on two molecules.

While Novo Nordisk stumbled, Eli Lilly pulled ahead — because its drug simply works better. Its tirzepatide (sold as Mounjaro for diabetes and Zepbound for weight loss) produces more weight loss than Novo's, and Lilly bet early and huge on making it.

How big it got

~$36B
Sales from those two drugs in 2025 — about 56% of the entire company. One drug family became the majority of a 148-year-old firm.
$1 trillion
Lilly's value in late 2025 — the first healthcare company ever worth a trillion dollars.

The move that won it: factories on faith

The real edge wasn't the pill — it was the plants

Lilly's smartest bet was made before anyone knew the drug would be a hit. Starting in 2020, it poured over $50 billion into new factories — building capacity on faith in the chemistry. So when demand exploded, Lilly could actually make the drug while Novo ran short. Owning the manufacturing was the quiet advantage.

And it's not resting: Lilly's daily pill (orforglipron) hit its targets in trials. A pill is cheaper and easier than a weekly injection, which could open the market to far more people — and Lilly is positioned to lead there too.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — everything rides on two drugs

About two-thirds of Lilly's revenue now comes from tirzepatide. Its trillion-dollar price tag assumes nothing goes wrong: no strong competitor, no big price cuts, no early generics. But rivals are coming (Amgen, Viking, Novo's pill), governments are pushing on drug prices, and patents don't last forever. Sky-high expectations leave little room for a stumble.

The surprising part

A single class of drugs made a sleepy legacy company the most valuable healthcare firm in history — not through decades of research brilliance, but through the nerve to build billion-dollar factories in 2020, before knowing if the drug would work. Lilly's advantage was less about inventing and more about believing early enough to build. Now that same concentration is the risk: a trillion dollars balanced on two molecules.

In one sentence

Eli Lilly in a nutshell

Its drug works better and it built the factories early, so it won the biggest new market in medicine and became the first trillion-dollar healthcare company — with almost everything now riding on two molecules staying on top.
