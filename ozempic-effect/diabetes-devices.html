Group D · Surprising medical ripples

Diabetes devices — the feared threat that became a customer

Dexcom and Insulet were supposed to shrink. Instead they started selling to the drug users.

Companies like Dexcom, Insulet, and Abbott make the glucose monitors and insulin pumps that diabetics use. The worry was obvious: if weight-loss drugs improve blood sugar and cut insulin needs, wouldn't fewer people need the gadgets?

The fear

In early 2023, investors bet on "demand destruction" — the idea that healthier, less-insulin-dependent patients wouldn't need the hardware. Device stocks (especially the insulin-pump maker Insulet) took a hit on that story.

What actually happened — it held up

  • The number of diabetics didn't drop. Around 40 million Americans have diabetes, and the rate stayed flat — no collapse.
  • The device market grew, not shrank.
  • They started selling to drug users. Dexcom launched an over-the-counter monitor ("Stelo," ~$89) aimed at non-diabetics and people on weight-loss drugs.
  • The drug and the device work better together. Abbott showed its monitor plus a weight-loss drug beat the drug alone — and users stuck with both.
The flip

The story went from "only diabetics need these devices" to "people on weight-loss drugs want glucose feedback to get the most out of them." The feared threat turned into a fresh market.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the long game

The real danger is farther out. If these drugs genuinely shrink obesity and type-2 diabetes over the next 5 to 10+ years, the core patient pool could finally get smaller. Future growth increasingly depends on selling outside the diabetic population — to wellness users and drug takers — rather than a growing base of patients.

The surprising part

This is the opposite of a demand-destruction story — at least so far. Rather than losing customers to the drugs, the device makers turned the drug users into customers. The company everyone thought the drugs would hurt built a product specifically for people on them.

In one sentence

Diabetes devices in a nutshell

Feared as a casualty, they adapted by selling monitors to the very people taking the drugs — real for now. The open question is whether, in a decade, a much healthier population finally shrinks their core market.
