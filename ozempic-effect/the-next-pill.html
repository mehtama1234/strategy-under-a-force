Group A · The makers

The next-pill race — everyone wants in

The prize could be the biggest drug market ever, so every giant and hungry biotech is chasing it.

Two companies own the weight-loss market today. But it's so enormous that everyone else is scrambling to break in — and the prize goes to whoever makes a version that's cheaper and more convenient, ideally a simple daily pill that works as well as the injections.

Why it's the biggest prize in medicine

~$100B+
What the weight-loss-drug market could be worth by 2030 — potentially the largest drug market ever created (some forecasts reach ~$190B by 2035).

Today's drugs are weekly injections that need refrigeration and discipline. A good daily pill, or a longer-lasting shot, could grab huge share — which is why the race is on.

The contenders

  • Amgen (MariTide) — a monthly shot. Promising weight loss, but too many people quit over nausea; it's tweaking the dose.
  • Viking Therapeutics — a tiny biotech that's a Wall Street darling; a pill version showed solid results and it's now in big final trials.
  • Pfizer — the giant that keeps failing at its own pills; it paid $10 billion to buy a startup (Metsera) just to get back in the race — outbidding Novo Nordisk.
  • Roche & Structure — both bought or built pipelines with strong early data, aiming to be a top-3 player.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the leaders are years ahead

Novo and Lilly have a 3–5 year head start, proven safety records, and enormous factories. And these trials frequently disappoint: too much nausea, people quitting, and worries about losing muscle along with fat. Catching up takes billions of dollars and years — time most challengers don't have before the leaders lock in the market.

The surprising part

Two things stand out. A tiny biotech can be worth billions on a single trial result — Viking is valued in the billions largely on promising data, a wild swing for a small company. And the giant, Pfizer, keeps failing at making these drugs itself — despite a massive research budget, it had to pay $10 billion to buy its way back in. In this race, faith and chemistry matter more than size.

In one sentence

The race in a nutshell

The weight-loss market is so huge that everyone's chasing a cheaper, easier version — but Novo and Lilly are so far ahead on results and factories that even a blockbuster rival trial may arrive too late.
