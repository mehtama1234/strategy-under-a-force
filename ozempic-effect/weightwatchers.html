Group B · The ones getting eaten

WeightWatchers — a shot did its whole job

The company that was the diet industry, killed by the thing it spent 60 years selling.

If you want the single clearest casualty of the weight-loss-drug boom, it's WeightWatchers. For 60 years it sold weight loss the slow way — points to count, weekly weigh-ins, a monthly fee, and Oprah's blessing. Then a weekly injection did the exact same thing, faster.

Why it was so exposed

WeightWatchers' whole business was paying every month to be coached toward eating less. Ozempic and its cousins delivered bigger, faster weight loss with a shot. Why pay for willpower coaching when a prescription does it better? Members walked, and the debt the company had piled up became crushing.

The collapse

~99%
Stock decline — from ~$100 a few years earlier down to about 13 cents. The company filed for bankruptcy in May 2025.
Even Oprah left

In early 2024, Oprah Winfrey stepped off the board — after revealing she was using a weight-loss drug herself. The stock dropped sharply that day. The company's most famous champion, using the very thing that was killing the company.

What it tried

WeightWatchers tried to sell the very drugs that were killing it — it bought a telehealth startup to prescribe them, trying to become the place you get the shot rather than the place you go to avoid it. That new business is actually growing — but it arrived after the company was already bankrupt and its shareholders wiped out. Right idea, far too late.

The lesson

The takeaway

When a new product does your exact job better and cheaper, your whole business can vanish fast — and pivoting to sell the thing that killed you is brutally hard. You go from the category-defining brand to a small, late, indebted reseller competing with everyone. (This is the GLP-1 world's version of Chegg — killed by a tool that did its one job for less.)

The surprising part

The company that was the diet industry got killed by a shot — and now sells that same shot to stay alive. It's like a newspaper trying to survive by reselling internet subscriptions: technically the right pivot, but you've become a small middleman for the exact thing that destroyed your original monopoly.

In one sentence

WeightWatchers in a nutshell

A pill did the one thing it sold, so members left and the stock lost ~99% — and its survival plan is to resell the very drug that ruined it, a pivot that came after bankruptcy.
