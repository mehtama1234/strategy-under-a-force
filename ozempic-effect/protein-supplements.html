Group C · Adapting & selling to the trend

The muscle-loss economy

The drugs melt away muscle along with fat — so protein, gyms, and even new drugs are cashing in on the fix.

Here's a side effect that became a business. Weight-loss shots don't just burn fat — a big chunk of what people lose is muscle. That one fact turned protein, supplements, and strength training from "optional" into "recommended" for anyone on the drugs.

Muscle loss became a selling point

~25–40%
Share of the weight lost on these drugs that can be muscle, not fat. So the pitch writes itself: "don't lose your muscle."

Who's cashing in

  • Protein-shake makers — Premier Protein's CEO flat out said "we are the brand that gets the most benefits from GLP-1," with sales up double digits; rival Optimum Nutrition raised its growth targets and blamed (thanked) the drugs.
  • Whey protein prices hit record highs — up well over 100% in two years.
  • Gyms & fitness — Equinox built a "GLP-1 Protocol" training program; Peloton credited strength-workout growth partly to the drugs.
  • Creatine and supplements — pitched as muscle insurance for drug users.

The main worry

Real trend, or just the protein craze on steroids?

This might be a turbocharged ride on the existing protein fad rather than a durable new goldmine — and it's hard to prove for any one company. Warning signs: drug prescription growth has flattened, half of users quit within a year, and scientists argue the "muscle crisis" is partly overblown and cheaply fixed with protein and lifting — which undercuts paying for special products to solve it.

The surprising part

Drugs to fix the drugs

The muscle economy isn't just protein powder. A side effect of the weight-loss drugs created a whole new class of drugs designed to be sold alongside them — muscle-preserving medicines (from Regeneron, Scholar Rock, Veru and others) meant to keep the muscle the shots strip away. In other words: a coming wave of injectable drugs to counteract the injectable drugs. (Even Eli Lilly tried one — and shelved it — a reminder the idea is hot but unproven.)

In one sentence

The muscle economy in a nutshell

The drugs cost people muscle, so protein brands, gyms, and even new muscle-preserving drugs are selling the fix — a real tailwind, though it may be the old protein craze supercharged rather than a permanent new market.
