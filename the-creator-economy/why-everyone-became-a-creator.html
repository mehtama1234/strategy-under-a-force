Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

Why everyone became a creator

Once you could publish to billions for free and turn attention into money, tens of millions tried.

This is the mechanism. A few things combined: smartphones and free platforms removed every barrier to publishing; algorithms could make anyone go viral, so distribution was democratized; ad money and fan payments made attention directly convertible to income; and culture shifted so that 'creator' became the aspirational career. The result: attention became a career path for tens of millions.

The core bet

Once anyone could publish to a billion people for free and turn attention into money, a huge share of people tried — creating became a mass aspiration and a real (if lottery-like) career.

Removing gatekeepers concentrated the money, not spread it

The old gatekeepers (studios, labels, publishers) vanished — but that didn't spread opportunity around. The algorithm became the new gatekeeper, and network effects make breaking through harder than ever. The barriers to posting collapsed; the barriers to earning a living stayed right where they were.

The single biggest worry

It's a winner-take-most lottery: the door opened for everyone, but the income is wildly concentrated at the top. You can build for months and earn nothing, or catch one viral clip and make a year's pay overnight — the odds, not the effort, decide.

The surprising part

The cultural surprise is how fast the aspiration outran the economics. 'Creator' became a more-wanted job than doctor or astronaut for a whole generation — before it was clear that the path is open to everyone and livable for almost nobody. The dream spread faster than the money ever could.

In one sentence

Once you could publish to billions for free and turn attention into money, creating became the most democratic and most brutal career at once — open to everyone, livable for almost nobody.
