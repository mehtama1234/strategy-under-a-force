Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

The limits

The economy promised a middle class and delivered a power law — with burnout as the cost of playing.

This is the skeptical piece. The creator economy has hard limits: there is no middle class (extreme income inequality — a tiny top earns almost everything); rampant burnout from the always-on content treadmill; total dependence on platforms that can change the algorithm or payouts overnight; and now an AI-content flood devaluing attention further.

The core bet

The dream of 'anyone can make a living creating' runs into a brutal power law — the barriers to posting vanished but the barriers to earning a living did not, and the treadmill breaks people.

Platforms need millions of unpaid creators

Most creators are effectively unpaid labor generating the content and attention that make platforms valuable, while the algorithm decides who gets paid. The model isn't sustainable for the humans in it — burnout hits, people quit, and the platform simply replaces them with the next batch of hopefuls chasing the same dream.

The single biggest worry

The always-on treadmill — post daily, chase the algorithm, stay relevant — breaks most people before they earn a real paycheck, and the platforms have every incentive to keep the supply of hopefuls flowing.

The surprising part

The deepest irony is that the 'democratized' economy is more unequal than the music, film, and publishing industries it replaced. It looks egalitarian — anyone can upload — but the outcomes are more extreme, and the platforms need the millions earning nothing to make the few winners look achievable.

In one sentence

The creator economy promised a middle class but delivered a power law: a tiny elite earning millions, the vast majority near zero, and burnout as the price of playing.
