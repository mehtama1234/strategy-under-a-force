Group A · The creator as a business

The influencer as a company

One person's attention became a real corporation — with staff, revenue, and products.

A person with millions of followers is now a business, not just someone making videos. The attention and trust they've built can be turned into dozens of revenue lines — merch, brand deals, apps, courses, whole companies. The biggest creators run production teams of hundreds and command valuations that rival legacy media firms.

The core bet

A person with a big audience is now a media company; the persona is the top of a funnel that can be turned into many businesses.

The videos are a loss-leader for the real business

For the biggest creators the content isn't the product — it's the funnel. The videos keep the audience growing; the money comes from the products, licensing, and spin-off companies built on top of that audience. A 26-year-old can build a private company worth more than publicly traded media firms, with no IPO and no outside board.

The single biggest worry

The whole business rests on one person's health, attention, and ability to show up on camera. Burnout, a scandal, or a single algorithm change can wipe out a company that took years to build — the creator is the asset, and assets break.

The surprising part

The striking inversion is that the thing audiences watch is the cheap part. The persona is a customer-acquisition machine, and the enterprise underneath — the brand, the products, the team — is where the value actually sits. The creator became the company, and the content became its marketing.

In one sentence

A person's attention became a corporation with staff, revenue, and products — the creator became the company.
