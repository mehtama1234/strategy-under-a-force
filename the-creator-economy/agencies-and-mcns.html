Group B · The picks & shovels

Agencies & MCNs

Hollywood's talent-agency model got rebuilt for TikTokers — and the first version already blew up once.

Around creators sits a layer of talent managers, influencer-marketing agencies, and (once) multi-channel networks that broker brand deals and take a cut — usually 10–20%. It's the same commission model Hollywood agencies used for decades, rebadged for TikTok. Traditional agencies (WME, CAA, UTA) moved in; the first wave of networks mostly collapsed.

The core bet

Creators need help selling their audience to brands, so managers and agencies broker the deals and take a percentage — the Hollywood talent model applied to influencers.

What the numbers say

$32.6B
Global influencer-marketing spend in 2025, up from ~$24B in 2024 — the deal flow agencies live on.
10–20%
Standard agency/manager commission on a brand deal, sometimes plus a monthly retainer.
$500M+
What Disney paid for Maker Studios (the flagship MCN) in 2014 — it collapsed by 2017 as ad splits shrank.
WME · CAA · UTA
Hollywood agencies that now run creator divisions, signing Charli D'Amelio, Addison Rae, and more.
700K+
Creators on brand-deal software (Grin) that lets brands and creators connect directly, bypassing agencies.

The middleman didn't die — it changed shape

The first wave of creator middlemen (MCNs) mostly disappeared when YouTube's ad splits shrank, but the value didn't vanish — it moved. Now it lives in software marketplaces that take a cut of brand deals and in old-school talent agencies that rebuilt their playbook for influencers. From network to agent, from ad-powered to deal-powered.

The single biggest worry

The MCN model already failed once, and the same threat remains: creators can now DM brands directly or use software to match with them, so a human middleman taking 20% is optional the moment creators can do it cheaper themselves.

The surprising part

The pattern worth noting is disintermediation pressure. Every middleman in the creator economy survives only while it's cheaper than the creator doing the job alone — and as tools make direct brand-to-creator deals easy, the cut the agency takes is constantly under threat.

In one sentence

When creators need brands and brands need audiences, middlemen take a cut — but they survive only if creators can't build those relationships more cheaply themselves.
