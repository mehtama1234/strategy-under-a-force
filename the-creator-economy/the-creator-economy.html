The creator economy

Attention became income, so millions became creators — but the money pooled with the shovels.

Pull the whole collection together and one line runs through it. The internet turned attention into income and made the influencer a company — persona, staff, revenue lines, sometimes a billion-dollar brand. But the reliable money pooled with the infrastructure (platforms, tools, agencies) and the few who own products, while most creators earn almost nothing. The democratization of publishing did not democratize the earnings.

The core bet

Attention became income so millions became creators, but durable profit pools with the infrastructure — platforms, tools, agencies — and the few who own brands, not the average creator.

Winners and losers

The economy sorts cleanly into who owns the infrastructure and who is chasing the views.

Who ends up ahead

The platforms (own the audience and take the cut), the tool and software makers (sell shovels), the agencies and ad-tech, and the top ~1% who own products and superfan bases.

Who's exposed

The ~99% of creators earning little, burning out on the always-on treadmill, and sitting one algorithm change away from zero.

The single biggest worry

The barriers to posting vanished but the barriers to earning a living didn't, so the 'democratized' economy is more unequal than the media world it replaced — and it runs on millions of unpaid or near-unpaid creators to make the few winners look achievable.

The surprising part

The note to end on is where the scarcity finally lands. In a world where anyone can publish and — increasingly — AI can generate infinite content for free, the only things left that are scarce are a real, owned audience and human authenticity. That's why the smartest creators are quietly moving off rented platforms toward owning the relationship and the product. The safest way to profit from the gold rush was always to sell shovels; the safest way to survive it as a creator is to own the ground you stand on.

In one sentence

Millions became creators because attention became income, but reliable profit pooled with the platforms and tools, not the creators — turning the most 'democratized' economy into the most unequal one.
