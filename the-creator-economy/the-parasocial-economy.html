Group C · The commerce turn

The parasocial economy

The feeling of a personal bond — even one-sided — is the most valuable thing a creator can sell.

The deepest creator money comes from parasocial bonds — fans who feel a personal relationship and pay for closeness: channel memberships, Patreon tiers, Twitch subs, Cameo, and above all OnlyFans. A small number of superfans pay far more than millions of casual viewers ever could, because intimacy is worth more than attention.

The core bet

The feeling of a personal relationship — even one-sided — is the most valuable thing to sell; superfans pay for access and belonging far more than advertisers pay for eyeballs.

What the numbers say

Intimacy at scale became a bigger business than most media

A creator with 100 fans paying $10 a month can out-earn one with a million casual viewers, because the superfan is buying a relationship, not content. Selling the feeling of one-on-one closeness to tens of thousands of paying subscribers turned out to be a more profitable model than traditional media — on a tiny fraction of the staff.

The single biggest worry

It runs on emotional labor and parasocial dependence that can be exploitative and hard to sustain — and on fragile payment rails: banks and processors have frozen or delisted creators (especially adult content) with little notice, erasing income overnight.

The surprising part

The paradox is that the business hinges on an illusion. The revenue depends on each of thousands of fans feeling they have a personal bond with someone who cannot possibly know them all — closeness manufactured at scale. It's the most lucrative thing in the economy and the most emotionally precarious, at the same time.

In one sentence

Creators earn more selling the feeling of a personal bond to a few hundred committed fans than selling ads to millions of strangers.
