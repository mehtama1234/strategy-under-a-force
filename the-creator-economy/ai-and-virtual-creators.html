Group C · The commerce turn

AI & virtual creators

As AI floods the feed with near-free content, human authenticity becomes the only scarce thing left.

AI is reshaping the creator economy from both ends: synthetic influencers (AI-generated personas earning real brand deals), AI tools that let one human produce far more (scripts, voices, dubbing into dozens of languages), and a flood of AI-generated 'slop' competing for the same attention and ad money. The value shifts to whoever owns the audience and can prove they're real.

The core bet

AI both supercharges human creators and threatens to replace them with synthetic ones and a flood of cheap content — so value moves to whoever owns the audience and the authenticity.

What the numbers say

240+ avatars
AI-avatar tools (Synthesia, HeyGen) that let anyone generate a talking presenter in 150+ languages.

Synthetic creators never age, get canceled, or ask for a raise

For brands, a virtual influencer is all upside: no key-person risk, works 24/7, never sparks a scandal or demands more money. For human creators, that's an extinction threat from above — while the AI-content flood attacks from below, generating a hundred cheap videos for the price of one.

The single biggest worry

A tsunami of near-free AI content could crater the value of human attention — why pay a creator when you can generate a hundred posts for $50? — and synthetic influencers remove every human friction brands dislike.

The surprising part

The twist is what scarcity does. As content becomes infinite and free, the one thing AI can't fake — a real person who actually lived something, the off-script moment, earned trust — flips from commodity to premium. The creator who owns their audience's trust wins; the one replaced by a 3D model doesn't.

In one sentence

AI is splitting the creator economy: synthetic influencers take the scalable, risk-free brand deals, while humans who own real audience trust hold the only thing that can't be copied — realness.
