Group C · The commerce turn

Creator commerce

The feed became a store — and China proved live shopping can eclipse search-based shopping entirely.

The newest turn is shopping built straight into content: TikTok Shop, live shopping, shoppable videos, and affiliate storefronts (Amazon Influencer Program, LTK). The gap between 'see it' and 'buy it' collapses — the creator becomes the salesperson and the feed becomes the checkout, earning a commission on every sale.

The core bet

Collapse the gap between seeing it and buying it — creators sell products live or via tagged links and earn commission, turning entertainment into a storefront.

The creator became the shelf

In China, Douyin's live-shopping sales rival entire marketplaces, and star hosts earn TV-anchor salaries selling on camera. The creator didn't just point at the store shelf — they became it. Content-commerce there is now a primary distribution engine, not a side feature, and the US is following.

The single biggest worry

Much of the US model sits on one platform, TikTok, whose future has been legally uncertain — a ban or forced sale would scramble it. And trust erodes fast: when every creator looks like they're selling, viewers tune out and conversion collapses without constant novelty.

The surprising part

The surprise from China is direction of causation: live shopping doesn't just add to e-commerce, it can eclipse it. The creator became QVC for the phone generation — and in the market that's furthest ahead, that turned out to be a bigger channel than searching a store for what you already wanted.

In one sentence

Creators turned themselves into salespeople earning 5–20% on every sale, and China proved content-commerce can grow bigger than search-based shopping.
