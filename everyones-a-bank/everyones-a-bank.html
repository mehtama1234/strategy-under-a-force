Group E · The big picture

Everyone's a bank — the whole force in one page

One idea ties every page together: the product gets you in the door, but the financing is where the money is made.

Step back from the twelve stories and one idea explains them all. Selling a thing is low-margin and one-time. But lending against it, processing the payment, holding the customer's money as float, and insuring it are high-margin, recurring, and data-rich. So company after company turned its product into a front for a financial-services business — because it already owned the customer, the payment relationship, and the data to lend, charge, and insure.

The one idea: banking got unbundled from banks

The whole force in a sentence

The car dealer earns more on the loan than the car; the retailer on store credit; the airline on the co-brand card; Apple on payments; the fintechs on lending; the payment networks on the toll; the insurers on the float. "The bank" got unbundled from banks and spread into cars, phones, stores, and apps — so banking is now everywhere and nowhere.

Where the frame holds — and where it bends

The frame is fair — but finance isn't a free lunch. It's high-margin and high-risk: defaults, rate swings, and regulation all bite. A lot of it was fuelled by cheap money, and higher rates now stress it (auto delinquencies up, BNPL layoffs). Not every company can lend well. And some of it is just old-fashioned credit rebranded with a friendlier button.

The scale of the shift

~$7.5T
US consumer debt (2025) — auto loans ~$1.5T, cards ~$1.1T, plus BNPL — much of it originated by the non-banks in this collection.
~$1.5–2T
Private credit — non-banks are now ~40–50% of the credit market, up from ~15% in 2008. Visa/Mastercard run at ~65% margins.

Who wins, who loses

Win

Whoever owns the customer + payment + data (Apple, Amazon, carmakers, retailers); the payment networks; the private-credit funds; the fintech lenders.

Lose

Traditional banks (disintermediated); consumers (layered debt, some invisible); and taxpayers, if the shadow system seizes up with no backstop.

The biggest tension

The open question

Rising consumer debt collides with a lightly-regulated shadow-banking system that has never faced a real downturn. The 2022–24 rate shock stressed captive auto lenders and BNPL firms but didn't break them. The unanswered question: does a deep recession or another rate shock finally reveal that all this borrowing is unsustainable — and force the shadow lenders to mark down their books?

The surprising part

Banking got unbundled — and then weaponized by the same data engine. Apple, Amazon, and Walmart own the customer, the transaction, and the data. That single data feed powers three parallel profit machines at once: lending (this collection), advertising (the ad economy), and loyalty (the loyalty economy). One data point — your purchase, your location, your habit — is simultaneously collateral for a loan, a signal for an ad, and a reason to keep you subscribed. No traditional bank ever had that entanglement. Now it's everywhere.

In one sentence

Everyone's a bank in a nutshell

The product is the moat and the financing is the profit, so banking got unbundled from banks into cars, phones, stores, and apps — powered by the same customer data that also sells ads and loyalty, and now resting on a mountain of consumer debt and a shadow system no one fully regulates.
