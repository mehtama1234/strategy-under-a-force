Group B · Tech becomes a bank

Apple's financial empire — a bank quietly built into the iPhone

Payments, a credit card, a savings account, and a wallet — Apple assembled a bank inside the phone in your pocket.

The most valuable company on earth has been quietly building a full bank around the iPhone — to own the payment relationship and lock you deeper into its ecosystem. Piece by piece: Apple Pay, Apple Cash, the Apple Card, a high-yield Apple Savings account, and its own buy-now-pay-later. But it also learned that banking is hard.

The stack it built

  • Apple Pay (2014): tap-to-pay, now billions in annual transactions.
  • Apple Card (2019, with Goldman Sachs): a credit card that lives in the Wallet app.
  • Apple Savings (2023): a ~4%+ account that pulled in $1B+ in deposits within weeks.
  • Tap-to-pay on iPhone (2022): any iPhone can now accept card payments — turning phones into card readers.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — banking is hard and regulated

Banking is a capital-heavy, low-margin, compliance-laden business Apple isn't built for. When Goldman retreated from consumer banking (2023), Apple was left holding millions of depositors and cardholders and scrambling for a new partner — and it quietly killed its in-house Pay Later in 2024, a year in.

The surprising part

The company that prides itself on controlling the whole stack discovered it couldn't own a bank alone. It had to hand the Card and Savings off to partners rather than build in-house, and pull the plug on Pay Later — a rare admission that financial regulation and capital rules can't be solved with slick design and software. Even Apple learned that inside the world's nicest app, you still have to play by bankers' rules.

In one sentence

Apple's financial empire in a nutshell

Apple built a full financial-services business into the iPhone to own the payment relationship and lock users in — then learned that banking is hard, regulated, and low-glamour, and had to lean on partners.
