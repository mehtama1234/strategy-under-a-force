Group C · The toll & the float

The float — the money you hold that isn't yours

Hold a customer's cash before you owe them anything, invest it, and keep the returns. Buffett built an empire on it.

One of the most lucrative financial tricks is the "float" — money a company holds temporarily, for free, before it has to pay it out or deliver. You load $25 onto an app; it's the company's to invest until you spend it. The master of this is insurance: premiums come in now, claims are paid years later, and the money in between is a free, investable loan.

Float, big and small

~$177B
Berkshire Hathaway's insurance float (2026) — the free capital Warren Buffett has invested for decades to build one of history's great fortunes. He sometimes accepts an underwriting loss just to hold the float.
  • Starbucks holds ~$1.85B in prepaid app/card balances — at ~4% rates, ~$80M a year in interest on money customers gave it for coffee.
  • "Breakage": 5–20% of gift cards are never redeemed (~$21B/yr) — pure profit, no cost, no delivery. Starbucks booked ~$207M of it in 2024.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Float is a liability that can flow back out. If rates fall, the returns shrink; if customers stop pre-loading, or the company hits trouble, that money has to be returned — turning an asset into a crunch. And consumer float (Starbucks Korea's ~$276M) often sits with little regulatory protection if the company fails.

The surprising part

The most valuable asset many companies own is money that isn't even theirs. An insurer can lose money on the actual insurance and still be hugely profitable, because the investing of the float more than covers it. Buffett understood this earlier and better than anyone: he buys insurance companies not for the underwriting, but for the float. A coffee chain runs a tiny version of the same trick every time you top up the app.

In one sentence

The float in a nutshell

Holding other people's money interest-free before you owe it is one of the most lucrative levers in business — the engine of Buffett's fortune, and a quiet profit center for any company you prepay.
