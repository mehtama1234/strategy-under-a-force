Group C · The toll & the float

Payment rails — a toll booth on nearly every swipe

Visa and Mastercard touch none of the money and bear none of the risk — yet skim a cut of almost every transaction on earth.

Visa and Mastercard are the most profitable "boring" business in the world. They sit on the rails between your bank and the merchant's bank and take a tiny slice of nearly every card payment. The genius: they bear zero credit risk — the banks that issue cards eat the defaults. Visa and Mastercard just run the network and collect a toll.

A toll booth with 65% margins

~$27T / ~65%
Combined annual payment volume Visa + Mastercard process, at ~65% (Visa) and ~56% (Mastercard) operating margins — among the highest in the entire stock market, on ~90% of card rails outside China.

Visa made ~$18B profit on ~$40B revenue (FY2025). It's a near-duopoly, and the margins dwarf airlines, telecoms, or software.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — regulators break the toll

Merchants have fought the "swipe fee" for 20 years; a big US settlement and bills like the Credit Card Competition Act aim to force competing networks. Their moat is the network effect, not innovation — so if a crack opens and merchants can route around them, defection could cascade and compress the margins fast.

The surprising part

Here's the sleight of hand: Visa and Mastercard don't really set the "swipe fee" people blame them for. They take a small network cut (~0.15%); most of the fee is "interchange" that goes to the banks as compensation for credit risk. That lets the networks claim they're just "neutral rails" — while quietly capturing a risk-free slice of nearly every transaction on the planet. It's a private tax on commerce, engineered to look like plumbing.

In one sentence

Payment rails in a nutshell

Visa and Mastercard are a ~90%-share duopoly earning ~65% margins on ~$27 trillion of payments a year while bearing no lending risk — a risk-free toll on almost every swipe, now finally in regulators' sights.
