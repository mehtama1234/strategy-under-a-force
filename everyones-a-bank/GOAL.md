# Everyone's a Bank — strategy under a force

The twelfth "strategy under a force" collection. The scout's top pick — sibling to the loyalty economy and "everyone's an ad business": same first-party data and customer relationship, but pointed at **lending, payments, insurance, and float** instead of ads.

**The force:** financialization — non-financial companies discovering that the most profitable thing they do is often the *financial* thing attached to the sale, not the sale itself. The car dealer makes more from the loan than the car; the airline more from the credit card than the flight; the retailer from store credit; and tech firms (Apple, BNPL, fintech) are quietly becoming banks. We ask of each company or sector: *what did financialization make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** the product gets you in the door; the **financing is where the money is made.** Selling a thing is low-margin and one-time. Lending against it, processing the payment, holding the customer's money as "float," and insuring it are high-margin, recurring, and data-rich. So company after company turned its product into a front for a financial-services business — because it already had the customer, the payment relationship, and the data to lend, charge, and insure. Value flows to whoever owns the payment and the credit; it drains from traditional banks (disintermediated) and often lands, as debt, on the customer. "The bank" got unbundled from banks and spread into cars, phones, stores, and apps.

**Groups:**
- **A — The product is a front for the loan:** car dealers & automakers · retail store credit · big-ticket point-of-sale loans
- **B — Tech becomes a bank:** Apple's financial empire · buy-now-pay-later · embedded finance
- **C — The toll & the float:** payment rails (Visa/Mastercard) · the float · brokerages & payment-for-order-flow
- **D — Finance eats industries:** insurance & warranties · private credit & the shadow banks · fintech super-apps
- **E — The big picture:** everyones-a-bank (capstone) + patterns

**Method / constraints:**
- Research on Haiku, done directly by each agent (NO sub-agents), facts separated from hype.
- Plain, everyday language. No jargon, no clichés. Short sentences, real numbers with dates, concrete examples.
- Self-contained dark-theme HTML pages sharing styles.css, grouped hub + patterns synthesis, cross-linked to the eleven sibling collections (esp. loyalty economy — airline miles / card points — and the ad economy).
