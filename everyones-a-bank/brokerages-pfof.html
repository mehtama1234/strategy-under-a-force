Group C · The toll & the float

Brokerages & "free" trading — you're the product

Robinhood made trading "free." It isn't — your orders are quietly sold to the firms that trade against them.

Robinhood made stock trading commission-free and everyone copied it. But "free" trading isn't free. Robinhood earns mainly from "payment for order flow" — it sells your buy and sell orders to high-frequency trading firms (like Citadel Securities), who pay for the right to fill them. You pay no commission; you are the product.

How "free" pays

  • Payment for order flow was ~80% of Robinhood's revenue (2020); Citadel alone reportedly paid it $200M+ a year.
  • It also earns from margin lending, interest on your idle cash, crypto, and a paid "Gold" subscription.
  • Gamification (confetti, streaks) nudged users to trade more — because more trades mean more order-flow revenue.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — a built-in conflict

The brokerage makes more money the more you trade — whether or not it helps you. Margin lending makes it worse: it profits when you borrow to bet and lose. The 2021 GameStop episode exposed the tangle — Robinhood halted trading citing clearing rules, but Citadel was both a big order-flow buyer and a party on the other side.

The surprising part

"Commission-free" was never generosity — it was a race to the bottom that turned retail trading into a product to be sold. Your orders flow through a pipeline that benefits high-frequency firms; the confetti is the noise covering the signal. The brokerage makes money on your activity, your cash, and your borrowing — the exact opposite of a fiduciary who's supposed to act in your interest.

In one sentence

Brokerages & "free" trading in a nutshell

"Free" trading is funded by selling your orders to high-frequency firms — so the brokerage wins when you trade more, borrow more, and lose more, not when you do well.
