The patterns

Line up everyone financialization touched, and one theme dominates: the product gets you in the door, and the financing — the loan, the toll, the float — is where the money is actually made.

Each page asks one question: what did financialization make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable for this company? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions keep showing up. Click any name to jump to its page.

The one thing to take away

Selling a thing is low-margin and one-time; lending against it, taking a cut of the payment, holding the float, and insuring it are high-margin, recurring, and data-rich. So the product became the moat and the finance became the profit — and "the bank" got unbundled from banks into cars, phones, stores, and apps.

The recurring patterns

Pattern 01

The product is a front for the loan

The car, the sofa, the phone, the store card — all get you in the door so a loan, a lease, or a payment plan can be sold. The financing, not the sale, is where the margin lives.

car dealersstore creditbig-ticket loans
Pattern 02

The financial arm is worth more than the "real" business

Ford Credit out-earns the factories; a retailer's card can be 40% of its profit; an insurer is really an investment fund. Strip out the finance and the "product" business is far smaller than it looks.

Ford Creditstore cardsinsurers
Pattern 03

The company with your data is the best lender

Shopify knows a merchant's sales; Uber knows a driver's earnings; a store knows your spending. That real-time data makes them better at judging credit than a legacy bank — so lending got embedded into every app.

embedded financeretailersBNPL
Pattern 04

Take a toll or hold the float — near-risk-free money

The purest plays don't even lend. Visa/Mastercard skim a cut of every swipe with zero credit risk; Buffett invests the insurance float for free; Starbucks holds your prepaid cash. Money that flows through you, or sits with you, is a machine.

payment railsthe floatinsurance
Pattern 05

"Free" isn't free — and the debt is often hidden

Free trading sells your orders; "pay in 4" is a loan; the store card is subprime lending. The cost is disguised — as a convenience, a payment button, or an invisible balance no credit bureau can see.

"free" tradingBNPLdeferred interest
Pattern 06

Banking left the banks — for lighter-regulated ground

Lending, payments, and float spread out of regulated banks into carmakers, tech firms, private-credit funds, and branchless apps — often into gray zones with fewer rules and less oversight.

private creditfintechApple
Pattern 07

Financialized profit is high-margin — and fragile

Finance is lucrative until it isn't. Loan books blow up in downturns; rates swing; and the shadow system has no backstop. The margin comes with risk that only a real recession will price.

auto loansprivate creditBNPL defaults
Pattern 08

One data feed, three profit engines

The same customer data powers lending here, ads in the ad economy, and repeat purchases in the loyalty economy. A single fact about you is at once collateral for a loan, a signal for an ad, and a reason to keep you subscribed.
