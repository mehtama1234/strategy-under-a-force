Group D · Finance eats industries

Insurance & warranties — the plan you never claim is pure profit

"Do you want the protection plan?" is one of the highest-margin questions in retail. And insurers are really investment funds.

Two related financial plays hide behind ordinary purchases. First, the extended warranty pushed at every checkout (Geek Squad, AppleCare) is one of the most profitable things a store sells — because most people never claim, so most of the price is pure profit. Second, and deeper: an insurance company isn't really in the insurance business. It's an investment fund that uses policies to raise cheap money.

Why "protection" is a goldmine

~50–70%
Gross margin on retail protection plans (Best Buy, AppleCare) — because only ~5–15% of buyers ever claim. It's the highest-margin product on the shelf.

"Embedded insurance" spread the same idea to checkout everywhere — travel, phone breakage, shipping protection, even at online carts.

The deeper play: float

Insurers are investment funds in disguise

Insurers collect premiums now and pay claims years later — and invest the money in between (the float). Many run an underwriting loss (pay out more in claims than they collect) and are still hugely profitable, because the investing covers it. That's why private-equity firms are buying insurers (Apollo bought Athene for its ~$40B+ of float) — not to price risk, but to control cheap capital to lend.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

If markets crash or rates fall sharply, insurers can lose both their float returns and their underwriting margin at once — the 2008-style squeeze. And warranty sellers have no diversification: they depend entirely on customers not claiming and on retail sales staying healthy.

The surprising part

The extended warranty is profitable precisely because you probably won't use it — and the friction (deductibles, denials) is designed to make sure. And the bigger surprise: an insurance company is fundamentally an investment fund that happens to sell policies. The policy is just the machine for raising cheap, long-term money — which is exactly why Wall Street is buying insurers to feed the private-credit boom.

In one sentence

Insurance & warranties in a nutshell

Warranties are near-pure profit because you rarely claim, and insurers are really investment funds that use policies to raise cheap capital — so "protection" is one of the loyalty economy's quietest financial engines.
