Group D · Finance eats industries

Fintech super-apps — the app that became your bank

The fastest-growing banks in the world have no branches — and started as a payment app.

A wave of apps started as one simple feature — usually payments — and grew into full banks: accounts, cards, loans, investing, crypto. No branches. The bet: a single phone app beats a branch network on speed, cost, and the young customer who's never set foot in a bank.

The branchless banks

  • Nubank (Brazil): started 2013 as a credit-card app, now 100M+ customers and Latin America's most valuable bank — with no branches.
  • Chime (US): 25M+ members, went public 2024; earns from card interchange and pay-advance.
  • Revolut (Europe): 50M users, ~$33B valuation; started as money transfer, now sells crypto, forex, stocks, insurance.
  • Cash App (Block) and PayPal/Venmo moved from payments into savings, investing, and BNPL.
~18%
Share of US Gen Z using a neobank as their primary account (2024) — traditional banks are losing young customers for the first time.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Many of these apps don't hold their own banking license — they rely on partner banks and gray-zone structures. If a partner fails or an app collapses, millions can lose access to their money with no branch to walk into and, in some places, unclear deposit insurance.

The surprising part

The West kept trying — and mostly failing — to build a true "super-app" like China's WeChat or Alipay (payments + chat + shopping + banking in one). Meta's Libra, Google's banking push, and others flopped. The winners took the opposite path: instead of bundling everything, they stayed narrow — just simple, fee-free financial services, one app, instant access. China's super-apps won by doing it all; the West's fintechs won by being the simplest.

In one sentence

Fintech super-apps in a nutshell

The fastest-growing banks in the world have no branches and started as payment apps — winning young customers by being simple and fee-free, even as the West keeps failing to build a true everything-app.
