Group B · Tech becomes a bank

Embedded finance — every app wants a wallet and a loan

If you already have the customer and their transaction data, you can lend to them better than a bank.

Software companies realized they can bolt banking onto any app. "Banking as a service" lets a business add accounts, cards, and loans without becoming a bank itself. The logic: if you see every transaction a customer makes, you can lend to them more accurately than a legacy bank — because you know their real numbers.

Banking, embedded everywhere

  • Shopify Capital lends to its merchants using their own sales data — it knows their revenue before the tax office does.
  • Stripe, Square/Block, Toast (restaurants) all offer payments + lending inside their dashboards.
  • Uber & DoorDash give drivers debit cards, instant pay, and small loans — becoming their gig workers' primary bank.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the plumbing can freeze your money

These apps usually don't hold a banking license themselves — they rely on middleware and partner banks. When the "banking as a service" provider Synapse collapsed in 2024, it froze an estimated 60,000+ customers' deposits for weeks: real paychecks and rent money, trapped, with no branch to walk into. The "your app is a bank" pipes are fragile.

The surprising part

The company with your transaction data is often a better lender to you than any bank — Shopify knows a merchant's sales in real time; Uber knows a driver's earnings. The data advantage is so strong that lending got embedded into every app. But it works too well: speed and data race ahead while the underlying custody and settlement plumbing stays fragile — until it breaks and traps real people's money.

In one sentence

Embedded finance in a nutshell

Every software company is becoming a lender because it has better data than a bank — until the payment plumbing behind "your app is now a bank" breaks and freezes real people's money.
