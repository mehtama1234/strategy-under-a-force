Group A · The product is a front for the loan

Big-ticket loans — the financing is the point, not the sofa

Furniture, mattresses, solar panels, dental work. For expensive things, the loan enables the sale — and is often the profit.

For big, expensive purchases, the loan is what makes the sale possible — and frequently is the real profit. Furniture and mattress stores push "0% APR." Solar installers run on loans. And elective healthcare — dental, vet, cosmetic, vision — runs on CareCredit, a medical credit card with a nasty trap in the fine print.

Where the money hides

  • Solar: ~80% of home installs are financed; the installer's margin on the panels can be thinner than the fee it pockets for arranging the loan.
  • Furniture: even on a "0% APR" deal, the store earns a 2–4% referral fee from the lender. It doesn't need you to pay in full — it wins either way.
  • Medical: CareCredit (Synchrony) runs ~$150B of volume across health procedures.

The main worry

The single biggest risk — the deferred-interest trap

Medical financing often uses "deferred interest": if you don't pay the whole balance by the promo deadline, it retroactively charges ~26–30% on the original amount. Roughly 40% of cardholders miss the deadline — so a $3,000 dental bill suddenly carries hundreds in back-interest. The moment of "yes" in the chair is divorced from the debt in the fine print.

The surprising part

The financing isn't a convenience bolted on — it's the economic engine. A solar installer earning 1–2% on the loan and less than 1% on the hardware is really a loan salesman; the panel is just collateral. A mattress store offering 0% doesn't expect you to pay it off — it's already paid by the lender. The product is the hook; the financial instrument is the catch — and the customer's later pain (late fees, rollover to 20%+ APR) is someone else's gain.

Big-ticket loans in a nutshell
