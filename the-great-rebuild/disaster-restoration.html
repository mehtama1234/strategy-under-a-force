Group B · The fragmented trades boom

Disaster restoration

The rare business that grows when things go wrong — and the insurer, not you, pays the bill.

As storms, floods, and fires grow more frequent and expensive, an industry of cleanup (water, fire, and mold remediation, then reconstruction) and resilience (fireproofing, flood barriers, backup power) is booming. It's insurance-funded, recurring, and counter-cyclical — the money keeps flowing no matter what the wider economy does — which is why franchises and PE-backed roll-ups are piling in.

The core bet

Disasters are rising and insurance pays the bill, so the steady money is in the cleanup-and-rebuild that happens regardless of the economy — plus helping people harden their homes before the next one.

The customer isn't the homeowner — it's the insurer

Because insurance funds and often directs the work, the business model is closer to B2B logistics than home retail. The company that wins the insurer's referral, not the homeowner's love, gets the job. And a new revenue stream is opening as climate money flows toward resilience and hardening, beyond just cleaning up after the fact.

The single biggest worry

The whole model rests on insurers staying in the game — and they're pulling out of the riskiest states (Florida, California) and hiking premiums, which can shrink the market. Claim disputes and scrutiny of over-billing also squeeze margins and strain the insurer relationships everything depends on.

The surprising part

It's one of the only businesses that grows when the news is bad. As climate disasters roughly multiply, restoration scales — and the same climate money that pays to clean up is starting to pay to prevent, turning a reactive cleanup trade into a proactive hardening one.

In one sentence

As disasters multiply and insurance foots the bill, the businesses that clean up and harden against the next one grow no matter what the economy does.
