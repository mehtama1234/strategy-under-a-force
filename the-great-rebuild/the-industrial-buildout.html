Group A · What needs building

The industrial build-out

Factories, data centers, and gigafactories — all going up at the same time.

The scarce thing is the ability to build enormous, complicated facilities fast. A wave of subsidized factories (semiconductor fabs, EV and battery plants), an AI-driven data-center boom, and years of warehouse building all landed at once. These aren't ordinary buildings — clean rooms, ultra-pure water, power-hungry server halls — so the specialized industrial contractors and engineers who can deliver them without blowing the budget hold the leverage.

The core bet

When money is plentiful (subsidies) but delivery is hard, the winner is whoever can actually build a $15-billion fab or a gigawatt data center on time. The constraint isn't demand — it's the crews, the engineering, and the project management to pull it off.

What the numbers say

~2x
U.S. manufacturing-construction spending roughly doubled from about $50B (2022) to ~$100B (2024).
$52B
The CHIPS Act (2022) subsidy pool for semiconductor fabs built on U.S. soil.
$200B+
Announced U.S. battery- and EV-factory investment following the 2022 Inflation Reduction Act.
$15–25B
Cost to build one advanced semiconductor fab — taking 3–5 years, and routinely 20–40% over budget.
2023–25
The data-center capex surge, as AI companies raced to build GPU-heavy facilities.
15+ yrs
Warehouse/logistics building has run continuously, accelerating with e-commerce and reshoring.

A government-policy boom wearing a factory costume

Strip away the branding and much of this is a policy boom: tens of billions were allocated to construction inside a very short window, and the market is scrambling — often overpaying — to deliver. That makes the pipeline strong but dependent on political appetite that could fade.

The single biggest worry

If subsidies weaken or politics shift, the pipeline can shrink fast, leaving contractors with idle crews. Fabs routinely run 20–40% over budget, skilled labor is short everywhere, and building too many data centers at once could crater their returns.

The surprising part

These three booms aren't separate — they fight each other for the same electricians, concrete, and engineers in the same regions. A fab in Arizona and a data center in Texas bid up the same rare specialists, delaying each other. The real story isn't 'America builds again'; it's that policy dumped billions into construction all at once and everyone is scrambling to deliver.

In one sentence

A subsidy-and-fear-driven wave of fabs, battery plants, and data centers made specialized industrial builders the scarce resource — and risks overbuilding if the political money fades.
