Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

Who profits

In a shortage, money doesn't spread evenly — it pools at the choke points.

This is the follow-the-money piece. When a huge wave of building hits a labor-and-materials wall, the profit doesn't spread across the activity of building — it collects at the bottlenecks: the local aggregates monopolies, the specialty-trade owners with pricing power, the equipment-rental fleets, the engineering firms, the landowners, the PE roll-ups that own the crews. The person swinging the hammer earns wages; the person who owns the scarce thing gets rich.

The core bet

In a shortage, profit flows to whoever owns the bottleneck — the quarry, the crew, the rental fleet, the permit know-how — not to the general act of building. Own what can't be duplicated overnight and you win.

Wall Street bought the picks and shovels before the gold rush

Private equity started buying quarries, equipment fleets, and trade crews around 2018–2019 — right as the labor-shortage signals appeared — because they saw the rebuild coming. The glamorous businesses (big builders, name architects) get squeezed; the unglamorous ones (gravel, equipment rental, HVAC service calls, portable toilets) quietly compound into cash machines.

The single biggest worry

It's cyclical, so a downturn hits the whole chain at once — equipment goes idle, pits shut, crews consolidate at fire-sale prices. And the inflated costs get passed to homebuyers and taxpayers (a $500k home becoming $650k), breeding a backlash against the very rebuild that's supposed to help people.

The surprising part

The takeaway is almost a rule: when everything breaks at once and you can't build fast enough, money flows from the people doing the work to the people who own the scarce resources — the gravel, the crew, the fleet, the permit expertise — and it pools there until the cycle finally turns.

In one sentence

When you can't build fast enough, profit flows away from the hammer-swingers and pools with whoever owns the scarce input — the quarry, the crew, the equipment, the permit know-how.
