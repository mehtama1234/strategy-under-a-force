Infrastructure & the grid

Roads, water, and — above all — the power lines that everything else now waits on.

America's public infrastructure is old and graded poorly, and a $1.2 trillion law is finally paying to fix it. But the real bottleneck is electricity: demand is rising for the first time in decades — data centers, factories, EVs — and the grid can't keep up. The scarce thing is grid capacity and the crews and engineers who can expand it, and whoever builds transmission lines, substations, and hardened infrastructure captures decades of spend.

The core bet

The grid is the strangled asset. Twenty years of flat demand meant utilities stopped expanding, and now the wires, transformers, and substations can't handle the new load. The contractors who can design and build transmission fastest own the constraint that gates U.S. industrial growth.

The bottleneck isn't chips or panels — it's copper and steel

The least glamorous thing on this list turns out to be the master key: transmission lines and transformers. Data centers and solar farms can be built quickly, but the wires to move their power can't, so the whole energy transition and AI build-out backs up behind the grid.

The single biggest worry

Permitting and land rights kill more grid projects than money does — reviews and local opposition can add five years before a shovel moves. And the funding is one-time: the infrastructure money is spread thin across 50 states and runs down by the end of the decade, just as the work should be accelerating.

The surprising part

The twist nobody planned for: after 20 flat years, demand turned up and caught everyone off guard. Utilities built for 1980s-style growth are now near capacity in booming regions, throttling new hookups — so the constraint on AI, on factories, on EVs, all comes down to whether the grid can carry the load.

In one sentence

Rising power demand after two flat decades made grid capacity the scarce thing, and the crews who can build transmission and substations now own the constraint that controls everything else.
