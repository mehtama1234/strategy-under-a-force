Group C · The enablers & bottlenecks

Engineering & permitting

Every project starts with a design and a permit — and the permit is often the slowest part of all.

Behind every building is engineering and design — architecture, civil and structural engineering, surveying — and then a maze of permitting, environmental review, and zoning. The design firms are busy and consolidating; meanwhile the review process is frequently the single biggest delay in getting anything built. The firms that can navigate the maze fast are worth a premium, because the speed of the permit is often the speed of the project.

The core bet

The front end of every project — design, engineering, surveying, and getting the permit — is both scarce expertise and a chokepoint. Firms that know exactly which questions regulators will ask, and get through fast, charge for it.

Slow permits are a hidden tax — and a whole industry lives on it

Permitting slowness funds an ecosystem of environmental lawyers, permit consultants, and engineering overhead. That's why 'permitting reform' cuts both ways: faster reviews would let housing and clean energy actually get built, but they'd also drain a revenue stream that some firms quietly depend on.

The single biggest worry

If permitting reform truly works, the friction that pays consultants and lawyers shrinks. But if it doesn't, the review drag keeps strangling the very housing and clean-energy projects the country is trying to build — a lose-lose that's hard to escape.

The surprising part

The paradox: the biggest obstacle to building green energy is often the environmental review meant to protect the environment. A wind farm can wait a decade for approval while older fossil plants run on permits granted in the 1980s. The system protects whatever already exists — not by design, but by default.

In one sentence

Design and permitting are scarce expertise and the real chokepoint, so the firms that can steer a project through years of review the fastest earn a premium — and slow permits act as a hidden tax on everything.
