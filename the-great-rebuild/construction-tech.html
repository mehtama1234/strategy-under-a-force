Group C · The enablers & bottlenecks

Construction tech

If you can't hire enough workers, build the building in a factory — an idea that keeps almost working.

Because labor is the bottleneck, there's a long-running push to industrialize construction: prefabricated parts, modular building (whole rooms made in factories), and software to run bidding, design, and the jobsite. Construction is famously the least-digitized big industry, and its productivity has barely moved in decades. Crack it and you own a huge, stagnant market — but almost every attempt to 'Henry Ford' the building has stumbled.

The core bet

If you can't hire enough workers, make more of the building in a factory and run the site with software. Whoever truly cracks that owns an $11-trillion industry that hasn't improved in fifty years.

The money is in paperwork software, not sci-fi robots

Procore — essentially organized email and spreadsheets for the job site — is a multi-billion-dollar public company growing fast, while Katerra's AI-optimized modular factories burned $2 billion and vanished. The lesson keeps repeating: digitize the coordination first; the robot factory comes much harder.

The single biggest worry

Modular keeps collapsing when it tries to scale past 10–15% of the market. Factories need steady volume, but construction financing won't guarantee five years of orders; building codes fracture by region so a module built for one place fails inspection in another; and builders fear losing control of a custom product.

The surprising part

The deeper twist is that standardization is the enemy of the customer. Every attempt to mass-produce homes runs into the fact that each site is different and each buyer paying $500,000 wants something specific. So the industry stays low-tech not because it's dumb, but because its economics and its customers resist the factory.

In one sentence

Construction stays labor-heavy and low-tech because fragmented demand and shaky financing defeat the factory dream — so the money that works is boring software, not modular robots.
