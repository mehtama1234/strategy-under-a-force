Group B · The fragmented trades boom

The trades roll-up

Your local HVAC and plumbing shop is probably owned by a private-equity fund now.

Private equity discovered that the boring 'essential trades' — heating and air conditioning, plumbing, electrical, roofing — are a near-perfect target. Thousands of small shops, run by owners in their sixties with no kid to take over, throw off recurring service revenue and demand that never really stops. Buy hundreds of them cheap, bundle them into a regional platform, standardize the software, and the bundle is worth far more than the sum of its parts.

The core bet

The trade is essential, fragmented, and full of retiring owners — so bundle small shops into one platform and pocket the gap between what a small shop costs and what a big one is worth. The recurring maintenance contract is the fuel.

What the numbers say

The retiring-boomer wave is the real fuel

This isn't PE inventing consolidation — it's PE exploiting a generational exit. A plumbing shop owner who assumed he'd leave the business to nobody suddenly gets a call offering seven figures for it, and takes it. The fund gets a fresh asset at a discount to what the bundle will later fetch. It's the same machine as the wider roll-up wave, aimed at the trades.

The single biggest worry

As funds bid against each other, prices climb and the arbitrage shrinks. Service quality often slips after a buyout as corporate cost-cutting drives out veteran staff, and the same trades-labor shortage caps how fast any platform can actually grow — bundling shops doesn't create new plumbers.

The surprising part

The quiet reality: 'your local AC guy' increasingly answers to a private-equity fund three states away. The demand is recession-resistant (your furnace breaks regardless of the economy) and licensing keeps out competition, which is exactly why Wall Street wants it — and why the neighborly, independent version of the trade is fading.

In one sentence

Private equity is buying retiring owners' HVAC, plumbing, and electrical shops cheap, bundling them into platforms worth far more, and betting recurring service revenue justifies the markup.
