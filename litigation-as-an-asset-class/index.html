Litigation as an Asset Class

How the lawsuit became an investment you can buy shares in — and justice started trading like a stock.

The 33rd force. Because a court judgment doesn't rise and fall with the stock market, its payoff is "uncorrelated" — exactly what big investors crave. So outside money poured into lawsuits: funders bankroll cases for a cut, mass-tort firms run assembly lines that advertise for plaintiffs, claims get packaged into portfolios, insurers wrap the outcomes, and even law firms take outside capital. The one idea: a lawsuit became a bet you can buy shares in — and justice started trading like a stock.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did turning lawsuits into an asset class make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? A winnable claim became a financeable asset; the injured plaintiff became a commodity to recruit; and a court result became something to insure and trade. It genuinely widens access to justice — but it also turns the courtroom into a casino, rewards funders and lawyers over plaintiffs, and makes "will this suit pay?" the question, not "is this right?"
