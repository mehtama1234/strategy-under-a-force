Group A · The money behind the lawsuit

The mass-tort machine

"Have you or a loved one taken drug X?" is the front end of a billion-dollar industry that manufactures its own plaintiffs.

Those TV and online ads are the front door of a huge industry. Firms spend billions advertising to recruit thousands of plaintiffs, bundle them into multi-district litigation (MDLs) or mass torts — Roundup, talc, 3M earplugs, Camp Lejeune — and funders bankroll the ad spend. It's a financing arbitrage: spend upfront to gather claimants, then extract settlements far larger than any individual could win alone.

The core bet

Law firms and funders spend billions upfront recruiting sick plaintiffs, bundle them into consolidated federal cases, and extract mass settlements — with attorneys, claims administrators, and funders each taking a cut. Litigation became a commodity you manufacture demand for.

Injury as a manufactured product

Funders became venture-capital-style players: they don't just lend against settlements, they effectively own cases, hire expert witnesses, and sometimes fund the science that supports the claim. That flips the logic — instead of injured people finding lawyers, funders chase the plaintiffs who fit a causation theory they've already backed, pre-positioning ads and rosters to monetize the moment a courthouse door opens.

Who ends up ahead

Plaintiff firms, lead-generation ad companies, and the funders who bankroll the whole assembly line.

Who's exposed

Individual claimants (netting the least), and defendants facing an industrialized wall of bundled suits.

The single biggest worry

The lead-generation layer is opaque and can be predatory: it targets the sick, elderly, and veterans with false certainty ("you WILL get money"), sells the same person's details to multiple firms, and has almost no oversight. The poorest claimants often net the least because they get swept into the cheapest bundled deal without independent counsel.

The surprising part

Camp Lejeune is the clearest tell: funders knew the base's water was contaminated years before Congress opened the courthouse in 2022, so they pre-loaded ad campaigns and plaintiff lists to fire the instant the law passed. The injury was real, but the timing and scale of the litigation were a financial product, engineered in advance.

In one sentence

Mass torts turned injury from a legal right into a product — funders market the disease, lead firms sell the plaintiffs, and the machine extracts value by making compensation efficient rather than just.
