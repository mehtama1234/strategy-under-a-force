The synthesis · what repeats across all 13

The patterns

Line up every corner of the litigation economy, and the same handful of mechanics repeat — all of them turning a court case into a financial instrument.

Each page asks: what did turning lawsuits into an asset class make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

A court judgment doesn't move with the stock market, so its payoff is uncorrelated — the one thing Wall Street couldn't find anywhere else. That single financial fact pulled billions into lawsuits and rebuilt the whole system around a new question: not "is this right?" but "will this pay?" It really does let the little guy fight the giant — and it also turns justice into a product line where funders and lawyers profit and the injured keep the scraps.

The recurring patterns

Pattern 01

Uncorrelated returns pulled the money in

A verdict has nothing to do with the S&P 500, so pensions and endowments chasing diversification poured in — finance, not justice, drove the boom.

Pattern 02

Once fundable, a claim becomes tradeable

Pool cases into a portfolio and a binary bet becomes a bond — securitized, borrowed against, and sold on a secondary market.

the claim as assetinsured
Pattern 03

Capital manufactures the cases

Funders don't just fund existing suits — they advertise for plaintiffs, aggregate mass torts, and become partly the inventors of the disputes they finance.

mass tortspatent trolls
Pattern 04

Asymmetry is the business

The troll or funded plaintiff risks little; the defendant faces millions in fees even if they win. So settling weak claims becomes the rational move.

nuisance settlementsjury anger
Pattern 05

The injured keep the scraps

Funder cut, lawyer's third, admin fees — the person who bore the harm is last in line, sometimes paying 100%+ effective interest on their own future payout.

the split
Pattern 06

The funder's clock overrides the client's

A creditor, not an agent, the funder pushes to settle when its return is met — even if the plaintiff could hold out for more.

settlement pressureduty shift
Pattern 07

Industrialization lowers the bar to sue

Cheaper case processing (AI, ALSPs) doesn't cut litigation rates — it lets you file more, smaller cases and win by attrition, not merit.

throughput
Pattern 08

Invisible money drives the case

Anonymous — even foreign or sovereign — capital now owns chunks of court dockets, and the disclosure fight is about whether the court gets to see the balance sheet.

disclosuresuing countries
