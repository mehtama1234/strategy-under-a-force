# Litigation as an Asset Class — strategy under a force

The 33rd collection (Tier-3) — Family II (hidden-mechanism business models). Financialization cousin of everyones-a-bank + private-credit.

**The force:** the lawsuit became an investable financial asset. Outside investors now fund lawsuits in exchange for a cut of the winnings (litigation funding), mass torts run like an industry (TV ads recruiting plaintiffs, warehoused claims), patents are litigated as a business, legal claims trade like securities, and law firms themselves get bought by outside capital. A payout that used to be about justice is now a return uncorrelated with the stock market — so Wall Street money poured in. We ask: *what did turning lawsuits into an asset class make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** a lawsuit became a bet you can buy shares in — and justice started trading like a stock. Because a court judgment doesn't rise and fall with the economy, its payoff is "uncorrelated," which is exactly what big investors crave. So funders (Burford and others) bankroll cases, mass-tort firms run assembly lines that advertise for plaintiffs, and claims get packaged into portfolios. It genuinely widens access to justice for people who couldn't otherwise afford to sue — but it also turns the courtroom into a casino, rewards the funder and the lawyer more than the plaintiff, and makes "will this suit pay?" a question of returns, not right and wrong.
