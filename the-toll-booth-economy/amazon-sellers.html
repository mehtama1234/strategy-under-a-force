Group B · The marketplace tax

Amazon sellers — Amazon takes about half of a seller's sale

Most of what sells on Amazon is sold by other people. Amazon just takes the toll — a growing one.

Most physical goods on Amazon are sold by third-party sellers, not Amazon. Amazon takes a referral fee (~15%), then fulfillment fees, storage fees, and — increasingly — advertising fees to be visible at all. Stack them up and Amazon's cut of a seller's sale has climbed toward ~50%.

The stacked toll

~45–50%
Total share of a third-party sale Amazon now captures (2023–24) via referral + fulfillment + near-mandatory ads. The seller provides the product and the risk; Amazon provides the customers and takes the toll.

~60–70% of Amazon's physical goods are third-party. The FTC sued Amazon (2023) partly over how it coerces and squeezes sellers.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Sellers have no exit. Amazon is where the shopping happens, so even at a ~50% take, staying off it means near-zero sales — they absorb the fees or disappear.

The surprising part

Amazon's fee stack is technically optional — you don't have to use Amazon's fulfillment or buy ads — but operationally mandatory. A seller who declines them gets buried in search and makes nothing. That's the FTC's whole case: Amazon built a world where "optional" full compliance is required to compete. So a "successful" Amazon seller can still be left with razor-thin margins after the toll.

In one sentence

Amazon sellers in a nutshell

Amazon built a toll booth on the road to customers where most sellers now pay roughly half their revenue to stay visible — and can't afford to leave.
