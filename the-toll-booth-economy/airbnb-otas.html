Group C · The gig & travel tax

Airbnb & booking sites — 15–30% to a middleman that owns no beds

The hotel owns the rooms; Booking.com owns the customer. Guess who takes the bigger cut.

Online travel agencies — Booking.com, Expedia, Airbnb — sit between travelers and places to stay and take a cut of every booking. Booking and Expedia charge hotels 15–30% commission; Airbnb charges guests + hosts ~14–16% plus notorious "cleaning fees." They own no hotels and no homes, yet capture up to a third of the value.

The travel toll

15–30%
Hotel commission to Booking/Expedia per booking (up from ~10% a decade ago). Together they control ~60% of the global OTA market — so 80–90% of bookings flow through them, and hotels can't say no.

Airbnb's 2023 "junk fees" backlash forced it to finally show all-in prices — after guests found the final total 27–50% above the sticker rate.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Independent hotels are margin-squeezed: 20–30% to the OTA plus labor and property costs leaves little — pushing consolidation toward big chains that can negotiate. Hotels even bid to appear on the very sites they pay commission to.

The surprising part

Everyone loses except the middleman. Guests feel nickel-and-dimed by fees revealed at checkout; hosts and hotels feel squeezed by commission; and both blame each other — while the OTA, which owns no beds and takes no inventory risk, quietly captures a third of the transaction. The EU has fined Booking for unfair "parity" clauses, but the lock-in deepens.

In one sentence

Airbnb & booking sites in a nutshell

Booking.com and Expedia own the traveler but not the beds — charging hotels 15–30% while hiding fees from guests, leaving both sides angry at a middleman that skims a third and risks nothing.
