Group B · The marketplace tax

Shopify — the "friendly" platform still skims every sale

Shopify's whole pitch is "you own your customer, not us." Then it takes a cut of every sale anyway.

Shopify is the anti-Amazon: instead of a marketplace, it gives each merchant their own store, so they "own" their customers. But Shopify still runs a toll — a monthly fee plus a cut of every payment (Shopify Payments), plus app fees, plus lending. And that toll, not the subscription, is now most of its money.

The toll wearing a friendly face

76%
Share of Shopify's revenue (2025) from "merchant solutions" — payments, capital, shipping — i.e. taking a cut of transactions, not the subscription. It processed ~$378B of merchant sales in a year.
  • Shopify Payments takes ~2.4–2.9% + 30¢ per sale; use a rival processor and you pay an extra surcharge.
  • Shopify Capital has lent merchants ~$5B, repaid as a cut of daily sales — a second toll.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Its dominance rests on lock-in (data, apps, convenience), not a contract. If the ~2.5% payments take becomes too visible and irksome, merchants could migrate to cheaper processors or rival platforms.

The surprising part

The company that promises merchants "we're on your side, not Amazon" makes 76% of its money from the exact playbook Amazon uses — skimming a percentage of every sale. It just calls it "merchant solutions" instead of "commission." The toll-booth model, wearing the friendliest possible face.

In one sentence

Shopify in a nutshell

Shopify sells itself as the merchant's advocate, but earns most of its money as a payments-and-lending toll on the ~$378B of sales flowing through it — a friendlier toll booth, but a toll booth.
