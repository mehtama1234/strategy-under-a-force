Group B · The marketplace tax

Etsy & eBay — a tax on success that keeps creeping up

Platforms built on tiny independent sellers keep quietly raising the toll. The sellers protest — then stay.

Etsy (handmade sellers) and eBay charge a cut of each sale — and it keeps rising. Etsy raised its transaction fee to 6.5% in 2022, then piled on "Offsite Ads," payment fees, and listing fees, so the effective take on many sellers is 20%+. The sellers make all the products; Etsy owns the buyers and the search.

The creeping toll

~20%+
Effective total take on many Etsy sellers once "Offsite Ads" (12–15%, mandatory once you pass $10k/yr) stack onto the 6.5% fee, payment fees, and listing costs. eBay's "final value fee" runs ~13–15%.

The 2022 fee hike triggered a seller strike — 48,000+ signatures, thousands of shops paused. Etsy kept the increase anyway.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Sellers have no bargaining power — the platform owns the buyer funnel and the algorithm. Even after a strike, Etsy kept every increase and added more fees; sellers pay or vanish.

The surprising part

"Offsite Ads" is the clearest sign of the trap: Etsy charges a 12–15% commission on any sale within 30 days of an ad impression — even if the seller never asked for the ad and it had nothing to do with the sale. Sellers call it "a tax on success," and once you cross $10k/year you're not allowed to opt out. It's the toll-booth equivalent of charging for the road and the air above it.

In one sentence

Etsy & eBay in a nutshell

Etsy and eBay are unavoidable middlemen that trap small sellers with escalating, "optional-but-mandatory" fees — turning marketplace success into a shrinking margin that rewards the platform, not the maker.
