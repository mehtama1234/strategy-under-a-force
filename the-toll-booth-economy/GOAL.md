# The Toll-Booth Economy — strategy under a force

The thirteenth "strategy under a force" collection (from the discovery scout's queue). Sibling to the bank/ad/loyalty economies.

**The force:** platforms that own a gateway and take a cut ("take rate") of transactions they didn't originate — a tax on the work other people do. The app store takes 30% of a developer's sale; Amazon takes ~half of a third-party seller's revenue; Ticketmaster tacks on fees; the delivery app skims the restaurant. We ask: *what did the toll-booth economy make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**The signature insight (plain English):** own the gateway, and you can tax everything that passes through it. The platform does the connecting; the seller, driver, host, or creator does the actual work and takes the risk — but the platform, sitting in the middle, skims a cut of every transaction at near-zero marginal cost. Value flows to whoever controls the chokepoint; it drains from the "supply side" (sellers, gig workers, hosts, creators) who have nowhere else to go. And the toll tends to *ratchet up* over time, because the captured supply can't leave.
