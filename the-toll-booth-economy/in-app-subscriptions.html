Group A · The storefront tax

The subscription tax — so big that apps route you out to dodge it

Ever hit "you can't sign up in the app"? That's a company refusing to hand Apple a third of your subscription.

Apple and Google take ~30% (then 15% after a year) of subscriptions bought inside an app. That cut is so painful that the biggest apps — Spotify, Netflix — refuse to let you subscribe in the app at all, forcing you to their website. They'd rather annoy you than pay the toll.

The dodge, and the fight

  • Netflix stopped in-app sign-ups years ago; the app is login-only.
  • Spotify's complaint led to a €1.8B EU fine on Apple (2024); a 2025 US ruling forced Apple to allow web-payment links in the app.
  • Match Group (Tinder) is fighting the same fee in India.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The toll is so high it makes companies deliberately sabotage their own sign-up experience to push traffic outside — degrading the product industry-wide, because Apple controls the payment and thus the design.

The surprising part

The line is arbitrary. Uber and Amazon pay Apple zero commission on rides and physical goods — but Tinder pays 30% on the same in-app sign-up, because a dating subscription is a "digital good." A ride is "physical," a chat subscription isn't. So the category, not the complexity, decides the tax — and e-commerce escapes the toll while streaming and dating are trapped.

In one sentence

The subscription tax in a nutshell

The app-store cut on subscriptions is so large that Spotify, Netflix, and Tinder treat forcing you out to their websites as cheaper than letting you sign up in the app.
