The patterns

Line up everyone the toll booth touched, and one theme dominates: the platform that owns the gateway taxes the work everyone else does — and the toll only goes up.

Each page asks: what did the toll-booth economy make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable for this company? Thirteen answers later, the same tensions repeat.

The one thing to take away

Own a chokepoint the supply can't route around, and you can skim 15–50% of every transaction at near-zero cost — while the seller, driver, host, or creator does the real work and takes the risk. And once they're locked in, the toll ratchets up, because they can't leave.

The recurring patterns

Pattern 01

Own the gateway, tax what passes through

The platform doesn't make the product, drive the car, or write the song — it owns the door to the customer, and charges everyone who walks through it.

Pattern 02

The middleman captures more than the maker

The delivery app keeps more per meal than the kitchen; Ticketmaster's fee can top the artist's cut; the platform does the least and takes the most.

Pattern 03

Lock-in beats price

Steam's 30% survived a 12% rival because leaving means losing your library and friends. The toll persists not because it's cheap, but because you can't go.

SteamAmazonEtsy
Pattern 04

"Optional" fees that are really mandatory

You don't have to use Amazon's fulfillment or Etsy's ads — but skip them and you're invisible. The fee is optional in the contract, required in practice.

AmazonEtsy
Pattern 05

Hide the split, pocket the spread

Upfront pricing shows riders and drivers different numbers; junk fees appear only at checkout; the base price is kept low to hide the toll. Opacity is the tool.

ride-hailingtravelTicketmaster
Pattern 06

Get big, then squeeze — the ratchet

Start cheap to win the market; raise the cut once supply is captured. The toll almost never reverses, even against strikes and backlash.

the ratchetEtsy strike
Pattern 07

Even "friendly" platforms take the toll

Shopify promises "you own your customer" and still makes 76% of its money skimming payments. The toll wears a friendly face, but it's still a toll.

Shopify
Pattern 08

Only regulation or a real rival forces the toll down

The EU's DMA, the DOJ's Live Nation suit, the FTC's Amazon case — antitrust is the one force that's cracked the tolls, because captured supply can't discipline them.

The point of the series

This is the thirteenth "strategy under a force" collection. Its signature: "own the gateway, tax what passes through." And it rhymes with its siblings — the platform that runs the toll booth usually also runs an ad business and a payments business on the same customers. Same method every time: pick a force, ask what it made scarce/cheap/risky/valuable, group by the answer, and read the patterns.
