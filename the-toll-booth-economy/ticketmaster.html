Group D · The ticket, the creator & the ratchet

Ticketmaster — fees you can't escape on every ticket

It owns the venues, promotes the tours, and sells the tickets. So it can tax you at every step.

Ticketmaster (merged with Live Nation in 2010) controls ~70% of US ticketing and locks venues into exclusive deals. So it piles "service fees," "facility fees," and "convenience fees" onto the face price — often 20–44% extra — that fans can't avoid, because there's nowhere else to buy.

The unavoidable toll

up to 44% / $16.4B
How much fees can add to a ticket's face price — and the amount a US suit alleges Ticketmaster extracted in hidden fees over ~six years. It keeps the base price artificially low to make the sticker look affordable.
  • 2022: the Taylor Swift "Eras Tour" presale meltdown made it a national scandal.
  • 2024: the US DOJ and 29 states sued to break up Live Nation/Ticketmaster as a monopoly; a jury later found it held an illegal monopoly.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Vertical integration: Live Nation owns the venues, promotes the tours, and controls ticketing — so it can suppress rivals at every stage and has no incentive to lower fees, because it owns the whole chain.

The surprising part

Even after a jury found it an illegal monopoly, the fees keep coming — because the legal remedy (breaking it up) hasn't happened yet, and it's still the only way into most big venues. It's the purest toll booth in the collection: control the one gate fans must pass through, and you can charge whatever you like on the way in.

In one sentence

Ticketmaster in a nutshell

Ticketmaster is a court-proven illegal monopoly on the gate to live music, so it adds fees of up to 44% that fans can't resist — protected by exclusive venue deals and owning the whole chain.
