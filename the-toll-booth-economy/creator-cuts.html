Group D · The ticket, the creator & the ratchet

Creator platforms — sharecroppers who can't leave

The creator does 100% of the work and owns nothing. The platform takes a cut — and can evict them overnight.

Platforms that host creators — YouTube, Twitch, Patreon, Substack, OnlyFans — take a cut of everything creators earn, because they own the distribution and the payment relationship with the audience. The creator makes all the content and brings the audience; the platform, sitting in the middle, takes 10–50% and can change the rules at will.

The cut

10–50%+
YouTube keeps ~45% of ad revenue; Twitch took ~50% of subs; OnlyFans 20%; Substack 10%; Patreon ~8–12%. The creator does the creative work; the platform bears almost none of the risk.

Worse than the cut is the power: platforms can demonetize a creator, change the algorithm, or ban them — often with little notice and no recourse.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Creators have no remedy when the rules change. The platform owns the audience and the payment rails — so if it decides you're no longer profitable or "on-brand," your livelihood vanishes, and you can't take your audience with you because you never owned the relationship.

The surprising part

The creator does all the creative labor and owns no infrastructure, yet the platform keeps 10–50% while risking almost nothing — and rarely even shares the earnings data, so creators can't audit whether they're paid fairly. Even the most "creator-friendly" platform (OnlyFans) still takes 20% and can change terms unilaterally. The toll isn't transparent or inevitable — it's simply the price of access to an audience you built but don't own.

In one sentence

Creator platforms in a nutshell

Creators are sharecroppers on platforms they can't afford to leave — doing all the work, owning nothing, paying a 10–50% cut, and able to be demonetized or evicted overnight. (See also the attention economy.)
