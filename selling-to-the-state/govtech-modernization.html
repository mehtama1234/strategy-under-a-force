Group A · The customer that never churns

Govtech modernization

Government runs on 1970s software that no longer works — and ripping it out is so hard nobody dares.

States and cities run on software written in the 1970s and 80s — mainframe programs in COBOL that can barely do the job. Govtech companies sell modern replacements for unemployment systems, DMV records, permits, court files, and tax collection. The catch, and the prize, is the same: once a government buys one, ripping out a system that millions of people depend on is so risky that the vendor stays for decades.

The core bet

Government software is ancient and failing, and replacing it is a giant, slow, sticky market. Become the system of record for a city or state and you're there for decades, because no one dares switch.

What the numbers say

The year New Jersey's unemployment system was written — states literally asked retired COBOL programmers to return during the 2020 COVID surge.

$100B+

~100%
Retention for entrenched records/permitting vendors — customers complain, but they stay.

The misery of switching is the moat

A city can't move to a competitor because the cost, time, and risk of migrating millions of records are enormous. That looks like dysfunction, but for the incumbent it's the whole business: near-total retention on systems nobody dares to touch. The pain protects the winner.

The single biggest worry

The sale takes years, and the migration is terrifying — move millions of records wrong and people don't get unemployment checks or can't register their cars. Budget trouble or a change in political leadership can freeze a deal that was nearly done.

The surprising part

The thing everyone calls a bug — you can't switch — is the feature. The most boring vendors (records management, permitting) have the best economics precisely because the data is messy and the stakes are high, so no elected official will risk being the one who broke the DMV. Complaints are constant; churn is almost zero.

In one sentence

Government runs on 50-year-old software that barely works, replacing it takes years and millions, and once you're in, the customer can't leave.
