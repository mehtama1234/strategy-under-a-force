Group E · The big picture

Selling to the state

Agonizing to win, nearly impossible to be fired from — so the barriers themselves are the business.

Pull the whole collection together and one line runs through it. Government is agony to sell to — byzantine procurement, clearances, certifications, months of waiting — but once you're in, you almost never get fired. The barrier to entry is the moat. The pain of winning is so extreme that whoever survives it locks in a durability private customers can never match, and that durability becomes a license to earn on public money for decades.

The core bet

Government is agonizing to sell to but nearly impossible to be fired by, so the barriers are the moat — and whoever endures them becomes a near-permanent toll-taker on public money.

Winners and losers

The market sorts cleanly into who has cleared the barriers and who is still stuck outside them.

Who ends up ahead

Embedded incumbents and integrators (Tyler, Maximus, Palantir, Anduril, Booz Allen, Leidos), certified vendors with FedRAMP or cleared staff, benefits contractors wired into welfare and health systems, PE firms rolling up government services, and well-connected lobbyists.

Who's exposed

Startups that run out of cash in the 18-month procurement 'valley of death'; taxpayers paying premium prices for locked-in mediocrity; and the public when entrenchment and capture beat quality and price.

The single biggest worry

The same red tape that makes the revenue durable also keeps out better, cheaper competitors and rests on politics that can cut a program — or automate the moat away. Reform the rules and you destroy the moat; keep them and you trap government in expensive mediocrity.

The surprising part

The note to end on is the deepest irony of the whole force: the 'inefficient' customer everyone mocks is the most reliable revenue on earth, and the red tape everyone hates is exactly what makes the contracts worth billions. A Fortune 500 buyer can drop you tomorrow; a government renews because the alternative is chaos. It isn't a bug in the business model — it's the feature the whole thing is built on.

In one sentence

Government spending is so vast and so painful to access that whoever endures the procurement gauntlet locks in near-permanent revenue — making entrenchment itself the most valuable asset in the market.
