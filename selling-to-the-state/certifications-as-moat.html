Group C · Why it's hard — and why that's the moat

Certifications as a moat

A mediocre product with FedRAMP beats a brilliant one without it — because the paperwork is the product.

To sell cloud software or services to government, you often need expensive, slow certifications: FedRAMP for cloud security, CMMC for defense cybersecurity, security clearances for staff, StateRAMP for state buyers. Each takes years and millions to earn — which locks out smaller competitors and turns the certification itself into a durable advantage. Once you hold the stamp, you can sell where rivals legally cannot.

The core bet

A certification that costs years and millions to earn is a wall. Once you have FedRAMP or a cleared workforce, you sell where competitors legally can't follow.

What the numbers say

$1–2M+
Cost of FedRAMP authorization, on top of 12–24+ months of audits and continuous compliance.
~530
FedRAMP-authorized cloud services — versus tens of thousands of commercial SaaS products that can't sell to the feds.
300,000+
Defense contractors and subcontractors that CMMC (begun 2021) will eventually require to certify.
$5k–$15k
Cost per employee for a security clearance — 6–18 months each, against a backlog of 250,000+ pending.
2020
StateRAMP launched as a cheaper state-level analog to FedRAMP — still months of security work.

The stamp is worth more than the software

Agencies buy the certification as much as the product, so a mediocre tool with FedRAMP can beat a brilliant one without it — rivals simply aren't allowed to bid. The compliance paperwork, not the technology, is often the real asset on the balance sheet.

The single biggest worry

The wall can shrink if governments automate certification — FedRAMP reform aims to speed approvals, which would lower the cost of entry and erode the moat. Meanwhile the stamp is a forever cost: endless audits, re-audits, and security updates eat margin for years.

The surprising part

The strange conclusion is that in this market the security paperwork can be worth more than the innovation. A company can win government business with an inferior product purely because competitors can't clear the same hurdles — so the rational move is to invest in the certification as heavily as in the technology.

In one sentence

Certifications are expensive walls that lock competitors out of government sales entirely, making the stamp itself often worth more than the product it protects.
