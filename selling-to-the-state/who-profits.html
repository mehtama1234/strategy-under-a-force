Group D · The mechanism & the tensions

Who profits

Some of the biggest, safest companies you've never heard of live entirely on government money.

This is the follow-the-money piece. When government is the customer, profit pools with whoever has cleared the barriers and become embedded: the integrators and primes (Booz Allen, Leidos, SAIC, Accenture Federal), the sticky govtech and defense leaders (Tyler, Palantir, Anduril), the benefits contractors (Maximus), the certified cloud vendors, and the PE firms rolling up government-services businesses. It's low-glamour, high-retention, and increasingly financialized.

The core bet

The durable money goes to whoever has cleared the barriers and become embedded — the integrator, the certified vendor, the benefits contractor — not to whoever has the best product.

Private equity discovered government revenue is a bond

PE realized government contracts are steadier than credit-card debt — less churn, longer terms, no price wars — and started buying up gov-services and govtech firms accordingly. The result: some of the biggest, safest companies in America (Maximus out-earns Intel; Booz Allen employs armies of engineers) exist almost entirely outside public view.

The single biggest worry

Concentration is the danger: a handful of firms control the mission-critical systems for benefits, immigration, defense IT, and health data, and cost overruns get passed straight to taxpayers. Reform that opened procurement to startups or demanded open tooling could wipe out a big share of these valuations.

The surprising part

The quiet surprise is how invisible the winners are. The companies that profit most from government are precisely the ones with no consumer brand — the integrator, the eligibility contractor, the data-fusion vendor — and PE prized them exactly because that boring, unfireable government revenue is closer to a bond than to a normal business.

In one sentence

The durable money pools with the embedded incumbents and integrators — often unknown to the public and increasingly PE-owned — because government revenue is as steady and safe as a bond.
