# Selling to the State — strategy under a force

The 39th collection (Tier-4, seeded by the Inc 5000 analysis) — Family I (macro forces).

**Inc 5000 evidence:** "Regulated-Industry Software" is the 3rd-biggest fast-growing theme — 634 companies, ~$33.7B — full of firms selling software and services into government and heavily regulated sectors (govtech, compliance, public-safety, benefits, defense).

**The force:** government has quietly become one of the best customers in the economy — huge, reliable, recession-proof, and extremely sticky. A wave of govtech modernization (replacing 1970s mainframes), a defense-tech boom (Anduril, Palantir, a flood of venture money into defense), and an ever-growing compliance-industrial complex mean that selling to the state — slow, painful, and gated as it is — is where a lot of durable money now lives. We ask: *what did "selling to the state" make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly valuable?*

**Signature insight (plain English):** the government is a miserable customer to win and a wonderful one to keep. Winning a public contract is slow, bureaucratic, and gated by certifications and procurement rules — but that pain is the moat: once you're in, you almost never get replaced, the budget rarely disappears, and competitors are locked out by the same barriers you climbed. So the scarce, valuable thing is the ability to endure the procurement maze and hold the certifications; whoever can becomes a near-permanent toll-taker on public money.

**Groups:**
- **A — The customer that never churns:** govtech modernization (legacy systems) · defense & the new defense-tech · state & local government
- **B — The compliance-industrial complex:** regulatory & compliance software · benefits & money administration · public-safety & justice tech
- **C — Why it's hard, and why that's the moat:** the procurement maze · certifications as a moat (FedRAMP etc.) · lobbying & relationships
- **D — The mechanism & tensions:** why government became a great customer · who profits · the limits (politics, budget cycles, capture)
- **E — The big picture:** selling-to-the-state (capstone) + patterns
