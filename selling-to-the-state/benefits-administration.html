Group B · The compliance-industrial complex

Running the money

The government decides who gets benefits — but pays private firms to actually run the plumbing.

Government sends money to hundreds of millions of people — Medicaid, food stamps, unemployment, tax refunds — and hires private contractors to run the eligibility, call centers, and payments behind it. Companies like Maximus, Conduent, Gainwell, and Deloitte operate huge chunks of the safety-net back office. It's recurring, enormous, and almost entirely out of public view.

The core bet

The government decides who qualifies but pays private firms to run the plumbing — eligibility, call centers, payments — a huge recurring contract business hidden behind public programs.

What the numbers say

Annual revenue at Maximus, with ~40,000 employees; about half its business is benefits and eligibility work.
~72M
People on Medicaid, a program whose federal and state spending tops $600B a year.
~42M

13M+

1 in 3

A private company is the face of 'the government'

For millions of low-income people, the government isn't a faceless bureau — it's the voice at a Maximus or Conduent call center, the website that decides if they qualify, the system that sends (or withholds) the check. These firms employ more benefits caseworkers than most state governments, yet operate with little public visibility or accountability.

The single biggest worry

When a contractor's system wrongly denies someone or demands repayment, the agency takes the political heat while the contractor keeps the contract. And the revenue depends entirely on program funding — if politicians shrink a program, the contractor's business can vanish with no backup.

The surprising part

The surprise is how invisible it all is. The safety net that hundreds of millions rely on runs on outsourced contractors most people have never heard of — and because switching them out is so disruptive, they absorb little of the blame when the systems fail and keep the contracts anyway.

In one sentence

Private contractors quietly run the eligibility and payment plumbing of the entire safety net, capturing hundreds of billions in recurring work while the government absorbs the blame when it breaks.
