Group C · Why it's hard — and why that's the moat

The procurement maze

Everyone complains about the red tape. The incumbents quietly protect it — because it's the wall.

Government buying is famously slow: RFPs, sole-source rules, bid protests, set-asides, and endless paperwork, plus the 'valley of death' that kills startups before their first dollar. This is usually described as dysfunction. But for whoever has already gotten across, the same maze that nearly killed them now blocks everyone else — the barrier to entry doubles as a barrier to competition.

The core bet

The difficulty of selling to government isn't just a cost — it's a moat that protects whoever has already gotten through it. Cross the maze once and it locks rivals out behind you.

The complaint is the moat

Incumbents loudly complain about the red tape in public — then quietly protect it with every legal and political tool they own, funding trade groups and hiring compliance teams too expensive for any startup to match. Reforms (OTAs, SBIR fast-tracks) keep trying to lower the wall, but each one adds new paperwork, because the government is terrified of waste and fraud.

The single biggest worry

The maze burns money and slows down what government actually needs — faster tools, better AI. And if reform ever genuinely lowered the wall, it would flood the market with competitors and collapse incumbent margins, which is exactly why incumbents resist it.

The surprising part

The paradox worth sitting with: the thing everyone agrees is broken is also the thing that makes these contracts so valuable to hold. A frictionless government market would be a commodity market. The friction is what converts a one-time win into a decade of protected revenue.

In one sentence

The procurement maze is slow and expensive by design, which makes it worthless for new entrants and priceless for whoever has already made it through.
