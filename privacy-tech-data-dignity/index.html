Privacy Tech / Data Dignity

How the surveillance economy grew its own antibodies — and turned privacy into a product you buy, not a right you have.

The 32nd force, and the counter-market to surveillance as a product. Once everyone realized they were the product, privacy became something to sell (Apple, Signal, VPNs), to mandate (GDPR and a whole compliance industry), to engineer (on-device AI, encryption), and to demand payment for (data dignity). The one idea: privacy itself became something to sell — but it's turning into a luxury, not a right.

The one question, asked of everyone

What did the privacy backlash make scarce, cheap, safe, risky, or newly worth money? Trust and un-tracked attention became sellable; a compliance-and-deletion industry sprang up to service the law; and real privacy became a paid premium. The uncomfortable twist: the biggest winner was Apple, the compliance layer is mostly cookie-banner theater, and killing the cookie entrenched the giants — so "privacy" often means the opposite of what it promises.
