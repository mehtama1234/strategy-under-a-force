Group B · The compliance & deletion industry

Data-deletion services

A whole market now exists to charge you a subscription for the privilege of asking strangers to stop selling your life.

Because data brokers hold thousands of details on each person, a market appeared to hunt down and delete your data: DeleteMe, Incogni (from the VPN maker Surfshark), Kanary, plus features from Google ("Results about you") and Apple. You pay a subscription and they file opt-out requests to hundreds of brokers on your behalf. California's DELETE Act (2023) aims to build a one-stop deletion registry. It's a strange business: you pay to undo surveillance you never agreed to.

The core bet

Data brokers assembled dossiers — address, phone, finances, health hints — and sell them to employers, insurers, and marketers. Deletion services turn escaping that into a recurring subscription, because opting out of thousands of brokers by hand would cost more time than the fee.

A treadmill by design

Deletion services treat the symptom, not the disease. Brokers re-harvest from public records — court filings, property records, voter rolls — and from each other, so opting out is a perpetual treadmill. A deletion company's business model actually depends on your data reappearing; if brokers faced real consequences for re-harvesting, the market would collapse.

Who ends up ahead

Deletion-service subscriptions — a parasitic layer profiting off the absence of real data-broker regulation.

Who's exposed

Everyone whose data was taken without consent, now paying rent to chase it around forever.

The single biggest worry

The trap is that the whole market exists because the US has no comprehensive data-broker law. Deletion services sell a painkiller, not a cure — and because the data keeps coming back, you can pay every year and still never know where all of it lives.

The surprising part

The setup is backwards: you pay a subscription to undo a violation that was never consensual, and most customers are already victims — the service is reactive, not preventive. Even the free built-in tools from Google and Apple only cover their own surface (search, Siri history), not the broker networks underneath. Deletion services are really a canary for regulatory failure.

In one sentence

You pay a subscription to repeatedly ask strangers to stop selling your life — a market that only exists because regulators let the theft happen free.
